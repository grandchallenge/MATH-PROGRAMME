# Volume II — COMPREHENSION: How Computational Worlds Are Built

## Preflight contract

**Series:** TYPE THEORY — The Grand Unified Theory of Computation  
**Volume:** II  
**Title:** COMPREHENSION  
**Subtitle:** How Computational Worlds Are Built  
**Governing question:** Which formation principles permit new kinds of computational objects and dependencies to exist?

The word *comprehension* is used in the type-theoretic/contextual sense: the disciplined formation of dependent families and context extensions. It does **not** mean unrestricted set comprehension.

## Inherited machinery

Volume II inherits from Volume I:

- judgments `Γ ctx`, `Γ ⊢ A type`, and `Γ ⊢ a : A`;
- contexts as ordered declarations with freshness conditions;
- capture-avoiding substitution on terms;
- weakening and generalized substitution as metatheoretic patterns;
- the distinction between operational reduction and definitional equality;
- the distinction between declarative typing and algorithmic checking;
- the publication discipline that theorem statements name their exact calculus.

For self-containment, Chapters 1–2 reintroduce judgment, context, free variables, alpha-renaming, and capture-avoiding substitution, but only to the extent needed to explain *dependency*. Volume I is not reproduced.

## Exact formal calculus in scope

The full volume develops a deliberately small dependent core, **COMP**, in layers.

### COMP-0 — family formation kernel (Chapters 1–2)

Judgments:

- `Γ ctx`
- `Γ ⊢ A type`
- `Γ ⊢ t : A`

Contexts are telescopes:

`Γ ::= · | Γ, x : A`

with `Γ ⊢ A type` required before extension.

Type expressions in the initial kernel:

`A ::= Nat | Bool | Fin(t)`

Term expressions:

`t ::= x | 0 | succ(t) | true | false`

Formation of the primitive indexed family is intentionally minimal:

`Γ ⊢ n : Nat` implies `Γ ⊢ Fin(n) type`.

`Fin` is used first as an *opaque family former*. Constructors are introduced only when indexed data are studied later; the first tranche therefore tests dependency without prematurely importing inductive-family machinery.

### COMP-ΠΣ — dependent function/pair core (Chapters 3–5)

Adds:

- `Π(x:A).B`
- `Σ(x:A).B`
- lambda, application, dependent pairs, projections;
- beta computation for application and projections;
- conversion under definitional equality.

### COMP-I — indexed and inductive families (Chapters 6–9)

Adds selected strictly-positive indexed families such as vectors and finite sets, with explicit constructor and eliminator rules. This fragment is pedagogical, not an attempt at a general positivity checker.

### COMP-CHECK — bidirectional implementation fragment (Chapters 10–13)

Adds explicit annotations and a syntax-directed checking discipline sufficient for a small executable checker. Algorithmic equality is intentionally narrower than arbitrary extensional equality.

Universes are only previewed as *codes and decoding problems* in Chapter 12/13; a full internal universe hierarchy is reserved for Volume VI.

## Semantics in scope

### Operational semantics

COMP-0 has no nontrivial computation beyond canonical base terms. Reduction becomes substantive only after COMP-ΠΣ is introduced. The volume then uses a deterministic small-step relation for beta-reduction and projections, extended only where the relevant eliminators have been defined.

### Definitional/equational equality

Definitional equality is the compatible equivalence generated by the computation rules explicitly admitted in the current fragment, together with alpha-equivalence and congruence. No extensional function equality is assumed. Eta principles, where discussed, are labelled separately as optional/extensional principles rather than silently folded into the kernel.

### Model/denotational readings

A contextual/comprehension-category reading is introduced late in the volume as semantics for the syntax. It is not used as a replacement for syntactic derivations and is not claimed to be the only semantics of dependent type theory.

## Central distinctions not to collapse

1. A dependent family `B(x)` is a type expression indexed by a term; it is not a runtime dictionary mapping values to tags.
2. Context extension is ordered: later declarations may depend on earlier ones, not conversely.
3. Substitution in dependent type theory rewrites both terms **and types**, including trailing context declarations.
4. `Π(x:A).B(x)` reduces to the ordinary arrow only when `B` is constant in `x`; `Σ(x:A).B(x)` reduces to an ordinary product only under the same constancy condition.
5. Definitional equality is not propositional equality. Identity types are not silently imported from Volume VII.
6. Indexed families do not by themselves prove semantic correctness of an implementation; they rule out only the states excluded by their indices and constructors.
7. A universe code is not “the type of all types.” Full universe stratification is deferred to Volume VI.
8. Categorical comprehension is a semantic organization of dependency, not a claim that syntax literally *is* a category.

## Bibliography and historical attribution plan

Primary-source priorities include Martin-Löf on intuitionistic type theory, de Bruijn/Automath for dependent formal language, Cartmell on contextual/generalized algebraic theories, Coquand–Huet on the Calculus of Constructions, and Dybjer on inductive families. Modern expository anchors include Nordström–Petersson–Smith, Hofmann, Jacobs, Harper, and selected dependently typed programming literature.

Historical claims requiring explicit verification include:

- the chronology and terminology of dependent function/product types;
- when “contextual category” and “comprehension category” terminology enters the literature;
- the development of inductive families and indexed programming;
- the separation between Russell-style and Tarski-style universe presentations.

No novelty or priority claim will be inferred from absence of a citation.

## Pressure points against the grand-unification thesis

This volume must keep four possible failures visible:

1. More expressive dependency can make equality/checking harder; “more type theory” is not automatically “better computation.”
2. Formation rules describe admissible constructions but do not alone describe cost, probability, concurrency, or physical interaction.
3. Indexed types capture only invariants encoded in the indices; the unencoded world remains outside the guarantee.
4. A sufficiently expressive internal language risks paradox or undecidability unless formation principles are stratified or otherwise constrained.

If these pressures force a weaker series thesis, the volume should weaken it rather than hide the failure.

## Next-volume threshold

Volume II ends when a dependent type ceases to look merely like a data specification and begins to behave like a proposition whose inhabitant is evidence. That forces Volume III: **PROOF / PROGRAM — Logic Becomes Executable**.

The threshold is not the slogan “propositions are types.” It is the need to explain why the same introduction/elimination and normalization structures organize both logical proof and computational construction.
