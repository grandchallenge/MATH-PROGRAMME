\chapter{One Protocol Standing In for Another}

A type system becomes more useful when an implementation may safely provide behavior more specific than the interface demands. For finite synchronous sessions, subtyping turns that intuition into a replacement relation. The label rules are easy to reverse by accident, so this chapter derives them from who controls the choice.

\section{The bounded relation}
Write $S\subsess T$ when behavior $S$ may replace behavior expected at $T$ in the selected \texttt{PROTO-S1} endpoint model. Payload types are invariant in this teaching fragment. Send, receive, and terminal constructors therefore compare only equal payload classes and recursively related continuations.

\section{Offer more, select less}
For internal choice,
\[
\sel{l_i:S_i}_{i\in I}\subsess
\sel{l_j:T_j}_{j\in J}
\quad\text{when }I\subseteq J
\]
and corresponding continuations are related. The implementation selects among fewer possibilities than the context is prepared for.

For external choice,
\[
\bra{l_i:S_i}_{i\in I}\subsess
\bra{l_j:T_j}_{j\in J}
\quad\text{when }J\subseteq I.
\]
The implementation offers at least every label the peer may select.

\section{Finite substitutability}
\begin{theorem}[T4.18, bounded synchronous session subtyping safety]
For finite \texttt{PROTO-S1}, if $S\subsess T$ and an endpoint implementing $S$ is connected to a peer implementing $\dual T$, then every first communication action admitted by the substituting endpoint is compatible with the peer; after the action, the continuation pair satisfies the corresponding relation. Repeating the argument gives safety for every finite run.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By induction on the derivation of $S\subsess T$. Send/receive cases use payload invariance and the induction hypothesis. For internal choice, the substituting label set is a subset of the expected set, so every selected label is offered by $\dual T$. For external choice, the substituting endpoint offers a superset of the labels the peer may select. Terminal cases are immediate.
\end{proof}

\section{Why asynchronous subtyping is excluded}
With queues, output anticipation and reordering can make subtyping substantially more subtle and can affect decidability. T4.19 therefore remains an explicit non-result: this volume does not claim a complete or decidable general asynchronous session-subtyping relation.

\section{Do not confuse: traces and syntax}
\begin{warningbox}
The finite syntactic relation is one safe replacement criterion. It is not claimed to coincide with every semantic trace-refinement relation. Behavioral refinement depends on the observation model and semantics.
\end{warningbox}
