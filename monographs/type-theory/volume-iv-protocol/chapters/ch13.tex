\chapter{Processes as Proofs, Carefully}

Volume III showed that proof terms and programs can share a constructive syntax. Concurrency invites a further correspondence: linear-logic structure can be interpreted as communication structure. The result is powerful precisely when kept calculus-specific. A slogan such as 'cut is communication' is not a universal theorem about every process calculus.

\section{Why linear logic is suggestive}
Linear logic makes resource use explicit \cite{girard1987}. Session calculi also track obligations that are consumed as communication advances. This shared discipline motivated exact proof/process correspondences in specific systems, including work by Caires and Pfenning \cite{cairespfenning2010} and Wadler's CP/GV development \cite{wadler2014}.

\section{Exact correspondence needs an exact calculus}
Caires--Pfenning interpret session types as intuitionistic linear propositions for their calculus \cite{cairespfenning2010}. Wadler's CP presents a calculus where propositions of classical linear logic correspond to session types and relates it to GV \cite{wadler2014}. These are exact mathematical constructions with their own syntax, typing, and operational semantics. They do not license rewriting the current \texttt{PROTO-0} theorems as logical cut-elimination theorems without a translation and proof.

\section{Cut as channel composition, with conditions}
\begin{workedexample}
A restriction $(\nu x\,y)(P\mid Q)$ structurally resembles connecting two derivations through a private interface. In a propositions-as-sessions calculus, a precise translation can make such a statement theorem-level. Here the picture is only a comparison until we define and verify the translation.
\end{workedexample}

\section{Do not confuse: structural consonance with identity}
\begin{warningbox}
The series thesis benefits from exact correspondences, but it is weakened by pretending that nearby structures are identical. 'Proofs as processes' is a family of calculus-specific results. Session duality is not logical negation in general; process reduction is not proof normalization in general.
\end{warningbox}
