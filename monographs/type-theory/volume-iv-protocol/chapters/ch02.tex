
\chapter{Two Ends of One Obligation}

A protocol becomes useful only when two endpoints can meet. If one endpoint promises to send a
natural number, the peer must be prepared to receive one. If one endpoint selects a branch, the
peer must offer that branch. The relation connecting the two endpoint views is
\keyword{session duality}.

\section{Complementary views}

For the finite Gate-1 language, duality is defined structurally:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \dual{\send{A}{S}} &= \recv{A}{\dual{S}}, &
 \dual{\recv{A}{S}} &= \send{A}{\dual{S}},\\
 \dual{\mathsf{end!}} &= \mathsf{end?}, &
 \dual{\mathsf{end?}} &= \mathsf{end!}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus
\[
 \dual{\send{\Nat}{\recv{\Bool}{\mathsf{end!}}}}
 =
 \recv{\Nat}{\send{\Bool}{\mathsf{end?}}}.
\]

\input{plates/plate03}

\begin{warningbox}
Duality is not a generic synonym for logical negation. It is an operation on a particular session
syntax that swaps complementary communication actions. Chapter 13 later studies proof/process
correspondences under explicit translations; no such unrestricted identification is assumed here.
\end{warningbox}

\begin{theorem}[T4.1, finite duality involution]
For every finite Gate-1 session type \(S\),
\[
  \dual{\dual{S}}=S.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By structural induction on \(S\). If \(S=\mathsf{end!}\) or
\(S=\mathsf{end?}\), the claim follows from the two terminal clauses. If
\(S=\send{A}{T}\), then
\[
  \dual{\dual{\send{A}{T}}}
  =\dual{\recv{A}{\dual{T}}}
  =\send{A}{\dual{\dual{T}}}
  =\send{A}{T}
\]
by the induction hypothesis. The input case is symmetric.
\end{proof}

The theorem is small, but it carries a critical burden: it justifies treating duality as a
two-sided endpoint transformation for this finite syntax.

\section{Paired endpoints}

We write
\[
  (\nu x\,y)P
\]
for a restriction that creates paired endpoints \(x\) and \(y\) inside \(P\). If \(x\) is governed
by \(S\), then \(y\) is governed by \(\dual{S}\).

Consider
\[
(\nu x\,y)(
  x!\chanval{42}.x?(b).\mathsf{close}\;x.0
  \mid
  y?(n).y!\chanval{\mathsf{true}}.\mathsf{wait}\;y.0
).
\]

The first visible pair of prefixes are complementary. A synchronous reduction can transfer the
value \(42\) from the sender to the receiver:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&(\nu x\,y)(
  x!\chanval{42}.P
  \mid
  y?(n).Q
)\\
&\qquad \to
(\nu x\,y)(
  P
  \mid
  Q[42/n]
).
\end{aligned}
\]

\input{plates/plate04}

\begin{formal}
The process reduction is accompanied by evolution of the channel environment.
If the pre-step environment contains
\[
  x:\send{A}{S},\qquad y:\recv{A}{\dual{S}},
\]
then after communication the corresponding obligations are
\[
  x:S,\qquad y:\dual{S}.
\]
We write this abstractly as
\[
   \Delta \envstep \Delta'.
\]

The later subject-reduction theorem will state that process reduction and environment evolution
remain synchronized.
\end{formal}

At Gate 1 we prove only principal-redex compatibility. The full subject-reduction induction is a
Chapter-3 obligation.

\begin{proposition}[Principal send/receive compatibility]
Suppose the restricted endpoints \(x,y\) carry dual finite session types whose first actions are
\(\send{A}{S}\) and \(\recv{A}{\dual{S}}\), and suppose the sent value has payload type \(A\).
Then the principal synchronous communication step advances the endpoint obligations to
\(S\) and \(\dual{S}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from the output/input typing premises, the duality definition, and the principal
communication reduction. The receiver continuation substitutes a value already typed at \(A\);
the remaining channel obligations are exactly the continuations recorded by the two session
constructors. This proposition intentionally does not yet claim preservation under arbitrary
evaluation contexts or structural congruence.
\end{proof}

\section{Linearity: one obligation, one owner in the derivation}

A process may use parallel composition:
\[
   P\mid Q.
\]
To keep a linear endpoint from being independently consumed by both sides, the channel
environment is split:
\[
\frac{
  \ptypes{\Gamma}{\Delta_1}{P}
  \qquad
  \ptypes{\Gamma}{\Delta_2}{Q}
  \qquad
  \Delta=\Delta_1\uplus\Delta_2
}{
  \ptypes{\Gamma}{\Delta}{P\mid Q}
}.
\]

The disjoint union symbol matters. It means that a linear channel binding belongs to at most one
premise.

\input{plates/plate05}

\begin{proposition}[T4.2, disjoint linear splitting]
If \(\Delta=\Delta_1\uplus\Delta_2\), then no endpoint binding occurs in both
\(\Delta_1\) and \(\Delta_2\). Conversely, the union reconstructs exactly the bindings of
\(\Delta\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is a property of the finite-map splitting relation used by `PROTO-0`. The derivation of a
split is built only from assigning each distinct binding to exactly one side. Induction on that
construction gives disjointness and reconstruction.
\end{proof}

\begin{warningbox}
A linear typing derivation controls formal use of an endpoint name. It does not prove that a
compiler, operating system, broker, network stack, or physical transport maintains unique
ownership. The implementation theorem would require an additional compilation/runtime model.
\end{warningbox}

\section{What duality prevents}

Two peers can have the same payload vocabulary and still be incompatible.

For example,
\[
S_1=\send{\Nat}{\recv{\Bool}{\mathsf{end!}}},
\qquad
S_2=\send{\Nat}{\recv{\Bool}{\mathsf{end!}}}.
\]
Both attempt to send first. They are not dual. Under the synchronous semantics there is no
principal send/receive redex between them.

The dual peer is instead
\[
\dual{S_1}=\recv{\Nat}{\send{\Bool}{\mathsf{end?}}}.
\]

\begin{workedexample}
The laboratory compares peers structurally rather than by checking only their payload multiset.
It therefore rejects the pair
\[
 \send{\Nat}{\recv{\Bool}{\mathsf{end!}}}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \send{\Nat}{\recv{\Bool}{\mathsf{end!}}},
\]
even though both mention exactly one \(\Nat\) and one \(\Bool\).
\end{workedexample}

\section{Safety is not enough}

It is tempting to jump from ``the endpoints are dual'' to ``the whole distributed program cannot
get stuck.'' That inference is false once a process can interleave several sessions.

Suppose one process waits on channel \(x\) before sending on \(u\), while another waits on \(v\)
before sending on \(y\), and the restrictions pair \(x\) with \(y\) and \(u\) with \(v\).
Each individual endpoint can be assigned a locally compatible session type, yet the network can
form a cycle of waits with no enabled communication.

\input{plates/plate06}

This is the most important negative boundary in the volume.

\begin{principle}
Session fidelity is a local type-preservation property. Global liveness requires additional
structure or hypotheses. Do not obtain deadlock freedom by renaming fidelity.
\end{principle}

The full counterexample is developed in Chapter 6. Gate 1 includes the plate now because the
reader must know from the beginning that the volume will not hide this distinction.

\section{Computational laboratory}

\begin{labbox}
Run
\[
  \texttt{python labs/lab02_duality_checker.py}.
\]
The laboratory parses finite sessions, computes duals, verifies involution over a fixture set,
accepts exact dual peers, and rejects same-direction or label-incompatible peers.
\end{labbox}

The checker is executable evidence for the finite syntax. It is not a proof of every theorem in

\section{Exercises}

\subsection*{Checkpoint}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{2.\arabic*}]
\item Compute
\(\dual{\send{\Nat}{\recv{\Bool}{\mathsf{end!}}}}\).
\item State the dual of \(\mathsf{end!}\) and explain why using one undirected terminal constructor
would hide a protocol obligation in this teaching calculus.
\item In one sentence, distinguish session duality from logical negation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Core}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{2.\arabic*},start=4]
\item Write the two endpoint types for a protocol in which the client receives a Boolean and then
sends a natural number before the client waits for close.
\item Give a principal synchronous reduction for
\((\nu x\,y)(x!\chanval{7}.P \mid y?(n).Q)\).
\item Explain why the parallel typing rule uses
\(\Delta_1\uplus\Delta_2\) rather than ordinary union.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Synthesis}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{2.\arabic*},start=7]
\item Compare ``dual endpoints'' with ``deadlock-free network.'' Give a concrete reason the first
does not imply the second in a multi-session setting.
\item Compare formal linearity of an endpoint variable with unique ownership of a real socket.
List two implementation assumptions needed to connect them.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Proof Workshop}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{2.\arabic*},start=9]
\item Prove T4.1 for the finite Gate-1 syntax using structural induction.
\item Prove that if a finite channel environment is split by assigning every binding to exactly one
of two subenvironments, then the two subenvironments are disjoint and reconstruct the original.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Design Clinic}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{2.\arabic*},start=11]
\item Design an error report for a duality checker that distinguishes payload mismatch, direction
mismatch, and premature termination. Explain why one generic ``protocol mismatch'' message loses
useful evidence.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Challenge}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{2.\arabic*},start=12]
\item Construct a two-session cyclic wait in prose or process pseudocode. Assign locally compatible
session actions to every endpoint, then identify the wait-for cycle. State precisely which property
fails and which local safety property can remain intact.
\end{enumerate}
