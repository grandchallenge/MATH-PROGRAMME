\chapter{Quantifiers Are Dependent Construction}
\label{ch:quantifiers}

The propositions-as-types reading becomes substantially more expressive when the type of the evidence may depend on a value. Volume II developed the dependent formation rules. Here we reuse them without changing their meaning and ask what logical construction they encode.

For a family $x:A\vdash B(x)\type$, a term of $\Pi(x:A).B(x)$ is a construction that, given each $a:A$, produces evidence of $B(a)$. A term of $\Sigma(x:A).B(x)$ is a witness $a:A$ paired with evidence of $B(a)$.

\begin{formal}[title={Dependent universal/existential reading}]
The core rules used here are
\[
\frac{\Gamma,x{:}A\vdash b:B(x)}{\Gamma\vdash\lambda x.b:\Pi(x{:}A).B(x)}
\qquad
\frac{\Gamma\vdash f:\Pi(x{:}A).B(x)\quad\Gamma\vdash a:A}{\Gamma\vdash f\,a:B(a)}
\]
and
\[
\frac{\Gamma\vdash a:A\quad\Gamma\vdash b:B(a)}{\Gamma\vdash\langle a,b\rangle:\Sigma(x{:}A).B(x)}.
\]
Elimination for $\Sigma$ exposes a witness together with its dependent evidence under the usual substitution discipline.
\end{formal}

\begin{proposition}[Rule-level quantifier correspondence]
Under the $\mathrm{CHD}\text{-}1$ reading, dependent product introduction/elimination has the constructor/eliminator shape of constructive universal introduction/instantiation, and dependent sum introduction/elimination has the witness/evidence shape of constructive existential introduction/elimination.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Read the displayed premises and conclusions propositionally. $\Pi$ introduction abstracts over an arbitrary $x:A$ and returns evidence whose type is $B(x)$; elimination instantiates at a supplied $a$. $\Sigma$ introduction supplies both the witness and evidence at that witness. Its eliminator binds both pieces before producing the motive result. The correspondence is rule-by-rule; no normalization result beyond the separately cited dependent metatheory is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{workedexample}[title={Finite universal construction}]
Let $\mathrm{Bit}$ have constructors $0,1$, and let $B(b)$ be a family whose evidence records that flipping twice returns $b$. A dependent function
\[
f:\Pi(b:\mathrm{Bit}).B(b)
\]
contains a method that works for either input. By contrast, a dependent pair
\[
p:\Sigma(b:\mathrm{Bit}).B(b)
\]
contains one selected bit and evidence about that bit. Universal construction supplies a procedure; existential construction supplies a witness.
\end{workedexample}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{6.\arabic*.}]
\item \textbf{Checkpoint.} Read $\Pi(x:A).B(x)$ constructively.
\item \textbf{Checkpoint.} Read $\Sigma(x:A).B(x)$ constructively.
\item \textbf{Checkpoint.} What substitution occurs when a dependent function is applied to $a:A$?
\item \textbf{Core.} Write the type of a function returning a proof family over a two-element domain.
\item \textbf{Core.} Construct a dependent pair and identify its witness and evidence components.
\item \textbf{Core.} Explain why ordinary $A\to B$ is a special case of $\Pi$ when $B$ does not depend on $x$.
\item \textbf{Synthesis.} Compare existential evidence with a database record whose second field is indexed by the first.
\item \textbf{Synthesis.} Explain why witness erasure can change the computational interpretation of an existential proof.
\item \textbf{Proof Workshop.} Prove the rule-level $\Pi$ correspondence by induction on the selected derivation forms.
\item \textbf{Proof Workshop.} State the substitution side condition needed by $\Sigma$ elimination.
\item \textbf{Design Clinic.} Specify a finite-domain checker for dependent witness packages and one malformed package it must reject.
\item \textbf{Challenge.} Identify exactly which $\mathrm{CH}\text{-}0$ metatheorems would require new proof obligations before being claimed for a richer dependent calculus.
\end{enumerate}
