\chapter{Solutions to Chapter 1}
\sol{1.1}{$\lambda x{:}P.x:P\to P$ by variable then implication introduction.}
\sol{1.2}{$\to E$: from $\Gamma\vdash f:A\to B$ and $\Gamma\vdash a:A$, infer $\Gamma\vdash f\,a:B$.}
\sol{1.3}{$\Gamma\vdash A$ hides the selected witness; $\Gamma\vdash t:A$ records it. Hiding a witness does not equate all witnesses.}
\sol{1.4}{Use $\lambda x{:}P.\lambda y{:}Q.x:P\to(Q\to P)$.}
\sol{1.5}{One term is $\lambda f.\lambda g.\lambda x.g(fx)$ with the declared types; it is function composition in the stated argument order.}
\sol{1.6}{Type $x:P$, then $f x:Q$, then $g(fx):R$; both application heads must have matching arrow domains.}
\sol{1.7}{Exact: the premises/conclusion of $\to I/\to E$ determine lambda/application. Difference: a derivation tree and a compact bound term have different bureaucracy.}
\sol{1.8}{The chapter relates logical and type constructors; it does not quotient data-bearing types to truth values.}
\sol{1.9}{Induct on typing; variable and application are immediate, while abstraction may require alpha-renaming before weakening.}
\sol{1.10}{Induct on the final natural-deduction rule; assumption, $\to I$, and $\to E$ are exhaustive in the implication fragment.}
\sol{1.11}{A correct design parses right-associative arrows into the checker AST and rejects dangling arrows, unbalanced parentheses, and reserved tokens as atoms.}
\sol{1.12}{With rule-node/AST-constructor size, the selected assignment can be size-preserving; counting discharge/context annotations introduces administrative overhead. Credit requires the metric first.}

\sol{2.1}{$(\lambda x{:}P.x)u\step u$.}
\sol{2.2}{Premises: $\Gamma,x{:}A\vdash t:B$ and $\Gamma\vdash u:A$; conclusion $\Gamma\vdash t[u/x]:B$.}
\sol{2.3}{Rename when a binder would capture a free variable of the replacement.}
\sol{2.4}{The term reduces first to $\lambda x{:}P.(\lambda z{:}P.z)x$ and then to $\lambda x{:}P.x$, preserving type $P\to P$.}
\sol{2.5}{For $(\lambda x{:}P.\lambda y{:}Q.x)y$, rename the inner binder before substitution, yielding $\lambda y_1{:}Q.y$.}
\sol{2.6}{Invert application and abstraction typing, then apply substitution to the body.}
\sol{2.7}{Proof detour elimination substitutes the supplied derivation for a discharged assumption; beta reduction performs the corresponding capture-avoiding term substitution under the assignment.}
\sol{2.8}{The reduction relation permits compatible redexes; a deterministic evaluator chooses one next redex and therefore one path.}
\sol{2.9}{Alpha-rename the binder fresh for $u$, weaken $u$ into the extended context, apply the induction hypothesis, and reintroduce the lambda.}
\sol{2.10}{Use the induction hypothesis on the function subterm and reapply application typing.}
\sol{2.11}{A stable path alphabet such as \texttt{fn,arg,body} is sufficient; recheck that the recorded path identifies a redex and preserves the invariant type.}
\sol{2.12}{Strong normalization forbids every infinite reduction sequence; termination of one strategy, and especially termination on fixtures, is strictly weaker.}

\chapter{Solutions to Chapter 6}
\sol{6.1}{$\Pi(x:A).B(x)$ is a construction producing evidence of $B(a)$ for every supplied $a:A$.}
\sol{6.2}{$\Sigma(x:A).B(x)$ packages one witness $a$ and evidence of $B(a)$.}
\sol{6.3}{Application substitutes the supplied $a$ for $x$ in the codomain, yielding $B(a)$.}
\sol{6.4}{For a two-element domain, a term $f:\Pi(b:\mathrm{Bit}).B(b)$ supplies both indexed cases through one dependent function.}
\sol{6.5}{A package $\langle a,b\rangle$ has witness $a:A$ and evidence $b:B(a)$.}
\sol{6.6}{If $B$ is constant in $x$, $\Pi(x:A).B$ reduces to the ordinary nondependent function shape $A\to B$.}
\sol{6.7}{Like an indexed record, the type of the second field is determined by the first field's value.}
\sol{6.8}{Erasing the witness can destroy the value that downstream computation needs, so erasure requires a relevance theorem/policy.}
\sol{6.9}{Read each dependent introduction/elimination premise propositionally and map it to the matching constructor/eliminator; no additional cases are needed for the displayed fragment.}
\sol{6.10}{The motive/branch must remain well formed after capture-avoiding substitution of the witness and dependent evidence.}
\sol{6.11}{Enumerate the finite witness domain, recompute the registered predicate for each package, and reject witnesses outside the domain or evidence with the wrong indexed claim.}
\sol{6.12}{Substitution, preservation, progress/canonical forms, and normalization all require fresh dependent proofs before transfer to a richer calculus.}

\chapter{Solutions to Chapter 7}
\sol{7.1}{Cut composes $\Gamma\Rightarrow A$ and $\Delta,A\Rightarrow C$ into $\Gamma,\Delta\Rightarrow C$.}
\sol{7.2}{$A$ is the cut formula: the explicit intermediate proposition introduced by one premise and consumed by the other.}
\sol{7.3}{The first derivation supplies an intermediate object that the second derivation uses; eliminating the intermediate composes the proofs directly.}
\sol{7.4}{Any correct sequent derivation that derives an implication then cuts it into a consumer earns credit if the end sequent is preserved.}
\sol{7.5}{Under the ND/term translation, substitute the proof/term for the open assumption corresponding to the cut formula.}
\sol{7.6}{One standard measure is lexicographic: cut-formula complexity first, then proof height or sum of premise heights.}
\sol{7.7}{Both remove an intermediate proof detour, but their syntax and local transformations are different and related by translation.}
\sol{7.8}{Finite traces cover only enumerated proof trees; Hauptsatz quantifies over every derivation in the calculus.}
\sol{7.9}{Expose the final implication-right and implication-left rules and replace the cut on $A\to B$ by cuts on structurally smaller components/premises.}
\sol{7.10}{Commute a cut upward past an inference in which the cut formula is not principal, preserving the end sequent while reducing height.}
\sol{7.11}{Store rule tag, premise trees, end sequent, and optional cut formula; validate the conclusion from the rule and premises.}
\sol{7.12}{Cut admissibility requires an induction proving every derivation with cut can be transformed into a cut-free derivation of the same end sequent.}

\sol{8.9}{Induct on formula structure; for implication use transitivity of the accessibility relation to propagate the universal extension condition.}

\chapter{Solutions to Chapter 11}
\sol{11.1}{Evaluation runs an already executable term; extraction transforms a proof-bearing artifact into the program intended for execution.}
\sol{11.2}{The selected existential extractor retains the witness and drops the already-checked certificate.}
\sol{11.3}{Replay verifies that the exact retained witness still satisfies the registered decidable specification.}
\sol{11.4}{Example: witness $2$ for $n^2=4$ over the finite domain, paired with a checked certificate.}
\sol{11.5}{The runtime needs the witness; the proof certificate may be dropped only after the pipeline has checked it under the stated policy.}
\sol{11.6}{Pair witness $3$ with a forged certificate claiming $3^2=4$; the checker must reject it before extraction.}
\sol{11.7}{Proof irrelevance concerns equality/observability of proofs; extraction is a transformation deciding what evidence is retained at runtime.}
\sol{11.8}{Classical axioms, opaque constants, effects, unsafe casts, or external oracles are acceptable examples.}
\sol{11.9}{Acceptance establishes predicate($a$); extraction returns that same $a$; replay recomputes the same predicate, hence succeeds.}
\sol{11.10}{A stronger theorem would quantify over all well-typed sources and prove compilation/erasure semantic preservation, including every source construct and runtime rule.}
\sol{11.11}{Checker validates proof-bearing input; extractor transforms only validated artifacts; replay independently checks the retained result/specification pair.}
\sol{11.12}{Execution validates one pipeline/output relation; it cannot detect that the formal source statement was an incorrect encoding of the intended informal theorem.}

\sol{13.2}{The kernel need not trust an elaborator success flag; it receives only explicit syntax/context/type data that it reparses and checks.}
\sol{13.3}{At minimum the kernel implementation, parser, runtime/hardware, foundational rules, and correctness of the formalized statement remain trusted obligations.}

\sol{13.5}{If elaborator and kernel share a buggy parse result, the apparent independent check may inherit the same interpretation error.}
\sol{13.6}{Formalize the unintended theorem $P\to P$ when the prose intended a stronger claim; the proof can be fully correct for the wrong statement.}
\sol{13.7}{Both carry an explicit object that a smaller verifier can check without trusting the producer's entire reasoning process.}
\sol{13.8}{A smaller checker reduces the trusted surface and improves auditability; bugs, foundational assumptions, and specification errors remain possible.}
\sol{13.9}{By interface definition the kernel discards producer authority and recomputes derivability from explicit inputs, so acceptance is a function of kernel rules and object only.}
\sol{13.10}{Parser correctness is a separate obligation: the checked AST must represent the supplied serialized certificate without ambiguity or producer-controlled reinterpretation.}
\sol{13.11}{Use explicit context, term, claimed type, and structured diagnostics; avoid accepting opaque producer-side validation tokens.}
\sol{13.12}{Credit requires identifying informal specification, formal statement, elaboration, kernel, compiler/runtime, OS/hardware; formal proof primarily addresses derivability inside the formal statement/calculus.}

\chapter{Solutions to Chapter 14}
\sol{14.1}{A service can be correct precisely by remaining available for further legal messages; termination may mean service failure.}
\sol{14.2}{Examples: who sends first, allowed message order, peer duality, resource linearity, or liveness.}
\sol{14.3}{A handler may terminate on each message while the sequence of messages violates the protocol transition relation.}
\sol{14.4}{Example: states \texttt{start} and \texttt{await\_ack}; \texttt{hello} moves to await-ack and \texttt{ack} returns to start.}
\sol{14.5}{An \texttt{ack} in the initial state violates the request-before-response ordering.}
\sol{14.6}{Safety: every observed transition is protocol-legal. Liveness: under stated scheduling/peer assumptions an enabled conversation eventually makes required progress.}
\sol{14.7}{Session fidelity concerns conformance to typed communication structure; distributed correctness additionally depends on failures, scheduling, networks, fairness, persistence, and other system assumptions.}
\sol{14.8}{Strongest surviving form: exact rule/term and principal normalization correspondences in scoped constructive calculi, with translations or added hypotheses required beyond them.}
\sol{14.9}{Use two terminating local handlers plus an illegal initial \texttt{ack}; local termination holds while the trace checker rejects the first transition.}
\sol{14.10}{Global progress needs complementary endpoint typing plus assumptions about channel semantics, scheduling/fairness, recursion, and absence/handling of failures appropriate to the theorem.}
\sol{14.11}{Expose endpoint role, protocol state, permitted send/receive labels and payloads, transition targets, resource usage, and recursion/termination points.}
\sol{14.12}{Minimum vocabulary: channels/endpoints, duality, send/receive, branch/select, protocol state, linear/resource discipline, recursion, operational semantics, fidelity, progress, and deadlock hypotheses.}
