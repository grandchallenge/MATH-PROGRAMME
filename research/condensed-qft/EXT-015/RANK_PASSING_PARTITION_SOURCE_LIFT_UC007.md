## 2. The scalar Fourier-kernel lemma

Put $r\in\mathbb R^3$ and

$$

\tag{UC7.5}
$$

**Proof.** Let

$$
g(\zeta)=a(\zeta/|\zeta|)e^{-E(\zeta)/2},\qquad
F=\mathcal F^{-1}g,\qquad
\ell_\epsilon=\mathcal F^{-1}\bigl(\psi a e^{-\epsilon^2E/2}\bigr).
\tag{UC7.6}
$$

The values at the single point zero do not affect these locally integrable Fourier amplitudes. Ellipticity on the support of $a$ makes $g$ and all its derivatives rapidly decreasing at infinity. All polynomial moments of $g$ are integrable, so $F$ is smooth. The amplitude for $\ell_\epsilon$ is compactly supported, integrable at zero, and all its physical Fourier moments and regulator derivatives are uniformly integrable on compact regulator intervals including zero. Thus $\ell$ is smooth jointly in $(\epsilon,r)$ through zero. Exact scaling gives

$$
k_\epsilon(r)=\epsilon^{-3}F(r/\epsilon)-\ell_\epsilon(r).
\tag{UC7.7}
$$

We record the tail estimate which is stronger than mere smoothness of $F$. For $j\ge0$ let $h_j(\zeta)=a(\zeta/|\zeta|)(-E(\zeta)/2)^j/j!$, a locally integrable tempered homogeneous distribution of degree $2j$. Its inverse Fourier transform $f_j$ is homogeneous of degree $-3-2j$ and smooth away from zero. The latter assertion follows by a dyadic annular decomposition and integration by parts in a Fourier direction parallel to $r$; arbitrarily many integrations make each annular sum and all its derivatives converge uniformly for $r$ in a compact annulus. Polynomial components of $h_j$ can contribute distributions supported at $r=0$; they contribute zero to the asserted smooth annular function.

For completeness the same decomposition controls the remainder, rather than treating a formal homogeneous expansion as an estimate. Subtract a cutoff near zero times $\sum_{j<N}h_j$ from $g$. On the dyadic annulus $|\zeta|\asymp t\le1$ its differentiated remainder is $O(t^{2N-|\alpha|})$. After $\beta$ derivatives in $r$, the Fourier integral on that annulus is bounded by

$$
C_{N,\beta,M}t^{3+2N+|\beta|}(1+t|r|)^{-M}.
\tag{UC7.8}
$$

Sum separately over $t\le|r|^{-1}$ and $|r|^{-1}\le t\le1$, choosing $M>3+2N+|\beta|$. The result is $O(|r|^{-3-2N-|\beta|})$ for $|r|\ge1$. The part away from zero is rapidly decreasing after Fourier transformation, by arbitrarily many integrations by parts. Replacing the cutoff homogeneous terms by the full $h_j$ changes their inverse transforms by functions rapidly decreasing on $|r|\ge1$: outside a neighborhood of zero their amplitudes are classical symbols, and sufficiently many derivatives of each amplitude are integrable. Consequently

$$
\partial_r^\beta\left(F(r)-\sum_{j<N}f_j(r)\right)
=O\bigl(|r|^{-3-2N-|\beta|}\bigr).
\tag{UC7.9}
$$

The constants are finite for every requested $N,\beta$ and compact angular patch. This also proves all angular and radial derivative estimates, since their vector fields are finite sums of polynomial multiples of physical derivatives.

\tag{UC7.10}
$$
