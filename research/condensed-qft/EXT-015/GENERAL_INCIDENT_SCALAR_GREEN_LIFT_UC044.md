# EXT-015 REACH / UC044 — scalar pushforward for the original Green source equation

**Candidate result.** The original distinct-retained-block closure lemma 150.L has a fixed-stage exact solution for arbitrary admitted scalar dependence on the integrated coordinate. The proof uses auxiliary real blowups to estimate one scalar kernel, then descends it to the original heat space. Thus its two source histories belong to the original class. Same-block histories are outside the domain.

A_\epsilon(u,v)T(u,v),\qquad
T\in E_{s,K,\Gamma},\quad A\in S_\lambda,
\tag{UC44.1}
$$

with the original full heat resolution for the scalar, and then to take the continuous extension to the completed projective input. Project the remaining old hidden slots at the end. No restriction of smooth dependence in $u$ is made on $T$. No profile form is assumed for $A$.

Take $k=\varphi e$ and the compact window $\theta(u)$ as in UC034, with $e(t)=|t|/2$, on the common compact support. Write $W_T(t,u,v)=k(t)T(u,v)$ and $t=x-u$. The graph map has the original finite-order norm and the same scalar stages for every 126 refinement.

## 2. The actual scalar to be estimated

In the removed cone let $\beta=\alpha-\tau$ and put $p=q\,\widehat k(\tau)$. The inequality $|\beta|\ge3|\tau|/4$ controls the entire frequency on its support. Fix an even $m>n+1$ and let $p_m=(i\beta)^{-m}p$, with the smooth zero extension outside the cone. Introduce actual independent physical copies $a=(a_u,a_v)$ of the old slots. Put

$$
y=(x-a_u,v-a_v),\qquad s=u-a_u .
$$

After the invertible frequency change $(\tau,\alpha,\zeta)\mapsto(\tau,\beta,\zeta)$ with absolute determinant one, let $K_m(y,s)$ be the inverse Fourier transform of $p_m$ in these coordinates. Its leading homogeneous frequency term has degree $-2-m$ and smooth angular coefficient, and the high-frequency difference is Schwartz. This uses $k''=\delta_0+g$ with $g$ compact smooth. The inverse transform has the ordinary smooth term plus the homogeneous power $r^{m+1-n}$ times a smooth angular coefficient, with a possible homogeneous polynomial of that degree times $\log r$, where $r=|(y,s)|$. The radial finite-part proof is the one in UC013 §2: in dimension $n+1$ subtract the radial Taylor polynomial of degree $m+1-n$ at zero. The last homogeneous Taylor coefficient can produce one logarithm. The actual high-pass correction has compact frequency support plus the Schwartz difference, hence gives a smooth physical term. Differentiated frequency integrations by parts give smooth angular coefficients. In particular $K_m$ is locally integrable and bounded because $m+1-n>0$.

The scalar is the exact physical integral, with fixed compact cutoffs understood in the base variables,

$$
B_{\epsilon,A}(x,v,a)
=\int_U K_m(x-a_u,v-a_v,u-a_u)
  \,\partial_u^m\!\bigl(\theta(u)A_\epsilon(u,v)\bigr)\,du .
\tag{UC44.2}
$$

The derivative is at fixed actual $(x,v,a)$. The even $m$ fixes the positive sign, and the compact window keeps every differentiated term and has no terminal boundary. For every positive regulator the Fourier transform in $s$ of the windowed factor and its derivatives is rapidly decreasing in $\beta$, with all base-variable derivatives on compact sets. Because $\beta$ controls the full cone, this proves ordinary smoothness of (UC44.2) in all base variables at positive regulator, including $y=0$, and continuity in interior seminorms. Fourier testing proves that pairing with $T(a)$ gives exactly the removed part of the original integral. This is true on all compact supported $T$ since $\widehat T$ has polynomial growth and the frequency integrals after the window pairing converge with all requested derivatives. The construction preserves the actual $du$ and $da$ densities.

## 3. A scalar pushforward on auxiliary spaces

Let $X$ be the original full heat space for the actual augmented slots $(x,u,v,a)$ and $Y$ the original full heat space for $(x,v,a)$. Forgetting $u$ is the 105.4 b-fibration. In physical coordinates define the three linear partition planes

$$
D=\{y=0\}\text{ in }Y,\qquad
P=\{y=0\}\text{ in }X,\qquad
C=\{y=0,\ s=0\}\text{ in }X .
$$

Here $P$ is the preimage of the physical $D$. The plane $C$ joins $x,u,a_u$ and identifies each other slot with its copy. The plane $P$ imposes the same equalities with $u$ free. Use tildes for strict physical lifts in the corresponding full heat spaces. The auxiliary spaces are

$$
Y'=[Y;\widetilde D],\qquad X'=[X;\widetilde C;\widetilde P].
\tag{UC44.3}
$$

The last center in $X'$ is the strict transform after blowing up $\widetilde C$. These are ordinary real radial blowups of embedded submanifolds transverse to the existing boundary coordinates where they meet them. They are auxiliary spaces for an estimate. The output scalar will descend to $Y$.

**Physical ideal check.** If a fixed partition plane $Q$ is given on physical coordinates, its defining vector on the full heat space factors locally as the product of the heat defining functions labelled by centers contained in $Q$, times a smooth vector. Near its zeros the latter vector has full normal rank in the old boundary strata and defines $\widetilde Q$. To check this use the deepest-first resolution. At a heat center contained in $Q$ divide its defining linear functions by the common polar radius. The divided linear equations are the quotient equations on the sphere. Wherever their common zero set meets the chart, the plane meets the sphere transversally, so these divided equations retain full normal rank along the face. At a center whose physical base is outside $Q$, first resolve the common intersection. The strict transforms of that center and of the physical plane have disjoint spherical directions there: the former directions have regulator component zero, and their physical quotient by the intersection has zero intersection with the latter quotient. Hence that face misses the strict physical plane. Incident centers form a chain, so this argument recursively extracts the old factors and leaves exactly the strict physical plane. In particular the same argument applies to the nested planes $C\subset P$ and to $D$. At intersecting charts their defining vectors change by positive smooth defining-function units and invertible normal frames.

Each original heat face has exponent one in the pullback of $\epsilon$. This holds at its generic point by the ordinary radial blowup of the strict regulator boundary and persists through the strict transforms. The natural forget map is b-normal and preserves $\epsilon$, hence each source heat defining function has exponent one in its unique target heat face. Its label is the projection of the partition plane by the heat-map description in 105.4. That label lies in $D$ exactly when the source plane lies in $P$. Thus the pulled-back normalized target defining vector for $\widetilde D$ agrees with the normalized source vector for $\widetilde P$ up to smooth units, and the strict preimage is exactly $\widetilde P$. Near $\widetilde C$ all incident source heat faces are labelled by subplanes of $C$. Their monomial factors normalize $s$ and the pullback of the target normal $y$ by the same product up to units. We may use the pullback of the actual target defining coordinates for $\widetilde D$ as the source normal $y$ in these charts. A normalized version of $s$ supplies the independent normal within $\widetilde P$ for $\widetilde C$. The common center and projection assertions also follow by the partition blowup charts used in [105](GAUSSIAN_WICK_COLLISION_NORMAL_FORM_105.md). [Kottke, Theorems 4.1 and 5.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.03902)

**Lifted b-fibration check.** The forget map lifts from (UC44.3) to a b-fibration. Near $\widetilde C$ use normal coordinates $(y,s)$ as just checked and write the other local target coordinates as the old boundary and tangential coordinates. Along $\widetilde C$ the original map to $\widetilde D$ is a b-submersion. The section is the admissible insertion $u=x$ of the corresponding retained coordinate on $\widetilde D$. It lifts by 105.4 and composes with forgetting to the identity, first on the positive interior and then on the strict lift. It accounts for all of $\widetilde C$: a limit of positive-scale points there equals the image under the continuous section of its projected limit. The section is an interior b-map on the two strict planes with their inherited old boundary coordinates. At $\widetilde P$ away from $\widetilde C$, restriction of the lifted differential to $\widetilde P$ maps onto the b-tangent of $\widetilde D$ because the defining $y$ is pulled back exactly and the original map is a b-submersion.

In charts of the new faces there are two normal formulas. Where the $y$ direction is nonzero use $y=r\omega,\ s=rS$ with target defining radius $r$ and angle $\omega$. Near the pure $s$ direction use $s=\pm r,\ y=rh\omega$, including the further blowup of the strict $\widetilde P$. The target radius is then $rh$ with the same angle $\omega$. Positive smooth units from defining coordinates can be included in both formulas. In the latter chart both the old normal dilation $r\partial_r$ and $h\partial_h$ give the target log-radius direction, and the angular columns give its angular tangent. In the former chart $r\partial_r$ gives that direction. The new angular derivatives of the old target coordinates vanish at the respective front where their dependence is through $y,s$; their log derivatives of old defining-function units have the same property. The b-tangent of the strict old plane covers the old boundary and tangential target rows by the preceding restricted submersion. This gives full b-rank also at corners. Away from $\widetilde C$ the ordinary $y$ radial chart for $\widetilde P$ gives the same test. The new source faces contribute only to the new target radial factor. Old source faces still map solely to their corresponding old heat face, since the target normal $y$ used in the new radius has already been normalized by the full old monomial. Thus every source face contributes to at most one target face, as required for a b-fibration.

**Pushforward.** Pull back $\partial_u^m(\theta A)$ from the original old heat space to $X$, then to $X'$. It is of fixed index and auxiliary weights by the admitted derivative and forgetting maps. The estimates for $K_m$ and the physical ideal check show that it becomes polyhomogeneous on $X'$: its physical radial factor is the monomial of the old heat faces contained in $C$, times the new radial factor of $\widetilde C$ and a positive smooth unit. Its angular part is smooth. Blowing up the further nested strict $P$ pulls this polyhomogeneous function back by the ordinary smooth angular and monomial maps. ## 4. Descent from the auxiliary target to the original heat space

Use local product coordinates $(r_H,y,z)$ near $\widetilde D$ in $Y$, with $r_H$ the old boundary variables, $z$ the remaining tangential ones and $y$ independent signed normal coordinates. For the polynomial property use a fixed continuous angular projection onto the finite-dimensional space of these polynomial restrictions. Subtract the finite polynomial radial expansion at any requested depth before taking physical $y$ derivatives. Hence the scalar lies on the original $Y$, with one fixed support, old index family and auxiliary weight choice determined before $A$ or $T$ is chosen.
