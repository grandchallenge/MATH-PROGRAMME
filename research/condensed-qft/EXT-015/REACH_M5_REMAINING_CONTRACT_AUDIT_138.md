## 5. Where full prior-family recovery matters

The original SPEC and first programme specify integration requirements for a precisely delimited renormalized model. Plan 090 adds smooth enrichment and Weiss descent of the extended model. The later retained-family and combined constructions give those requirements more than one possible target. The sources do not define the older real retained sheaf as the result of the combined construction by fiat.

The recovery claim (126.14) is exactly the local epimorphism condition for the known monomorphism on the real manifold axis. For every prior retained representative $u$ near every manifold point, it asks for a source-controlled $v$ with $u-v$ uniformly rapid in each mixed parameter/regulator jet in one finite output order for that jet, independent of the requested rate of decay. Local lifts of the classes imply epimorphy. Conversely epimorphy gives a locally represented preimage after sheafification, whose equality means precisely that rapid relation after a further localization. This uses the actual quotient and the prescribed representative rules of 126. An isomorphism on that real axis follows from both conditions. [126, Theorem 126.3 and (126.14)](COMBINED_PARAMETER_LIQUID_REALIZATION_126.md)

Consequently the claim that 126 realizes **all** the prior 123 families on the real manifold axis requires 126.14. So does transferring the real-axis completed Weiss theorem or support/connection statements to the broader target by that particular isomorphism. A direct proof on the larger sheaf, or an actual comparison with acyclic weightwise cones compatible with the full cover diagram, would be a distinct route to a descent statement. Neither is supplied here. The original smooth, support and completed Weiss clauses on the **constructed** model, the real-unit obstruction, and the original support Ext comparison have no 126.14 premise. Even success on 126.14 alone would give no arbitrary-profinite evaluation exactness or internal liquid colimit theorem.

The old 123 rule gives output graph, cone and scalar-index bounds and local history labels. The reviewed 126 lifting lemma starts with continuous entire-source jet witnesses in compatible history quotients. No implication from the evaluated output bounds to bounded whole-source representatives has been established. The proposed compactness route would suffice if it provided bounded whole-history lifts modulo uniform rapid error, compact transitions after finite order loss through the relevant completed coefficient tensors or history quotients, and continuity of the resulting quotient jets. A scalar-stage compactness calculation by itself would leave the first and the general-arity tensor/quotient steps open.
