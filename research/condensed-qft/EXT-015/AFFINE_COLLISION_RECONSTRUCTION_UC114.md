# EXT-015 follow-on / UC114 — reconstructing the collision algebra from the affine diagram

**Theorem.** The completed collision algebra of the declared renormalized free scalar is the totalization of the actual derived Amitsur diagram of its affine readout, on every physical open of the line. This also reconstructs every complete filtered module over the collision algebra from the corresponding diagram. The comparison with partial totalization in cosimplicial degrees at most $n$ is an equivalence in weights below $2(n+1)$. For the collision algebra on every nonempty open, the first failure occurs at weight $2(n+1)$. The maps respect inclusions, the shared vacuum and disjoint products. The result uses the actual derived overlap maps.

## 1. Category and comparison

Use ZFC and the fixed real liquid site with $0<p\le1$. Work in the stable category $\mathcal K$ of complete, nonnegatively indexed decreasing filtered objects in the joint liquid parameter category of UC099, with the completed symmetric tensor and the regulator scalar $K=\mathbf C$ of UC105. Use UC111's all-open algebras $D(U)$ for collision moments and $P(U)$ for the affine readout, with their actual map $\pi:D(U)\to P(U)$ and common quantum vacuum $B^\wedge$. Fix the admitted Gaussian/Wick scale and the same whole-source rapid quotient. In this theorem, $\pi$ keeps the full regulator scalar and the formal quantum parameter. The multiplicative section $\sigma$ identifies the affine algebra with the subalgebra on singleton generators and the vacuum.

Write $D=D(U)$ and $P=P(U)$. Let $I=\mathrm{fib}(D\to P)$ as a module in $\mathcal K$. The real graded polynomial model and the natural coordinate change of UC108.2 identify $I$ with the image of the positive collision ideal under the exact coefficient functor $\mathfrak F$. In that model the ideal is generated by $\rho(\xi)=\xi-\sigma\pi(\xi)$ for the block generators $\xi$ of size at least two. Thus $I$ has no grades below weight two, is concentrated in cochain degree zero in its split weight model, and its grade at weight two is the free $K$ copower of the three-dimensional pair moment space in each component. In all further expressions relative tensors are derived and use the completed monoidal product.

Let $M$ be an arbitrary module over $D$ in $\mathcal K$. In degree $a\ge0$ form the actual Amitsur term

$$
C^a(M)=M\widehat\otimes_D P^{\widehat\otimes_D(a+1)} .
\tag{UC114.1}
$$

Its cofaces insert the unit $\pi$, its codegeneracies multiply adjacent copies of $P$, and its augmentation is from $M$. At $M=D$, the first two terms are the affine model and the derived self-tensor computed in UC111. Write $T_n(M)$ for the limit over the full subcategory of the simplex category on degrees at most $n$. All decisions about the unit, the boundary maps and the balancing in this diagram are inherited from the actual map $\pi$.

## 2. Finite totalizations and weight convergence

The category of modules over $D$ in $\mathcal K$ is stable and presentable and its relative tensor preserves colimits separately, by the monoidal category and two-sided bar construction of UC105. Finite limits commute with tensoring in each variable, by exactness in the stable category. Use the punctured cube of the $n+1$ copies of $D\to P$. A nonempty subset of its $n+1$ positions gives that many copies of $P$, with all remaining factors the unit $D$. Ordering the selected positions identifies restriction from the Amitsur diagram with this cube. The nonempty-subset category is coinitial for the partial totalization. For the last assertion and the passage from the punctured cube to the finite totalization, use Mathew–Naumann–Noel, Proposition 2.14. Iterating the fiber in each of the $n+1$ cube directions gives the completed tensor of $n+1$ copies of $I$, by exactness. Including $M$ in this calculation is valid because the cube limit and the iterated fibers are finite. Thus there are natural fiber sequences

$$
M\widehat\otimes_D I^{\widehat\otimes_D(n+1)}
\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow T_n(M).
\tag{UC114.2}
$$

The first map tensors the indicated copies of $I\to D$. The tower maps from degree $n+1$ to degree $n$ use one additional $I\to D$ in the fiber. This is the natural tower comparison of the same proposition. The argument uses its finite-totalization assertion for the monoidal unit and tensor exactness. [Mathew, Naumann and Noel, Proposition 2.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.06869#page=10)

The associated grade of the ambient completed product is the nonnegative graded tensor of UC105.6. Associated grade preserves colimits, so applying it to the two-sided relative bar computes the graded relative tensor. In each bar term, the weights of the factors add. Intermediate copies of $D$ have nonnegative weights, as does $M$. Every copy of $I$ has minimum weight two. Therefore the fiber in (UC114.2) has zero grades below $2(n+1)$. This also gives equivalence on the finite filtered truncation through weight $2n+1$, since that truncation is detected by its finitely many grades.

Here associated grade also preserves all limits. The limit of complete sequences is computed levelwise because the complete category is a reflective localization with fully faithful right adjoint. The grade is a cofiber of two evaluations, equivalently a finite-limit expression in a stable category. Module limits are created in the underlying category. Consequently the inverse limit of the fibers in (UC114.2) has zero grade at every weight, since the tower in each fixed grade is eventually zero. Completeness gives conservativity of associated grade by UC099. Limits of the fiber sequences now give

$$
M\xrightarrow{\ \simeq\ }\mathrm{Tot}\,C^\bullet(M).
\tag{UC114.3}
$$

In particular $\pi$ detects zero objects by relative tensor on complete filtered modules over $D$. Indeed if $M\widehat\otimes_D P$ is zero then so are all terms (UC114.1). This consequence uses the actual relative model and includes nonsplit filtered inputs.

## 3. The first weight of failure and a pair calculation

Set $k=n+1$ and take $M=D$. Let $V$ be the real coproduct of the pair moment spaces over the components of $U$. The weight $2k$ part of the fiber in (UC114.2) is the $K$ copower of $V^{\otimes_{\mathbb R} k}$ in cochain degree zero. To see this on the relative bar, each $I$ must have weight exactly two in that grade, and every intermediate $D$ factor has weight zero. Its weight-zero algebra is the tensor unit $K$. The remaining relative tensors in this grade are therefore ordinary copowers of the indicated real tensor. The map to $\mathrm{gr}_{2k}D$ is multiplication of the independent pair differences. In the polynomial coordinates of UC108.2 its image is the $K$ copower of $\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathbb R}^k V$, embedded by the degree $k$ monomials in these differences.

For a nonempty open, choose a mass coordinate in one component. Its power of order $k$ is nonzero in the chosen grade of $D$, as seen already at the parameter point and coefficient singleton. It maps to zero at that grade of $T_n(D)$. This proves the sharp guarantee and also shows that the untruncated unit does not become equivalent at a single finite stage. The empty open has $I=0$ and $T_n(D)=B^\wedge$ for every $n$.

On one interval $V$ has dimension three. At $n=0$ the readout discards the three independent pair differences in weight two. At $n=1$, all weights through three are recovered. At weight four, the fiber map is the symmetrization of the nine ordered pairs onto the six quadratic monomials. Its kernel consists of the three antisymmetric differences. In (UC114.2) these produce three degree-minus-one classes at the partial totalization, and the six quadratic monomials are lost in degree zero. More explicitly, weight four of $D$ has rank $40$ over $K$. Its arity-four rank is $33$, with contributions $5,9,6,8,5$ from the partitions by block sizes. The weight also has six arity-two coordinates multiplied by $\hbar$ and one vacuum coordinate multiplied by $\hbar^2$. The weight-four cohomology of $T_1(D)$ thus has ranks $34$ in degree zero and $3$ in degree minus one. The canonical augmentation $D\to T_2(D)$ recovers that entire grade.

## 4. Inclusion, composition and scope

The actual affine fold, its polynomial coordinate change and the derived relative bar are natural for inclusions of opens, including mergers and opens with infinitely many components, by UC111. For $M=D$, (UC114.1) is a diagram of commutative algebras over $B^\wedge$. Its limits are formed in the underlying complete category. The comparison (UC114.3) is the natural augmentation of algebras and its underlying equivalence proves the algebra statement.

For disjoint opens the pairs $(D,P)$ compose over the single $B^\wedge$ by the component-generator formulas. The relative tensor of commutative algebras is their pushout in commutative algebras. Thus the derived overlapped terms for disjoint inputs compose by the natural interchange of these pushouts with the coproduct over $B^\wedge$. The product at a partial or full totalization is induced from the termwise products by the limit cones. The natural augmentations intertwine these products. At full totalization they are equivalences by (UC114.3), so the disjoint products agree with the original ones on the collision algebra. The comparison uses no interchange of an arbitrary tensor with an infinite limit. The completed Weiss augmentation for the reconstructed object is the one for $D$ by this natural equivalence. The common scalar algebra on the empty open remains the unit.

This theorem operates on the admitted polynomial moment algebra and its affine readout in the completed joint category.
