## 1. Model-derived rational preconditioning

Use WP10's rational minimal predictive quotient of dimension $d$, row $\ell$, terminal column $e_0$, and generators $M_a$. Its actual conditional states $x_u$ have first coordinate one. The transfer law and minimality are exact, independently of coordinate conditioning.

Let $G$ be any invertible rational matrix with first column $e_0$. Set

$$
y=xG^{-1},\qquad A_a=GM_aG^{-1}.
$$

The normalized projective updates in the new affine chart intertwine exactly with the original predictive updates. The stationary row becomes $\ell G^{-1}$; the terminal column remains $e_0$. No quotient directions are discarded. The chart may have signed matrices.

For a finite nonnegative source or history-lift realization with positive stationary row $\pi$, form the projected hidden rows $z_i$ using the actual predictive probe columns. Their affine span has dimension $d-1$: the span of projected reachable rows is the minimal quotient, and reachable rows are linear combinations of the microscopic rows. The stationary predictive row is $\ell=\sum_i\pi_i z_i$. Use the candidate tangent directions $\pi_i(z_i-\ell)$, which span the tangent plane since all $\pi_i>0$. Select a full-rank basis with first row $\ell$ by exact rank tests. A finite max-volume row-swap pass replaces a tangent basis row by a candidate when the absolute value of its coefficient in that row position is greater than one. The determinant magnitude increases by that coefficient, by multilinearity. The pass has an explicit swap budget; it does not claim globally optimal conditioning. Alternative candidates are unweighted projected rows, actual reachable probe rows, and the centered raw coordinate system. The preconditioner is model-derived, not an intrinsic invariant of every presentation of the same law. All resulting claims are about the exact observable quotient.

We also choose a positive diagonal tangent metric with weights $w_j$:

$$
\|v\|_w=\max_{1\le j<d}|v_j|/w_j.
$$

Below, nonnegative componentwise secant matrices have a common entrywise upper envelope $E$. The candidate metric solves $(I-E^{\mathsf T})w=\mathbf1$. A positive exact solution gives $E^{\mathsf T}w<w$. Round the rescaled weights outward to a modest dyadic grid to reduce rational description size. No contraction is inferred from a rounded solution; the resulting positive weights are checked against the actual edge inequalities. If the solve fails or is not positive, unit weights are tried instead.

## 2. Exact projective image boxes without corner enumeration

A rational box in the affine plane is specified by intervals $l_i\le y_i\le u_i$ for the free coordinates. Degenerate intervals are allowed. Let $a(y),b(y)$ be affine functions and suppose $b$ has positive minimum on the box. This minimum is computed by taking the minimizing endpoint of each linear term. Consider the ratio $r(y)=a(y)/b(y)$.

**Lemma (breakpoint sweep).** The exact maximum of this ratio can be computed by evaluating at most $d$ vertices, generated by a single sorted sweep of the ratios $a_i/b_i$ for the nonzero linear coefficients $b_i$. The minimum is minus the maximum with numerator negated. This costs $O(d\log d)$ rational arithmetic/comparison operations per coordinate, rather than enumeration of the $2^{d-1}$ box corners.

**Proof.** At the maximum ratio $r_*$ the maximum of the linear function $a-r_* b$ on the box equals zero. For arbitrary real $r$, a maximizing vertex chooses the upper endpoint when $a_i-rb_i>0$, the lower endpoint when it is negative, and either endpoint when zero. As $r$ increases from minus infinity, each coefficient with nonzero $b_i$ changes sign at its unique breakpoint $a_i/b_i$; the others do not change. Begin with the endpoint choices at minus infinity, then flip a single coordinate at each sorted breakpoint. At a tied breakpoint any of the choices for zero coefficients is maximizing, so a vertex immediately before or after the tied flips suffices. Thus the sweep includes a vertex maximizing $a-r_*b$, and its ratio is exactly $r_*$. Each evaluated vertex is feasible, so the largest evaluated ratio is the desired maximum. Incremental updates of the numerator and denominator require constant rational work per flip. Positive denominators justify every division. QED.

For a matrix $A$, apply the lemma to every coordinate of

$$
F_A(y)=\frac{yA}{(yA)_0}.
$$

This gives the exact smallest coordinate-aligned box enclosing its projective image. The image need not itself be a box. The cost is $O(d^2\log d)$ and no vertex list is required. The same coordinate extrema suffice to compute the componentwise numerator bounds used below. The older exhaustive corner evaluator is retained only as a small-dimensional regression oracle.

## 3. Adaptive cover and finite inclusion certificate

Fix a primitive block length $s\ge1$; the alphabet of blocks has $m^s$ members, where $m$ is the original alphabet size. The implementation defaults to trying $s=1$ first. For each block symbol $a$ use its exact chart matrix $A_a$ (the product of its original generators). Let $C_1,\ldots,C_N$ be finitely many rational prediction boxes. Supply a deterministic successor index $f(i,a)$ for every box and every block symbol. Check:

1. Every normalized stationary prefix of length below $s$ exists and lies in at least one box.
2. Every original one-symbol likelihood is positive on every box, with common lower bound $c>0$.
3. For every box and block symbol, the block denominator has positive minimum $d_{i,a}$ on the box and its exact image envelope lies inside $C_{f(i,a)}$.

These are exact finite checks using the previous lemma. The union of boxes need not be convex or connected. It can include points which are not physical conditional states; the checks are conservative there. From the seed coverage and positive block denominators, every word has positive mass: decompose it into a prefix of length below $s$ followed by complete blocks. Induction gives that every normalized state of every word lies in the union. In particular every finite-history single-symbol conditional probability is at least $c$.

Two model-driven synthesis modes are implemented. The first initializes boxes at short context predictions and widens a target hull only when an exact image is not contained, rounding endpoints outward with padding to a chosen dyadic resolution. Short context depth and primitive block length are explicit bounded parameters. The second starts with the stationary seed(s), processes each cell and symbol, reuses an existing cell only when it contains the whole exact image envelope, and otherwise creates a new outward-rounded local image box. Newly created cells are processed in turn. This mode adapts the topology of the cover to reachable images, with no uniform ambient grid or long-word enumeration; in the tested fallback it uses single-symbol matrices. Both modes enforce separate finite extent, cell, iteration and search budgets. A failure is undecided, not non-Gibbs. Verification does not depend on the synthesis heuristic.

## 4. Synchronized two-point graph theorem

For two arbitrary boxes $C_i,C_j$ and the same primitive symbol $a$, the exact secant identity is

$$
F_A(x)-F_A(y)=
\frac{(x-y)A-((x-y)A)_0 F_A(y)}{(xA)_0},
\qquad A=A_a.
$$

In particular, for tangent indices $u,v\ge1$, define a nonnegative comparison matrix

$$
B_{(i,j),a}(u,v)=
\frac{\max_{y\in C_j}|A_{uv}-A_{u0}F_A(y)_v|}
     {d_{i,a}}.
$$

The numerator maximum is exactly the larger absolute value at the two extrema of coordinate $F_A(y)_v$; they are computed by Section 2. This does not require the simultaneous attainment of all coordinate maxima. The bound is deliberately componentwise. For any $x\in C_i,y\in C_j$ it implies, in row-vector orientation,

$$
|F_A(x)-F_A(y)|_{\rm tan}
\le |x-y|_{\rm tan}\,B_{(i,j),a}
$$

coordinatewise. Here the notation means the componentwise absolute tangent vector, not a scalar norm. The two points can lie in different disconnected cells; no derivative along a segment outside the cover is used.

Build the directed graph of ordered cell pairs $p=(i,j)$, with edge labelled $a$ to $q=(f(i,a),f(j,a))$. All common observation sequences are represented. Let

$$
L_{p,a}=\max_v\frac{\sum_u w_u B_{p,a}(u,v)}{w_v}.
$$

This is the induced weighted maximum-norm bound on the edge. The graph includes all pairs and labels, so it may include unrealizable paths. Overapproximating them is safe; discarding paths without a proof is not.

### Finite-horizon graph test

Initialize $R_0(p)=1$ and $D_0(p)=I$ on all pair nodes. Iterate

$$
R_{t+1}(p)=\max_a L_{p,a}R_t(q),
\qquad
D_{t+1}(p)=\max_a^{\rm entrywise} B_{p,a}D_t(q).
$$

The scalar recursion computes the largest product of scalar edge bounds along all paths of length $t$. By induction and nonnegative matrix multiplication, $D_t(p)$ dominates every comparison-matrix product along such paths entrywise. The entrywise maximum can combine different paths, so this second recursion is an upper bound rather than an equality assertion. Put

$$
\eta_t=\min\left(\max_pR_t(p),
\max_{p,v}\frac{\sum_u w_uD_t(p)_{uv}}{w_v}\right).
$$

If $\eta_t<1$, every synchronized update of $t$ primitive symbols contracts every pair in the cover by at most $\eta_t$ in the fixed weighted norm. This follows from either of the two independent upper bounds. In dimension one the tangent difference is zero by convention. No enumeration of words of length $st$ is required; only the finite graph and $t$ iterations.

### Local rational potential test

Whenever the scalar horizon bound $\eta_{\rm sc}=\max_p R_t(p)<1$, choose rational $0<\rho<1$ with $\eta_{\rm sc}<\rho^t$ by dyadic search. Let $h_p$ be the maximum product of the rescaled edge bounds $L/\rho$ over paths of length at most $t-1$ starting at $p$, including the empty product one. Compute it by the scalar recursion with a maximum against one. Every length $t$ rescaled product is below one. It follows that prepending any edge to an optimizing path for $h_q$ yields a product at most $h_p$, even if the resulting path has length $t$. Therefore

$$
L_{p,a}h_q\le\rho h_p .
\tag{1}
$$

Conversely a supplied strictly positive rational vector $h$ and rate $\rho<1$ satisfying (1) prove contraction along all graph paths by telescoping. In the reweighted pair distance $h_p\|x-y\|_w$ each edge contracts by $\rho$. In the original fixed norm, a sequence of $r$ primitive symbols contracts with upper bound $(\max h/\min h)\rho^r$. This gives a local replay method: validate the chart, all inclusion/positivity checks, and (1) only. No horizon recursion is necessary to verify a supplied potential. Reweighting is a pair-dependent distance bound; we do not assert it is a metric satisfying the triangle inequality on the entire cover.

The componentwise horizon test can succeed even when the scalar one fails, as for repeated nilpotent comparison matrices with large scalar one-step norm. In that case retain the exact horizon witness; a scalar potential is not claimed.

## 5. Direct thermodynamic margins

From either graph test choose an observation horizon $L=st$, rate $\eta=\eta_t<1$, and diameter bound $D$ equal to the weighted coordinate diameter of the cover. For a local potential replay instead take $L=s,\eta=\rho$, multiplying that diameter by $\max h/\min h$. The latter is an upper-bound prefactor rather than the geometric diameter itself.

For histories with a common suffix of length $n$, absorb the initial remainder modulo $L$ into the preceding histories. All resulting normalized states belong to the cover. Apply the common complete groups to obtain the bound

$$
\|y_{uw}-y_{vw}\|_w\le D\eta^{\lfloor n/L\rfloor},
\qquad |w|=n .
$$

Write $J_{\rm lik}=\max_a\sum_{i\ge1}w_i|A_a(i,0)|$ using original one-symbol chart matrices. Because every finite-history likelihood is at least $c$, both the conditional logarithmic discrepancy and the absolute relative conditional discrepancy are bounded by

$$
C\eta^{\lfloor n/L\rfloor},\qquad C=J_{\rm lik}D/c.
$$

Uniform convergence of the finite conditional probabilities on infinite pasts, positivity, and martingale identification follow exactly as WP10. WP07 applied in reversed time gives positive Hölder reverse conditionals and the exponentially summable two-sided Gibbs interaction. This is an all-word conclusion, not ordinary mixing with the separator unobserved.

### Explicit rational concatenation margin

Choose rational bounds $c_-\le c$, $\bar C\ge C$, $0<\bar\eta<1$ with $\bar\eta\ge\eta$. The implementation rounds to convenient dyadic values. Take an integer $q\ge0$ with

$$
R_q=\frac{\bar C L\bar\eta^q}{1-\bar\eta}\le\frac12.
$$

Compare $p(v\mid u)$ with $p(v)$ by factoring over successive symbols of $v$. At step $j$, the compared histories have a shared suffix of length $j$. The ratio of likelihoods is at least $c_-$ and at least $1-\bar C\bar\eta^{\lfloor j/L\rfloor}$. Use the former or the latter rounded downward, whichever is larger. For the tail from $qL$ onward the sum of absolute relative errors is at most $R_q$; the product of ratios is at least $1-R_q$ by the elementary inequality $\prod(1-e_i)\ge1-\sum e_i$ for errors in $[0,1]$. If the word ends before the tail, the remaining head lower-bound factors are at most one, so the same lower bound holds. Thus a computable positive rational quasi-Bernoulli constant is

$$
b=(1-R_q)\prod_{i=0}^{q-1}
\max(c_-,1-\bar C\bar\eta^i)^L .
$$

Rounding each second argument downward preserves this bound, as in the implementation. Empty products equal one. Consequently $p(uv)\ge b\,p(u)p(v)$ for all words.

### Explicit observable exterior margin for WP11

Let $H_0$ be the invertible actual-word Hankel matrix used in the finite entry-bound computation of WP11; let $J=\|H_0^{-1}\|_\infty$. This gives the uniform entry bound $|(M_w)_{ij}|\le Jp(w)$ in the original predictive chart. Put

$$
G_*=\max_{j\ge1}\sum_{i\ge1}w_i|G_{ij}|.
$$

Then the common-suffix state discrepancy in the original predictive chart is at most $G_*D\eta^{\lfloor |w|/L\rfloor}$. Each row of $H_0M_w$ is $p(u_iw)x_{u_iw}$. Its mass is at most $p(w)$ and the coordinates of $x_{u_iw}$ lie in $[0,1]$. Thus the absolute value of every two-by-two minor of $H_0M_w$ is at most

$$
2G_*D p(w)^2\eta^{\lfloor |w|/L\rfloor}.
$$

Indeed the normalized determinant of two rows is bounded by the sum of their two coordinate differences. The row-sum norm of the exterior square of $H_0^{-1}$ is at most $J^2$: for two rows, the sum of absolute minors is bounded by the product of their absolute row sums. Multiplication by this exterior square yields the same bound for minors of $M_w$ with factor $J^2$. Choose rational $0<\theta<1$ with $\eta<\theta^L$. Since $\eta^{\lfloor n/L\rfloor}\le\theta^{n-(L-1)}$, the valid uniform normalized minor bound is $H\theta^n$, with

$$
H=2G_*D J^2\theta^{-(L-1)}.
$$

For zero tangent dimension set the minor bound to zero. The zero-contraction case uses exponent-zero value one and vanishes after a complete horizon. All $b,J,H,\theta$ needed by WP11 are now computable from a verified model-derived graph witness. These secondary bounds can be very conservative, especially the Hankel inverse factor; the direct graph certificate avoids needing them for acceptance. No estimate is inferred merely from finite observed-word comparisons.

## 6. Verification complexity and scope

Let $N$ be the cell count, $k=d-1$, and $m_0=m^s$ the primitive alphabet size. Once the minimal quotient and primitive matrices are given, all exact projective envelopes cost $O(Nm_0d^2\log d)$. The one-symbol likelihood tests cost $O(Nmd)$. For each cell and symbol, precompute the denominator minimum and the coordinatewise numerator suprema. For every ordered pair only division by the corresponding source denominator is needed. Thus forming the pair matrices and checking a supplied local potential takes $O(N^2m_0d^2)$ further rational operations. Seed inclusion adds at most $O(Nkd_{\rm seed})$ comparisons where $d_{\rm seed}=\sum_{r<s}m^r$. Horizon synthesis takes $O(tN^2m_0d^3)$ arithmetic operations for the dense componentwise recursion, or $O(tN^2m_0)$ for scalar recursion once scalar edge bounds are computed. There is no $2^k$ corner enumeration and no $m^{st}$ horizon-word enumeration. For fixed single-symbol primitives all these counts are polynomial in the supplied cell, dimension and alphabet counts and the chosen horizon. Generating primitive matrices for $s>1$ still costs exponential in $s$. A common denominator of the finite comparison matrices bounds rational numerator/denominator bit length of a length $t$ Bellman recursion by a polynomial in the finite matrix description and $t$; maximum selection cannot increase the common denominator bound. Local potential verification is polynomial in the explicit rational witness size. No bound on the number of cells or the success of the discovery heuristic purely in terms of input bit size is asserted.

As an existential certificate language allowing arbitrary primitive lengths, this contains a complete witness family for positive Hölder rational laws. Indeed take WP11's sufficiently long strict suffix-grid witness. Use its generating boxes as cells, add singleton cells for the stationary prefix states of lengths below the primitive length (these states lie in the WP11 polytope by stationarity padding), and route each primitive symbol to its own suffix box. WP11's local image inclusion proves all these edges. In the proof of its quantitative theorem, uniform entrywise secant numerator bounds give the same $D\theta^s$ upper bound after summing coordinatewise suprema, by the individual minor estimate; choose the primitive length sufficiently large. Thus the graph certificate succeeds for some cover, even with unit metric weights. This is not a proof of completeness of the bounded single-letter adaptive search or of polynomial-time decidability; the primitive length in this existence argument may be large.

## 8. Prior art and next boundary

The graph-stability mechanism has close established predecessors. Ahmadi, Jungers, Parrilo and Roozbehani, [Joint Spectral Radius and Path-Complete Graph Lyapunov Functions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.3427), Section 2 and Theorem 4, uses labelled graphs and Lyapunov inequalities for linear switched systems; the HTML source was inspected. [Abstraction based verification of stability of polyhedral switched systems](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1751570X20300030) explicitly uses weighted finite state graphs and cycle product conditions; abstract inspected, publisher full-text access failed. The present synchronized pair graph uses exact normalized observable two-point secants, with all label continuations represented and positive denominators. It is not a claim to invent weighted graph stability or path-complete Lyapunov methods. Linear fractional programming and sign-based endpoint optimization are established; the breakpoint lemma is an elementary box specialization, not a claim to invent ratio optimization. The model-derived chart, adaptive cover, factorized exact verifier and conversion back to all-word thermodynamic margins are the scoped research contribution. Prior source-proof concordance and originality adjudication remain open.
