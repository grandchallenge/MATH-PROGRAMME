## 1. Two different minimality questions

Let X be a finite sector, P a stochastic physical transition acting backwards on functions, L_a positive observation likelihoods, and F a finite target family. Write M_a=P diag(L_a). Include a wildcard L=1 if unobserved evolution is permitted. For any prior row probability p, the evidence of word w is p M_w 1; the unnormalized terminal target is p M_w f and its normalized value is their ratio. Observations before the first transition can be handled as separate diagonal actions. Multiple physical kernels are handled by adding actions.

Define V_0=span(1,F), V_{h+1}=span(V_h, M_a V_h for all a). By induction V_h is exactly the span of all evidence and target functions for words of length at most h. A linear moment representation must recover these functions from its moments to preserve evidence and normalized targets for every prior. Indeed if a function is outside the represented subspace containing 1, a separating signed row annihilates that subspace but not the function. Its positive and negative parts have equal mass, giving two priors with identical represented moments and distinct evidence or unnormalized target. Conversely containment suffices. Thus V_h is the unique least linear observable space for that finite-horizon contract. The chain stabilizes after at most |X|-dim(V_0) strict increases. At stabilization the restricted matrices provide exact normalized moment transport, with dimension dim(V). This is an all-priors statement; fixed-prior reachable reductions can be smaller. If only normalized targets are required without evidence, the necessity argument does not automatically apply.

A unital pointwise algebra on a finite real function space is exactly the algebra of functions constant on a partition. Proof: separate distinct evaluation classes by a linear combination of generators avoiding finitely many hyperplanes, then interpolate the class indicators by polynomials. For a Markov quotient preserving target functions and all likelihoods, begin with equality of their values, then split each class by vectors of transition masses into every current class for every allowed P. Repeat until stable. The final partition is the coarsest stable refinement: any admissible stable partition refines the initial partition and each successive refinement by induction. It defines the least unital algebra containing targets and likelihoods and invariant under every P; equivalently invariant under likelihood multiplication and P. The resulting quotient carries exact forward, backward and posterior conditioning for every prior because both the likelihood and transition masses descend. This is classical strong lumpability/probabilistic bisimulation. It is a stronger contract than matching a selected terminal predictive span, and need not have the same dimension. In particular the algebra generated by all microscopic occupation coordinates is the entire function algebra, even when linear predictions close.

## 2. Conservation-specific degree hierarchy and a no-go theorem

On the binary count K sector of N sites put d=min(K,N-K). Let V_r be restrictions of polynomials of degree at most r. Degree d spans all functions: if K<=N/2 the product of occupied coordinates of a configuration is its indicator; otherwise use the product of empty coordinates. This bound is sharp: choose d disjoint pairs, one particle in each, and fix the remaining coordinates with K-d occupied. The parity sign on this d-dimensional subcube annihilates every monomial of degree <d because at least one pair is absent from its support. It does not annihilate a product choosing one coordinate from every pair. Uniform even and odd parity priors therefore agree through degree d-1 but differ on a degree d target.

Uniform symmetric random-scan bond heat bath is a mixture of identity and coordinate transpositions (arbitrary fixed nonnegative bond selection weights). Hence it preserves every V_r exactly. In particular all unobserved occupation means close at degree one regardless of graph. A single positive site likelihood 1+c x_j with c>-1 raises degree by at most one; nonzero c permits extracting x_j f from (L_j f-f)/c. With arbitrary same-slice site clamps and the constant function, repeated multiplication generates the full algebra. Even evidence alone for unrestricted products of these clamps distinguishes the parity priors: the d chosen coordinates have product likelihood whose parity difference is a nonzero multiple of c^d. Thus no fixed low-degree exact sufficient space can work uniformly over all sectors and arbitrary microscopic conditioning. The degree bound for at most h single-site clamp actions and degree-r terminal tests is V_{min(d,r+h)} under uniform exchange, including evidence. This is an upper bound, not a claim of minimality for every physical graph and observation language. For interacting heat bath the transposition mixing weights depend on state, so the degree-preservation proof does not apply.

## 3. A physical coarse-scale positive and negative criterion

Partition spatial sites into blocks. Observe only block counts; the target algebra is functions of the count vector. For uniform exclusion with weighted undirected bond selection (normalized to total one), a move of one particle from A to B has probability half the total weight of edges from occupied sites of A to empty sites of B. If all cross-block bond weights are constant on each complete bipartite pair (including zero), the aggregate probability is

$$
\frac12 w_{AB} k_A(n_B-k_B).
$$

Within-block bonds do not change counts. The count process is then exactly Markov and arbitrary count-dependent likelihoods descend. In fact the cross-block uniformity condition is necessary if exact count lumpability is demanded for every sector: K=1 implies the total weight from any i in A to B is constant over i. K=2 with one particle i in A and one j in B implies the move A to B has weight half this constant minus half w_ij, so w_ij must be constant over all i,j (trivial singleton and empty-block cases included). No necessity is asserted for just one selected sector. Equitable single-particle graph partitions alone do not suffice: the alternating bipartition of the 6-cycle is equitable, but with one particle in each block adjacent versus opposite occupied sites have different move probabilities. Exact refinement discovers the missing distinction. This criterion concerns uniform exclusion, not arbitrary interacting Hamiltonians.

There is also a positive interacting case. Suppose site weights are constant within blocks, pair factors are constant within each unordered block pair (including same-block pairs), and physical bond selection weights are constant across each pair of distinct blocks. The stationary weight W(k) then depends only on block counts: products of site weights to their counts, same-block factors to binomial(k_A,2), and cross-block factors to k_A k_B. A physical heat bath moving a particle from A to B has probability w_AB k_A(n_B-k_B) times W(k')/(W(k)+W(k')), with k' the transferred count vector. This follows from the exact microscopic bond conditional and applies to genuinely nonunit pair factors. Within-block exchanges do not alter counts. Thus arbitrary count-dependent likelihoods still descend. More generally any symmetry group preserving the dynamics and likelihoods yields a stable orbit partition; symmetry is sufficient but not necessary for lumpability. No general interacting local-graph reduction is inferred.

## 4. Scale-dependent approximate sufficiency

An exact quotient can be unnecessarily expensive at a finite physical resolution. Fix a partition and a finite schedule of nonnegative transfer actions M_t (each may represent multiple physical steps and intervening observations). For each action define R_t(x,D)=sum_{y in D} M_t(x,y). Choose a nonnegative coarse matrix B_t with common row support across microscopic states in each class C, and numbers 0<l_t<=u_t such that

$$
l_t B_t(C,D)\le R_t(x,D)\le u_t B_t(C,D)
\quad (x\in C).
$$

These inequalities can be checked rationally, including zeros; support mismatch means this particular multiplicative certificate is undecided. One choice of B_t uses a representative state for each row; optimize by other coarse matrices if useful.

For every coarse path prefix, the microscopic unnormalized mass of the next class is the mass of that prefix times a convex combination of R_t(x,D) over its last class. Hence the entire unnormalized coarse path mass lies between products of l_t and u_t times the coarse model path mass, for every prior projected exactly at the initial slice. This follows inductively even though microscopic posterior distributions depend on the entire history. After conditioning on any fixed admissible observation sequence (and any additional positive coarse-path likelihood common to both models), normalized path mass ratios are between 1/Gamma and Gamma, where

$$
\Gamma=\prod_t u_t/l_t.
$$

Thus TV is bounded by 1-1/Gamma using common mass, and also by (Gamma-1)/(Gamma+1) since for densities r in [1/Gamma,Gamma] with mean one, |r-1| is bounded by its chord on this interval. In particular any terminal or path target of oscillation one has error at most (Gamma-1)/(Gamma+1). The bound is independent of the magnitude of common coarse likelihoods, but grows with the number of inaccurate blocks. It controls joint coarse histories, not all microscopic observables. This defines a finite-horizon sufficient tolerance test, with exact lumpability as Gamma=1. A search over all refinements of the target partition yields a minimum-class certified quotient for this particular test and chosen B rule, not the unique optimal approximate representation nor a polynomial-time guarantee. No hydrodynamic limit is inferred.

Exact reference specimens: on a 6-site half-filled ring, all single-site means without clamps require a 6-dimensional linear space, but the coordinate-generated algebra has dimension 20. The evidence-only predictive dimensions with arbitrary same-slice single-site likelihood multipliers 2 and wildcard exchange are 1, 6, 15, 20. Half-filled degree-space ranks for N=4,6,8 are respectively (1,4,6), (1,6,15,20), (1,8,28,56,70). For the 6-cycle alternating bipartition at K=2, exact refinement increases 3 count classes to 4; complete bipartite exchange retains 3. For a 4-site, 2-particle complete bipartite exchange with one bond selection weight 11/10 and the others 1, two actions (one count likelihood and one wildcard) give per-action lower/upper ratios 61/62 and 11/10 and TV bound 11628/104653. Exhaustive search over the 15 refinements of the three count classes proves the minimum certified class counts for tolerances 1/8, 1/10, 1/20 and 0 are 3,5,5,5, respectively, for this representative-row certificate. The five-class solution has zero error. The search refuses to certify minimality if the partition budget is exhausted.

## 5. Link to WP19 response closure

For the posterior at time u, a chosen observable subspace W containing constants has orthogonal projection Q_u in its actual posterior L2 space. For s<u<t, conditional independence yields exact covariance decomposition into the inner product of projected endpoint conditional means plus the inner product of their orthogonal residuals. The residual magnitude is bounded by the product of their L2 norms. Enlarging W decreases each residual norm, although the signed closure defect need not decrease monotonically. The optimal subspace depends on the ensemble of endpoint functions and the chosen norm. A pointwise algebra of all microscopic coordinates cannot be a small algebra; the scale-dependent contract should instead be given by coarse observables, finite-horizon predictive spaces, or certified approximations.
