## 1. Physical protocol and equilibrium inside count fibers

For a block count vector k let X_k be the configurations with these counts and pi_k the Gibbs law conditioned on X_k. Let F_k be the physical fast kernel: choose one of b blocks uniformly, choose an internal physical bond uniformly, and heat-bath its occupations; singleton/no-active-bond blocks hold. Internal bond graphs are connected. These operations preserve pi_k, and their mixture is reversible positive semidefinite (each individual bond heat bath is an orthogonal conditional expectation).

One macroscopic action comprises t fast steps and then one slow opportunity S: choose a cross-block physical bond with a fixed probability, heat-bath its occupations. More general mixtures including holds are allowed. Observations occur at macroscopic boundaries and are strictly positive functions of counts. Define the averaged count kernel B(k,l)=E_pi_k S(X,X_l). It is stochastic and reversible for the Gibbs count law when S is Gibbs reversible: the joint mass of fibers k,l is symmetric by detailed balance. The actual count process is generally not Markov, but this defines a candidate reduction. No microscopic observation during fast steps is allowed by this contract.

Suppose for every x,y in X_k,

$$
(1-e)\pi_k(y)\le F_k^t(x,y)\le(1+e)\pi_k(y),\qquad 0\le e<1.
$$

Summation against S(y,X_l) yields the same multiplicative envelope around B(k,l) including zero entries. This holds for arbitrary initial microscopic state and hence arbitrary history. WP20 then controls joint count histories of H slow opportunities and arbitrary common positive count-path likelihoods by a ratio Gamma<=((1+e)/(1-e))^H. In fact the optimal TV bound from a likelihood-ratio RANGE of width Gamma (unnormalized mass ratio upper/lower) is

$$
d_{\rm TV}\le\frac{\sqrt{\Gamma}-1}{\sqrt{\Gamma}+1}.
$$

Proof: let r be the normalized density, min r=a, max r<=Gamma a, E r=1. The chord bound on (r-1)_+ gives (1-a)(Gamma a-1)/((Gamma-1)a); maximize over a in [1/Gamma,1] at a=1/sqrt(Gamma), with trivial Gamma=1 case. This sharpens WP20's safe bound. Rational sufficient bound H e follows since the right hand side is tanh(H atanh(e)/2) <= H e (use tanh addition or monotonicity). The same argument works for different per-step envelopes using sum e. Thus a uniform per-step error epsilon/H suffices on any prescribed horizon.

## 2. Local interaction certificate

Let c_AB=min(1, product over interactions between A,B of max(lambda_ij,1/lambda_ij) minus 1), c_AA=0. This is symmetric. Conditional Gibbs laws of a block A at fixed count, given exterior configurations differing only in B, have likelihood density ratio range at most the indicated product: the difference in log energies is a linear sum with oscillation bounded by sum |log lambda_ij|. Normalized density ratio is at most the product, so TV <= c_AB. This bound is independent of exterior counts and internal energies. Let q=max_A sum_B c_AB<1. Block Gibbs resampling G chooses a uniform block and samples its whole conditional law. For two states in the same count fiber differing only at B, use the same chosen block: at B coalesce, elsewhere create at most c_AB mismatch in expectation. Hamming distance on blocks contracts by 1-(1-q)/b. Glue along single-block paths, which stay in X_k. The Lipschitz eigenfunction argument for finite reversible G gives spectral gap >=(1-q)/b (any nonconstant eigenfunction has positive Lipschitz seminorm). No physical whole-block refresh is substituted for the actual fast kernel: it is only a comparison instrument.

For each nonsingleton block A, its internal bond conditional chain with exterior fixed has uniform placement lower bound eta_A>0. With n_A sites, m_A=n_A(n_A-1)/2, E_A internal bonds, the tree leaf-fixing argument of WP19 gives all m_A-step entries >=(eta_A/E_A)^m_A. A common minorization of at least this mass follows, even without multiplying by number of states. Its spectral gap is at least g_A=(eta_A/E_A)^m_A/m_A by oscillation contraction, eigenvalues, and Bernoulli inequality. Singleton fibers contribute zero conditional variance; use g_A=1 for such chains. The bound may use a uniform positive lower placement probability across all exterior configurations; e.g. WP18's R B^2 odds bound restricted to endpoints. Conditional Dirichlet form comparison to exact block resampling, averaged over pi_k and block choice, gives

$$
{\rm gap}(F_k)\ge g=(1-q)\min_A g_A/b.
$$

Positive semidefiniteness gives relative mixing bound e_t <= (1-g)^t/pi_min, by expanding density of a point mass in L2(pi_k) and evaluating with Cauchy-Schwarz. A lower bound pi_min >= 1/(|X_k| W), where W is the product over sites and pairs of max(factor,1/factor), bounds weight range; |X_k|=product binomial(n_A,k_A). Use singleton fiber separately. This yields a completely explicit sufficient schedule for any finite H; for example t>=g^{-1} log(H/(epsilon pi_min)). The result is not a uniform diffusive mixing theorem: constants depend on block size, q, volume and the imposed fast/slow separation. It supplies a quantified schedule even when H is proportional to a squared macroscopic length, not a proof that ordinary homogeneous local exchange equilibrates on that schedule.

## 3. Local computation of averaged slow transfer

Computing B globally is not required to certify its rates. For a slow bond (i,j) between A and B, the conditional probabilities of its three count outcomes (A to B, B to A, hold) are functions only of endpoint occupations and their interaction neighbors. Let D be the set of blocks containing these sites (including A,B). Let U be the r-neighborhood of D in the block interaction graph. Compare pi_k to any pi_k' whose counts agree on U; total global counts of the extension need not agree, since the comparison takes place on the full product of block sectors, with the different counts treated as exterior sources. All relevant moves still conserve the actual total.

For a common block with equal counts, couple random block Gibbs updates maximally. For different-count blocks use arbitrary coupling; mismatches there are at most one. For a stationary coupling (finite state space), mismatch probabilities e satisfy e_C<=sum_J c_CJ e_J on common blocks: telescope the exterior by changing one block at a time. Set e<=1 elsewhere. Iterating on U with the substochastic matrix c, whose row sums are <=q, yields e_C<=q^{dist(C,U^c)} for C in U: every contribution from the boundary has a path of at least that length, and the bound follows equivalently by iterating e<=1 for that many steps within the neighborhood. Consequently each slow outcome probability differs by at most |D| q^{r+1}, by coupling and union bound. In disconnected components with no changed counts the error is zero. One can calculate the local approximation by fixing arbitrary exterior occupations beyond U and using the finite Gibbs conditional over U; the same coupling proof applies with frozen exterior, no need to enumerate the rest of the system. The block counts in U are the actual ones. This approximation depends only on counts in U and fixed boundary choice.

For positivity control, let W_A be the product of max(factor,1/factor) over all site factors in A and all pair factors incident to A, counted once. For fixed exterior, the conditional Gibbs mass of any event on A is at least its uniform count-fiber mass divided by W_A. In particular the probability that i occupied and j empty, with distinct endpoint blocks, is at least (k_A/n_A)(1-k_B/n_B)/(W_A W_B) by conditioning successively on the exterior of A and of B. Given an active bond, either placement has probability at least eta_s>0. Thus every allowed oriented move of the bond has expectation at least eta_s/(n_A n_B W_A W_B), and hold has expectation at least eta_s. Impossible orientations are identically zero by the counts, and the approximate local law has exactly the same support. If p_* is the minimum of these positive lower bounds over slow bonds, then absolute local error d=max_b |D_b| q^{r+1} gives relative error at most a=d/p_* (require a<1). Sum the normalized per-bond three-outcome approximations to obtain a stochastic local count kernel Bhat: each bond uses its own window but its own outcomes sum to one. Relative inequalities persist under summation; there is no subtraction of approximated rates from a possibly negative diagonal.

Combining physical equilibration error e and local rate error a, true coarse transfer rows relative to Bhat lie between (1-e)(1-a) and (1+e)(1+a). Hence H-step posterior coarse-path TV <= H(e+a), with arbitrary positive count-path likelihoods, via the range bound in Section 1. Choosing e,a<=epsilon/(2H) suffices. If q is bounded strictly below one, endpoint block sizes, degree and factor bounds are uniform, then D size and p_* are uniformly controlled; r=O(log(H/epsilon)) suffices, independent of microscopic total volume (or use entire component if smaller). On polynomial diffusive horizons in macroscopic length this is a logarithmic-radius local selection rule. This is a conditional theorem under the explicit fast/slow protocol and weak interblock influence; the local window cardinality and enumeration cost can still grow exponentially with radius in general graphs, and even polynomial volume in radius does not make enumeration polynomial in radius. No claim of general scalable optimum.

## 4. Nonlinear closure control at observation boundaries

For a future bounded count-path test f starting at a macroscopic boundary u, with a fixed future sequence of count likelihoods, the comparison holds starting from every microscopic state x in the fiber. The reduced prediction is a function of the count vector alone. Thus the actual conditional mean F(x)=E[f|X_u=x,observations] is within epsilon_future osc(f) of a count-measurable function uniformly in x. Past observations do not change this conditional future prediction given X_u by Markov factorization, provided the past and future likelihood factors are slice-local or separately factor across u. Orthogonal projection onto the count algebra in the actual posterior L2 has residual norm at most that error. For an arbitrary past test g, its conditional mean G has oscillation at most osc(g) and residual norm at most osc(g)/2 by comparison to its midrange constant. WP19's conditional independence and projection identity therefore give closure-defect bound

$$
|\langle F-QF,G-QG\rangle|\le \epsilon_{\rm future}\,\mathrm{osc}(f)\,\mathrm{osc}(g)/2.
$$

This controls the information discarded by the count representation without assuming the reverse protocol has fast equilibration before its slow step. If a corresponding past certificate exists, use the product of two residual bounds. Arbitrary nonfactorized trajectory likelihoods are allowed for path TV comparison in Sections 1-3, not for this conditional independence argument.

## 5. Exact reference specimens

For a chain of 8192 two-site blocks, internal pair factor 3/2 and one interblock factor 17/16 per neighboring pair, the sparse influence certificate stores 16382 directed nonzero entries, with q=1/8. The site weights are one. Uniform placement eta=1/4 is valid since the endpoint factor product is at most (3/2)(17/16), so the swap odds are bounded by its square. W_A is at most (3/2)(17/16)^2, hence p_*=1/100 is a conservative positive lower bound. With tolerance 1/100 divided equally between rate and equilibration error, the rate window radii for H=16,256,4096 are 6,7,9 respectively (D size two). These are rate certificates only; no 8192-block physical equilibration is simulated or asserted. Exact small-fiber tests independently evaluate actual local-bond fast transitions, Gibbs reversibility of averaged count rates, strong multi-clamp count paths, conditional closure residual, local windows with arbitrary frozen boundaries, and the sharp range-TV extremizer. The gap producer takes connected internal graphs and uniform placement bounds as hypotheses, not as graph checks from edge counts alone.

## 6. Limits and frontier

The exact range-TV inequality is the sharp Hilbert-metric bound of Cohen and Fausti (https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02413); Dobrushin comparison, Dirichlet-form comparison and averaging of fast/slow Markov chains are established tools. See Rebeschini and van Handel, Comparison theorems for Gibbs measures (2014), https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.4117; Levin and Peres, Markov Chains and Mixing Times (2nd ed.), https://pages.uoregon.edu/dlevin/MARKOV/; no priority claim. The bounded CSM synthesis is the actual physical internal-bond schedule, localized count-kernel rate computation, joint posterior tolerance and closure-residual link. Strong interblock interaction, microscopic observations during fast steps, unseparated homogeneous exchange and genuine positive-temperature phase coexistence are unresolved.
