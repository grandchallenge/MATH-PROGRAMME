# THURSTONIAN_ETHOS.md

## Mathematical progress is larger than theorem production

This programme takes inspiration from William Thurston's *On Proof and Progress in Mathematics*. The central lesson for MATHFORGE, MATHSOLVE, and MATHCERT is that mathematics advances through understanding, communication, conceptual compression, examples, analogies, proof, correction, and shared memory. Formal proof matters deeply, but proof is not the only form of progress.

The Grand Challenge doctrine therefore rejects two opposite errors:

1. **Proof theatre**: impressive formalism with no communicated understanding.
2. **Understanding theatre**: fluent exposition with no checkable claim boundary.

The aim is neither theatre nor mere productivity. The aim is accountable mathematical understanding.

## The three obligations

Every serious artifact must satisfy three obligations.

### 1. Obligation to the object

Represent the mathematical object faithfully. Do not simplify until the structure is visible. Do not hide the obstruction. Do not make the problem easy by changing it.

### 2. Obligation to the reader

Teach the path. Explain why definitions are chosen, why reductions matter, where examples come from, what computations check, and why the next target is sharp.

### 3. Obligation to the claim

Classify every claim. State what is proved, what is computed, what is conjectural, what is literature-derived, and what awaits certification.

## Understanding as infrastructure

A Grand Challenge Work Package should increase the stock of durable mathematical understanding even if it does not solve the original problem. It may do this by:

- clarifying definitions;
- revealing an obstruction;
- proving a restricted theorem;
- eliminating a false route;
- building a normal form;
- constructing exact computational tools;
- formalizing a statement;
- translating a problem into a more useful category;
- writing a synthesis that prevents duplicated confusion.

## The role of formal certification

Formal certification is not a replacement for understanding. It is a discipline that prevents understanding from becoming wishful thinking. Lean/equivalent formalization should therefore be connected to human exposition. Every formal theorem should have a human theorem statement and an explanation of its role in the Work Package.

## Pedagogy as structural revelation

The required pedagogy is not simplification. It is structural revelation. It should show:

- what the variables mean;
- what the invariants are;
- what the witnesses witness;
- why a boundary case is dangerous;
- why a finite screen is evidence but not proof;
- why an exact certificate changes the status of a claim;
- why a failed proof attempt is informative.

## Grand Challenge voice

The voice should be ambitious, lucid, and exact. It should not be corporate. It should not be mystical. It should not be falsely humble. It should speak plainly about uncertainty and sharply about progress.

## Binding maxims

> Every result must teach its own necessity.

> No theorem without a spine.

> No computation without a ledger.

> No conjecture without a map.

> No proof without a reader.

## Source note

William P. Thurston, *On Proof and Progress in Mathematics*, arXiv:math/9404236. The paper is a response to debates about rigor, proof, and progress in mathematics, and argues for a broader view of how mathematics advances as a human enterprise.
