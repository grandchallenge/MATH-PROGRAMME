# Lay companion

A union-closed family is a finite collection of sets with one rule: whenever two sets are present, their union is present too. Frankl's conjecture says that such a family cannot spread all of its elements too thinly. At least one element should occur in half of the sets.

The difficulty is the word **every**. Small families can be listed. Families containing a singleton or a two-element set are controlled. Average set size is bounded below. Entropy proves that some element must appear in a positive constant fraction, now roughly 0.3824 in rigorous general bounds. Lattice theory constrains what a smallest counterexample would have to look like. Lean and exact replay certify several restricted statements.

None of these facts reaches one half for every finite family.

UC-DOC-WP00 turns this mature landscape into a governed illustrated monograph. It locks the source, bibliography, theorem statuses, formal and computational boundaries, and release identities. It deliberately stops before web publication so the manifest cannot contain an edition without its semantic reader and accessibility contract.

The next move is not more mathematical speculation. It is a bounded conversion of the locked source into the established browser-reader substrate.
