## The question in plain language

An infinite set $A$ is an *asymptotic additive basis of order $k$* if every sufficiently large positive integer is a sum of **exactly $k$** elements of $A$, with repetitions allowed. It is **strongly minimal under infinite deletion** if removing *any* infinite subset $B\subseteq A$ destroys that order $k$ property.

[Erdős Problem #881](https://www.erdosproblems.com/881) asks whether, nevertheless, **some** infinite subset $B\subseteq A$ can be removed while leaving $A\setminus B$ an asymptotic basis of order $k+1$. This is a universal quantifier on deletions at order $k$ followed by an existential quantifier at order $k+1$. Neither ordinary single-element minimality nor finite-prefix experiments settle that distinction.

The original exact number of summands also matters. An exactly $k$ representation need not be equivalent to an at-most $k$ representation unless suitable padding by zero is available.

## A small example

For $k=1$, let $A$ be all positive integers and remove the infinite set of powers of two,

$$
B=\{1,2,4,8,16,\ldots\},\qquad D=A\setminus B.
$$

Then $D$ is not an asymptotic basis of order one, because it omits infinitely many numbers. But $D$ is a basis of order two. To represent a sufficiently large integer $n$, consider pairs $n=x+(n-x)$ with $n/3\le x\le2n/3$. There are linearly many candidate choices, and only $O(\log n)$ can be excluded by either summand being a power of two. Thus a representation remains.

The exact finite verifier checks targets $30\le n\le2000$ and finds valid ordered two-term representations in every case. That table illustrates, but does not replace, the general counting proof.

## What is known

Erdős posed the problem in [*Some of my new and almost new problems and results in combinatorial number theory*](https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110809794.169), Eger proceedings (1998), pp.169–180. The [maintained problem #881](https://www.erdosproblems.com/881) continues to classify the question as **open**.

A [May 3, 2026 forum submission](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/881) proposed a positive answer for $k=1$ and a negative answer for every $k\ge2$, using thin strongly minimal additive bases. Independent public discussion on the same date identified **major gaps** in the argument and requested transparent attribution of AI assistance. No complete repaired construction or independent proof verification was available in the sources checked for this article. The purported negative results for $k\ge2$ therefore remain **unadjudicated claims**, not results promoted by GCL.

The affirmative result for $k=1$ follows from the independent elementary sparse-deletion argument in this article. An asymptotic order-one basis contains all sufficiently large positive integers, so the proof is not limited to the particular starting set of all positive integers.

## GCL's current state

A more general useful lemma is available. Suppose $D$ is a set of positive integers whose complement $E$ satisfies

$$
|E\cap[1,x]|=o(x).
$$

Then $D$ is an asymptotic basis of order two. For a sufficiently large $n$, there are at least $n/3-O(1)$ integer choices of $x$ in the central interval $[n/3,2n/3]$. At most $2|E\cap[1,n]|=o(n)$ of those choices fail because either $x\in E$ or $n-x\in E$. At least one choice survives.

If $A$ is any order-one asymptotic basis, then its complement is finite. Removing the infinite powers of two contained in $A$ leaves a complement that is finite plus $O(\log n)$ powers, so the lemma proves the desired order-two property. Removing infinitely many powers also guarantees the order-one property is lost.

The finite tests and this counting argument do **not** give a strongly minimal example or settle the open cases $k\ge2$. Strong minimality quantifies over every infinite deletion and is not decidable from any fixed finite computation.

## What remains

A bounded successor task is to **audit the May 2026 proposed higher-order counterexample** against its documented referee objections. Required inputs are the complete proposed construction, the exact original strong-minimality definition, and all corrections. Verify three separately quantified properties: eventual coverage by exactly $k$ terms; loss of order $k$ coverage after **every** infinite deletion; and loss of order $(k+1)$ coverage after **every** infinite deletion.

Acceptance requires a complete independently checked paper argument or a pinned no-placeholder Lean build with actual axiom output, resolving each identified proof gap. An explicit missing target or an infinite deletion that preserves an order $(k+1)$ basis falsifies the proposed negative example. Stop without changing the maintained open status until independent adjudication is complete.
