## The question in plain language

List the positive integers representable as a sum of **two nonnegative integer squares**:

$$
1=s_1<s_2<\cdots,\qquad s_i=a_i^2+b_i^2.
$$

Erdős asks for good upper and lower bounds on the **consecutive gaps** $s_{i+1}-s_i$. Both the maximum size of gaps along an infinite sequence and their growth as a function of $s_i$ matter.

Allowing zero as one of the two summands is the standard convention: a perfect square counts as a sum of two squares. Requiring both summands positive would change the sequence and its gap structure.

## A small example

The first positive sums of two squares are $1,2,4,5,8,9,10,13,16,17,\ldots$. The gap from $10$ to $13$ equals three: the intervening integers $11$ and $12$ are not sums of two squares. A candidate gap is certified only after checking **every** intervening integer.

Fermat's two-square criterion says that an integer is a sum of two squares exactly when each prime congruent to three modulo four occurs with **even exponent** in its prime factorization. This yields an independent arithmetic checker for finite gap calculations.

## What is known

The optimal gap bounds remain **OPEN**. Bambah and Chowla (1947) proved the upper estimate

$$
s_{i+1}-s_i\ll s_i^{1/4}.
$$

Erdős (1951) established infinitely large gaps of size at least a constant multiple of $\log s_i/\sqrt{\log\log s_i}$. Richards (1982) improved the lower construction to

$$
\limsup_{i\to\infty}
\frac{s_{i+1}-s_i}{\log s_i}\ge\frac14.
$$

Subsequent work, most recently attributed in the maintained catalogue to Dietmann, Elsholtz, Kalmynin, Konyagin and Maynard (2022), increases the logarithmic lower constant to about $0.868$. A logarithmic lower bound and an upper bound by the fourth root leave a substantial asymptotic gap. The average density of sums of two squares does not determine their **maximal** consecutive gaps.

## What remains

A bounded task enumerates all sums of squares through $N=100000$, sorts them, and computes exact consecutive gaps with witnesses. Acceptance: the first terms agree with those above, the missing integers between every selected gap are verified by a second prime-factorization test, and ratios to $\log N$ and $N^{1/4}$ are presented as finite diagnostics, not limiting conclusions.
