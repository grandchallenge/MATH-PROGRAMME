## The question in plain language

In a graph $G$, let $\nu_\triangle(G)$ be the maximum number of **edge-disjoint triangles**, and let $\tau_\triangle(G)$ be the minimum number of edges whose deletion destroys **every** triangle. Tuza's conjecture asks whether

$$
\tau_\triangle(G)\le2\nu_\triangle(G)
$$

for every finite graph. The edge-disjoint triangles may share vertices but **not edges**. A triangle-hitting set must meet every triangle, whether or not it belongs to a particular chosen packing.

## A small example

In $K_4$, every two distinct triangles share an edge. Therefore at most one triangle can be chosen edge-disjointly, so $\nu_\triangle(K_4)=1$. Removing two opposite edges destroys all four triangles, and deleting only one does not, so $\tau_\triangle(K_4)=2$. The proposed factor two is **sharp**.

The immediate trivial upper bound is three: choose a maximal edge-disjoint packing of $k$ triangles, then remove all three edges from each chosen triangle. Any remaining triangle would be edge-disjoint from the packing and contradict maximality. Thus $\tau_\triangle(G)\le3\nu_\triangle(G)$; improving three to two is the genuine challenge.

## What is known

**OPEN and FINITELY FALSIFIABLE.** Tuza's conjecture is recorded in [Erdős #167](https://www.erdosproblems.com/167), with $K_4$ and $K_5$ demonstrating optimality of the factor two. Haxell (1999) proved the improved asymptotic bound

$$
\tau_\triangle(G)\le\left(3-\frac3{23}+o(1)\right)\nu_\triangle(G)
$$

in the applicable asymptotic setting. Kahn and Park (2022) established the conjectured inequality for random graphs in their model; random-graph validity does not imply the universal theorem.

There are also related **fractional** versions, but fractional triangle packings or hitting weights are not integer edge-disjoint packings or integer edge deletions. Substituting one for another changes the conjecture.

## GCL's current state

The pinned status is **FALSIFIABLE**, meaning a single finite counterexample would refute it. The maintained source, edited October 2025, still calls it open. Original source issue: [#1436](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1436). No purported finite counterexample or independently verified full proof is claimed. Exact edge semantics and both optimization directions are the material source-fidelity requirements.

The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1436#issuecomment-6168010738). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded task enumerates simple graphs on at most six vertices, lists every triangle, computes maximum pairwise edge-disjoint triangle families and minimum edge hitting sets using integer brute force, and checks $\tau_\triangle\le2\nu_\triangle$. Acceptance: $K_4$ produces $(\nu,\tau)=(1,2)$ and each candidate optimization is verified independently by exhaustive search. Stop after a finite certificate; observing no small counterexample cannot prove Tuza's conjecture.
