## The question in plain language

For n≥1 define h(n) as the smallest h≥2 such that gcd(2^n−1,3^n−1,...,h^n−1)=1. The maintained text calls these numbers 'mutually coprime', but this must mean their COMBINED gcd is one, not PAIRWISE gcd one. Under pairwise interpretation the stated elementary property h(n)=n+1 iff n+1 prime fails. The source asks whether the density of {n:h(n)=p} exists for each prime p, whether liminf h(n)=∞, and a prime-divisor criterion involving p>n^ε; these remain OPEN.

## A small example

Take n=4: 2⁴−1=15, 3⁴−1=80, 4⁴−1=255 all divisible by 5, but 5⁴−1=624 is coprime to 5. The overall gcd of 15,80,255,624 is 1, so h(4)=5, matching n+1=5 prime. Yet 15 and 80 share gcd 5, and are NOT pairwise coprime. This exact n=4 calculation exposes the semantic necessity of collective gcd. For n=2, gcd(3,8)=1, so h(2)=3.

## What is known

The maintained catalogue asserts h(n)=n+1 iff n+1 is prime and h(n) unbounded along odd n; it also conjectures h(n)=3 for infinitely many n. The site's literal word 'minimal ... mutually coprime' might be misread as pairwise-coprime, which would make the minimal h=2 trivial for one term or fail the n=4 prime characterization. The source-consistent collective-gcd formulation is mathematically nontrivial; its infinite density claims are not resolved.
