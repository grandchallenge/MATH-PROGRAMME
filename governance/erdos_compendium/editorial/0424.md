## The question in plain language

Start with the two positive integers $2$ and $3$. Whenever two **distinct** values $x$ and $y$ have already appeared, add the integer $xy-1$ to the collection. Repeat this process indefinitely, using new entries as well as old ones. Erdős Problem 424 asks whether the resulting set of positive integers contains a positive proportion of the integers, in an appropriate asymptotic sense.

This formulation was suggested by Douglas Hofstadter. It is not a conventional linear recurrence: each generation may contain many new integers, and previously generated values can participate in many different products. The set of all eventual values, not their order of discovery, is the mathematical object under study.

The word **density** needs particular care. Positive lower density means that a fixed positive fraction of integers up to $X$ belongs to the set for every sufficiently large $X$. Positive ordinary natural density additionally requires the density ratio to converge to a limit. These statements are related but not identical. A stronger historical formulation, that *almost all* integers occur, has a simple counterargument.

## A small example

The initial set is $\{2,3\}$. Combining its two different elements gives $2\cdot3-1=5$. Combining $5$ with the earlier entries gives $2\cdot5-1=9$ and $3\cdot5-1=14$. Continuing yields $2\cdot9-1=17$ and $3\cdot9-1=26$, among many other values. This explains the familiar prefix $2,3,5,9,14,17,26,\ldots$ associated with [OEIS A005244](https://oeis.org/A005244).

There is a rigorous invariant hidden in this simple arithmetic. Both starting values lie in the residue classes $0$ or $2$ modulo $3$. If $x,y$ are each congruent to $0$ or $2$ modulo $3$, then $xy-1$ is again congruent to $0$ or $2$ modulo $3$:

$$
0\cdot0-1\equiv2,\quad
0\cdot2-1\equiv2,\quad
2\cdot2-1\equiv0\pmod3.
$$

By induction, **no generated integer is congruent to $1$ modulo $3$**. These excluded integers have natural density $1/3$. Consequently the eventual generated set has upper density at most $2/3$; in particular, it cannot contain almost every positive integer. This is a complete elementary argument, not just a finite computational pattern. It does not, however, prove that the generated set has any positive lower density.

## What is known

The question appears in Erdős, *Problems and results on combinatorial number theory, III* (1977), printed p. 71, and Erdős and Graham, *Old and New Problems and Results in Combinatorial Number Theory* (1980), printed p. 84 of the [scanned book](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~ronspubs/80_11_number_theory.pdf). The maintained [Erdős Problems #424](https://www.erdosproblems.com/424) page explains that the 1980 “almost all” wording is too strong, using the modulo-3 obstruction attributed to Stefan Steinerberger. It interprets the intended question as one about positive density, likely positive *lower* density.

The maintained page, last edited on March 31, 2026, labels the question **OPEN**. There is also newer source material not automatically covered by that status. A public [candidate proof claim](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/424/proof-claims#proof-claim-91) attributes an argument for positive lower density to Samuel Korsky with AI assistance, accompanied by a separate [Lean formalization](https://github.com/plby/lean-proofs/blob/main/src/latest/ErdosProblems/Erdos424.lean) associated with Boris Alexeev. External reports describe a successful Lean build of that formalization. They do not, by themselves, establish that the formal theorem exactly matches the intended historical problem.

This distinction is mathematically substantive. The external formal development identifies its result as **positive lower density**, while the older pinned FormalConjectures statement uses a predicate named HasPosDensity. The exact semantics of that predicate, the generated-set definitions and the implications between the two claims require comparison. A machine can correctly verify one proposition without establishing that it is the one the historical author intended. No GCL-independent reproduction of the external 6,394-line build was performed for this article.
