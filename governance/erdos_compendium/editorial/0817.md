## The question in plain language

Given k>=3, let g_k(n) be the LEAST N for which some set A subset {1,...,N} of size n has all its {0,1}-subset sums free of any NONTRIVIAL arithmetic progression of length k. The subset-sum family includes the empty sum 0. Is g_3(n)>>3^n with absolute constant? OPEN.

## A small example

Take A={1,3,9}. Its eight subset sums {0,1,3,4,9,10,12,13} contain no nonconstant 3-term AP. Digitwise proof: if x,z and y have ternary digits 0/1 and x+z=2y, there is no carrying, so in each digit x_i+z_i=2y_i, which forces x_i=y_i=z_i. Therefore the progression is trivial. This exact proof applies to all n and establishes an upper bound, not the desired lower bound.

## What is known

Maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/817, read 2026-10-14: Erdős–Sárközy obtained g_3(n)>>3^n/n^O(1), but the stronger constant-times-3^n lower bound remains unknown. Historical theorem original bibliography appears implicit and full source not verified. A simple upper construction A={1,3,...,3^(n-1)} has all subset sums represented by ternary digits 0 or1, so a nontrivial 3-term AP cannot occur; g_3(n)<=3^(n-1).

There is an explicit family proving the upper bound $g_3(n)\leq3^{n-1}$: take $A=\{1,3,\ldots,3^{n-1}\}$. Every subset sum has base-three digits only 0 and 1. If three such sums $x,y,z$ satisfy $x+z=2y$, adding digits makes no carries. At each digit, the equation $x_i+z_i=2y_i$ with all digits 0 or 1 forces $x_i=y_i=z_i$. Thus $x=y=z$ and there is no nontrivial arithmetic progression. This elementary constructive lemma is a genuine finite/combinatorial derivation, but it establishes an UPPER bound, not the conjectured constant times $3^n$ lower bound.

The relevant quantity is the LEAST possible maximum generator,

$$

g_k(n)=\min\left\{\max A:\ |A|=n,\
\left\{\sum_{a\in A}\epsilon_a a:\epsilon_a\in\{0,1\}\right\}
\text{ has no nontrivial }k\text{-term AP}\right\}.

$$

The powers-of-three construction already proves $g_3(n)\leq3^{n-1}$. This upper construction does not resolve the open conjecture that $g_3(n)\gg3^n$ with a constant independent of $n$.
