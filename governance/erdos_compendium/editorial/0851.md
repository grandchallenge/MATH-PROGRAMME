## The question in plain language

Romanoff proved that powers of two plus primes cover a set of positive density. The stronger problem allows boundedly-many prime factors and asks for density arbitrarily close to one. A solved status is reported, but the model-assisted proof needs independent source review.

For every epsilon>0, does there exist a finite r=r(epsilon) such that integers of the form 2^k+n (k>=0, positive n with at most r prime factors) have LOWER NATURAL DENSITY at least 1-epsilon? The count of prime factors must specify distinct omega(n) versus multiplicity Omega(n), and limiting density/lower density convention must be source-locked.

## A small example

Finite integers 10=2^0+9=1+3² (nine has one distinct prime factor but two with multiplicity), and 10=2^3+2 (one factor). Thus the classification of near-primes differs for omega and Omega and k may equal zero. Enumerating small targets cannot certify density arbitrarily close to one.

This finite calculation tests definitions, not a result quantified over all large N or infinite sets.

## What is known

Maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/851 read 2026-10-14 reports YES by Price using GPT-5.2 Pro in the comments, as opposed to Romanoff [Ro34] positive density for prime n (r=1). Sawhney and Green reportedly claim a quantitative version r<<log(1/epsilon)/loglog(1/epsilon), but that appears in comments, not a fully reviewed published theorem in current audit. The entire external proof, its constants, denominator for small epsilon and meanings of 'prime divisors', and whether the bound is really valid for epsilon tending to zero remain pending independent source review.

A prime factor count can mean either the number of distinct prime factors, conventionally omega, or the count WITH multiplicities, conventionally Omega. For example nine has omega(9)=1 and Omega(9)=2. Exact example ten can be written as 2^0+9 or 2^3+2. Without checking which convention Price's source proof uses, one cannot turn a finite membership example into the global density theorem. The asserted quantitative dependence on epsilon from Sawhney and Green remains a source-comment claim pending primary proof verification.
