## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #579](https://www.erdosproblems.com/579) concerns the **octahedron graph**, written $K_{2,2,2}$. It has six vertices in three pairs: vertices in different pairs are connected, while the two vertices in the same pair are not. The question asks whether forbidding this graph as a subgraph forces every sufficiently dense graph to have a large independent set—a collection of vertices with no edges among them.

The original assertion says that for **every** edge-density threshold $\delta>0$, there should be a constant $c(\delta)>0$ such that all sufficiently large $K_{2,2,2}$-free simple graphs with at least $\delta n^2$ edges have an independent set of size at least $c(\delta)n$. Here “free” means the forbidden configuration cannot occur even as a *non-induced* subgraph. The precise order of the quantifiers matters.

**Status of the original universal assertion: disproved by an externally authored Lean proof, independently checked and admitted by GCL MATHCERT at Level 5 in November 2026.** This report concerns that specific assertion; it does not determine the exact Ramsey–Turán density of the octahedron.

## A small example

A complete graph on three vertices is automatically $K_{2,2,2}$-free: six vertices would be needed to contain an octahedron. But this small example says nothing about what happens for arbitrarily large graphs.

The new counterexample is an **asymptotic family**, not a six-vertex trick. The formal result gives a fixed density

$$
\delta=\frac{3}{2048}>0.
$$

For every $c>0$ and every threshold $N$, it produces an integer $n\ge N$ and a finite simple graph $G$ on $n$ vertices such that:

$$
K_{2,2,2}\not\subseteq G,\qquad
e(G)\ge \frac{3}{2048}n^2,\qquad
\alpha(G)<cn.
$$

Here $e(G)$ is the number of *unordered* edges and $\alpha(G)$ is its maximum independent-set size. Because $c$ can be chosen arbitrarily small, no uniform positive lower bound on $\alpha(G)/n$ can hold for this fixed positive density. Thus the original universal claim is false. The finite triangle above illustrates the forbidden-subgraph definition only; it is not the construction behind the proof.

## What is known

Erdős, Hajnal, Sós and Szemerédi studied this question in [*More results on Ramsey–Turán type problems*](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1983-09.pdf), *Combinatorica* **3** (1983), 69–81; see printed page 72. Their historical bound places the relevant critical density at or below $1/8$.

A later **external** [Conjectures.io disproof](https://conjectures.io/results/e4934265-aa96-4bf5-a3c0-d01153dfcfaa) supplies the positive-density counterexample at $3/2048$. GCL did **not** originate that proof. Its credited author and exact source are to be taken from the external provider record, not inferred from GCL's verification work.

The two source-qualified bounds together locate the critical density between $3/2048$ and $1/8$ under the corresponding definitions, but **neither establishes the sharp value**. The external author's proof and GCL's formal replay establish the negative answer to the original universal statement, not an optimal density theorem.
