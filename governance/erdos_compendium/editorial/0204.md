## The question in plain language

Choose a positive integer $n$ and assign a residue class $a_d\bmod d$ to **every divisor $d>1$ of $n$**. Can these classes cover every integer while satisfying a special restriction on intersections? Namely, whenever the classes for two **distinct** moduli $d,d'$ have a common integer, require $\gcd(d,d')=1$.

The condition does **not** demand that every pair of classes be disjoint. It permits overlaps only when their moduli are coprime, a much weaker-looking requirement than complete disjointness but still too strong for full coverage.

## A small example

The classes $0\bmod2$ and $0\bmod4$ intersect at every multiple of four, while their moduli have gcd two. Such a pair violates the overlap rule. The classes $0\bmod2$ and $0\bmod3$ also intersect, but their moduli are coprime, so that overlap is allowed.

For each $d$ the residue is chosen independently. Checking **only** adjacent divisors, or excluding prime-power divisors, does not verify the full question.

## What is known

**DISPROVED.** Sarosh Adenwalla, [*A Question of Erdős and Graham on Covering Systems*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15170), 2025, proves that **no positive integer $n$** admits this full covering with the prescribed intersection constraint. The paper also defines *nice integers*, whose divisors support congruence assignments with the restricted-overlap condition **without requiring coverage**, and obtains necessary conditions for niceness.

Zhengkun Jia and Huixi Li, [*Resolving Adenwalla's conjecture related to a question of Erdős and Graham about covering systems*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09579), develop the subsequent characterization of nice integers. It is a **different** existence question: being nice does not produce the forbidden *full covering*.

The maintained [Erdős #204](https://www.erdosproblems.com/204) identifies the original question as disproved and notes a residual optimization problem: how large a **density** can congruence classes cover subject to the restricted-overlap rule? That density problem survives the impossibility of density one.

## What remains

A bounded successor computes all divisors for $2\le n\le24$, enumerates residue assignments, tests pairwise overlap by the Chinese remainder theorem, and calculates the fraction of a least-common-multiple period covered. Acceptance: overlap is forbidden **only** when gcd exceeds one; the full-covering predicate is separately tested and no candidate is claimed to resolve the universal theorem. Preserve the all-divisors quantifier.
