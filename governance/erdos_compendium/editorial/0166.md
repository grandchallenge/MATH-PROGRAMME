## The question in plain language

The off-diagonal Ramsey number $R(4,k)$ is the least $N$ such that every graph on $N$ vertices contains either a complete graph on four vertices or an independent set on $k$ vertices. Erdős conjectured that it has a **cubic lower bound up to polylogarithmic factors**, namely that there is some fixed $C$ with

$$
R(4,k)\gg\frac{k^3}{(\log k)^C}.
$$

This asks about growth on the cubic scale, not a precise leading constant. It is unrelated to $R(C_4,k)$, in which a red **four-cycle** is forbidden instead of a four-clique.

## A small example

The five-cycle is $K_4$ free and has no independent triple, so it gives $R(4,3)>5$. A large $K_4$ free graph with independence number below $k$ witnesses a lower bound for $R(4,k)$. Such graphs are the objects constructed by extremal probabilistic or algebraic methods.

## What is known

**PROVED.** Sam Mattheus and Jacques Verstraete, [*The asymptotics of $r(4,t)$*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04007), 2023, prove

$$
R(4,k)=\Omega\left(\frac{k^3}{\log^4 k}\right).
$$

The explicit fourth power of the logarithm meets the original unspecified-polylogarithm conjecture. It is not a claim that $R(4,k)\sim c k^3/\log^4 k$. Ajtai–Komlós–Szemerédi earlier proved the upper estimate

$$
R(4,k)\ll\frac{k^3}{(\log k)^2}.
$$

Thus a gap of roughly $(\log k)^2$ remains between the known bounds. Spencer's prior lower estimate $(k\log k)^{5/2}$ was superseded on the polynomial scale.
