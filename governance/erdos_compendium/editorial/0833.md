## The question in plain language

A hypergraph requiring three vertex colors cannot have all its edges spread too thinly among the vertices. Erdős and Lovász established an exponential lower bound for a necessary high-degree vertex, resolving the qualitative exponential question without computing every exact small-uniformity value.

For r>=2, an r-uniform hypergraph with chromatic number EXACTLY 3 must have some vertex degree at least c_r 2^r/r, and hence at least (1+c)^r for some absolute c>0 for sufficiently large r. f(r) is the greatest integer guaranteed as maximum vertex degree among all such hypergraphs; the existence of a universal exponential lower bound is proved, but exact f(r) need not be determined.

## A small example

For r=3, the Fano plane has seven edges and each vertex lies in exactly three; its chromatic number is 3, so it witnesses maximum degree exactly three for that example. For r=2, a triangle has chromatic number 3 and maximum degree two.

This finite calculation is not an asymptotic or all-parameter proof.

## What is known

Maintained /latex/833 directly read 2026-10-14. Erdős–Lovász [ErLo75], *Problems and results on 3-chromatic hypergraphs and some related questions* (1975) pp609–627 prove a vertex of degree at least 2^(r-1)/(4r). It reports f(2)=2 and f(3)=3; exact f(4) is NOT resolved by the stated exponential lower bound. No original full theorem-proof replay yet.

Erdős and Lovász's source-reported vertex-degree guarantee is

$$

\Delta(H)\geq\frac{2^{r-1}}{4r}.

$$

For sufficiently large r, this exceeds (1+c)^r for some fixed positive c because the numerator grows like 2^r and the denominator is only linear in r. The Fano plane provides a finite three-uniform, three-chromatic witness that is regular of degree three.
