## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1200](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1200) has maintained status **OPEN — MANDATORY CRITICAL** at this audit.

Erdős–Ruzsa ask whether there is **one absolute constant $C$** such that for **every sufficiently large real $x$** one can choose distinct primes $p_1<\cdots<p_k<x$ and **one residue class $a_i\bmod p_i$ per prime**, with
$$\sum_{i=1}^k\frac1{p_i}<C,$$
and **every positive integer $n<x$** belonging to at least one selected class. This is coverage of a **finite initial interval**, not a distinct covering system of all integers; imposing all-integer periodic coverage would change the question. The primes and residue classes may depend on $x$, but $C$ must not. The related **opposite** ErRu80 question asks whether for every fixed reciprocal-sum bound all residue assignments leave a positive proportion of the interval uncovered; proving that would disprove the covering conjecture.

## A small example

For $x=12$, the only available primes are $2,3,5,7,11$. Independently enumerate every choice of no class or exactly one class per prime: $(2+1)(3+1)(5+1)(7+1)(11+1)=6912$ assignments. Exactly **14** cover all integers $1,\dots,11$. The **minimum reciprocal mass** among these 14 is $2927/2310$ for the classes $(0\bmod2),(0\bmod3),(1\bmod5),(0\bmod7),(5\bmod11)$. This finite minimum is NOT a universal bound $C$, and covering the initial interval is NOT the same as covering all integers.

## What is known

The maintained catalogue https://www.erdosproblems.com/1200 leaves this **OPEN**, originally [Er80,p.106] and Erdős–Ruzsa [ErRu80]. Their published partial result: for fixed $C>0$ there exist prime families with $\sum1/p\le C$ whose **zero-residue divisibility classes** cover a positive proportion $\gg_C x$, far weaker than **all** of $[1,x)$. The page connects #688: a uniform positive lower bound for a separate epsilon parameter would imply this statement. No general bounded-reciprocal-sum covering or unavoidable-uncovered-density theorem was source-certified by GCL.
