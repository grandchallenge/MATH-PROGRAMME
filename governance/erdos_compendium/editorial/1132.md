## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1132](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1132) asks whether polynomial interpolation must become unstable at a **fixed point** as more nodes are added. For an infinite sequence of distinct nodes $x_1,x_2,\ldots\in[-1,1]$, let $\ell_{k,n}$ be the Lagrange basis for its first $n$ nodes and set

$$
L_n(x)=\sum_{k=1}^n|\ell_{k,n}(x)|.
$$

Must some fixed $x\in(-1,1)$ satisfy $L_n(x)>(2/\pi)\log n-O(1)$ infinitely often? Does $\limsup_n L_n(x)/\log n\ge2/\pi$ hold for almost every $x$? The order of quantifiers matters.

## A small example

For nodes $-1,0,1$, exact rational computation gives $L_3(0)=1$ and $L_3(1/2)=5/4$. These finite values do not establish growth for a *fixed point* along a nested sequence.

## What is known

Erdős established a universal bound on $\max_x L_n(x)$ for each finite $n$, but those maximizing points may vary with $n$. Bernstein's work gives an **everywhere-dense** set of points attaining the limiting logarithmic coefficient. The maintained catalogue reports that Tao (2026) obtained a dense set of points with

$$
L_n(x)\ge(2/\pi)\log n-\omega(n)
$$

infinitely often for every prescribed divergent $\omega(n)$. This weaker error does not imply bounded $O(1)$ error, and density is not the same as full Lebesgue measure. The source also questions whether the $O(1)$ constant may depend on $x$. The full problem remains open.

## What remains

Inspect Bernstein, Erdős and Tao primary arguments, check the point-dependent constant and dense-set quantifier, and investigate actually nested interpolation nodes. A sequence of separately optimized node configurations is not interchangeable with one infinite sequence.
