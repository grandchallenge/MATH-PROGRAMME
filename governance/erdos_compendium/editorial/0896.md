## The question in plain language

Choose two sets $A$ and $B$ of positive integers, each contained in $[1,N]$. For each positive integer $m$, count **ordered pairs with prescribed roles** $(a,b)$ satisfying $a\in A$, $b\in B$ and $ab=m$. Let $F(A,B)$ be the number of different products $m$ having **exactly one** such representation.

[Erdős Problem #896](https://www.erdosproblems.com/896) asks how large $F(A,B)$ can be, over all choices of $A,B\subseteq[1,N]$. The target counts products of *multiplicity one*, not all products and not all ordered pairs. Swapping the factors can create a second representation if both assignments are permitted.

The **order of magnitude** is reported solved. Determining its leading constant or proving the detailed construction directly in GCL is a separate task.

## A small example

Take $A=\{2\}$ and $B=\{3\}$. The only represented product is $6$, and it has exactly one permitted representation, so $F(A,B)=1$. If both sets are instead $\{2,3\}$, the product $6$ is represented twice, as $(2,3)$ and $(3,2)$, and does **not** contribute to $F$.

An exhaustive computation over every one of the $4^N$ ordered pairs of subsets, for all $1\le N\le8$, gives:

| $N$ | Maximum $F(A,B)$ |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 2 |
| 3 | 4 |
| 4 | 7 |
| 5 | 10 |
| 6 | 13 |
| 7 | 18 |
| 8 | 22 |

These are exact **finite** maxima, not asymptotic growth estimates. The executable fixture records attaining sets and the sizes of the entire multiplication tables.

A useful infinite family of examples is elementary. Let $P$ be the primes in $(N/2,N]$, take $B=P$ and $A=[1,N]\setminus P$. A prime $p>N/2$ has no other positive multiple at most $N$. Thus an $a\in A$ has no prime divisor in $P$. Any product $ap$ uniquely identifies $p$ as its sole prime divisor drawn from $P$, hence uniquely identifies $a$. Therefore

$$
F(A,B)=(N-|P|)|P|.
$$

This yields an accessible lower-bound mechanism of order $N^2/\log N$ using standard prime-density estimates. It is weaker than the sharp published logarithmic exponent.

## What is known

Kevin Ford, [*The distribution of integers with a divisor in a given interval*](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2008.168.367), *Annals of Mathematics* **168** (2008), pp.367–433, establishes the growth order of the set of **all distinct** products in an $N$ by $N$ multiplication table:

$$
\#\{ab:1\le a,b\le N\}
\asymp
\frac{N^2}{(\log N)^\delta(\log\log N)^{3/2}},
\quad
\delta=1-\frac{1+\log\log2}{\log2}.
$$

Consequently this quantity is an **upper bound** for every $F(A,B)$, since a uniquely represented product is certainly an element of the unrestricted multiplication table. Ford also gave an accessible derivation using integers with a divisor in $(y,2y]$, in [*Integers with a divisor in $(y,2y]$*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0607473).

The lower bound of the *same order* was reported by Przemek Chojecki on April 26, 2026, based on a GPT-5.5 Pro construction that generalizes Szemerédi's large-prime idea and employs Ford's divisor-distribution methods. The maintained [Erdős Problem #896](https://www.erdosproblems.com/896) adopted the sharp-order conclusion on May 2, 2026, recording

$$
\max_{A,B\subseteq[1,N]}F(A,B)
\asymp
\frac{N^2}{(\log N)^\delta(\log\log N)^{3/2}}.
$$

The complete lower-bound derivation has **not** been independently replayed by GCL. A public commenter reported finding no issue in a standard review, but that does not replace an independent source-level proof audit or a formal certificate.

The historical question is indexed [Er72, p.81] in the catalogue. The 1972 source page has not been independently recovered and visually checked in this execution; its exact original wording remains a source-fidelity obligation. There is no corresponding protected Formal Conjectures file in GCL's pinned provider snapshot.

## GCL's current state

The finite problem can be expressed by the multiplicity function

$$
r_{A,B}(m)=\#\{(a,b)\in A\times B:ab=m\},
\qquad
F(A,B)=\#\{m:r_{A,B}(m)=1\}.
$$

This definition makes the roles of $A$ and $B$ explicit and excludes products with **two or more** representations. The exact script at governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_896_897.py enumerates every pair of subset masks for $N\le8$, builds the complete multiplicity map and verifies the maximizing witnesses. It also checks the elementary large-prime construction at $N=8,12,24,40,80$, confirming that all its products are unique.

The proof of uniqueness for the large-prime construction does **not** require checking a finite range. Given $m=ap$ with $p\in P$, the factor $a$ has no prime divisor in $P$. If also $m=a'p'$ with $p'\in P$, the number $m$ has exactly one prime divisor from $P$, so $p=p'$ and $a=a'$. The formula for $F(A,B)$ follows immediately.

The published sharp-order lower construction is not obtained by taking $A=[N]\setminus P$ and $B=P$; claiming otherwise would silently replace its more substantial analytic argument with a weaker bound.

## What remains

A bounded reusable task is the **unique-representation multiplicity interface**. Prove that the ordered-fiber function $r_{A,B}$ counts exactly the admissible representations and that for the large-prime construction each fiber has size one. Acceptance requires a complete elementary proof or pinned, compiled no-placeholder Lean theorem with literal axiom readback, exact $4^N$ enumeration for $N\le8$ and independent regeneration of the maximizing witnesses. A duplicate factorization of a purportedly unique product falsifies the lemma.
