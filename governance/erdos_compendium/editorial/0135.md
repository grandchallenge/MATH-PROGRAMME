## The question in plain language

Choose $n$ distinct points $A$ in the Euclidean plane. Count the **different positive distances** between unordered pairs. Erdős and Gyárfás imposed a strong local condition: **every four-point subset** must determine at least five distinct distances among its six pairs.

They conjectured that this forces a quadratic number of different distances globally:

$$
|\{|x-y|:x,y\in A,\ x\ne y\}|\gg n^2.
$$

The constant should be independent of $n$ and the particular point set. It is insufficient that some four-point subsets determine five lengths; the condition is universal.

## A small example

A unit square has six unordered point pairs but only **two** distinct lengths, its side and diagonal. It violates the local hypothesis and cannot serve as a counterexample. Four generic points may determine six different distances, satisfying the local condition for that finite set; this does not establish a global quadratic bound for large configurations.

The local condition restricts repetitions inside each four-point induced distance pattern but does **not** prohibit repetitions between pairs belonging to unrelated four-point configurations.

## What is known

**DISPROVED.** Terence Tao's original 2024 paper, [*Planar point sets with forbidden 4-point patterns and few distinct distances*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01343), constructs for all sufficiently large $n$ an $n$ point planar set satisfying the every-four-point condition but determining at most

$$
O\left(\frac{n^2}{\sqrt{\log n}}\right)
$$

distinct distances. This is $o(n^2)$ and therefore contradicts the proposed positive-constant quadratic lower bound.

Tao's [author explanation dated 3 September 2024](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2024/09/03/planar-point-sets-with-forbidden-four-point-patterns-and-few-distinct-distances/) identifies three main components: Dumitrescu's classification of eight forbidden four-point distance patterns, Thiele and Dumitrescu's algebraic construction that avoids some patterns, and a randomized transformation eliminating the rest. The classification is a tool; it is not alone the desired asymptotic construction.

The problem belongs to a broader family asking how many distances are forced when every set of $p$ points determines at least $q$ distinct distances. The negative answer here does not automatically settle other pairs $(p,q)$.

## What remains

A bounded finite fixture enumerates all four-point subsets of small rational-coordinate planar sets and counts distinct exact **squared distances** among their six unordered pairs. Acceptance: a unit square fails with two distinct squared lengths, while a generic fixture with six distinct lengths passes. Stop at finite semantics. This cannot certify the asymptotic construction or improve the asymptotic exponent.
