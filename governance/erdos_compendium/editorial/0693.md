## The question in plain language

Fix an integer k≥2. Let A be the increasing sequence of integers in [n,n^k] having at least one divisor STRICTLY between n and 2n. Erdős asks how large the largest gap between consecutive elements of A can become as n→∞ with k fixed. Is the maximal gap bounded by some fixed power of log n (constants may depend on k)? The maintained catalogue remains OPEN, with original [Er79e] uninspected.

## A small example

For n=10,k=2, the first qualifying values are 11,12,…,19 and then 22,24,26,28,... . Exact enumeration of all a∈[10,100] gives 51 qualifying integers and maximum internal consecutive gap 4. For n=12,k=2, the maximum is 6. The interval endpoints and the strict divisor inequality matter: 20 does not qualify when n=10 merely because 20 divides itself or 10 divides 20.

## What is known

Every integer d in (n,2n) qualifies itself, so A contains n+1,n+2,…,2n−1. Beyond that initial block, A is the union of positive multiples of each d∈{n+1,…,2n−1}, truncated to [n,n^k]. Simple periodicity gives an eventual repetition of a finite modulus lcm(n+1,…,2n−1), but this modulus is vastly larger than any power of log n, so it does not prove the desired bound. For a finite n,k, the membership test is existential divisibility by a number in (n,2n); neither the density of the union nor a positive proportion of covered integers rules out long sparse gaps. The maintained page cross-references #446 without claiming an established polylogarithmic upper bound.
