## The question in plain language

Let f(n) be the smallest value of gcd(n,C(n,k)) for integers k with 2≤k≤n/2. Erdős and Szekeres ask three different questions about this function in [Problem #700](https://www.erdosproblems.com/700): when f(n)=n/P(n) for composite n; whether infinitely many composite n have f(n)>sqrt(n); and whether f(n) enjoys savings beyond every fixed power of log n. Here P(n) is the largest prime factor of n. All three remain OPEN.

## A small example

For n=30, exact binomial gcd arithmetic gives f(30)=6, achieved at k=5, and 30/P(30)=30/5=6. For n=25, f(25)=5, which is exactly sqrt(25), not STRICTLY greater. At n=12, the minimum is f(12)=3, reached at k=4. These exact finite values cannot resolve infinitude or universal asymptotic bounds.

## What is known

The maintained source credits Erdős and Szekeres with elementary f(n)≤n/P(n) for composite n, f(p^a)=p for prime powers a≥2 and f(pq)=p when p<q are distinct primes. Prime squares therefore satisfy f(p²)=sqrt(p²), meeting a NONSTRICT bound but not the second conjecture's strict inequality. The [pinned #700 Lean file](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/700.lean) includes separate main statements with unresolved answer and proof placeholders. Its auxiliary special-case lemmas require actual environment replay before certification.
