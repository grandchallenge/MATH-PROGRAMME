## The question in plain language

For any infinite cardinal m and graph G with chromatic number exactly m, does G contain a (not necessarily induced) subgraph with chromatic number n for every infinite n<m? Erdős #739 is NOT PROVABLE AS A UNIVERSAL ZFC THEOREM if the underlying set-theoretic consistency results are accepted. The maintained source records relative consistency of a negative answer and a positive result under the axiom of constructibility for m=aleph_2 and n=aleph_1. It remains open under the Generalized Continuum Hypothesis in the source, so 'not provable' must not be read as an unconditional ZFC theorem of negation.

## A small example

A finite analogy is elementary: the complete graph K_4 has chromatic number four, and contains a triangle K_3 of chromatic number three, as well as an edge K_2. This simple finite monotonicity of chromatic number is NOT a proof that one can realize all intermediate infinite cardinal chromatic numbers in an arbitrary infinite graph; transferring it would ignore the set-theoretic consistency phenomenon.

## What is known

Galvin [Ga73] proved a countable-chromatic special result and pointed out that requiring the subgraph to be INDUCED imposes much stronger cardinal-arithmetic consequences. Komjáth [Ko88b], 'Consistency results on infinite graphs', Israel Journal of Mathematics 61 (1988) 285–294, gives a model where a graph of chromatic number aleph_2 has no subgraph of chromatic number aleph_1, under the relative consistency of stated cardinal arithmetic. Shelah [Sh90] proves a positive statement under constructibility. A finite graph search cannot test independence from set theory or forcing arguments. The primary technical model constructions were not independently verified.
