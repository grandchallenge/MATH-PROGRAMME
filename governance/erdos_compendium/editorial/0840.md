## The question in plain language

A quasi-Sidon family has asymptotically almost no repeated unordered pair sums. Its asymptotic size may exceed that of a Sidon set. The precise leading square-root extremal constant remains unknown.

How large can quasi-Sidon $A\subset[1,N]$ be as $N\to\infty$, where $|A+A|=(1+o(1))\binom{|A|}{2}$? This is an ASYMPTOTIC condition on a family A_N, not a decidable Boolean property of one finite set A at one N without specifying an allowed error function. A+A includes equal summands; both O(|A|) diagonal sums and o(|A|²) collisions are tolerated.

## A small example

For finite genuine Sidon $A=\{1,2,4,8\}$, the unordered pair sums WITH repetitions are all ten distinct, so $|A+A|=10$; $\binom{4}{2}$=6. The ratio 10/6 illustrates why o(1) allows diagonal O(|A|) overhead only as |A| grows and cannot be used as exact finite equality.

This finite calculation is not an asymptotic or all-parameter proof.

## What is known

Maintained /latex/840 read 2026-10-14. Erdős–Freud [ErFr91] and Erdős [Er81h] establish lower $(2/\sqrt{3}+o(1))\sqrt{N}$ and upper $(2+o(1))\sqrt{N}$. Pikhurko [Pi06], *Dense edge-magic graphs and thin additive bases* (2006), improves upper to $((1/4+1/(\pi+2)^2)^{-1/2}+o(1))\sqrt{N}$, roughly 1.863sqrt N. Exact leading constant still OPEN. Related #819. The finite f(N) definition needs quantified asymptotic family semantics and source validation.

For a family $A_N\subseteq[1,N]$ with $m_N=|A_N|\to\infty$, the quasi-Sidon condition means

$$
|A_N+A_N|=(1+o(1))\binom{m_N}{2}.
$$

A genuine Sidon set has $\binom{m_N+1}{2}$ unordered pair sums with repetition, and the extra diagonal $m_N$ is asymptotically negligible. The quasi-Sidon condition does not provide a Boolean test for a single isolated finite set without specifying a tolerance. The remaining square-root leading constant is open.
