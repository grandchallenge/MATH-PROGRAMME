## The question in plain language

Let $\tau(n)$ count the positive divisors of $n$. Define $F(x)$ to be the maximum length $k$ of a block $n+1,\ldots,n+k\le x$ on which all the values

$$
\tau(n+1),\ldots,\tau(n+k)
$$

are **pairwise distinct**. Erdős and Mirsky asked for the growth of $F(x)$. In particular, is

$$
F(x)\le(\log x)^{O(1)}
$$

for sufficiently large $x$? This is still open. [Maintained Erdős Problem #945](https://www.erdosproblems.com/945).

## A small example

The integers $2,3,4$ have divisor counts $2,2,3$, so this three-term block fails. The block $4,5,6$ has counts $3,2,4$, all different, and establishes $F(x)\ge3$ for every $x\ge6$. Even such simple examples show that the condition concerns **divisor counts**, not divisibility or primality of the integers themselves.

There is a useful exact finite algorithm: initialize a divisor-count array $\tau(m)=0$ for $1\le m\le X$; for each $d\le X$, increment the entries for all multiples of $d$. Then scan consecutive windows while maintaining a frequency table of divisor counts. The longest collision-free window gives $F(X)$. This computation checks the definition but cannot determine asymptotic growth.

## What is known

Erdős and Mirsky [ErMi52] proved

$$
\frac{(\log x)^{1/2}}{\log\log x}\ll F(x)
\ll \exp\!\left(O\!\left(\frac{(\log x)^{1/2}}{\log\log x}\right)\right).
$$

Erdős [Er85e] suggested the lower-bound method could be sharpened to $(\log x)^{1-o(1)}$, but that remark is not itself a proved upgrade.

Adrian Beker subsequently improved the upper estimate, as recorded in the maintained catalogue, to

$$
F(x)\ll\exp\!\left(O\!\left((\log x)^{1/3+o(1)}\right)\right).
$$

This still grows faster than any fixed power of $\log x$. The stronger bound is attributed to Beker's argument, and source/publication status must be verified before labeling it a formally adjudicated theorem.

Stijn Cambie observed that **Cramér's conjecture** would imply $F(x)\ll(\log x)^2$. A different sufficient hypothesis is that every interval of length a sufficiently large multiple of $\log x$ inside $[x,2x]$ contains a squarefree integer; that stronger local-squarefree assumption also implies $F(x)\ll(\log x)^2$. These are conditional implications, not currently unconditional proofs of the polylogarithmic bound.

## Why the problem is difficult

The function $\tau(n)=\prod_{p^a\parallel n}(a+1)$ depends on the prime-exponent pattern. It fluctuates markedly between neighboring integers. The assertion demands that **every sufficiently long interval** contain a repeated divisor count, so average statistics alone do not suffice. A short exceptional window with unusually diverse factorizations can defeat local heuristics.

One must also distinguish a block ending at $x$ from a block contained anywhere in $[1,x]$. The definition uses the latter maximum, and replacing it with the former changes the problem.

## GCL's current state

This article records the proven Erdős–Mirsky bounds, the later attributed refinement, and precisely conditional polylogarithmic conclusions. It does not establish the conjecture or independently reproduce Beker's proof. A finite computation may discover long collision-free blocks but cannot certify the limiting upper bound.
