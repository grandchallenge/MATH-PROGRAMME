## The question in plain language

Let $A=\{a_1<a_2<\cdots\}$ be an infinite increasing sequence of positive integers. Write $[b_1,\ldots,b_k]$ for their least common multiple, and set

$$
F(A,X,k)=\#\{i\geq1:[a_i,\ldots,a_{i+k-1}]<X\}.
$$

Erdős and Szemerédi asked whether, for every $\varepsilon>0$, one can choose a block length $k$ so that $F(A,X,k)<X^\varepsilon$. The live wording does not explicitly attach a range of $X$ to the final inequality. The standard asymptotic interpretation is **for all sufficiently large $X$**; the distinction must be made explicit before claiming semantic equivalence with a Lean statement. The bound developed below is uniform over all sequences $A$ and implies the strict all $X>0$ version for every $\varepsilon>1/4$ after enlarging $k$.

## A small example

A small least common multiple says that the integers in a block share enough prime factors to keep their combined divisibility information small. Three consecutive terms of a specially chosen sequence can exploit this overlap repeatedly. However, the more consecutive terms we demand, the harder it becomes to maintain such extensive sharing. The question is whether a sufficiently long window forces the number of exceptional blocks to grow more slowly than *every* positive power of $X$.

The classical $k=3$ theorem gives $F(A,X,3)\ll X^{1/3}\log X$; Erdős and Szemerédi also constructed sequences meeting that order for infinitely many $X$. This is a theorem about triples, not an obstruction to better bounds for longer windows.

## What is known

The maintained problem page was still marked **OPEN** in the accessible 2026 snapshot. Its discussion contains April 2026 partial bounds, but the page warns that comments are not vetted. Patrick Letendre's 2025 paper concerns divisors of one integer in a short interval; it does **not** itself state a solution to #873. An independent July 2026 working report by Erdős Problem a Day derives the explicit finite-block exponents below using a prime-valuation inequality. This is useful reproducible prior work, **not** a new GCL priority claim or a solution of the full problem.

For $k\geq2$, put

$$
\Delta_k=\begin{cases}
(k+1)/(4k),&k\text{ odd},\\
k/(4(k-1)),&k\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
$$

The report establishes the elementary bound

$$
F(A,X,k)\leq k\lceil\log_2 X\rceil(1+X^{\Delta_k}),\qquad X\geq2.
$$

For example $\Delta_3=1/3$, $\Delta_5=3/10$, and $\Delta_{11}=3/11$. As $k\to\infty$, these exponents approach $1/4$ from above. Thus every $\varepsilon>1/4$ is covered, but **the range $0<\varepsilon\leq1/4$ is not settled** by this argument.

## GCL's current state

The underlying inequality is elementary and can be verified without trusting an asymptotic calculation. For distinct positive integers $d_1,\ldots,d_k$, write $L=[d_1,\ldots,d_k]$. For $0\leq t\leq k$,

$$
L^{t(t+1)/2}\prod_{i<j}\gcd(d_i,d_j)
\geq\prod_{j=1}^{k}d_j^t.
$$

To see this, fix a prime and sort its valuations $x_1\leq\cdots\leq x_k=M$. The exponent on the left from the gcd product is $\sum_j(k-j)x_j$. Subtracting it from $t\sum_jx_j$ leaves $\sum_j(t-k+j)x_j$, whose positive terms have coefficients $1,\ldots,t$ and are bounded by $t(t+1)M/2$. This proves the inequality prime by prime.

If the $d_i$ lie in an interval of span $H$, with every $d_i\geq U$ and $L<X$, then $\gcd(d_i,d_j)\leq H$ yields

$$
H\geq U^{2t/(k-1)}X^{-t(t+1)/(k(k-1))}.
$$

For starts $a_i\in[U,2U)$, split start indices into $k$ residue classes. Within one class the corresponding windows are disjoint; the span inequality limits their number. Optimizing over $t$ and summing dyadic intervals gives the stated $\Delta_k$ bound. This is a **reconstruction of existing elementary reasoning**, not a proof past the $1/4$ barrier.

The accompanying `finite_873_874.py` tests 9,856 exact prime-valuation configurations and computes the finite extremum $M_k(X)=\max_A F(A,X,k)$ by two distinct methods: exhaustive enumeration of subsets of $[1,X)$ and a memoized longest-path recursion. For $X=14$, both find $M_2=5$, $M_3=4$, $M_4=3$, $M_5=2$. These finite computations neither prove nor refute the full asymptotic conjecture.

## What remains

A meaningful bounded successor would derive a **uniform divisor-clustering improvement** yielding a packing exponent below $1/4$, even by a fixed positive amount, for a specified block length. Its acceptance test must state the exact hypotheses, prove the improved span inequality, and independently check every transition from local divisibility to global disjoint-window packing. If the argument still maximizes at exponent $1/4$, record that precise obstruction instead of asserting a breakthrough.
