## The question in plain language

For n-element set A of POSITIVE integers and graph G on A with at least n^(1+c) edges, define graph-restricted sumset A+_G A={a+b: {a,b} edge of G} and product set A*_G A similarly. The strong conjecture for every c,epsilon>0 demanded max cardinality >= n^(1+c-epsilon) for all sufficiently large n.

The graph-restricted sum/product conjecture does not inherit the truth status of the familiar unrestricted sum-product question. A dense chosen edge set may create comparatively few distinct sums and products. The published counterexample explicitly exploits this restriction, while a different, weaker universal lower bound remains valid.

## A small example

For A={1,2,3,4} and G with edges {1,2}, {1,3}, {2,4}, the graph-restricted sums are {3,4,6} and products {2,3,8}; both have size three, while the full unrestricted A+A and A*A may be larger. Graph edges are UNORDERED distinct pairs.

This finite fixture tests the conventions. It does not certify any of the asymptotic or all-graphs assertions.

## What is known

Maintained #808 DISPROVED by Alon–Ruzsa–Solymosi [ARS20], *Sums, products, and ratios along the edges of a graph* (Publ. Mat. 2020): constructs graphs with >=n^(5/3-o(1)) edges but graph-sum and graph-product counts O(n^(4/3+o(1))). They separately prove lower max(|sums|,|products|) >=constant m^(3/2) n^(-7/4) for m edges. This does not disprove ordinary unrestricted sum-product question #52, and original ARS20 full proof not reproduced.

The distinctions between established partial theorems, original open parts, and disproved universal claims have been preserved. Historical publications are attributed, rather than presented as new GCL mathematics.

The positive theorem cited alongside the counterexample is, writing $m=|E(G)|$,

$$
\max\bigl(|A+_GA|,\ |A\cdot_GA|\bigr)
\gg m^{3/2}n^{-7/4}.
$$

The stronger proposed exponent $1+c-\varepsilon$ is false for graph-restricted sums and products. The theorem about the unrestricted sets $A+A$ and $A\cdot A$ is a separate problem.
