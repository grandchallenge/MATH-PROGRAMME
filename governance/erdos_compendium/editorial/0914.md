## The question in plain language

Suppose a graph has exactly $rm$ vertices, where $r\ge2$ and $m\ge1$. If **every** vertex is adjacent to at least $m(r-1)$ others, must the graph contain $m$ completely separate cliques of size $r$? Separate means **vertex-disjoint**, so together those cliques use every vertex.

This is [Erdős Problem #914](https://www.erdosproblems.com/914), a classical affirmative theorem of Hajnal and Szemerédi. For $r=2$ it asserts a perfect matching, and for $r=3$ it concerns a triangle factor. The general result is known as the **Hajnal–Szemerédi theorem**.

An equivalent formulation colors the **complement graph** with exactly $m$ colors so that every color class contains exactly $r$ vertices. This is equitable vertex coloring: vertices assigned the same color have no edge between them in the complement, hence form a clique in the original graph.

## A small example

Consider six vertices with $r=3$ and $m=2$. The hypothesis requires every vertex to have degree at least

$$
2(3-1)=4.
$$

Then there are two vertex-disjoint triangles covering all six vertices. The complete graph on six vertices plainly has many such partitions. A more informative borderline example is the complete bipartite graph $K_{3,3}$: every vertex has degree **three**, one less than the required four, and the graph contains **no** triangle at all.

This shows that the degree bound cannot simply be lowered by one for all parameters. The condition is a universal sufficient threshold, not a claim that a graph with lower minimum degree can never admit a clique partition.

## What is known

Corrádi and Hajnal, [*On the maximal number of independent circuits in a graph*](https://www.erdosproblems.com/914), *Acta Mathematica Academiae Scientiarum Hungaricae* **14** (1963), pp.423–439, established the triangle case. Hajnal and Szemerédi, [*Proof of a conjecture of P. Erdős*](https://www.erdosproblems.com/914), in *Combinatorial Theory and Its Applications*, volume II, Colloquia Mathematica Societatis János Bolyai **4** (1970), pp.601–623, proved the general theorem.

Henry Kierstead and Alexandr Kostochka later published [*A Short Proof of the Hajnal–Szemerédi Theorem on Equitable Colouring*](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0963548307008619), *Combinatorics, Probability and Computing* **17** (2008), pp.265–270. Its publisher abstract states the stronger standard version: every graph with maximum degree at most $\Delta$ has an equitable proper coloring with $\Delta+1$ colors. Their proof yields a polynomial-time coloring algorithm. The exact parameters of #914 follow from this result by complementing the graph.

The maintained [Erdős catalogue #914](https://www.erdosproblems.com/914) marks the result **PROVED (LEAN)**. A [2026 comment](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/914) reports that Aristotle completed a formalization of the Kierstead–Kostochka argument after a long computation. A [public formalization file](https://github.com/Woett/Lean-files/blob/main/ErdosProblem914.lean) contains several thousand lines of Lean, and [Formal Conjectures #914](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/main/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/914.lean) links a separate external proof. The public upstream theorem definitions still have placeholders. GCL has **not** independently pinned, compiled or axiom-audited either external proof.

## GCL's current state

For a graph $G$ on $rm$ vertices, the degree identity

$$
d_{\overline G}(v)=rm-1-d_G(v)
$$

makes the hypothesis equivalent to

$$
\Delta(\overline G)\le m-1.
$$

A coloring of the complement with $m$ color classes of equal size $r$ is exactly a partition of the original graph into $m$ disjoint complete subgraphs of order $r$. This equivalence is elementary and does not require importing the difficult general existence theorem.

The executable finite fixture at governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_914_915.py enumerates all labelled simple graphs on two through six vertices. For every factorization $n=rm$ with $r\ge2$, it tests each graph satisfying the degree condition, then performs an **exact-cover search** over its cliques of size $r$ to find a partition of all $n$ vertices. All **1,949** qualifying graph-and-parameter cases pass. The same fixture verifies the six-vertex $K_{3,3}$ sharp-threshold example.

These finite checks are regressions for the source statement and complement-coloring interface. They do not establish the Hajnal–Szemerédi theorem for arbitrarily large graphs.

## What remains

The first bounded theorem is the **complement equivalence**: formally show that minimum degree at least $m(r-1)$ for a graph on $rm$ vertices is equivalent to complement maximum degree at most $m-1$, and that a clique partition corresponds bijectively to an equitable proper complement coloring. Acceptance requires a complete proof or a no-placeholder Lean theorem with exact axiom reporting, plus all 1,949 finite regressions.

The deeper independent task is to reconstruct the 2008 equitable-coloring proof, including its algorithmic invariant and the exact degree assumptions. If GCL adopts the reported 2026 Lean result, pin the original source commit, Lean and Mathlib versions, verify there are no hidden placeholders or additional axioms, compile it and compare the theorem statement against the #914 degree and cardinality conventions. Until then the published mathematical result is established, but a **GCL-certified formal proof** is not.
