## The question in plain language

Write the primes in increasing order as $p_1,p_2,\ldots$ and their successive gaps as $d_n=p_{n+1}-p_n$. [Erdős Problem #6](https://www.erdosproblems.com/6), originating with Erdős and Turán, asked whether infinitely many starting positions satisfy

$$
d_n<d_{n+1}<d_{n+2}.
$$

This is a question about **three successive gaps between four consecutive primes**. It is **proved**, not an outstanding Erdős conjecture: Banks, Freiberg and Turnage-Butterbaugh established the affirmative answer in 2015 and proved arbitrarily long increasing and decreasing runs.

## A small example

Four consecutive primes are $17,19,23,29$, with gaps $2,4,6$. They provide one strictly increasing run, but no finite example proves that there are infinitely many.

Why can arbitrarily long runs be constructed? Suppose exponents $e_0<e_1<\cdots<e_m$ are positive integers. For shifts $x_j=2^{e_j}$, the successive differences satisfy

$$
x_{j+1}-x_j >\sum_{i<j}(x_{i+1}-x_i)\quad(j\ge1).
$$

Indeed, the sum on the right telescopes to $2^{e_j}-2^{e_0}$, while $2^{e_{j+1}}-2^{e_j}\ge 2^{e_j}$. This is a finite elementary inequality, not the deep assertion that these shifts can become **consecutive primes**.

## What is known

William Banks, Tristan Freiberg and Caroline Turnage-Butterbaugh, *Consecutive primes in tuples*, *Acta Arithmetica* **167** (2015), 261–266, Corollary 2 (printed pp. 262–263), prove that for every prescribed finite run length there are infinitely many strictly increasing prime-gap runs and infinitely many decreasing runs. Their Theorem 1 uses the Maynard–Tao machinery to arrange selected elements of suitable prime tuples as **consecutive** primes. The elementary power-of-two inequalities alone cannot replace that deep sieve input.

## GCL's current state

The [GCL source audit](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1275#issuecomment-6103346576) compares the published theorem with the frozen Formal Conjectures statement and checks an important index convention: the pinned Lean definition uses a zero-based prime enumerator. A parameter in a formal variant counts the number of **gap comparisons**, not the number of gaps; translation from the published theorem is therefore shifted by one. The provider proof bodies and GCL audit are not a newly replayed, kernel-certified proof of the 2015 publication.

## What remains

**Nothing remains to resolve the historical existence question**; it was answered affirmatively in the literature. Separate formalization work may still be needed to state, import and kernel-check the published theorem with exact index conventions and its external sieve dependencies. Publication here is educational exposition, not new theorem certification.
