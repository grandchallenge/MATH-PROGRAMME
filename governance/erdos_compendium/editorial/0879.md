## The question in plain language

Select a finite set of **pairwise coprime** positive integers not exceeding $n$. Define $G(n)$ to be the greatest possible sum of these selected integers. Erdős and van Lint compared this extremum with

$$
H(n)=\sum_{\substack{p<n\p\ {\rm prime}}}p+n\pi(\sqrt n),
$$

where $\pi(x)$ counts primes not exceeding $x$. [Problem #879](https://www.erdosproblems.com/879) asks whether the error between these quantities is at most $n^{1+o(1)}$ in the proposed one-sided sense, and whether for each fixed $k\ge2$ **every** maximizer for sufficiently large $n$ must contain an integer with at least $k$ different prime divisors.

The integer $1$ is legal and coprime to every other positive integer, so it contributes one to the maximum. This is a different extremal problem from #878, which restricts all prime divisors of the chosen summands to those dividing $n$.

## A small example

For $n=30$, the set

$$
S=\{1,11,13,17,19,23,25,27,28,29\}
$$

is pairwise coprime and has sum **193**. Exhaustive prime-support optimization proves $G(30)=193$, while the comparison function has value $H(30)=219$. For $n=60$, the exact optimizer obtains $G(60)=594$, versus $H(60)=680$, with one valid witness

$$
\{1,17,23,29,31,37,41,43,47,49,52,53,55,57,59\}.
$$

At $n=30$ the largest sum obtainable by allowing only integers with **at most one distinct prime divisor** is 188. Here prime powers such as 25 and 27 are allowed. Therefore every maximizing set at this particular cutoff contains an integer with two different prime factors, but this does not prove the eventually for all $n$ assertion.

## What is known

The primary source is Erdős, [*On Two Unconventional Number Theoretic Functions and on Some Related Problems*](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1984-16.pdf), 1984, printed p.120. Erdős reports joint work with van Lint giving the approximate comparison

$$
H(n)-n^{3/2-o(1)}<G(n)<H(n),\qquad
\frac{H(n)-G(n)}n\longrightarrow\infty.
$$

The proposed stronger bound is of order $n^{1+o(1)}$. The 1984 paper discusses it under additional prime-distribution assumptions, **not** as an unconditional theorem. The maintained [Erdős catalogue #879](https://www.erdosproblems.com/879) records the two-distinct-prime-factor case as solved and the higher fixed values of $k$ statement as unresolved; the multipart problem is **open**.

A source-fidelity issue is material: Erdős's 1984 formulation uses summands strictly **less** than $n$, whereas the maintained problem page permits summands **at most** $n$. A prime $n$ itself is therefore treated differently in the two formulations. This article and its finite computations explicitly use the maintained inclusive convention and do not present it as identical to the historical strict inequality.

## GCL's current state

We reduce the finite problem to **weighted packing of prime supports**. Each positive integer greater than one corresponds to the set of its distinct prime factors. Two chosen integers are coprime exactly when these supports are disjoint. For a fixed nonempty prime support, replacing a selected integer by the largest number at most $n$ with exactly that support can only increase the sum and cannot create an overlap.

An exact dynamic program maximizes the sum of the weights assigned to disjoint prime-support sets, with the legal summand $1$ included once. Primes greater than $n/2$ form forced singleton choices in an optimum, since their only positive multiple at most $n$ is the prime itself. A separately implemented direct GCD-subset search checks small values.

The finite research fixture computes $G(n)$ for every $1\le n\le70$, directly cross-checks $1\le n\le14$, verifies witnesses, and evaluates both $G$ and $H$ at $n=30,60$. These are finite results; no independent reproduction of the analytic van Lint estimate or of the infinite prime-factor condition is claimed.

## What remains

A bounded successor is the **finite support-partition identity**, including the permitted 1 and the prime greater than $n/2$ simplification. Given all prime supports of integers in $[1,n]$ and the largest weight for each support, prove the exact weighted disjoint-packing formula for $G(n)$. Its acceptance test is a complete elementary proof or independently compiled Lean theorem without placeholders, plus direct enumeration for $n\le14$ and the correct numerical witnesses at 30 and 60.

A pairwise-coprime set whose sum exceeds the dynamic-programming optimum falsifies the reduction. Stop at this finite exact certificate; no fixed finite range can settle the asymptotic error or eventual requirement for every $k\ge3$.
