## The question in plain language

Let δ_1(n,m) be the natural density of integers having **exactly one positive divisor** in the open interval (n,m). For a fixed n, Erdős asked whether the values as m increases beyond n+1 rise to a maximum and then fall (unimodality), and where any maximum occurs. The maintained status is DISPROVED (externally LEAN-reported): the uniform unimodality claim is false, even for n=3. This does not mean all detailed maximizing indices are classified.

## A small example

For n=3, only divisors 4 and 5 count when m=6. In one period L=lcm(4,5)=20, seven integers have exactly one of the divisors: δ_1(3,6)=7/20. Adding divisor 6 gives δ_1(3,7)=1/3. Adding divisor 7 gives δ_1(3,8)=38/105. These exact rational values obey 7/20 > 1/3 < 38/105, a strict dip followed by a rise, so unimodality already fails at n=3. All calculations are finite CRT densities, not numerical sampling approximations.

## What is known
