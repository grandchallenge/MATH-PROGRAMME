## The question in plain language

A Ramsey graph is a graph with neither a large clique nor a large independent set. Erdős #78 asks for a **constructive**, rather than merely probabilistic, proof that the diagonal Ramsey number satisfies

$$
R(k)>C^k
$$

for some fixed constant $C>1$ and all sufficiently large $k$. Here $R(k)$ is the least number of vertices forcing a monochromatic $K_k$ in every two-edge-colouring of the complete graph. Finding a graph with no clique or independent set of order $k$ shows $R(k)$ exceeds its vertex count.

## A small example

Color the five-cycle's edges red and its complement blue. Both red and blue graphs are $C_5$, so neither contains a triangle and $R(3)>5$. This explicit **finite** witness has no bearing on the required uniform exponential family.

For a graph on $N$ vertices with both clique number and independence number below $k$, one must exhibit a single algorithm that constructs the graph for arbitrarily large $k$ while keeping $N>C^k$. A family whose forbidden homogeneous size is merely $N^{0.01}$ does not suffice: $N^{0.01}$ grows much faster than $\log N$.

## What is known

The problem remains **OPEN** in its stated constructive exponential form. Erdős's probabilistic argument gives $R(k)\gg k2^{k/2}$, but sampling a random graph and showing that a suitable outcome exists does not supply the intended deterministic, efficient construction.

Gil Cohen's primary paper [*Two-Source Dispersers for Polylogarithmic Entropy and Improved Ramsey Graphs*](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1096219) constructs explicit Ramsey graphs with no homogeneous set of order $2^{(\log\log N)^c}$ for a universal $c>0$. The maintained problem page records an improvement by Li to a polylogarithmic bound $(\log N)^C$. Both are major advances but fall short of controlling the largest homogeneous set by a **constant** multiple of $\log N$.

## What remains

A bounded task is to formalize the equivalence between a deterministic $N$ vertex graph with $\omega(G),\alpha(G)<k$ and the lower bound $R(k)>N$. Acceptance: an exact finite checker verifies the five-cycle witness and the order of quantifiers for a proposed graph-construction algorithm. Falsification: any monochromatic $K_k$, inappropriate dependence of the construction on a random oracle, or only polynomially small homogeneous sets. Stop before claiming any new exponential family.
