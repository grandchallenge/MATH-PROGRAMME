## The question in plain language

Let $a,b,c>1$ be **pairwise coprime** integers. Can every sufficiently large positive integer be expressed as a sum of distinct elements of

$$
S(a,b,c)=\{a^ib^jc^k:i,j,k\ge0\}
$$

such that **no selected summand divides another**? The property is called *divisibility-free completeness*, or $d$ completeness. Ordinary additive completeness, in which some summands divide others, is weaker.

## A small example

For the coprime bases $(2,3,5)$, the numbers $2$, $3$ and $5$ are pairwise nondividing members of $S$, and their sum equals $10$. A sum containing $2$ and $6$ violates the divisibility restriction, since $2$ divides $6$. A single finite representation says nothing about representations of all sufficiently large integers.

The weaker hypothesis $\gcd(a,b,c)=1$ is insufficient. The source gives $(6,10,15)$: modulo three, every allowed power product is zero unless it is a pure power of $10$, which is one modulo three. A sum congruent to two therefore requires at least two pure powers of $10$, and these divide one another.

## What is known

**PROVED in a published 2026 proof claim, with reported third-party Lean formalization.** The maintained [Erdős #123](https://www.erdosproblems.com/123), edited 17 July 2026, attributes the affirmative answer to GPT-5.6 prompted by Colin Snyder. It links a [mathematical proof summary](http://www.thomasbloom.org/erdos123.pdf). The current upstream [Formal Conjectures entry](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/main/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/123.lean) credits a formalization by Boris Alexeev and identifies [this third-party Lean proof snapshot](https://github.com/plby/lean-proofs/blob/a28a04b6b8ce43d5260a7466677c1f23833bfc38/src/latest/ErdosProblems/Erdos123.lean).

The protected statement at revision 85f86371 was directly read. Its theorem quantifies over $a,b,c>1$, pairwise coprimality, and a finite antichain of summands under divisibility. Its body contains a proof hole: existence of the protected *statement* is not itself proof execution. Earlier Erdős–Lewin work proved special cases, including $(3,5,7)$. An additional *snugness* problem for $(2,3,5)$ requires all selected summands to be close in size; the general $d$ completeness theorem does not settle that stronger variant.
