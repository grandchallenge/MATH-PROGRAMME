## The question in plain language

For every bipartite graph G the Erdős–Simonovits question asks whether ex(n,G)~c n^α for some c>0 and α∈[1,2), and whether α must be rational. A weaker recorded version only asks ex(n,G) asymptotic up to constant factors to a power. The source's literal universal scope needs a degeneracy qualification: for G=K_2 (a single edge), ex(n,G)=0 for every n≥2, incompatible with c>0, α≥1. Intended discussion evidently concerns suitable nontrivial bipartite forbidden graphs; the exact original hypotheses must be source-locked. The meaningful nondegenerate problem remains OPEN.

## A small example

For G=C_4=K_{2,2}, classical extremal theory gives ex(n,C_4)=(1/2+o(1))n^(3/2): an exponent α=3/2 and positive constant 1/2 are known. For G=K_2, any graph with even one edge contains G, so ex(n,K_2)=0 identically, an exact counterexample to the UNQUALIFIED wording. These do not refute a carefully restricted nondegenerate Erdős–Simonovits conjecture.

## What is known

The maintained source (updated 2026-03-07) differentiates the original claim that α belongs to special forms 1+1/k or 2−1/k (already DISPROVED by Erdős–Simonovits 1970) from the still-OPEN existence/rationality question for a general bipartite G. Frankl–Füredi 1987 and Füredi–Gerbner 2021 provide counterexamples to analogous pure-power behaviour for uniform hypergraphs of uniformity at least five; these hypergraph results do NOT settle graph r=2.
