## The question in plain language

Colour the positive integers red and blue. For each positive common difference $d$, let $L(d)$ be the maximum length of a **monochromatic arithmetic progression** with difference $d$; it may be infinite. Erdős and Cohen asked for the strongest growth guarantee $f(d)$ such that **in every two-colouring** there are infinitely many differences $d$ for which $L(d)\ge f(d)$.

The quantifiers are central: one colouring must be tested across infinitely many values of $d$. Requiring a long progression for *every* $d$, or allowing a different colouring for each $d$, would be a different problem.

## A small example

The alternating colouring of the positive integers has no monochromatic two-term progression of odd difference, but every arithmetic progression of even difference is monochromatic, of arbitrarily large length. Thus the example gives large $L(d)$ on even differences. It does not establish a universal result across arbitrary colourings.

For a finite colouring prefix, calculate maximal same-colour runs on each residue class modulo $d$; consecutive terms in such a run are a progression of difference $d$. A finite prefix cannot certify infinitely many good differences.

## What is known

The optimal universal growth remains **OPEN**. Van der Waerden's theorem forces any valid universal growth threshold to tend to infinity along appropriate differences. Erdős observed that a colouring based on the fractional parts of $\sqrt2 n$ limits an achievable universal $f(d)$ to $O(d)$; Petruska and Szemerédi improved this to $O(\sqrt d)$.

Beck's 1980 probabilistic construction improves the obstruction sharply: it produces a colouring showing that one cannot force more than approximately

$$
(1+o(1))\log_2 d
$$

as a universal threshold. This is an **upper obstruction** on what can be guaranteed, not a lower guarantee of logarithmic monochromatic progressions. Erdős conjectured an upper scale $d^{o(1)}$, consistent with Beck's result but much weaker. The [maintained #187](https://www.erdosproblems.com/187), updated April 2026, still labels the best possible guaranteed growth open.

## What remains

A bounded task constructs the alternating-colouring example and a second periodic colouring, enumerates monochromatic runs for differences $d\le12$ on finite prefixes, and checks the exact definition of $L(d)$. Acceptance: odd-difference two-term progressions fail under alternating colours, while even differences have unbounded monochromatic runs. Stop before extrapolating finite data into a guarantee for infinitely many $d$.
