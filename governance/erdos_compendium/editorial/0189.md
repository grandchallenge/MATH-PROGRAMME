## The question in plain language

Colour **all points** of the plane with finitely many colours. Erdős and Graham asked whether some colour class must contain vertices of a rectangle of **every prescribed positive area**. The rectangle may translate and rotate, and its side lengths may vary as long as their product is the required area.

This is stronger than forcing one congruence class of rectangles and different from asking for an axis-aligned rectangle only. Its quantifiers ask that, for every colouring and every target area, **some** colour class realizes that area; a single colouring avoiding one area disproves the universal statement.

## A small example

A rectangle with perpendicular side vectors $u,v$ has four vertices $x$, $x+u$, $x+v$, $x+u+v$ and area $|u||v|$. The square with vertices $(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)$ has area one. Proving that a finite colouring avoids **every** such rectangle of area one requires checking all translations and rotations, not just the unit grid.

Rescaling the whole plane changes rectangle areas quadratically, so a construction forbidding unit area can be rescaled to forbid any other **single prescribed** positive area. This does not imply a single colouring can exclude every area simultaneously.

## What is known

**DISPROVED.** Vjekoslav Kovač, [*Coloring and density theorems for configurations of a given volume*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09973), constructed a **25-colouring of the plane** with no monochromatic rectangle of area one (Theorem 3). The journal version, [*Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society*](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms.70143), supplies an explicit construction. Its proof uses an alternating quadratic complex-coordinate invariant for parallelograms.

A newer improvement by Gary Hu and Yumo Liu, [*A 21-Coloring of the Plane Without Monochromatic Unit-Area Rectangles*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00583) (September 2026), replaces Kovač's square tiling by hexagonal cells and reduces the stated number of colours to **21**. This is an external preprint claim, not an independently reproduced construction. The disproof is already complete with 25 colours regardless of its verification.

The related question for **parallelograms of every prescribed area** remains distinct, as does Graham's positive theorem for right triangles. The maintained [Erdős #189](https://www.erdosproblems.com/189) labels the original rectangle statement **DISPROVED (LEAN)**.

## What remains

A bounded task verifies the finite colouring rule of Kovač's Theorem 3, proving that any monochromatic parallelogram has consecutive side-length product different from one. Acceptance: the exact 25-colour function, the quadratic invariant, and the all-translations/all-rotations quantifiers are checked; a separate comparison can examine the 21-colour refinement. Stop before claiming an independent formal certification.
