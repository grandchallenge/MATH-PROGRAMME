## The question in plain language

Two disjoint groups of vertices $A$ and $B$ are called *anticomplete* when **no edge** of a graph runs from one group to the other.

[Erdős Problem #1111](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1111) asks whether graphs that need very many vertex colours, but do not contain large complete subgraphs, must contain **two anticomplete subsets that each need many colours themselves**.

More precisely, given $t,c\ge1$, is there a threshold $d(t,c)$ such that every finite graph $G$ with

$$
\chi(G)\ge d(t,c),\qquad \omega(G)<t
$$

has disjoint anticomplete $A,B$ with $\chi(G[A]),\chi(G[B])\ge c$?

## A small example

The five-cycle $C_5$ has chromatic number three and clique number two. It contains five pairs of disjoint edges, but none of those pairs is anticomplete: in every case another cycle edge connects an endpoint of one to an endpoint of the other.

Thus $C_5$ has no anticomplete pair $A,B$ for which both induced graphs need at least two colours. The exact five-edge enumeration illustrates why the threshold for $(t,c)=(3,2)$ cannot be three. The maintained source reports $d(3,2)=4$.

## What is known

El Zahar and Erdős (1985) studied the question and established several bounded-chromatic cases. The maintained catalogue reports results for $c=2$ and a bound $d(3,3)\le8$.

A later theorem of Nguyen, Scott and Seymour (2024) produces anticomplete $A,B$ with $\chi(G[B])\ge c$ while the graph induced on $A$ has **minimum degree at least $c$**.

That distinction matters. Minimum degree at least $c$ does **not** imply chromatic number at least $c$: a complete bipartite graph $K_{c,c}$ has minimum degree $c$ but requires only two colours. The newer result therefore cannot be substituted for the two-chromatic-subset assertion without a further argument.

The broad two-subset chromatic question remains listed as **open**.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source audit #2382](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2382#issuecomment-6168850250) checks the exact $C_5$ obstruction and separates the minimum-degree variant from the original chromatic conclusion.

The original 1985 and 2024 proofs are not yet independently source-reviewed. The maintained catalogue does not list a formalized version of the original problem.

## What remains

Check both original theorems and determine whether there are additional hypotheses that could bridge minimum degree to chromatic complexity in the anticomplete setting. Independently review the small values of $d(t,c)$ and their proof methods.
