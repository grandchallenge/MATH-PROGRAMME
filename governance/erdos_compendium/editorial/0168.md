## The question in plain language

Let $F(N)$ be the greatest cardinality of a subset $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ containing **no full triple** of the form

$$
\{n,2n,3n\}
$$

for any positive integer $n$. Erdős and Graham asked for the limiting maximum density

$$
\delta=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{F(N)}N
$$

and, in particular, **whether $\delta$ is irrational**. The existence of the limit, its numerical value and its irrationality are **three distinct** questions.

## A small example

For $N=3$, the only forbidden triple is $\{1,2,3\}$, so a maximal admissible set has two elements: $F(3)=2$. For $N=6$, both $\{1,2,3\}$ and $\{2,4,6\}$ are forbidden, and one can choose four or more positions without creating a complete triple; the exact maximum should be computed rather than inferred from density.

A useful structural observation is that each positive integer has a unique factorization $m\,2^a3^b$ with $\gcd(m,6)=1$. A forbidden triple $\{n,2n,3n\}$ remains in the **same** $m$ component, so the problem decomposes into independent finite optimization problems on lattices of powers of two and three.

## What is known

The **existence** of $\delta$ was proved by Graham, Spencer and Witsenhausen (1977). They express it as a convergent series involving the **3-smooth** numbers $d_1<d_2<\cdots$ (numbers with prime factors only two and three) and finite extremal counts $f(k)$:

$$
\delta=\frac13\sum_{k\in K}\frac1{d_k},
\qquad K=\{k:f(k)>f(k-1)\}.
$$

This formula makes the constant computable. In the [maintained #168 discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/168), Sean Eberhard gives a corrected high-precision numerical estimate

$$
\delta=0.80096575500655898909042032638808241\ldots
$$

which agrees with the site's summary $0.800965\ldots$. **Irrationality remains OPEN**. A long nonrepeating-looking decimal expansion is no proof of irrationality. The maintained catalogue calls the umbrella problem open because that arithmetic characterization is not known.

There is a further source-fidelity warning: the discussion reports that an older proposed finite smooth-number equality fails at $k=45$. An implementer must recompute the true finite optimum rather than reuse an unverified shortcut.

## GCL's current state

The pinned and maintained catalogue classify #168 **OPEN**; issue [#1437](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1437). The statement separates the already proved limit and numerical estimate from the unproved irrationality. The original 1977 proof and the full precision computation have not been independently reproduced.

The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1437#issuecomment-6168033189). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded successor decomposes all integers up to $N=100$ by their unique $m$ coprime to six, enumerates the 3-smooth subproblem, and computes exact $F(N)$ with a second whole-interval integer-programming formulation. Acceptance: both formulations agree for every tested cutoff, the $N=3$ fixture yields two, and contributions are explicitly linked to each smooth-number index. Stop after exact finite checks and rigorous series-tail bounds; do not claim an irrationality proof.
