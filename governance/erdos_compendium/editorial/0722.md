## The question in plain language

For fixed integers k>r≥2, a Steiner system $S(r,k,n)$ is a family of k-element blocks in an n-element set such that EVERY r-element subset belongs to exactly one block. Erdős asked whether, for all sufficiently large admissible n, such systems exist whenever $\binom{k-i}{r-i}$ divides $\binom{n-i}{r-i}$ for all i=0,…,r−1. The answer is PROVED by Peter Keevash; 'sufficiently large depending on k,r' is essential.

## A small example

The Fano plane is $S(2,3,7)$: take seven points indexed modulo 7 and lines {j,j+1,j+3} modulo 7, j=0,…,6. Every unordered pair of points occurs in exactly one of the seven triples. Parameters satisfy $\binom{3}{2}$=3 | $\binom{7}{2}$=21 and 2|6. This finite example is not Keevash's arbitrary-parameter existence proof.

## What is known

Peter Keevash, 'The existence of designs', Keevash's primary preprint *The existence of designs* (arXiv:1401.3665, 2014) establishes general existence from admissibility for all sufficiently large n. The arXiv author abstract and Princeton's 2014 theorem announcement corroborate the large-n qualification; no journal volume is assigned here without bibliographic verification. Earlier Kirkman S(2,3), Hanani S(3,4), and Wilson S(2,k) special cases are distinct. Counting incidences proves the integrality/divisibility conditions necessary, but not sufficient without Keevash's deep theorem. The maintained catalogue #722 says PROVED; proof pages and assumptions not GCL-replayed.
