## The question in plain language

Imagine a dense graph where every edge lies in at least one triangle. Must some edge lie in **many** triangles? Triangles sharing an edge form a *book*. For a fixed $c>0$, Erdős #80 asks how large a book can always be guaranteed in an $n$ vertex graph with at least $cn^2$ edges and every edge in a triangle.

Writing $f_c(n)$ for the largest universally guaranteed number of pages, the historical questions include whether $f_c(n)>n^\varepsilon$ for some $\varepsilon>0$, or at least whether $f_c(n)$ is bounded below by a positive multiple of $\log n$.

## A small example

Every edge of the complete graph $K_5$ belongs to exactly three different triangles: choose the third vertex among the other three. Thus the largest book in $K_5$ has three pages. In a graph with triangles whose edges are used only once, the largest book may instead be one page.

The essential additional condition is that **every** edge belongs to a triangle. Mere existence of many triangles without that quantifier gives a different extremal problem. The density $c$ is fixed independently of $n$, and the interesting sparse-dense regime is $0<c<1/4$.

## What is known

The general **estimation problem remains OPEN**, but the proposed polynomial lower bound has been **DISPROVED** for every fixed $c<1/4$. Jacob Fox and Po-Shen Loh, [*On a problem of Erdős and Rothschild on edges in triangles*](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/5618ed32-08ce-49d4-a351-28622e0eb050), *Combinatorica* 32 (2012), 619–628, establish the primary upper-bound construction

$$
f_c(n)\le n^{O_c(1/\log\log n)}.
$$

This grows slower than every fixed positive power of $n$. Their manuscript abstract explicitly describes the result as a negative answer to the 1987 question of Erdős. By contrast, for $c>1/4$, the classical Edwards and Khadzhiivanov–Nikiforov theorem guarantees an edge in at least $n/6$ triangles. Szemerédi's regularity lemma implies $f_c(n)\to\infty$ for fixed $c>0$, but the best general quantitative lower bound at fixed $c<1/4$ is weak. The stated logarithmic lower-bound question remains open.

## GCL's current state

GCL checked Fox–Loh's author-manuscript abstract, including its universal edge-in-triangle hypothesis and the strict $c<1/4$ range. The pinned catalogue remains **OPEN** because estimating $f_c(n)$ and the logarithmic lower bound are not settled. ## What remains

A bounded computational task is to enumerate labelled graphs up to seven vertices and compute $b(G)=\max_{uv\in E(G)}|N(u)\cap N(v)|$, rejecting graphs with an edge in no triangle. For each edge-density threshold, compute the minimum of $b(G)$ over valid graphs. Acceptance: exact triangle counts and a witness for each extremal value; stop at the finite enumeration. Do not infer asymptotic $\log n$ growth from these fixtures.
