## The question in plain language

Let \(S\subseteq\mathbb R\) be **sum-free**, meaning there are no \(a,b,c\in S\) satisfying \(a+b=c\). Repetition of elements is allowed in this definition, so \(0\notin S\) unless \(S\) is empty. Erdős asked whether there must be a set
\[
A\subseteq\mathbb R\setminus S,\qquad |A|=|\mathbb R|,
\qquad A+A\subseteq\mathbb R\setminus S,
\]
where \(A+A=\{a+a':a,a'\in A\}\). The question is open in the maintained catalogue. Note that every element of \(A\) lies outside \(S\), and **every** pairwise sum, including \(a+a\), must also avoid \(S\). [Erdős Problem #949](https://www.erdosproblems.com/949).

## A small example

Take \(S=(0,\infty)\), which is **not** sum-free, since \(1+1=2\). It is therefore not an admissible example. Instead take \(S=(1,2)\): the sum of two points in \(S\) is greater than \(2\), so \(S\) is sum-free. Set \(A=(-\infty,0)\). Then \(A\subseteq\mathbb R\setminus S\), \(A+A=(-\infty,0)\subseteq\mathbb R\setminus S\), and \(A\) has continuum cardinality. This proves the assertion for this particular \(S\), not for all sum-free subsets.

If \(S=\varnothing\), any continuum-sized \(A\), including \(A=\mathbb R\), works. Neither special example resolves the universal question.

## A graph formulation

On the vertex set \(V=\mathbb R\setminus S\), draw an edge between two **distinct** vertices \(x,y\) when \(x+y\in S\), and place a forbidden loop at \(x\) when \(2x\in S\). The desired \(A\) is an independent set of size continuum in this graph **avoiding all looped vertices**. This reformulation is exact. A standard simple-graph independence assertion that ignores the diagonal \(2x\) condition would be too weak.

A key difficulty is that \(|V|=|\mathbb R|\) does not automatically imply that such a large independent set exists: even an enormous graph can have small independence number.

## The special Sidon variant

Erdős proposed a fallback if the general answer were negative: assume \(S\) is **Sidon**, meaning unordered pair sums of elements of \(S\) are unique, apart from the unavoidable interchange of summands. The maintained catalogue reports a positive argument for the Sidon variant by Yael Dillies, found using AlphaProof. This claim is recorded in site comments; its proof and precise Sidon convention require independent adjudication before GCL certifies it. Importantly, the Sidon variant is **not** the full sum-free hypothesis. Neither property may silently be substituted for the other. [Catalogue discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/949).

The associated statement-formalized metadata only records that a mathematical statement has been encoded, not that the full problem has a formally verified affirmative solution.

## Why the problem is difficult

The condition on \(S\) prohibits additive triangles wholly within \(S\), but the desired \(A\) lives **outside** \(S\) and asks that sums of its elements never land in \(S\). Sum-freeness alone gives no immediate upper bound on the number of prohibited pairs among vertices of \(\mathbb R\setminus S\). Cardinality arguments must handle continuum many points, an uncountable family of constraints, and diagonal sums.

An attempted transfinite recursion choosing points one at a time needs to show that fewer than continuum prior choices cannot exhaust all admissible new points. That statement does not follow from sum-freeness without additional control over the forbidden translate sets \(S-a\).

## GCL's current state

The example \(S=(1,2)\) and the graph reformulation are elementary, exact checks. The general existence question remains **open**. The reported Sidon-case proof is attributed rather than independently certified, and no claim of a GCL Lean proof is made.
