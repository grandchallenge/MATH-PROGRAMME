## The question in plain language

A *Behrend sequence* is a set of positive integers such that almost every integer is divisible by at least one member of the set. [Erdős Problem #26](https://www.erdosproblems.com/26) asks whether, for **every infinite** set $A$ of positive integers, some fixed positive shift $k$ makes the shifted set $A+k$ a Behrend sequence.

The public question is **disproved**. However, there is an important formalization distinction: the pinned Formal Conjectures statement adds a hypothesis that $A$ is *thick*, meaning its reciprocal sum diverges. A counterexample using a thin set does not by itself refute that different, restricted statement.

## A small example

Consider the infinite set

$$
A=\{2^{2^j}:j\ge1\}=\{4,16,256,\ldots\}.
$$

For every positive shift $k$,

$$
\sum_{a\in A}\frac1{a+k}
\le \sum_{j\ge1}2^{-2^j}<\frac13.
$$

Among the integers up to $X$, the proportion divisible by one of the numbers $a+k$ is at most the sum of their reciprocal frequencies, up to vanishing finite-cutoff errors. Therefore those multiples cannot cover almost all integers, regardless of $k$. This directly disproves the **unrestricted** assertion. It does not address the thick-set variant because the reciprocal sum of $A$ converges.

## What is known

Classical work of Davenport and Erdős shows that a Behrend sequence must have divergent reciprocal sum. Ruzsa's congruence constructions provide more informative obstructions; the maintained source also describes later counterexamples to stronger variants, including thick-sequence formulations. These deeper claims require the precise hypotheses of their original constructions, not an extrapolation from the elementary thin example.

## GCL's current state

[Source audit #1295](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1295#issuecomment-6121415960) compared the maintained public wording with the pinned Formal Conjectures file, where the universal hypothesis explicitly includes a divergent reciprocal series. The frozen file has an unfilled theorem body but links to an external Lean proof. GCL has not replayed the external formal proof or certified the thick-case counterexample.

## What remains

The original unrestricted question is resolved negatively. Documentary and formal work must **separately** verify the stronger thick-sequence counterexample and its source/axiom provenance. Failing to distinguish these versions would allow a correct elementary argument to be falsely presented as a deep theorem.
