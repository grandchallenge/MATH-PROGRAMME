## The question in plain language

Prime numbers become less frequent as we count upwards, but the distances between successive primes fluctuate. If $p_n$ is the prime with index $n$, write

$$
d_n=p_{n+1}-p_n.
$$

These distances are *prime gaps*. Erdős [asked in 1985](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1985-17.pdf) how long a consecutive run of prime gaps can be before some gap size repeats. More precisely, $h(x)$ is the largest integer $H$ for which there exists a starting **prime index** $1\le n<x$ with

$$
d_n,d_{n+1},\ldots,d_{n+H-1}
$$

all different. The variable $x$ counts prime *indices*, not the numerical size of the primes. Erdős asked for estimates of $h(x)$, especially whether some fixed $c>0$ makes $h(x)>(\log x)^c$ for all sufficiently large $x$, and whether $h(x)=o(\log x)$.

Both inequalities are about the *longest available run* among all starting positions before $x$. The first asks whether long runs must eventually occur. The second asks whether even the best run is small compared with $\log x$. The questions are compatible: a run can be longer than a small power of $\log x$ yet much shorter than $\log x$.

## A small example

The consecutive primes $17,19,23,29,31$ have gaps $2,4,6,2$. Starting with $p_7=17$, the first three gaps are pairwise distinct, but the fourth repeats $2$. Thus $h(10)\ge3$. In fact, checking the nine allowed starting indices $1\le n<10$ gives maximal initial distinct-run lengths

$$
2,1,2,2,2,2,3,3,2,
$$

so $h(10)=3$. This finite calculation illustrates the definition. It cannot establish an asymptotic bound, since a claim about all sufficiently large $x$ is not decided by checking a particular $x$.

## What is known

Erdős observed that Brun's sieve implies $h(x)\to\infty$: arbitrarily long finite blocks with distinct gaps must occur. This growth statement alone does not specify a rate.

A [manuscript by Przemek Chojecki dated 24 April 2026](https://www.ulam.ai/research/erdos852.pdf), entitled *A Power Lower Bound for Runs of Distinct Prime Gaps*, states in Theorem 1 that, for some constant $c_0>0$ and every sufficiently large $x$,

$$
h(x)\ge c_0(\log x)^{1/3}.
$$

If the submitted proof is valid, this answers the first of Erdős's particular questions affirmatively: every fixed exponent $0<c<1/3$ then works eventually. The manuscript counts blocks of prime gaps of moderate total length and argues that blocks with repeated gap sizes are sufficiently rare. Repeated equal gaps give configurations of three, four or five primes that are controlled using Selberg's upper-bound sieve and average singular-series estimates. Those external analytic ingredients are not proved in this Compendium article. GCL has **not independently certified** the full manuscript proof, and the maintained problem still lists the overall question as open.

There is also an instructive elementary upper bound. In any run of $H$ distinct prime gaps, at most one gap can equal $1$; every other gap is an even positive integer. Consequently,

$$
p_{n+H}-p_n=\sum_{j=0}^{H-1}d_{n+j}\ge 1+2+4+\cdots+2(H-1)=H^2-H+1.
$$

The standard estimate $p_m=O(m\log m)$ gives $H^2=O((x+H)\log(x+H))$. For sufficiently large $x$, this first implies $H<x$, and then

$$
h(x)=O\!\left(\sqrt{x\log x}\right).
$$

This is an elementary consequence of known prime estimates, not a newly certified theorem. It is much too weak to establish $h(x)=o(\log x)$: a bound proportional to $\sqrt{x\log x}$ is enormously larger than $\log x$.

## GCL's current state

The [authenticated source audit for Erdős #852](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2123#issuecomment-6142008756) recovered the original statement from Erdős's 1985 paper, identified the post-registry lower-bound manuscript, and independently checked the small prime-gap example. Its recorded disposition is `PARTIAL`, because the published sieve inputs and the manuscript's handling of degenerate prime patterns have not received a complete independent mathematical review. The registry's frozen `open` label and the lack of a protected GCL formal statement must not be mistaken for an updated proof judgment.

This explanatory draft is not a MathCert submission. Neither the finite calculation nor the elementary upper bound resolves Erdős's logarithmic upper-bound question.

## What remains

First, a bounded source-fidelity check must verify the manuscript's analytic counting step, including what happens when the five proposed prime positions overlap. The audit should check the hypotheses of the Selberg-sieve and average singular-series results and show that the constants are uniform over the parameter ranges used. A missing hypothesis or counterexample would invalidate an asserted bound; a successful check would support attribution of the reported lower bound but would not make it a GCL discovery.

Second, the central unresolved question is the growth of the *largest* distinct-gap run relative to $\log x$. Proving an upper bound of smaller order, or producing counterexamples to that upper bound, would require information that the elementary sum-of-gaps argument does not supply.
