## The question in plain language

Let $F(N)$ be the largest possible cardinality of a Sidon subset of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$: every sum $a+b$ with $a\le b$ drawn from the set must have a unique unordered representation. Erdős asked whether, **for every fixed integer $k\ge1$**, there is a threshold after which enlarging the permitted interval by $k$ increases its maximum Sidon-set size by at most one:

$$
F(N+k)\le F(N)+1
\qquad\text{for all sufficiently large }N.
$$

The threshold is allowed to depend on $k$; one cannot reverse the order of these quantifiers. The suggested strengthening allows $k$ to grow like a small multiple of $\sqrt N$.

## A small example

For a Sidon set in $[1,N+k]$, deleting all elements larger than $N$ leaves a Sidon subset of $[1,N]$. Hence the elementary universal inequality is only

$$
F(N+k)\le F(N)+k.
$$

For $k=1$ the desired inequality already follows from this argument. The challenge is that it predicts the **same increase of one** for *each larger fixed $k$*, no matter how many new positions become available.

A Sidon set of three elements such as $\{1,2,4\}$ has six distinct unordered sums $2,3,4,5,6,8$. This is the correct convention: counting only sums of different elements would change the extremal function.

## What is known

The general fixed-window conjecture remains **OPEN**. Classical Erdős–Turán estimates and later finite-field constructions give the leading order $F(N)\sim\sqrt N$, but this asymptotic estimate does **not** control bounded changes in $F$. An error of order $N^{1/4}$ in a global extremal estimate is much larger than one. The maintained [Erdős #155](https://www.erdosproblems.com/155) identifies the proposed strengthening for $k\approx\varepsilon\sqrt N$ separately from the fixed-window question.

The original question appears in Erdős's [Er92c], [Er94b] and the Erdős–Sárközy–Sós [ESS94] collection. No verified source currently supplies an eventual eventual proof for any fixed $k\ge2$.

## GCL's current state

The pinned and maintained catalogue label #155 **OPEN**, and its original issue is [#1424](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1424). The elementary one-step monotonicity argument is self-contained; the general conjecture and the growing-window variant are not certified.

The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1424#issuecomment-6167533292). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded task exactly enumerates $F(N)$ for $N\le24$ by searching Sidon subsets and testing all unordered sums, then checks the inequality for $k=1,2,3$ on every finite pair within this range. Acceptance: produce maximizing witnesses and independent pair-sum verification, and explicitly distinguish a finite violation from asymptotic falsification. Stop without extrapolating finite cases into an eventual theorem.
