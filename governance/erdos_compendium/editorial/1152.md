## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1152](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1152). This asks whether interpolation with a degree allowance that shrinks to zero must fail almost everywhere for some continuous target function, no matter how the nodes were chosen.

$$p_n(x_{kn})=f(x_{kn}),\quad\deg p_n<(1+\varepsilon(n))n,\quad\varepsilon(n)\to0.$$

For every array of n distinct interpolation nodes x_{1n},…,x_{nn} in [-1,1] and epsilon(n)→0, ask whether there exists continuous f such that EVERY polynomial sequence p_n satisfying interpolation p_n(x_{kn})=f(x_{kn}) and degree <(1+epsilon(n))n fails to converge to f almost everywhere. Quantifier order and whether epsilon(n)>0 matters; if overhead negative and interpolation is impossible the claim could become vacuous. Nodes need NOT form a nested sequence.

## A small example

For n=3 nodes -1,0,1 and f(x)=x^3, the unique degree<3 interpolant is p(x)=x. At x=1/2 the discrepancy is exactly 3/8. One finite interpolant with a discrepancy proves nothing about all admissible sequences or convergence a.e.

## What is known

https://www.erdosproblems.com/1152 OPEN, Vardi [Va99,2.42]. Erdős, Kroó and Szabados [EKS89] proved contrasting result: for FIXED epsilon>0 they could choose favorable node arrays such that every continuous f has some interpolating p_n with degree <(1+epsilon)n converging UNIFORMLY. This existence at fixed overhead is NOT a disproof of the universal near-zero-overhead question. Primary proof not independently replayed.
