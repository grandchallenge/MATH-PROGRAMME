## The question in plain language

Select a set $A$ of positive integers at most $n$. Whenever **four members, allowing repetitions**, satisfy

$$
a\le b\le c\le d,\qquad abcd\text{ is a perfect square},
$$

require also $ad=bc$. [Erdős Problem #888](https://www.erdosproblems.com/888), attributed to Erdős, Sárközy and Sós, asks how large an admissible set can be. Let $F(n)$ denote its maximal possible cardinality.

The maintained catalogue describes the asymptotic **order of magnitude** as solved:

$$
F(n)\asymp \frac{n\log\log n}{\log n}.
$$

The lower construction is explicit, while the matching 2026 upper-bound argument was reported publicly and still requires an independent GCL mathematical source review. This is a different claim from any proposed exact extremal formula for every sufficiently large finite $n$.

## A small example

The set $\{1,6,10,15\}$ fails: the four values multiply to $900=30^2$, but the outer product $1\cdot15=15$ differs from the inner product $6\cdot10=60$. Conversely $\{2,3,4,6\}$ is admissible, even though the product of its four elements is $144=12^2$, because $2\cdot6=3\cdot4$.

A useful lower construction takes all primes at most $n$ and all **squarefree semiprimes**, products of two *distinct* primes, at most $n$. Its elements have one or two distinct prime factors. The exact finite checker evaluates the original condition, including repeated members, for this construction at $n=10,15,30,60,100,120$, giving respectively $6,10,17,34,55,65$ elements.

The same verifier computes exact maximal admissible cardinalities through $n=17$:

$$
1,2,3,3,4,5,6,6,6,7,8,8,9,10,10,10,11.
$$

These finite maxima do not establish a universal exact formula or an asymptotic upper theorem.

## What is known

The [maintained problem #888](https://www.erdosproblems.com/888), last updated May 28, 2026, cites Erdős's Eger 1998 chapter (printed p.178), with a reported result of Sárközy that $F(n)=o(n)$. Terence Tao subsequently gave a short proof in the public discussion. The primes supply $F(n)\gg n/\log n$.

Stijn Cambie and Desmond Weisenberg identified the stronger lower construction using squarefree semiprimes, of asymptotic size

$$
(1+o(1))\frac{n\log\log n}{\log n}.
$$

Przemek Chojecki [reported a GPT-5.5 Pro upper-bound argument](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/888?order=newest) in May 2026, based on decomposing a squarefree element as $cpq$, with $p<q$ its two largest prime factors. The remaining factor $c$ colors edges in a bipartite graph on prime factors; the original four-term condition rules out certain simultaneously colored four-cycles. Prime-distribution summations are then reported to give the matching upper order. The exact range decompositions, combinatorial lemma and analytic errors have not yet been independently checked by GCL.

The reported Aristotle formalization remained **incomplete**, including analytic prerequisites such as Mertens' theorem. The public [Formal Conjectures #888 file](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/main/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/888.lean) still contains a theorem placeholder rather than an admitted kernel proof. An independent [online candidate](https://github.com/neelsomani/gpt-erdos/blob/main/data/solutions/888/candidate_solution.md) proposing an exact formula for all $n\ge15$ is **not** supported by the finite checks or the reported order-of-magnitude theorem and is not admitted as proved.

## GCL's current state

There is an elementary **squarefree-kernel uniqueness lemma**. Every positive integer can be uniquely written as $x=\kappa(x)u^2$ with $\kappa(x)$ squarefree. Suppose different members $x<y$ of an admissible $A$ have the same kernel. Then $xy$ is a square; the product $x^3y=x^2(xy)$ is also a square. But the repeated quadruple $(x,x,x,y)$ would require

$$
xy=x^2,
$$

which contradicts $x<y$. Therefore an admissible set contains **at most one integer from each squarefree-kernel class**. The repetition convention is indispensable to this proof.

The shared exact verifier at governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_887_888.py enumerates all forbidden multisets of four members with repetition, optimizes an exact forbidden-hyperedge formulation for $1\le n\le17$, and directly checks returned admissible examples against the source predicate. It tests every construction quadruple through $n=120$ and all repeated-kernel pairs with entries up to $49$.

The finite output is replayed byte-for-byte against its stored JSON. The independent special-case theorem is elementary; the global matching upper asymptotic is an **imported public mathematical claim**, not reconstructed by the finite calculation.

## What remains

The immediate formalizable obligation is the kernel-uniqueness lemma with repeated entries. Acceptance requires a complete proof or a pinned kernel-compiled Lean development with no placeholders and a literal axiom report. Reproduce all exact finite maxima through $17$ and every tested lower-construction witness through $120$. An omitted repeated-input configuration, a missed square or an incorrect witness falsifies the check.

For the substantial upper-bound theorem, obtain the precise version of Chojecki's complete argument and verify the $cpq$ factorization ranges, the colored four-cycle lemma, the weighted edge count, and every application of prime-distribution estimates, particularly Mertens' theorem and any needed uniform error terms. Accept the full asymptotic claim as independently certified **only after** source-grounded mathematical review or a source-matched, no-placeholder formal replay. An exact formula conjectured from finite data remains a separate assertion.
