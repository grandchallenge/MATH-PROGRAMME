## The question in plain language

For $m\ge2$, let $p(m)$ be its smallest prime factor. Erdős and Graham ask whether some fixed $C>0$ ensures

$$
\sum_{x\le m\le x+C\sqrt{x}(\log x)^2}\frac{p(m)}m\gg1
$$

for **every** sufficiently large $x$, with a lower-bound constant independent of $x$.

There is a primary-source ambiguity. The original 1980 **global** reference sum explicitly excludes primes, but the **short-interval** question says to sum over *all integers*. The modern tracker retains that wording. A composite-only version is stronger: proving it would imply the literal prime-inclusive statement, but the two questions must not be silently conflated.

## A small example

A prime $q$ contributes $p(q)/q=1$. Thus a prime in every interval of the prescribed length would suffice for the literal question, but the required prime-gap theorem is not established by the sources inspected. A semiprime $m=pq$ with primes $p\le q$ contributes exactly $1/q$. For example, $143=11\cdot13$ contributes $1/13$. A balanced semiprime near $x$ contributes roughly $1/\sqrt{x}$, so a constant total needs on the order of $\sqrt{x}$ such contributions, not just one.

Finite checks verify the semiprime identity for every semiprime below $6000$, confirm that prime terms change the literal sum, and compare composite-only, semiprime-only and prime-inclusive sums on three small intervals.

## What is known

The 1980 source states a global composite-only asymptotic

$$
A(X)=\sum_{\substack{m<X\\m\ {\rm composite}}}\frac{p(m)}m
\sim c\frac{\sqrt X}{(\log X)^2}.
$$

The August 2026 Xu Zhang preprint computes $c=8$ using the prime number theorem. The main contribution is from semiprimes. If $m$ has at least three prime factors, with multiplicity, then $p(m)\le m^{1/3}$ and its total contribution is at most $O(X^{1/3})$, negligible on this scale. For $m=pq$, $p\le q$, the exact semiprime decomposition, up to endpoints, is

$$
A_2(X)=
\sum_{\substack{q\le\sqrt X\\q\ {\rm prime}}}\frac{\pi(q)}q+
\sum_{\substack{\sqrt X<q\le X/2\\q\ {\rm prime}}}
\frac{\pi(X/q)}q.
$$

Prime-number-theorem summation gives $4\sqrt X/(\log X)^2$ from each range. This is **global information**, not a lower bound for every short interval.

It does imply arbitrarily large **good starting points**. Put $H_X=C\sqrt X(\log X)^2$ and let $W(t)$ be the composite-only sum over $(t,t+H_X]$. Each composite $m\in[X+H_X,2X]$ is counted for $H_X$ starting points, so

$$
\frac1X\int_X^{2X}W(t)\,dt
\ge\frac{H_X}{X}\bigl[A(2X)-A(X+H_X)\bigr]
=8C(\sqrt2-1)+o(1).
$$

At least one $t\in[X,2X]$ has a positive sum, and $H_X\le C\sqrt t(\log t)^2$. This establishes infinitely many good $t$, **not all $t$**.

The August 2026 preprint additionally claims that the composite-only window sum has mean $4C$ and variance $O_C((\log X)^{-2})$, hence an almost-all result. But on printed page 23 its cross-term estimate includes

$$
S_A\ll \frac{L^2}{X^2}\frac{X}{(\log X)^2},
\qquad L=C\sqrt X(\log X)^2,
$$

which it then evaluates as proportional to $C^2(\log X)^2/X$ and treats as vanishing. **Direct substitution gives instead**

$$
\frac{L^2}{X^2}\frac{X}{(\log X)^2}
=C^2(\log X)^2.
$$

This displayed upper bound **does not tend to zero**. Thus that step cannot establish the asserted variance estimate. The variance theorem might still be true; a different or repaired covariance argument is needed. This is a source-critical proof-gap finding, not a disproof.
