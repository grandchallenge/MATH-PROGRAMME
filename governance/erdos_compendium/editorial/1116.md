## The question in plain language

For a function $f(z)$, count the solutions of $f(z)=a$ inside a disk of radius $r$. Different target values $a$ may be attained very different numbers of times.

[Erdős Problem #1116](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1116) asks whether there is a **single meromorphic, or even entire, function** for which the root counts of *every two distinct target values* fluctuate so drastically that

$$
\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{n(r,a)}{n(r,b)}=\infty
\qquad(a\ne b).
$$

Here $n(r,a)$ counts roots of $f(z)=a$ in $|z|<r$. The multiplicity convention and treatment of early radii with a zero denominator require primary-source normalization.

## A small example

For the ordinary polynomial $f(z)=z^2$, the equations $f(z)=1$ and $f(z)=4$ each have two distinct roots. Once $r>2$,

$$
n(r,1)=n(r,4)=2,
\qquad \frac{n(r,1)}{n(r,4)}=1.
$$

A polynomial therefore gives no evidence for the extraordinary simultaneous oscillations Erdős requested. The example fixes how disk root counting works without implying any result about the desired entire-function construction.

## What is known

The problem is **solved in the affirmative**. The maintained Erdős catalogue credits **Toppila (1976)** and **Gol'dberg (1978)** with constructing *entire* functions having the requested simultaneous property.

Because the statement says **for every pair** of target values, merely showing unusually many zeros for one particular value is insufficient. Entire-function constructions supporting such comprehensive value-distribution irregularity are fundamentally different from a polynomial's eventually constant root counts.
