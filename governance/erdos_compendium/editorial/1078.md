## The question in plain language

Partition a graph's vertices into $r$ disjoint classes, with exactly $n$ vertices in each. Edges may join different classes but not vertices in the same class.

How large must the **minimum vertex degree** be to ensure that the graph contains a complete graph $K_r$, with one vertex chosen from each class?

[Erdős Problem #1078](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1078) asked whether a minimum degree of about

$$
\left(r-\frac32-o(1)\right)n
$$

would suffice. The maximum possible degree is $(r-1)n$, since a vertex can join every vertex outside its own class.

## A small example

Take $r=3$ parts containing two vertices each. The complete tripartite graph $K_{2,2,2}$ connects every vertex to all four vertices outside its class. Its minimum degree is $4$ and it contains

$$
2\cdot2\cdot2=8
$$

triangles, one from each choice of three vertices, one per part.

Now remove every edge between the second and third classes, retaining edges from the first class to both others. No triangle remains, since a potential triangle would require an edge between the second and third classes. Vertices in those classes now have degree $2$; those in the first class still have degree $4$. This simple comparison illustrates why a **global minimum-degree** condition, rather than merely a large total number of edges, is relevant.

## What is known

**The conjectured sufficient threshold is proved.** The maintained catalogue attributes the affirmative result to **Haxell (2001)**. A later paper by **Haxell and Szabó (2006)** determined a sharper threshold, expressed in the catalogue with an integer ceiling:

$$
(r-1)n-\left\lceil\frac{s n}{2s-1}\right\rceil,
\qquad s=\lfloor r/2\rfloor.
$$

The equality/strictness convention and exact small-$r$ hypotheses of this sharper expression require checking against the original theorem rather than inferring a finite-n statement from the catalogue synopsis.

## What remains

The remaining editorial task is to read the 1975 conjecture and the 2001 and 2006 proofs page by page, verifying the range of $r$, the rounding, equality versus strict inequality and explicit extremal constructions. Small multipartite cases can be checked with exhaustive graph enumeration, but they do not certify the entire theorem.
