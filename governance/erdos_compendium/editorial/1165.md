## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1165](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1165). At time n, which sites has a planar random walk visited the most often, and how many ties persist infinitely often?

$$F(n)=\{x\in\mathbb Z^2:f_n(x)=\max_y f_n(y)\},\quad\mathbb P(|F(n)|=r\ \mathrm{i.o.}).$$

For planar random walk S_k, local time f_n(x)=number of k in [0,n] such that S_k=x and FAVORITE SET F(n)={x:f_n(x)=max_y f_n(y)}. For integer r≥3 ask P(|F(n)|=r infinitely often). Cardinality of current tie set, not the union over past times. A self-avoiding 7-site walk at time6 can have seven simultaneous favorites: consistent with an EVENTUAL almost-sure ≤3 result.

## A small example

Exhaustively 4^6=4096 walks of length6 from origin: histogram |F(6)|=1 for2036 paths, 2 for1124, 3 for156, 7 for780. The 7-favorite paths are self-avoiding. These finite laws do not establish an event infinitely often.

## What is known

https://www.erdosproblems.com/1165 SOLVED, [Va99,6.77]. Tóth [To01] proves P(|F(n)|=r infinitely often)=0 for r≥4; Hao–Li–Okada–Zheng [HLOZ24] 2024 proves probability 1 for r=3. Taking countable union over all integer r≥4 yields eventually no ≥4 favorites a.s. Original proofs pending review.
