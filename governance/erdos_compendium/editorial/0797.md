## The question in plain language

The acyclic chromatic number of a graph is least colors in a proper vertex coloring with no bichromatic cycle. f(d) is maximum of this number among all graphs of maximum degree at most d. Is f(d)=o(d^2)?

The key is to preserve the order of the quantifiers and the exact distinction between a finite example and a theorem about arbitrary large inputs. Replacing an extremum over every admissible set by an extremum over one convenient set would change the problem.

## A small example

Cycle C4 has proper 2-coloring but it is bichromatic, hence is NOT acyclic with two colors. A proper three-coloring 1,2,3,2 around its 4 vertices is acyclic because all cycles use three colors. Thus acyclic chromatic number C4=3 while ordinary chromatic number=2.

The calculation illustrates the exact definitions and hypotheses. No single finite example establishes the reported asymptotic assertion.

## What is known

Maintained /latex/797 cites N. Alon, C. McDiarmid, B. Reed, Acyclic coloring of graphs, Random Structures & Algorithms (1991), 277–288, whose reported bounds are d^(4/3)/(log d)^(1/3) << f(d) << d^(4/3); hence o(d²).
