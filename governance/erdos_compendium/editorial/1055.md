## The question in plain language

Take any prime $p$ and factor $p+1$ into primes. Erdős and Selfridge proposed a recursive way of assigning $p$ to a class. It lies in **class 1** if all prime factors of $p+1$ are $2$ or $3$. Otherwise, its class is one greater than the largest class of any prime factor of $p+1$.

[Erdős Problem #1055](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1055) asks whether there are infinitely many primes in **every fixed class**, not merely whether the first few classes are populated. It also asks how quickly the least prime $p_r$ of class $r$ grows. Erdős suspected that

$$
p_r^{1/r}\longrightarrow\infty.
$$

Selfridge suspected instead that this root might remain bounded. These are competing conjectural expectations.

## A small example

Since $7+1=2^3$, the prime $7$ has class 1. Since $13+1=2\cdot7$, the prime $13$ has class 2. Next, $19+1=2^2\cdot5$; since $5+1=2\cdot3$, the prime $5$ has class 1, hence $19$ has class 2. Therefore

$$
37+1=2\cdot19
$$

puts $37$ in class 3, and $73+1=2\cdot37$ puts $73$ in class 4. Finally, $1021+1=2\cdot7\cdot73$ puts $1021$ in class 5. The primality and factorizations in this chain were independently checked.

A separate exact computation now enumerates **all 303 primes up to 2,000** using two independent class-rank algorithms. They agree on every tested prime and establish that $2,13,37,73,1021$ really are the smallest primes in classes 1 through 5. This is a finite classification, not proof that every higher class is populated.

## What is known

The [canonical catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1055) records the problem as **open** and lists the initial least representatives $2,13,37,73,1021$. It also reports that the number of class-$r$ primes up to $x$ is at most $x^{o(1)}$ for each fixed $r$. Such a sparsity statement is compatible with both finite and infinite classes: it does not resolve the infinitude question.

The historical sources include Erdős's earlier discussion and Guy, *Unsolved Problems in Number Theory*, problem A18. Their exact printed statements and page-level bibliography have not been independently checked in this review.

## GCL's current state

[Source audit #2326](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2326#issuecomment-6144495356) verifies a chain of finite prime-class assignments and inspects the [frozen Lean source](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1055.lean).

The Lean file describes the recursive class definition and the infinitude and asymptotic conjectures. Crucially, its declaration asserting a prime exists in every class has an unproved placeholder; subsequent definitions of least representatives depend on it. Neither the file's existence nor its metadata independently establishes these conjectures.

## What remains

The historical question requires either a proof that each class contains infinitely many primes or a counterexample, plus a determination of how the least representatives grow. As a bounded source and formalization audit, GCL can first compare the exact historic recursion with the frozen Lean definition, retain the independently reproduced finite-rank checks and verify the historic source statements. Finite computations alone cannot establish the asymptotic results.
