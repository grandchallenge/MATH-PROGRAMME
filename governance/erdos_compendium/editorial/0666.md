## The question in plain language

The graph of the $n$ dimensional cube, called $Q_n$, has the $2^n$ binary strings as vertices. Two strings are joined by an edge if they differ in precisely one coordinate. Thus the cube has $n2^{n-1}$ edges.

[Erdős Problem #666](https://www.erdosproblems.com/666) asked whether **every** subgraph retaining any fixed positive proportion of those edges must contain a cycle of length six, $C_6$, when the dimension becomes large. The answer is **NO**, because the edges can be divided into just **four** subgraphs, each containing no six-cycle.

This is a classical published counterexample by Fan Chung and by Brouwer, Dejter and Thomassen, not a new GCL theorem.

## A small example

For $n=3$, the vertices are binary strings of length three. Start at $000$ and flip coordinates in the order $1,2,3,1,2,3$. The successive vertices are

`000 → 100 → 110 → 111 → 011 → 001 → 000`.

These six distinct intermediate vertices form a genuine $C_6$ inside the full cube. If one colors each edge according to the coordinate that is flipped, then all edges of any one of the three colors form a matching: no colored class contains a cycle. This finite example helps explain edge coloring, but it uses $n$ colors in general and therefore does **not** prove the four-color theorem.

## What is known

The 1992 paper of [Fan R. K. Chung](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.3190160311), *Subgraphs of a hypercube containing no small even cycles*, *Journal of Graph Theory* **16**, 273–286, explicitly states in its publisher abstract that the cube can always be **edge-partitioned into four subgraphs each having no six-cycle**. A related construction and four-color assertion also appears in [Brouwer, Dejter and Thomassen](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1022472513494), *Highly symmetric subgraphs of hypercubes*, *Journal of Algebraic Combinatorics* **2** (1993), 25–29.

Why is four a decisive number? If the four edge-disjoint color classes are $E_1,E_2,E_3,E_4$, then

$$

\sum_{j=1}^4|E_j|=|E(Q_n)|=n2^{n-1}.

$$

At least one class has at least one quarter of the edges. Each class is six-cycle-free by the published theorem, so one of them is a $C_6$ free subgraph with

$$

|E_j|\geq\frac14n2^{n-1}.

$$

Choose $\varepsilon=1/4$. Such a subgraph exists for every dimension, contradicting the assertion that **every** sufficiently large cube subgraph with a fixed positive edge fraction must contain $C_6$.

The same conclusion holds for smaller positive density parameters. It does not settle all proposed questions about $C_{2k}$ for $k>3$; particularly the eight-cycle statements require their own analysis.
