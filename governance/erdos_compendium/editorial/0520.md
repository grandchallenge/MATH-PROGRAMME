Erdős Problem #520 asks whether partial sums of a squarefree-supported random multiplicative function obey a classical law of the iterated logarithm with a **positive constant**. The official maintained catalogue still labels it **OPEN**, but there is substantial **2026 evidence for the answer NO**: Durkan and Pearce-Crump's July preprint proves an almost-sure upper bound implying a limit superior of zero, and a separate Lean development by Høystad is reported to have been compiled and independently audited. ## The question in plain language

At every prime number $p$, independently toss a fair coin and assign a random sign

$$
f(p)\in\{-1,+1\}.
$$

For a **squarefree** number $n=p_1p_2\cdots p_r$, define

$$
f(n)=f(p_1)\cdots f(p_r),
$$

and define $f(1)=1$. When a prime factor appears twice, set $f(n)=0$. Thus $f(4)=f(9)=f(12)=0$. This is the **Rademacher random multiplicative function**, supported only on squarefree integers.

Study the partial sums

$$
M_f(N)=\sum_{1\le n\le N}f(n).
$$

They fluctuate as $N$ increases. The question is whether there exists **one deterministic constant** $c>0$, independent of the random outcome, for which

$$
\boxed{
\limsup_{N\to\infty}
\frac{M_f(N)}{\sqrt{N\log\log N}}=c
\quad\text{almost surely}.
}
$$

*Almost surely* means the property holds for probability one in the infinite product space of the independent prime signs. The question's limit superior is **signed**, not the limit superior of an absolute value.

The model must not be confused with a random **completely multiplicative** sign function. That model sets $f(p^2)=f(p)^2=1$, while here $f(p^2)=0$. It is associated with another Erdős problem, #1144.

## A small example

Let the first three independent prime signs be

$$
f(2)=a,\qquad f(3)=b,\qquad f(5)=c,
\qquad a,b,c\in\{-1,+1\}.
$$

By multiplicativity and squarefree support,

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(1)&=1,&f(2)&=a,&f(3)&=b,\\
f(4)&=0,&f(5)&=c,&f(6)&=ab.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus the sum through six is

$$
M_f(6)=1+a+b+c+ab.
$$

There are only eight possible assignments of the prime signs here, each equally likely. The values at $2$ and $6$ are **not independent** because the same sign $a$ appears in both.

This dependence is the major reason that simply invoking the classical independent-sign law is invalid.

There is nevertheless a useful exact second-moment identity. For squarefree integers $m,n$, independent mean-zero prime signs imply

$$
\mathbb E[f(m)f(n)]
=
\begin{cases}
1,&m=n,\\
0,&m\ne n.
\end{cases}
$$

If either argument is non-squarefree, its value is zero. Consequently, writing $Q(N)$ for the count of squarefree positive integers up to $N$,

$$
\boxed{
\mathbb E M_f(N)=1,\qquad
\mathbb E|M_f(N)|^2=Q(N),\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(M_f(N))=Q(N)-1.
}
$$

The mean is one because $f(1)=1$ and every $f(n)$ with $n>1$ has mean zero. The squarefree count grows proportionally to $N$, so the second moment suggests a scale of order $\sqrt N$. It does **not** establish the almost-sure maximum growth, the sign of extreme fluctuations, or a law of the iterated logarithm.

## What is known

### Erdős's original 1961 formulation

Erdős introduced the multiplicative model in *Some unsolved problems* (1961), printed [p.251](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1961-22.pdf#page=31) and [p.252](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1961-22.pdf#page=32), Section V.1. Both original pages were visually inspected.

He recalled the classical law of the iterated logarithm for independent Rademacher signs:

$$
\limsup_{N\to\infty}
\frac{\sum_{n\le N}\varepsilon_n}
{\sqrt{2N\log\log N}}=1
\quad\text{almost surely}.
$$

He then considered **independent prime signs extended multiplicatively**, asking for an analogous theorem. In 1961 he could not even prove that the multiplicative partial sums have unbounded positive limit superior after normalization by $\sqrt N$. He cited Wintner's earlier $N^{1/2+o(1)}$ bound and an unpublished logarithmic improvement.

The maintained [Erdős #520 page](https://www.erdosproblems.com/520) formulates the sharper question with an almost-sure positive constant and denominator $\sqrt{N\log\log N}$. This is a reasonable precise descendant of the original inquiry, but not literally the equation Erdős printed.

Caich, *Almost sure upper bound for random multiplicative functions*, [arXiv:2304.00943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00943), proved

$$
|M_f(N)|\ll_{\omega,\varepsilon}
\sqrt N\,(\log\log N)^{3/4+\varepsilon}
$$

almost surely. Harper, *Almost sure large fluctuations of random multiplicative functions*, [arXiv:2012.15809](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.15809), proved that the absolute sums reach scale $\sqrt N(\log\log N)^{1/4-o(1)}$ infinitely often, almost surely. The maintained catalogue still says OPEN based on the older progress.

### The July 2026 negative-answer preprint

Benjamin Durkan and Andrew Pearce-Crump, *A sharp almost sure upper bound for partial sums of random multiplicative functions*, [arXiv:2607.29429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29429), dated July 31, 2026, state in **Theorem 1** that for either the squarefree-supported Rademacher model or the Steinhaus model, for every $\varepsilon>0$,

$$
\boxed{
|M_f(N)|\ll_{\omega,\varepsilon}
\sqrt N\,(\log\log N)^{1/4+\varepsilon}
\quad\text{almost surely}.
}
$$

Their original complete author-supplied arXiv HTML was read directly, including the exact Rademacher model, theorem, proof outline and explicit discussion of Erdős #520. They explicitly state that this result **negatively answers** the problem.

The deduction is elementary once this theorem is granted. Choose $\varepsilon=1/8$. Then, on a probability-one event and for all large $N$,

$$
\frac{|M_f(N)|}{\sqrt{N\log\log N}}
\ll_\omega
(\log\log N)^{1/4+1/8-1/2}
=(\log\log N)^{-1/8}\longrightarrow0.
$$

Therefore the signed ratio also tends to zero; its limit superior cannot be a positive constant.

The complete probabilistic proof uses prime-block decomposition, martingales, conditional high moments, maximal inequalities and exceptional-event estimates. It has **not** been independently reconstructed in GCL. The source inspected is a 2026 preprint, not a completed GCL mathematical certification.

The paper credits closely timed contributions by Samuel Korsky and Victor Reis, Høystad, and William Verreault during July–August 2026. These sources concern this squarefree-supported model, not the distinct completely multiplicative problem #1144.
