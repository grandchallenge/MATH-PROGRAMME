## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1168](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1168) is classified **OPEN** in the maintained catalogue.

For the **first uncountable successor above the singular limit** $\mu=\aleph_\omega$, let $\kappa=\aleph_{\omega+1}$. The problem asks for a proof **without the generalized continuum hypothesis** of the *negative* partition relation
$$
\kappa\not\to(\kappa,3,3,\ldots)_{\aleph_0}^{2}.
$$
This means constructing a countably colored complete graph on $\kappa$ vertices with no homogeneous subset of size $\kappa$ in the distinguished color and no monochromatic triangle in any other color. A coloring with one **unique color per pair** on a finite graph is not an infinite/countable-color disproof: $\kappa$ has far more than countably many pairs.

## A small example

For the seven-vertex complete graph, assign each of its 21 edges a distinct color. Every triangle has three differently colored edges, exactly checked by the phase-20 fixture. It illustrates negative monochromatic-triangle avoidance, but uses 21 colors and does not force the distinguished color to avoid a $\kappa$-set in an infinite graph. There is no finite analogue of the cardinal-arithmetic obstruction that would prove the theorem.

## What is known

Erdős–Hajnal–Rado [EHR65] proved the stated negative relation **under GCH**, as reported by Erdős–Hajnal [ErHa71,p.21], Vardi [Va99,7.80] and Komjáth [Ko25b,p.3]. The maintained catalogue marks the unrestricted-from-GCH assertion **OPEN**. A theorem under GCH cannot simply be read as a ZFC proof; the required strengthening is precisely removal of that extra axiom. The meaning of the $\aleph_0$-indexed list and the distinction between *negative* and *positive* arrow are essential.
