## The question in plain language

In **size Ramsey theory**, a graph $H$ is a *Ramsey host* for a graph $G$ when **every** red-blue coloring of the edges of $H$ contains a monochromatic copy of $G$. The *size Ramsey number* $\widehat R(G)$ is the minimum number of edges in such a host. It counts host **edges**, not host vertices.

Erdős asked whether a target graph that has many edges **per vertex** must become unusually expensive to force monochromatically. More precisely, if a graph on $n$ vertices $G$ has $e\ge Cn$ edges, is there a function $f$ such that

$$
\frac{f(C)}C\longrightarrow\infty
\quad\text{as }C\to\infty,
\qquad
\widehat R(G)>f(C)e
$$

for every sufficiently large $C$ and **all** such targets $G$?

This is [Erdős Problem #911](https://www.erdosproblems.com/911), which remains **open**. The desired factor grows *faster than linearly* in the density parameter $C$; proving a fixed factor or merely $f(C)=O(C)$ is insufficient.

## A small example

There is an elementary universal bound. If a target $G$ has $e$ edges, any host with at most $2e-2$ edges can be partitioned into a red set and a blue set, each containing at most $e-1$ edges. Neither color can possibly contain a copy of $G$. Therefore

$$
\widehat R(G)\ge2e-1.
$$

This applies regardless of the target's density. It is far weaker than #911's proposed factor $f(C)=\omega(C)$.

As a basic structural example, a complete bipartite graph $K_{d,d}$ has $2d$ vertices, $d^2$ edges and minimum degree $d$, with edge density $e/n=d/2$. For this family, modern bounds on complete-bipartite size Ramsey numbers grow exponentially in $d$, and are much stronger than the conjecture needs.

But **not every dense graph contains a large biclique**. The point-line incidence graph of the projective plane of order $q$ has

$$
2(q^2+q+1)\text{ vertices},\qquad
(q+1)(q^2+q+1)\text{ edges}.
$$

It has minimum degree $q+1$, yet has **no four-cycle**, hence no complete bipartite subgraph with at least two vertices in each part. Thus one cannot solve #911 merely by finding a large complete bipartite subgraph in every dense target.

## What is known

Erdős stated the problem on printed p.78 of [*Some of my favourite problems which recently have been solved*](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1982-33.pdf), *North-Holland Mathematics Studies* **74** (1982), pp.59–79. The paper's printed page calls it a late addition. The [maintained catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/911) still marks the question OPEN and records no accepted proof.

The invariant was introduced by Erdős, Faudree, Rousseau and Schelp, [*The size Ramsey number*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02018930), *Periodica Mathematica Hungarica* **9** (1978), pp.145–161. It minimizes the number of edges of the **host** under all two-colorings, rather than ordinary vertex Ramsey number.

Conlon, Fox and Wigderson, [*Three early problems on size Ramsey numbers*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.05420), *Combinatorica* **43** (2023), pp.743–768, prove strong lower bounds for complete bipartite targets. Their Theorem 1.1 gives, for $s\le t$,

$$
\widehat R(K_{s,t})=
\Omega\!\left(s^{2-s/t}\,t\,2^s\right).
$$

In the range $t=\Omega(s\log s)$ the order is $\Theta(s^2 t2^s)$. These results cover useful subclasses, but do **not** imply the required universal statement for arbitrary graph targets. Pikhurko's fixed-template asymptotic theory also does not provide the needed uniformity as both part sizes grow.

A detailed July 27, 2026 [independently labelled working report](https://erdosproblemaday.com/report/911) derives an equivalence with a minimum-degree bipartite formulation, supported by an exhaustive finite verifier. Its outcome label is **PARTIAL**, not PROVED. We reproduce the elementary reduction here and keep the still-missing Ramsey inequality explicit.

## GCL's current state

Define an auxiliary function

$$
\beta(d)=\inf_{\substack{F\text{ bipartite}\\\delta(F)\ge d}}
\frac{\widehat R(F)}{e(F)},
$$

where $F$ ranges over all finite simple bipartite graphs with minimum degree at least $d$. Then #911 is equivalent, up to harmless absolute constants, to

$$
\frac{\beta(d)}{d}\longrightarrow\infty.
$$

Here is the **forward direction**. A bipartite graph with minimum degree $d$ has $e(F)\ge d\,v(F)/2$. If the original conjecture holds, apply it to $F$ with $C=d/2$, giving $\beta(d)\ge f(d/2)$, hence $\beta(d)/d\to\infty$.

For the **reverse direction**, take a target $G$ with $n$ vertices and $e\ge Cn$. A uniformly random partition of its vertex set retains each edge as a crossing edge with probability one half. Therefore some spanning bipartite subgraph $B$ has at least $e/2$ edges. Delete from $B$ any vertex with current degree **strictly less than** $C/4$, until no such vertex remains. At most $n$ deletions remove strictly fewer than $Cn/4\le e/4$ edges, so the remaining graph $F$ satisfies

$$
e(F)>e/4,\qquad\delta(F)\ge C/4.
$$

It is nonempty. Since $F$ is a target subgraph of $G$, every host that arrows $G$ also arrows $F$, and therefore

$$
\widehat R(G)\ge\widehat R(F)
\ge\beta(\lceil C/4\rceil)e(F)
>\frac14\beta(\lceil C/4\rceil)e.
$$

If $\beta(d)/d\to\infty$, choosing $f(C)=\beta(\lceil C/4\rceil)/4$ gives the requested asymptotic. This reduction is elementary and **does not resolve** the missing uniform lower bound for $\beta$.

The executable exact fixture at governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_910_911.py independently examines all **33,861 nonempty labelled simple graphs** on two through six vertices. For each it enumerates maximum cuts, checks the bipartite half-edge bound, applies the degree-pruning algorithm and verifies the retained edge and minimum-degree guarantees. It also constructs projective-plane incidence graphs for prime orders $q=2,3,5,7,11$, checks regularity, exact edge counts and unique common neighbors of same-side pairs. These demonstrate the need for techniques beyond biclique extraction.

## What remains

The central **open obligation** is now precise: prove a uniform inequality

$$
\widehat R(F)\ge g(d)e(F)
$$

for **every** finite simple bipartite $F$ of minimum degree $d$, where $g(d)/d\to\infty$. This would solve Erdős #911. It must work even for regular, high-girth bipartite graphs with no large biclique; a proof only for complete bipartite graphs does not meet the quantifiers.

A bounded formal and computational task is to verify the cut-plus-pruning lemma, target monotonicity, two-pile bound and the projective-plane incidence specifications. Acceptance requires complete elementary proofs or pinned no-placeholder Lean theorems with actual axiom reports, independent replay of all 33,861 graph checks and the five finite projective planes. A counterexample violating retained edge count, minimum-degree guarantee or absence of four-cycles falsifies the corresponding certificate.

The substantive task is a new size-Ramsey coloring lower bound that depends **uniformly on minimum degree**, not just on large codegree intersections. The 2023 biclique results and the July 2026 reduction provide starting points, not a completed proof. A finite search over a few graphs cannot establish the required uniform asymptotic statement.
