## The question in plain language

For a finite simple graph, $\mathrm{ex}(n,C_4)$ is the maximum number of edges among graphs on $n$ vertices that contain no four-cycle. The following leading asymptotic is PROVED using projective-plane constructions and Reiman's upper bound:

$$
\mathrm{ex}(n,C_4)\sim\tfrac12 n^{3/2}.
$$

However Erdős's STRONGER proposed second-order formula

$$
\mathrm{ex}(n,C_4)=\tfrac12 n^{3/2}+\tfrac14 n+O(\sqrt n)
$$

is DISPROVED by Ma and Yang (2023). The maintained 'SOLVED (LEAN)' aggregate must not suggest both statements have affirmative proofs.

## A small example

The five-cycle $C_5$ has five vertices, five edges, and contains no four-cycle; hence $\mathrm{ex}(5,C_4)\ge5$. In ANY graph containing no $C_4$, two different vertices have at most one common neighbour, because two common neighbours would create a 4-cycle. Counting two-edge paths gives the bound

$$
\sum_v\binom{\deg(v)}{2}\le\binom{n}{2}.
$$

This is the elementary basis for the $O(n^{3/2})$ upper bound.

## What is known

Brown and independently Erdős–Rényi supplied finite projective-plane/polarity graph constructions with $n=q^2+q+1$ and at least $\tfrac12 q(q+1)^2$ edges, leading with Reiman's upper bound to

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{ex}(n,C_4)}{n^{3/2}}=\tfrac12.
$$

Füredi proved exact counts for these $n$ when $q>13$. Ma and Yang's [2023 peer-reviewed publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms.12810) explicitly confirms that their new extremal upper bound disproves Erdős's second-order conjecture with linear coefficient 1/4.
