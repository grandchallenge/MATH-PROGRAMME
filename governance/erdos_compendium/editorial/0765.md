## The question in plain language

For a finite simple graph, ex(n,C₄) is the maximum edges among n-vertex graphs without a four-cycle. The main asymptotic ex(n,C₄)~(1/2)n^(3/2) is PROVED using projective-plane constructions and Reiman's upper bound. However Erdős's STRONGER proposed second-order formula ex(n,C₄)=(1/2)n^(3/2)+(1/4)n+O(√n) is DISPROVED by Ma and Yang (2023). The maintained 'SOLVED (LEAN)' aggregate must not suggest both statements have affirmative proofs.

## A small example

The five-cycle C5 has five vertices, five edges, and contains no C4, so ex(5,C4)≥5. In ANY C4-free graph, two different vertices have at most one common neighbour, because two common neighbours would create a 4-cycle. Counting two-edge paths gives Σ_v binom(deg(v),2)≤binom(n,2), the elementary basis for the O(n^(3/2)) upper bound.

## What is known

Brown and independently Erdős–Rényi supplied finite projective-plane/polarity graph constructions with n=q²+q+1 and at least (1/2)q(q+1)² edges, leading to lim ex(n,C₄)/n^(3/2)=1/2 for all n with Reiman's upper bound. Füredi proved exact counts for these n when q>13. Ma and Yang's [2023 peer-reviewed publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms.12810) explicitly confirms that their new extremal upper bound disproves Erdős's second-order conjecture with linear coefficient 1/4. The maintained catalogue further reports a positive-density quantitative strengthening; that exact stronger formulation is attributed to the catalogue pending full-paper replay.
