## The question in plain language

For every A subset {1,...,n} of cardinality <=sqrt(n), find B subset of ALL integers such that A subset B+B with B much smaller than sqrt(n). The sumset B+B allows b1=b2 and B is not required to lie in [1,n].

There is a uniform asymptotic construction with B+B covering A and |B| at most a constant multiple of sqrt(n) loglog(n)/log(n). This tends to zero as a proportion of sqrt(n), proving the conjecture. The historical lower example shows that the order of this improvement is essentially tight.

## A small example

For A={2,4,6} subset [1,9] with |A|=3<=sqrt(9), B={1,3} has B+B={2,4,6} (1+1,1+3,3+3), so a smaller exact cover exists in this small example. Repetition is essential; requiring DISTINCT b1,b2 would yield only 4.

This finite fixture tests the conventions. It does not certify any of the asymptotic or all-graphs assertions.

## What is known

Maintained #806 PROVED: Alon, Bukh and Sudakov [ABS09] establish |B| = O(sqrt(n) loglog n/log n). Erdős–Newman [ErNe77] supply some sets requiring Omega(sqrt(n) loglog n/log n). Thus the uniform order is optimal up to constants. Primary ABS09 theorem full text and original 1977 lower-bound proof need independent verification. A bound O(sqrt n loglog n/log n)=o(sqrt n).

The distinctions between established partial theorems, original open parts, and disproved universal claims have been preserved. Historical publications are attributed, rather than presented as new GCL mathematics.

The cited sharp order of the auxiliary basis size is

$$
|B|\ll\sqrt n\,\frac{\log\log n}{\log n},
\qquad A\subseteq B+B.
$$

Because $\log\log n/\log n$ tends to zero, this is the desired little-o statement. Matching lower examples are separately attributed to Erdős and Newman.
