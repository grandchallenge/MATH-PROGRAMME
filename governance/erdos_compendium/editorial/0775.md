## The question in plain language

Can a 3-uniform hypergraph on $n$ vertices have **maximal** cliques of almost every possible size, missing only a fixed number of sizes? Here each edge contains three vertices. A vertex set is complete when every triple of its vertices is an edge, and a maximal clique is a complete set that cannot be enlarged while remaining complete. Counting all complete subsets instead of maximal ones would make this a different problem.

Erdős asked for $n-O(1)$ distinct maximal-clique sizes. The conjectured existence is **DISPROVED** by Jun Gao. An external Lean proof has been reported, but GCL has not replayed that certificate.

## A small example

Consider the complete 3-uniform hypergraph on the vertex set $\{1,2,3,4\}$: all four triples are edges. Every subset is complete, but only the full four-vertex set is inclusion-maximal. Consequently there is **one maximal-clique size**, namely four, rather than four different sizes.

In the empty 3-uniform hypergraph on four vertices, each pair is vacuously complete, and adding any third vertex makes an incomplete triple. Thus every maximal clique has size two. There are many maximal cliques, but still only **one size**. The distinction between the *number of cliques* and the *number of distinct sizes* is essential.

## What is known

Jun Gao, [*On cliques in hypergraphs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14804), arXiv:2510.14804v3 (2025), Theorem 1.1 on p. 1, proves a stronger statement for every fixed uniformity $k\geq3$: for every fixed integer $C\geq0$, all sufficiently large $k$ uniform hypergraphs on $n$ vertices have at most $n-C$ different sizes of maximal cliques. Crucially, the lower threshold on $n$ is allowed to depend on **both** $k$ and $C$. This says the number of missing sizes grows without bound as $n$ grows.

Equivalently, for fixed $k\geq3$ there is a function $f_k(n)$ tending to infinity such that the number of distinct maximal clique sizes is at most

$$

n-f_k(n).

$$

The theorem does not itself license a particular numerical rate for $f_k(n)$. Erdős's earlier construction obtained at least $n-\log_*n$ different sizes, where $\log_*n$ counts successive applications of the \logarithm needed to reach a bounded value. That lower construction is compatible with Gao's qualitative obstruction. Gao's later journal article appeared in *Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B* **180** (2026), 84–93, DOI 10.1016/j.jctb.2026.07.001.

The contrast with ordinary graphs matters: for graphs, Moon–Moser and Spencer studied the related maximal-clique spectrum and its \logarithmic deficit. Gao's theorem specifically treats $k\geq3$.
