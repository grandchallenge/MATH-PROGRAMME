## The question in plain language

In infinite Ramsey theory, one asks whether colouring every edge of a complete graph red or blue must produce a large monochromatic structure. Erdős and Hajnal considered an ordinal or cardinal $\alpha$ for which every red-blue colouring of the pairs of $\alpha$ contains either a red complete graph of order type $\alpha$ or a **blue triangle**.

In arrow notation this is

$$
\alpha\longrightarrow(\alpha,3)^2.
$$

They conjectured that this property would imply $\alpha\to(\alpha,m)^2$ for **every finite** $m\ge3$: a red copy of order type $\alpha$ or a blue clique of size $m$ would always appear.

## A small example

The blue-triangle condition forbids one tiny monochromatic pattern. A graph may have no blue triangle yet contain a blue four-cycle; the presence of such a cycle is not the same as a blue $K_4$. This illustrates why controlling blue triangles gives no obvious control of all larger cliques.

The ordinal $\omega$ is the order type of the natural numbers. Larger ordinal powers, such as $\omega^{\omega^2}$, have richer order structures. An infinite subset need not be order-isomorphic to the whole ordinal, so cardinal and ordinal Ramsey statements require careful distinction.

## What is known

**DISPROVED.** Schipperus and Darby independently established counterexamples to the implication, with results published or circulated around 1999–2010. An explicit example recorded in the maintained [Erdős #118](https://www.erdosproblems.com/118) is due to Larson:

$$
\alpha=\omega^{\omega^2},
\quad
\alpha\to(\alpha,3)^2,
\quad
\alpha\not\to(\alpha,5)^2.
$$

The final failure means **there exists** a two-colouring with neither a red set of order type $\alpha$ nor a blue $K_5$. The claim is not that *every* two-colouring avoids them.

The UCSD primary historical collection [*Ordinal Ramsey 1*](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~erdosproblems/erdos/newproblems/OrdinalRamsey1.html) distinguishes positive Chang/Milner theorems for $\omega^\omega$ from the higher-ordinal counterexamples of Schipperus, Larson and Darby. Importing a positive theorem for the smaller ordinal $\omega^\omega$ to $\omega^{\omega^2}$ would be invalid.

## What remains

A bounded formal-semantics task is to encode $\alpha\to(\alpha,3)^2$ and its negation for $(\alpha,5)^2$ as quantified statements about edge colourings and order embeddings. Acceptance: correctly transform $\alpha\not\to(\alpha,5)^2$ into **existence** of a counterexample colouring, preserve the order type $\alpha$, and distinguish a blue $K_5$ from arbitrary blue five-vertex subgraphs. The cited construction remains an imported result.
