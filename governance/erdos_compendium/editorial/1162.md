## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1162](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1162). How fast does the number of subgroups of the symmetric group grow, and what can be said about the orders of typical subgroups?

$$f(n)=\#\{\text{subgroups of }S_n\}=2^{\,n^2/16+o(n^2)}.$$

Let f(n)=number of SUBGROUPS (not isomorphism classes) of symmetric group S_n. Erdős–Turán/Vardi #1162 asks (A) sharp asymptotics for f(n), and separately (B) a statistical theorem describing the ORDERS of subgroups of S_n. Do not conflate (A) solved with (B) unsolved or ambiguous, and specify LOGARITHM BASE.

## A small example

Exact closure enumeration of S3 subgroup lattice gives SIX distinct subgroups, with order histogram {1:1,2:3,3:1,6:1}. For n=3 this is exhaustive and demonstrates the distinction between number of subgroups and distribution of their orders; no asymptotic subgroup counting is certified by the tiny example.

## What is known

https://www.erdosproblems.com/1162 currently OPEN because statistical order-distribution question remains. Pyber [Py93] established log f(n)~order n². Roney-Dougal and Tracey [RoTr25], https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05416, prove f(n)=2^{n²/16+o(n²)}. Therefore LOG BASE 2 has (log_2 f(n))/n²→1/16; for natural logarithm the constant is (ln 2)/16. The live website writes log f(n)=(1/16+o(1))n² WITHOUT base; that requires a source-fidelity precision note. The paper abstract also includes nontrivial random subgroup properties, but none automatically answer the distribution of subgroup ORDERS.
