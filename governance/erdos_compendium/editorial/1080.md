## The question in plain language

Consider a bipartite graph on $N$ vertices with one part of size about $N^{2/3}$. Is there a fixed constant $c>0$ such that every sufficiently large such graph with at least $cN$ edges must contain a six-cycle $C_6$?

[Erdős Problem #1080](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1080) asks this question. The answer is **no**. There are bipartite graphs with superlinearly many edges and without six-cycles.

## A small example

The star $K_{1,12}$ has thirteen vertices, twelve edges and no cycle of any length. It illustrates why a cycle is not automatic, but is **not** a disproof of the conjecture: a fixed $c$ larger than one excludes this example. To refute an existential constant $c$, we need graphs with $e(G)/N$ becoming arbitrarily large while still avoiding $C_6$.

## What is known

De Caen and Székely (1992) constructed stronger examples avoiding **both** $C_4$ and $C_6$. With bipartition sizes $n$ and $\lfloor n^{2/3}\rfloor$, the maintained catalogue records more than

$$
n^{58/57+o(1)}
$$

edges. Lazebnik, Ustimenko and Woldar (1994) increased the reported lower exponent to $16/15+o(1)$. Since both exponents exceed one, a universal linear threshold cannot work.

The $n$ in those papers is a **part size**, not the total number of vertices $N=n+\lfloor n^{2/3}\rfloor$. Because $N\sim n$, the superlinear conclusion still follows.

[Authenticated source audit #2351](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2351#issuecomment-6160836554) distinguishes the actual asymptotic obstruction from the elementary finite star. The [frozen Lean translation](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1080.lean) says the proposed forcing statement is false and points to an external Lean proof. ## What remains

Check the primary 1992 and 1994 constructions, their part-size constraints and their cycle exclusions; then replay the cited external Lean proof against immutable dependencies. No finite star establishes the asymptotic disproof.
