## The question in plain language

Erdős, Hajnal and Mader asked whether there exists one absolute constant C such that EVERY finite graph on n vertices with at least $Cr^2n$ edges contains a subdivision of $K_r$. The maintained source says PROVED independently by Komlós–Szemerédi and Bollobás–Thomason (1996). 'Subdivision' means internally vertex-disjoint replacement paths, not merely a graph minor with connected branch sets.

## A small example

For r=4,n=4, a $K_4$ subdivision requires the complete $K_4$ itself, with six edges. $K_4$ has six edges and contains the required subdivision; $K_4$ minus one edge has five edges and cannot contain a K4 subdivision on only four vertices. This finite threshold check is unrelated to the unknown optimal absolute C for arbitrary r,n.

## What is known

Mader's older bound required roughly $2^{\binom{r}{2}}n$ edges, exponentially worse than quadratic in r. Komlós–Szemerédi [KoSz96] and Bollobás–Thomason [BoTh96] independently established the $Cr^2n$ sufficient condition. Dirac had earlier shown 2n−2 edges suffice for a K4 subdivision and conjectured a bound for K5. The exact constants, strict-versus-nonstrict conditions and small-n vacuity require original paper inspection; the catalogue abstracts are not new GCL proofs.
