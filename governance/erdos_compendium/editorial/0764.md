## The question in plain language

For $A$ a set of positive integers, define the triple convolution

$$
r_3(n)=(\mathbf{1}_A*\mathbf{1}_A*\mathbf{1}_A)(n).
$$

This counts ORDERED triples $a+b+c=n$. Erdős–Turán's stronger-summand analogue asks if the cumulative representation total $\sum_{n\le N}r_3(n)=cN+O(1)$ can hold for some c>0. Vaughan [Va72] proved the answer NO; the source reports a stronger impossibility with discrepancy $o(N^{1/4}/\sqrt{\log N})$, extending to general h-fold convolutions under suitable main terms.

## A small example

If A={1,2}, its ordered triples give $r_3(3)$=1, $r_3(4)$=3, $r_3(5)$=3 and $r_3(6)$=1, totaling 8 through N=6. For A=all positive integers, $r_3(n)=\binom{n-1}{2}$ for n≥3, so the cumulative count grows like N³/6 rather than cN. These examples illustrate ordering and triple convolution but cannot prove Vaughan's uniform no-go theorem.

## What is known

Erdős–Fuchs #763 is the two-fold analogue. The maintained page attributes the three-fold impossibility to Vaughan 1972 [Va72], including impossibility of error $o(N^{1/4}/(\log N)^{1/2})$, and a more general h-fold theorem allowing different main terms. Do not casually transfer the later Montgomery–Vaughan $o(N^{1/4})$ improvement for two-fold sums to h=3 without inspecting primary hypotheses. The original Vaughan proof was not independently rederived.
