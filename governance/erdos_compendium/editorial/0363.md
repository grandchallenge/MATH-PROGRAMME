## The question in plain language

Erdős Problem 363 asks whether only finitely many finite collections of pairwise disjoint intervals of integers can have a square as the product of all their entries, when every interval contains at least four integers. For positive starting points, write a block of length $k$ as $f(n,k)=n(n+1)\cdots(n+k-1)$. The separated version orders the blocks so that $n_i+k_i\leq n_{i+1}$; this prevents overlap while allowing the lengths $k_i$ to vary.

The answer to the broad finiteness question is no. The point is not that every choice of the number of blocks or their lengths has infinitely many examples. Rather, one explicit fixed-length, fixed-number family is enough to refute universal finiteness. The original issue packet is [MATH-PROGRAMME issue #1633](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1633), and the maintained [Erdős Problems entry](https://www.erdosproblems.com/363) records the same resolution.

## A small example

The construction of Bauer and Bennett uses the Pell-type equation $u^2-3v^2=-2$. The recurrence they give begins $u=1,5,19,71,265$; the corresponding value at $u=265$ is $v=153$. Their first family then gives starting points

$$
(x_1,x_2,x_3)=((u-5)/4,(v-3)/2,(u+1)/2)=(65,75,133).
$$

Thus the three blocks are $[65,68]$, $[75,78]$, and $[133,136]$. Direct integer arithmetic gives

$$
(65\cdot66\cdot67\cdot68)(75\cdot76\cdot77\cdot78)(133\cdot134\cdot135\cdot136)=218942618242667679360000=467913045600^2.
$$

The blocks are disjoint and each has length four, so this is an exact finite witness. It does not by itself establish infinitude; infinitude comes from the Pell recurrence and the identity proved in Bauer–Bennett, not from extrapolating this one calculation. It also does not settle the separate two-block length-four problem.

## What is known

Maciej Ulas, “On products of disjoint blocks of consecutive integers,” *L’Enseignement Mathématique* 51 (2005), 331–334, supplied the first relevant infinite constructions: length-four blocks with four blocks, and with any fixed number at least six. The live entry attributes these cases to Ulas and states that he also conjectured a sufficiently-large-number-of-blocks result for each fixed length.

Bauer and Bennett, “On a question of Erdős and Graham,” *L’Enseignement Mathématique* 53 (2007), 259–264, prove in Theorem 2.2 that for every number of blocks $j\geq3$, when all lengths are four, the separated square equation has infinitely many positive-integer solutions. Their proof includes the Pell family used above and extends the earlier cases $j=4$ and $j\geq6$ to $j=3$ and $j=5$. This is a primary proof of the counterexample needed for Problem 363’s general finiteness assertion.

Bennett and Van Luijk, “Squares from blocks of consecutive integers: a problem of Erdős and Graham,” *Indagationes Mathematicae* 23 (2012), 123–127, prove in Theorem 1.1 that for every $r\geq5$, there are infinitely many separated collections of $r$ blocks of length five whose product is a square. These results concern specific uniform lengths; they should not be inflated into a theorem covering every length and every block count.

## What remains

The bounded successor task is to reconcile the formal interface with the primary statement, then formalize only the already-published three-block length-four counterexample or its polynomial/Pell identity. Its input should be the pinned Lean file, the exact Theorem 2.2 family, and a normalization requiring a nonempty finite list of positive starts, strict separation, and explicit block lengths. The target is one theorem saying that the normalized predicate has an infinite family of witnesses, with the parameter recurrence and square identity stated separately.

Acceptance should require a source-linked semantic comparison, a checked identity over integers, and a Lean theorem whose hypotheses exclude empty and zero-containing artefacts. Falsification is any mismatch in the interval endpoints, separation inequality, Pell congruence, or square-root expression. Stop after one faithful family is specified and its finite-index arithmetic has been checked; do not launch an exhaustive build or claim that all length/count cases are resolved. If the Ulas original remains inaccessible, retain that as an explicit documentary gap rather than silently treating secondary citations as a read primary source.
