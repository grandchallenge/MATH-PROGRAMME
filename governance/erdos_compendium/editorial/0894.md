## The question in plain language

Suppose an increasing sequence of positive integers $A=\{n_1<n_2<\cdots\}$ grows by at least a fixed factor: there is an $\varepsilon>0$ such that $n_{j+1}\ge(1+\varepsilon)n_j$ for every $j$. Such a sequence is called *lacunary*, meaning its successive terms are separated on a multiplicative scale.

[Erdős Problem #894](https://www.erdosproblems.com/894) asks whether the positive integers admit a colouring with **finitely many colours** such that no pair of numbers of the same colour differs by an element of $A$. Equivalently, does the Cayley graph on the integers joining $x$ and $y$ whenever $|x-y|\in A$ have finite chromatic number?

The answer is **yes**, proved by Yitzhak Katznelson and strengthened quantitatively by Yuval Peres and Wilhelm Schlag. The finite colour count may depend on $\varepsilon$. It is not required to be uniform over all lacunary sequences or to use a fixed number such as three.

## A small example

Consider the lacunary sequence of powers of two, $A=\{1,2,4,8,16,\ldots\}$. Colour each positive integer by its remainder modulo 3. Each power of two has remainder 1 or 2 modulo 3, never zero. Two integers of the same colour differ by a multiple of 3, so their difference cannot be a power of two. **Three colours suffice for this entire infinite example.**

Similarly, powers of three are always odd, so colouring the integers by parity gives a proper **two-colouring** for the difference set $\{1,3,9,27,\ldots\}$. These elementary examples are special. They do not imply that an arbitrary lacunary sequence can be handled by choosing a modulus that divides none of its terms.

The reproducible finite fixture checks these modular colourings on all vertices $1,\ldots,2000$, for each difference present in the sequence below 2000. The modular arithmetic actually proves the colouring works for every positive integer; the finite program is an exact regression rather than the proof.

## What is known

Katznelson, [*Chromatic numbers of Cayley graphs on $\mathbb{Z}$ and recurrence*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s004930100019), *Combinatorica* **21** (2001), pp.211–219, explains that Erdős posed the question in 1987 and records a solution based on recurrence in a circle rotation. Katznelson relates the graph colouring to finding an angle $\theta$ and a constant $\delta>0$ satisfying

$$
\|\theta n_j\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\ge\delta
\qquad\text{for every }j,
$$

where the norm is distance to the nearest integer. The condition is a *uniform separation* over every member of the lacunary sequence; a finite list of numerical tests would not suffice.

Peres and Schlag, [*Two Erdős problems on lacunary sequences: Chromatic number and Diophantine approximation*](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms/bdp126), *Bulletin of the London Mathematical Society* **42** (2010), pp.295–300, use the Lovász local lemma to prove the quantitative estimate

$$
\chi(G_A)\le C\varepsilon^{-1}\log(1/\varepsilon)
$$

in the regime of small $\varepsilon$, for a universal implicit constant. Their published abstract describes the bound as sharp up to the logarithmic factor. The preliminary Katznelson bound was of order $\varepsilon^{-2}|\log\varepsilon|$.

This is a solved problem in the [maintained Erdős catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/894). The adjacent Diophantine approximation question [#464](https://www.erdosproblems.com/464) supplies the uniform circle separation; the colouring reduction below is a further elementary step. The original Katznelson and Peres–Schlag proofs are attributed to the published sources, not independently rebuilt by GCL.

## GCL's current state

The reduction from circle separation to finite colouring is elementary and rigorous. Suppose there are real numbers $\theta$ and $\delta>0$ such that the distance from $\theta n_j$ to the nearest integer is at least $\delta$ for all $j$. Choose a positive integer $m$ with $1/m<\delta$ and partition the circle into $m$ successive **half-open** arcs of length $1/m$. Colour each integer $x$ by the arc containing the fractional part of $\theta x$.

If $x$ and $y$ have the same colour, the ordinary separation of their two circle representatives is smaller than $1/m$ and hence their circle distance is also smaller than $\delta$. Therefore their difference cannot belong to $A$. This proves

$$
\chi(G_A)\le m
$$

conditional only on the displayed circle-separation hypothesis. The general existence of $\theta,\delta$ for arbitrary lacunary $A$ is the substantial *imported* theorem of Katznelson and Peres–Schlag.

The exact research program at governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_894_895.py verifies explicit modular examples for powers of two and three, including moduli 2, 3 and 5, and checks thousands of rational circle-bin instances using Python's exact fractions. These bounded checks are evidence for the elementary interface, not a reconstruction of the Lovász local lemma proof.

## What remains

A bounded reusable theorem is the **circle-separation colouring lemma**. Inputs are an integer sequence $A$, a real rotation angle $\theta$, an explicit positive separation $\delta$, and a positive integer $m$ with $1/m<\delta$. Target: construct the colouring with $m$ colours and prove that a monochromatic pair has difference outside $A$.

Acceptance requires a complete paper proof or pinned, compiled Lean theorem with no placeholders and literal axiom output, covering negative differences, half-open arc endpoints and all integer vertices. The exact modular colouring for powers of two and three and rational circle-bin fixtures provide regression tests. A monochromatic edge in any constructed colouring falsifies the lemma.
