## The question in plain language

Suppose $n$ distinct points lie in the real plane and **no line contains more than $n-k$ of them**, where $1\le k<n$. Erdős asked whether these points must determine at least a constant multiple of $kn$ distinct lines:

$$
\#\{\text{lines through at least two points}\}\gg kn.
$$

The lines counted here need **at least two** points, unlike #210, which counts *exactly two*. If almost all points lie on one line, the parameter $k$ measures how far the configuration is from that degeneracy.

## A small example

Take $n-1$ points on the $x$ axis and one point off the axis. The maximum collinearity is $n-1$, so $k=1$. The off-axis point determines $n-1$ distinct lines with the on-axis points, together with the $x$ axis itself: exactly $n$ distinct lines. This realizes order $kn$ when $k=1$.

By contrast, if no three points are collinear, each of the $\binom n2$ point pairs defines a distinct line, matching order $n^2$ for $k$ close to $n$.

## What is known

**PROVED** by József Beck and independently by Endre Szemerédi and William Trotter, both in 1983. Their incidence-geometric results show that an absolute constant $c>0$ satisfies

$$
\#\{\text{spanned lines}\}\ge ckn
$$

subject to the maximum-collinearity hypothesis. For $2n$ points with at most $n$ on any line, the result yields quadratically many distinct lines.

Erdős speculated about the sharper coefficient $(1+o(1))kn/6$, but identified the possibility as optimistic. The sixth-factor is constrained by configurations with about $n^2/6$ three-point lines due to Burr, Grünbaum and Sloane and Füredi–Palásti. The **order-of-magnitude theorem** is settled; the historical coefficient speculation should not be presented as proved.

## What remains

A bounded task enumerates spanned lines for rational-coordinate point configurations with specified maximum collinearity, including one point off a line of $n-1$ vertices and a set with no three collinear. Acceptance: confirm the exact counts $n$ and $\binom n2$, normalize lines by rational coefficients, and verify the threshold $n-k$ exactly. Stop before inferring an absolute constant from finite examples.
