## The question in plain language

A graph H is D-balanced when its maximum vertex degree is at most D times its minimum degree (so a meaningful positive-minimum-degree convention is required). Erdős and Simonovits asked whether every sufficiently dense n-vertex graph with >=n log n edges contains, for arbitrary target size m, a uniformly O(1)-balanced m-vertex subgraph with Omega(m log m) edges.

A D-balanced graph has largest degree at most D times its smallest positive degree. Alon's counterexamples invalidate the hoped-for logarithmic edge density. Janzer and Sudakov's later existence theorem recovers a weaker square-root-of-logarithm density. One should not compare the constants in these bounds without retaining their different quantifiers.

## A small example

The three-leaf star K1,3 has maximum degree three and minimum degree one, so it is 3-balanced, not 2-balanced. A triangle is 1-balanced. An isolated vertex produces minimum degree zero, requiring explicit convention: it must not be used to argue a meaningful bounded degree ratio.

This finite fixture tests the conventions. It does not certify any of the asymptotic or all-graphs assertions.

## What is known

Maintained #803 cites Alon [Al08], *Problems and results in extremal combinatorics II* (Discrete Math. 2008) showing a counterexample for every fixed D>1: D-balanced H has O(m sqrt(log m)+log D) edges in some host graphs having >=n log n edges. Janzer–Sudakov [JaSu23], *Resolution of the Erdős–Sauer problem on regular subgraphs* (Forum Math Pi 2023), guarantees only Omega_k(m sqrt(log m)/(loglog m)^(3/2)) for m>=k. Registry DISPROVED correct for original log m goal. Proof bodies not independently reproduced.

For a fixed balance parameter $D>1$, the recorded obstruction limits the edge count in the counterexample's balanced $m$ vertex subgraphs to an expression of order

$$

m\sqrt{\log m}+\log D.

$$

For fixed $D$, this is smaller than the originally demanded order $m\log m$. The positive Janzer–Sudakov result has a different scope and weaker bound.
