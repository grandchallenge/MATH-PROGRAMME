**Editorial status:** UNACCEPTED_DRAFT. Erdős Problem #500 is **OPEN**. It is Turán's celebrated $(3,4)$-hypergraph extremal problem: determine the largest number of triples on $n$ vertices without four vertices containing every possible triple. The maintained catalogue's quoted upper bound has a material **decimal and asymptotic-normalization source discrepancy**, documented below. This article claims neither a resolution nor prize eligibility.

## The question in plain language

An ordinary graph has edges joining *pairs* of vertices. A **3-uniform hypergraph**, or *3-graph*, has edges made of **three distinct** vertices. The complete 3-graph on four vertices, written $K_4^{(3)}$, contains **all four** triples on those vertices.

Write

$$
\operatorname{ex}_3(n,K_4^{(3)})
=\max\bigl\{|E(H)|:|V(H)|=n,\ H\text{ contains no }K_4^{(3)}\bigr\}.
$$

How large can the edge set be? Is the natural construction proposed by Pál Turán optimal?

The 1961 original formulation, printed p.243, instead asks for the **smallest number of triples that always forces** a forbidden four-vertex configuration. For $n\ge4$ the two normalizations differ by exactly **one**:

$$
f(n)=\operatorname{ex}_3(n,K_4^{(3)})+1.
$$

Confusing a *forcing threshold* with a *largest allowed edge count* loses this endpoint. The conjectural asymptotic edge density is

$$
\pi(K_4^{(3)})=
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\frac{\operatorname{ex}_3(n,K_4^{(3)})}{\binom n3}
\stackrel{?}{=}\frac59.
$$

The limit itself exists by standard averaging for hereditary forbidden-subgraph problems. Its numerical value remains unknown. The maintained [Erdős #500 catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/500) classifies the question **OPEN**.

## A small example

For $n=4$, there are exactly four possible triples. Taking all four creates the forbidden $K_4^{(3)}$. Taking any three is allowed, hence

$$
\operatorname{ex}_3(4,K_4^{(3)})=3,\qquad f(4)=4.
$$

This simple exact value will also expose an incorrect finite-$n$ upper-bound normalization below.

For the classical lower-bound construction, partition the vertices into nearly equal colour classes $X_0,X_1,X_2$, and retain a triple precisely when its colours are either

- **rainbow:** one vertex from each class; or
- **cyclic:** two vertices from $X_i$ and one from $X_{i+1}$, with indices modulo three.

No four vertices have **all four** triples among these allowed patterns. This can be proved by inspecting how four vertices distribute among three colour classes: type $4$, $3+1$, $2+2$, or $2+1+1$. In every case at least one of their triples is omitted. Thus the construction is genuinely $K_4^{(3)}$-free, not merely a high-density guess.

When $n=3k$ and all classes have size $k$, the rainbow triples contribute $k^3$ edges. The three directed two-plus-one types contribute $3\binom{k}{2}k$. Therefore

$$
|E(H)|=k^3+3k\binom{k}{2}
=\frac{5k^3-3k^2}{2}.
$$

Since $\binom{3k}{3}\sim(9/2)k^3$, division gives limiting density exactly $5/9$. At $n=6$ this construction contains $14$ edges, while $n=5$ admits an exact $7$-edge construction. Small exhaustive checks verify $\operatorname{ex}_3(4,K_4^{(3)})=3$ and $\operatorname{ex}_3(5,K_4^{(3)})=7$.

These are concrete witnesses, **not** a proof that Turán's construction is always optimal.

## What is known

### The original hypergraph problem

P. Erdős, *Some unsolved problems* (1961), printed p.243, Problem II.8, asks the *minimal forcing threshold* $f(n)$ and credits Turán's 1954 paper, *On the theory of graphs*, *Colloquium Mathematicum* **3**, pp.19–30. The [original page](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1961-22.pdf#page=23) was visually inspected. Although Turán's graph theorem gives a sharp triangle threshold for ordinary graphs, its three-uniform hypergraph analogue is substantially harder. Exact finite counts and the asymptotic $5/9$ construction do not resolve the general problem.

### A conditional Razborov theorem is not the unrestricted answer

Alexander A. Razborov, *On 3-hypergraphs with forbidden 4-vertex configurations*, *SIAM Journal on Discrete Mathematics* **24** (2010), 946–963, DOI [10.1137/090747476](https://doi.org/10.1137/090747476), develops flag-algebra inequalities. His [author preprint](https://homepage.mi-ras.ru/~razborov/turan) (dated December 2008) was inspected, including p.3 and its Theorem 1.

Theorem 1 gives an asymptotically sharp $5/9$ bound for $K_4^{(3)}$-free hypergraphs **with an additional forbidden induced four-vertex configuration having exactly one edge**. That extra restriction is not part of Erdős #500. Dropping it from the theorem statement would falsely appear to solve the famous general conjecture.

The same page of the author preprint discusses a **separate numerical flag-algebra calculation** that suggests a complementary density bound approximately $0.438334$, corresponding to an upper density approximately $0.561666$. It expressly notes that this floating-point result was **not converted into a rigorous proof in that paper**.

### Source discrepancy in the reported best upper bound

The maintained Erdős #500 entry attributes an upper bound of $0.5611666$ to Razborov and writes it in the form of a plain inequality for $\operatorname{ex}_3(n,K_4^{(3)})$. Both aspects require care.

First, a constant upper bound without an asymptotic error cannot hold for all $n$. At $n=4$,

$$
\frac{\operatorname{ex}_3(4,K_4^{(3)})}{\binom43}
=\frac34>0.5611666.
$$

A genuine density result must instead have the form

$$
\operatorname{ex}_3(n,K_4^{(3)})
\le \bigl(c+o(1)\bigr)\binom n3
$$

or equivalently $\pi(K_4^{(3)})\le c$.

Second, the published **Flagmatic User's Guide**, version 1.5, by Emil Vaughan, provides a reproducible **exact rationalized** level-six flag-algebra calculation, documented with 964 admissible six-vertex configurations and a verification script. Its reported upper bound is

$$
\pi(K_4^{(3)})\le
\frac{2246662543771}{4000000000000}
=0.56166563594275.
$$

This is **not** the catalogue decimal $0.5611666$. The numerical difference is approximately $0.000499036$. The guide's [indexed primary PDF](https://lidicky.name/flagmatic/UsersGuide.pdf) reports the exact rational number and a procedure for certificate generation and verification. The PDF itself denied direct fetch during this pass and GCL did **not** independently execute the Flagmatic certificate. Accordingly, this manuscript treats the rational inequality as **documented by Flagmatic**, not as newly machine-certified by GCL.

Razborov's conditional $5/9$ theorem, his explicitly uncertified numerical estimate, and Flagmatic's documented rational certificate are three different evidence levels. No source found in this audit justified the catalogue's smaller decimal. The source-conflict return asks for a governed correction rather than silently rewriting protected evidence.

## What remains

The genuine research question is whether $\pi(K_4^{(3)})=5/9$ or some more efficient family of $K_4^{(3)}$-free hypergraphs exists. One rigorous direction is a **certified flag-algebra upper bound**, with rational matrices, exact enumeration, a machine-readable certificate and independent verification. Another is a construction with density provably larger than $5/9$, which would falsify the classical conjecture.

The bounded immediate tasks are to reproduce the exact small-$n$ cases and settle the catalogue upper-bound attribution with the original Razborov/Flagmatic documentation. The full 3-graph Turán problem remains open and is not reducible to a finite computation alone.
