## The question in plain language

Given a configuration of $n$ distinct points in the plane, call a line *rich* if it contains at least four of those points. Fix a constant $c>0$ and suppose the configuration determines at least $cn^2$ **distinct rich lines**.

Erdős and Purdy's #102 asks whether this much incidence forces at least one line to contain more and more points as $n$ grows. More precisely, if $h_c(n)$ is the greatest lower bound on the maximum number of collinear points guaranteed under the hypothesis, must

$$
h_c(n)\longrightarrow\infty
$$

for each feasible fixed $c>0$? The richness condition is **four points per line**, and the $cn^2$ count concerns distinct lines, not incidences or point pairs.

## A small example

Take four collinear points. They produce a single four-rich line, even though all six unordered pairs lie on it. Thus the number of point pairs is not the number of rich lines.

Each pair of distinct planar points determines a unique line. Since a four-rich line contains at least $\binom42=6$ pairs, and different lines never share a pair, a configuration with $L$ rich lines satisfies

$$
6L\le\binom n2.
$$

Consequently $L\le n(n-1)/12$. In particular a fixed density $c\ge1/12$ is impossible for any positive finite $n$ under the literal $L\ge cn^2$ hypothesis. Only feasible small positive $c$ are relevant to the asymptotic problem; otherwise the implication is vacuous.

## What is known

The intended question remains **OPEN**. The maintained [Erdős #102](https://www.erdosproblems.com/102) explicitly reports that even the guarantee $h_c(n)\ge5$ is unknown in the interesting regime. The related [#101](https://www.erdosproblems.com/101) is a distinct, already-accepted catalogue entry and must not be confused with this problem.

The same maintained source describes a construction noted by Zach Hunter: high-dimensional grids have very many rich lines but no line with more than $O_d(n^{1/d})$ points, and a generic projection into the plane can preserve the finite collinearity structure. This defeats an earlier suggested lower bound $h_c(n)\gg_c\sqrt n$; it does **not** disprove unbounded growth of $h_c(n)$. The precise density constants and projection-genericity conditions of the Hunter construction have not been independently written out as a complete proof.

## What remains

A bounded verification task is to formalize $6L\le\binom n2$ by assigning each unordered point pair to at most one line, with exactly six pairs already present on every four-rich line. Enumerate rational point configurations of limited size and count each distinct rich line once, by a primitive integer line-equation normalization. Acceptance: the four-collinear-point fixture reports one line, not six; the necessary $c<1/12$ asymptotic restriction follows. Stop without inferring any growing lower bound on $h_c(n)$.
