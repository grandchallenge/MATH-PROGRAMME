## The question in plain language

In a graph with chromatic number four, list distinct lengths of all cycles in increasing order. Erdős asked if their successive gaps can all be made arbitrarily large, perhaps even when the girth grows. Bondy–Vince 1998 DISPROVED the possibility: every graph with minimum degree≥3 has two cycles with distinct lengths differing by at most two; every 4-chromatic graph contains a 4-critical subgraph of minimum degree≥3, so its cycle-length set contains two lengths with gap at most two. This works independently of girth.

## A small example

The graph K4 is 4-chromatic and has cycles of lengths 3 and 4, so its minimum gap between distinct cycle lengths is one. This is a finite witness, not the Bondy–Vince universal theorem. For every 4-critical graph H, each vertex must have degree≥3: a vertex of degree≤2 could be removed, the rest 3-coloured, and the colouring extended, contradicting χ(H)=4.
