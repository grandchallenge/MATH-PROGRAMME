## The question in plain language

An Egyptian fraction is a sum of reciprocals of positive integers. For a positive integer $k$, consider every identity

$$
1=\frac{1}{n_1}+\frac{1}{n_2}+\cdots+\frac{1}{n_k},
\qquad 1\leq n_1<n_2<\cdots<n_k.
$$

The strict inequalities are part of the question: denominators must be pairwise distinct. Let $D_k$ be the set of all positive integers that occur as one of the denominators in at least one identity with $k$ summands. Erdős Problem #293 asks about

$$
v(k)=\min\{m\in\mathbb Z:m>1\text{ and }m\notin D_k\}.
$$

Thus the quantifiers are important. For each fixed $k$, a number belongs to $D_k$ if there exists one suitable decomposition containing it; $v(k)$ is the first integer greater than $1$ for which no such decomposition exists. The problem asks how $v(k)$ grows as $k$ grows. It does not ask whether one particular decomposition is unique, nor whether every denominator can occur in every decomposition.

The original Erdős–Graham scan explicitly includes the condition $v(n)>1$. The maintained [Erdős Problems entry](https://www.erdosproblems.com/293) states the modern version but flags the original source as ambiguous. This article uses the pairwise-distinct normalization above because it is the formulation in the inspected modern paper; the notation-to-source mapping remains a documented semantic exception, not a silently certified equivalence.

## A small example

The denominator $42$ occurs in a four-term decomposition:

$$
1=\frac12+\frac13+\frac17+\frac1{42}.
$$

The exact check has common denominator $42$:

$$
21+14+6+1=42.
$$

The denominators $2,3,7,42$ are positive, strictly increasing, and distinct. Consequently $42\in D_4$. This proves only membership in $D_4$; it says nothing about whether $42$ is the first missing denominator, and it cannot establish a value of $v(4)$ by itself.

An independently reproducible exact-arithmetic check is:

```python
from fractions import Fraction

denominators = (2, 3, 7, 42)
assert len(set(denominators)) == len(denominators)
assert all(a < b for a, b in zip(denominators, denominators[1:]))
assert sum((Fraction(1, n) for n in denominators), Fraction(0)) == 1
```

This uses integer rational arithmetic, not rounded decimal arithmetic. The authenticated source audit also records a much larger exact nine-term certificate, but the associated claim that a nearby integer is missing was not promoted from a reported computation to a theorem.

## What is known

The historical source is Erdős and Graham, *Old and New Problems and Results in Combinatorial Number Theory*, printed p. 35. It records that results of Bleicher and Erdős imply a factorial-scale lower bound and speculates about substantially faster growth. The directly inspected Bleicher–Erdős paper, *The Number of Distinct Subsums of $\sum_{i=1}^{N}1/i$*, *Mathematics of Computation* 29 (1975), 29–42, proves lower bounds for the number of distinct harmonic subsums. It is relevant primary evidence, but this manuscript does not reconstruct every step needed to turn that subsum theorem into the claimed bound for $v(k)$.

The strongest directly checked modern theorem is Theorem 1.1 of Wouter van Doorn and Quanyu Tang, *The smallest denominator not contained in a unit fraction decomposition of 1 with fixed length*:

$$
\exists c>0\ \forall k\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\qquad v(k)\geq e^{c k^2}.
$$

This is a genuine published-source theorem statement inspected in the authors’ arXiv text, with pairwise-distinct denominators and the same $D_k$ interface. The paper also says that extracting the older $v(k)\gg k!$ claim from the earlier literature is not straightforward. That creates a historical evidence conflict about the ease and status of the factorial implication; it does not change the present open status of #293.

For the other direction, the maintained entry records an elementary upper bound of the form

$$
v(k)\leq k c_0^{2^k},
$$

where $c_0=1.26408\ldots$ is the Vardi constant. These lower and upper bounds leave a very wide gap. The maintained page also reports a conditional route to doubly exponential growth through a stronger estimate for the related rational-representation quantity $N(b)$; that conditional suggestion is not a theorem about #293 as written.

## What remains

The status is **open**, with the caution that this is the reconciled documentary status, not a new mathematical adjudication. A useful bounded successor task is to reproduce exact rational certificates for a finite interval of candidate denominators, beginning with a specified fixed $k$, while checking denominator distinctness, the identity sum, duplicate records, and interval coverage. A separate exact branch-and-bound search can test a reported small missing-value case. Each search must state its denominator bounds and termination rule.

The acceptance test must distinguish positive and negative evidence. Finding a valid decomposition proves membership in $D_k$. Finding no decomposition proves non-membership only when the search is exhaustive under a stated finite bound and the bound is known to contain every relevant branch. A finite list of successful certificates never proves that the next integer is $v(k)$ unless all smaller candidates have also been excluded.

The full reported search around a large value was not executed here: the audit did not replay its C search, checker, certificate release, or 2000-shard computation. The reported website comments are unverified discussion evidence. The smallest missing lemma for stronger exposition is therefore not merely a coding detail; it is a source-faithful, independently checkable argument that connects the complete bounded computation, its exact inputs, and the claimed absence statement.
