## The question in plain language

Suppose a set of nonnegative integers represents every sufficiently large integer as a sum of two members. Suppose, more strongly, that the number of such representations grows without bound. Can the set always be split into two disjoint sets, **each** of which remains an additive basis? Erdős and Nathanson asked this in 1988. [Erdős Problem #871](https://www.erdosproblems.com/871) records the answer as **no**. The counterexample is attributed to Daniel Larsen, building on Erdős and Nathanson's earlier construction; a 2026 paper studies three independent robustness properties of additive bases.

A basis is an inexhaustible supply of two-summand representations. Splitting it into two bases demands that both resulting supplies work for *every sufficiently large* target, not merely for many targets or for a finite test interval. Divergence of the total representation count does not force this simultaneous decomposability.

Let $A\subseteq\mathbb N$ and put

$$
R_A^{\mathrm{ord}}(n)=\#\{(a,b)\in A^2:a+b=n\}.
$$

The original question asks whether the conjunction of eventual two-summand coverage and $R_A^{\mathrm{ord}}(n)\to\infty$ implies the existence of disjoint $B,C$ with $A=B\sqcup C$ and both $B,C$ asymptotic bases of order two. The negative answer is an **existential counterexample**, not a claim that no such partition ever exists.

## A small example

A formal statement may count unordered representations rather than ordered pairs. Define

$$
R_A^{\mathrm{unord}}(n)=\#\{(a,b)\in A^2:a\le b,\ a+b=n\}.
$$

Every off-diagonal unordered pair gives two ordered pairs; a diagonal pair $(n/2,n/2)$ gives only one. Therefore the exact identity is

$$
R_A^{\mathrm{ord}}(n)=2R_A^{\mathrm{unord}}(n)
-\mathbf 1_{\{n\ \mathrm{even},\ n/2\in A\}}.
$$

Consequently

$$
2R_A^{\mathrm{unord}}(n)-1\le R_A^{\mathrm{ord}}(n)
\le 2R_A^{\mathrm{unord}}(n).
$$

It follows in **both directions** that $R_A^{\mathrm{ord}}(n)\to\infty$ if and only if $R_A^{\mathrm{unord}}(n)\to\infty$. This is an important distinction from Erdős #868: a *fixed numerical logarithmic coefficient* does change when one changes the counting convention, but the qualitative divergence hypothesis of #871 does not.

An offline fixture independently evaluated both counts for all 255 nonempty subsets of $\{1,\ldots,8\}$ and targets $2\le n\le17$ (4,080 identities); no discrepancy occurred. The finite test is a check on the implementation, while the displayed pairing argument proves the identity for arbitrary $A$.

## What is known

The primary historical reference is P. Erdős and M. B. Nathanson, *Partitions of bases into disjoint unions of bases*, Journal of Number Theory **29** (1988), 1–9, Part III; [archival scan](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1988-25.pdf). Their later *Additive bases with many representations*, Acta Arithmetica (1989), constructs bases with an arbitrarily prescribed fixed lower bound on representation counts that cannot be partitioned into two bases. An arbitrary **fixed** lower bound is not the same as divergence to infinity.

D. Larsen, [*Three Questions of Erdős–Nathanson on Asymptotic Bases of Order 2*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03472), arXiv:2603.03472v1, submitted 3 March 2026, proves independence of three properties: divergent representations, decomposability into two bases, and possession of a minimal subbasis. In particular, the reported construction with divergent representations but no two-basis partition answers #871 negatively. The paper's abstract and the maintained catalogue corroborate the conclusion; its full inductive proof has **not** been independently replayed for this article.

The protected statement in `google-deepmind/formal-conjectures` at commit `85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020`, file `FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/871.lean` (blob `fdf5d953049115f140b1cff25b6376016566b1e8`), uses finite sets of pairs with $a\le b$ and arbitrarily large cardinality for all sufficiently large $n$. By the identity above, this matches the divergence condition of the original ordered-convolution question. The provider file still contains a `sorry` for the target theorem: a formal *statement* is not a checked proof.

A separate [Lean development](https://github.com/plby/lean-proofs/blob/main/src/v4.29.1/ErdosProblems/Erdos871.lean), source blob `a004c9e4a1822d7c9dd0d2c1817febb62d8053cb`, declares `Erdos871.not_erdos_871`, constructs a counterexample set, and ends with `#print axioms Erdos871.not_erdos_871`. Its source comment claims only the standard axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. Static source inspection is **not** an independent Lean build or verification of that printed output.

## GCL's current state

The exact ordered/unordered identity and the equivalence of the two divergence formulations have been proved directly above. This discharges a narrowly scoped source-fidelity obligation for the counting convention. The fixture `finite_871_872.py` reproduces 4,080 finite checks, but the mathematical identity does not depend on computation.

The protected theorem expresses a universal partition implication under `answer(False)`. The external Lean theorem expresses the **existence** of a counterexample to the same implication. Classically these are complementary logical forms, provided the natural-number conventions, eventual quantifiers, and partition predicates are matched. The current source-level comparison finds the same eventual-basis and unordered-divergence predicates; it does **not** establish a kernel-certified equivalence between independently compiled projects.

For intuition, imagine distributing an increasing supply of witnesses among two recipients. Knowing the combined supply becomes arbitrarily large does not guarantee an allocation in which both recipients can meet every future demand. The obstruction is global: choices that work on a long finite prefix need not extend indefinitely.

A second bounded task is a human-readable audit of Larsen's inductive interval construction, specifically its simultaneous proof of (i) divergence at every sufficiently large target and (ii) impossibility of a partition into two asymptotic bases. A counterexample that merely supplies large representation counts along a subsequence is insufficient. The stopping condition is either a source-locked proof chain for both properties or a precise unresolved lemma and source location.

Neither task is a reason to reclassify #871 as open. The correct present editorial state is **reported disproved; independent proof replay pending**.
