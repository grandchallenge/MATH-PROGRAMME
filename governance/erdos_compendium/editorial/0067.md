## The question in plain language

Imagine an infinite row of plus and minus signs. Choose a step size $d$, and read the signs in positions $d,2d,3d,\ldots$. Must some initial portion of some such progression show an arbitrarily large imbalance between pluses and minuses? Erdős conjectured yes; Terence Tao proved it.

The exact question asks whether every $f:\mathbb N\to\{-1,+1\}$ satisfies

$$
\forall C>0\ \exists d,m\ge1:\quad
\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}f(kd)\right|>C.
$$

## A small example

For a row of $N$ signs, define the finite discrepancy

$$
D_N=\max_{md\le N}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{kd}\right|.
$$

A complete enumeration of the $4096$ possible twelve-sign rows shows that the smallest achievable $D_{12}$ is $2$, attained by $370$ rows. The calculation is finite evidence, not a proof of the infinite theorem. If one appends another sign, $D_N$ cannot decrease, because all the old progressions remain available.

## What is known

Tao proved in 2015–2016 that the discrepancy is unbounded for every infinite sign sequence. His argument uses a Fourier-analytic reduction developed in the Polymath5 project, results on correlations of multiplicative functions, and the structure of functions resembling Dirichlet characters. The theorem also holds for sequences of unit vectors in real or complex Hilbert space. See [Tao, *Discrete Analysis* 2016:1](https://doi.org/10.19086/da.609) and [arXiv:1509.05363v6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.05363v6).

There is a distinct, stronger quantitative question: must the maximum discrepancy among progressions with $md\le x$ grow at least as fast as a constant times $\log x$? The maintained catalogue records partial quantitative progress, but Tao's yes/no theorem alone does not establish this rate.

## What remains

A bounded follow-up is to formalize the finite functional $D_N$, its monotonicity, and the twelve-sign enumeration. Compactness supplies a useful logical bridge: the infinite theorem is equivalent to saying that for each integer $C$ some finite length $N(C)$ forces discrepancy greater than $C$ for every sign vector of that length. The existence of these witnesses for every $C$ is the substantive result, not something established by checking $N=12$.
