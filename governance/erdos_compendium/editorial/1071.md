## The question in plain language

Put straight line segments of length one inside a square. They must be disjoint, so we cannot add a segment crossing one already chosen. A family is called **maximal** when there is no further admissible unit segment that can be added while preserving the disjointness rule.

[Erdős Problem #1071](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1071) asks two surprisingly different questions. Can such a family inside the unit square be **finite** and maximal? Can some region contain a **countably infinite** maximal family?

“Maximal” means *cannot add anything*, not *has the largest possible cardinality*. Whether line segments include their endpoints also affects how disjointness is formalized.

## A small example

Consider the open horizontal unit segment from $(0,0)$ to $(1,0)$ in the unit square. This singleton family is not maximal. A second horizontal open segment from $(0,1)$ to $(1,1)$ can be added without intersection.

The observation proves only that this **particular** arrangement is extendible; it does not rule out a different finite maximal arrangement. Rotating and placing segments can create much more restrictive patterns than parallel segments do.

## What is known

The catalogue records an affirmative finite construction by **Ludwig Danzer**, discussed in Erdős's 1987 geometric survey. It also attributes a countably infinite maximal-family construction to a 1999 source and reports a more recent **Boris Alexeev** result in Lean for a unit-square variant.

The catalogue therefore marks #1071 **proved (Lean)**. These are externally established or externally reported results; GCL has not independently reconstructed the original drawings or replayed the formal proof.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source audit #2342](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2342#issuecomment-6157264885) directly inspected the [frozen Lean formulation](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1071.lean). Its definition of segment disjointness allows intersection only at endpoints, matching an important aspect of the open-segment convention.

The frozen statement also requires an open, connected region for its second part. External Lean proofs are linked, but the pinned file's own theorem bodies have unproved placeholders. Consequently, their existence in a catalogue is not a GCL proof certificate.

## What remains
