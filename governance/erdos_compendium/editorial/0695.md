## The question in plain language

A prime chain is a strictly increasing sequence p_1<p_2<... of primes such that p_{i+1}≡1 (mod p_i). Erdős asks (a) whether for every infinite prime chain p_k^{1/k}→∞, and (b) whether SOME chain grows as slowly as exp(k(log k)^{1+o(1)}). Both remain OPEN. The first universal lower question and the second existential upper question must not be conflated.

## A small example

An exact five-term prime chain is 2,3,7,29,59: 3≡1 mod2, 7≡1 mod3, 29≡1 mod7, and 59≡1 mod29. Each term is prime and strictly larger. This demonstrates a finite chain but cannot decide the possible growth of all infinite chains.

## What is known

Dirichlet's theorem supplies at least one larger prime ≡1 mod p for each current prime p, so finite chains can be extended indefinitely by successive choices (with the usual classical theorem, not a GCL new proof). Linnik's theorem gives a coarse doubly exponential upper estimate for the greedy least-successor chain. The source reports a much sharper upper rate conjecture, conditional on finding prime successors ≤p(log p)^{O(1)}. The pinned #695.lean directly inspected has two `answer(sorry)` questions and `by sorry` proof bodies; no certificate of either asymptotic. Ford–Konyagin–Luca [FKL10] original finite-chain study was not rechecked.
