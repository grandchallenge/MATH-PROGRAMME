## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1213](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1213) currently has source status **PROVED — HEGYVÁRI**.

Let $1\le a=a_1<\cdots<a_s$ have **gaps** $a_{i+1}-a_i\le K$, and let $I,J$ be **distinct contiguous intervals of sequence indices**, not arbitrary subsets or numerical intervals of the real line. Ask whether a function $f(a,K)$ exists such that $a_s>f(a,K)$ guarantees equal interval sums $\sum_{i\in I}a_i=\sum_{j\in J}a_j$. The intervals need not be disjoint according to the printed question, although explicit disjoint witnesses are stronger.

## A small example

For the sequence $(2,3,4,5)$, with $a=2,K=1$, the first two terms sum to $2+3=5$, equal to the final singleton term $5$. Those index intervals $[1,2]$ and $[4,4]$ are **disjoint** and distinct. The phase25 check enumerates all nonempty contiguous intervals. This one example is not a proof of Hegyvári's general $a e^{O(K)}$ upper bound.

## What is known

The maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/1213 reports **PROVED** by Hegyvári [He86], with a quantitative bound $f(a,K)\ll a\exp(O(K))$. Hegyvári suggested exponential dependence on the maximum gap $K$ is likely not optimal. This is a statement about the *last term exceeding a threshold*, not merely having many terms without a bound on gaps. The primary proof and exact constants have not been independently replayed by GCL.
