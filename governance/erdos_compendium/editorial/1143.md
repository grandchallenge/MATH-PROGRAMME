## The question in plain language

Choose finitely many primes $p_1<\cdots<p_u$. No matter where we look, how many multiples of **at least one** selected prime must occur in every block of $k$ consecutive positive integers?

[Erdős Problem #1143](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1143) defines the sharp guaranteed count

$$
F_k(p_1,\ldots,p_u)
=\min_{s\ge1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}
{\bf1}\{\text{some }p_i\mid s+j\}.
$$

The problem asks for estimates, particularly when

$$
k=\alpha p_u,\qquad \alpha>2.
$$

The **minimum over all starting positions** distinguishes it from merely computing the expected proportion of multiples.

## A small example

For the selected primes $\{2,3,5\}$, the divisibility pattern repeats every

$$
\mathrm{lcm}(2,3,5)=30
$$

integers. Consequently, checking the 30 possible starting residues gives **exact** answers for any fixed $k$.

The arithmetic fixture finds:

| Interval length $k$ | Guaranteed minimum $F_k(2,3,5)$ |
|---|---:|
| 1 | 0 |
| 5 | 3 |
| 10 | 6 |
| 16 | 11 |

The case $k=16$ has $k>3p_u=15$. Thus even a tiny example enters a parameter range where the general problem is difficult.

## What is known

The maintained catalogue reports that **Erdős and Selfridge** obtained an “exact bound” when $2<\alpha<3$, based on Vardi (1999).

However, the catalogue's editor explicitly states that **no reference or corresponding paper has been found**. That makes this a source-fidelity obligation, not a verified mathematical theorem whose formula can be filled in by assumption.

For $\alpha>3$, the source reports that much remains unknown. The problem is connected to covering systems, reduced residue patterns and minimum counts across shifted intervals.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source audit #2414](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2414#issuecomment-6181194423) independently proves the finite periodic reduction and computes the four sharp counts.

The unsourced Erdős–Selfridge claim has **not** been promoted into an accepted theorem statement.

## What remains

Find Vardi's original citation chain and any actual Erdős–Selfridge proof. Develop a verified periodic or Chinese-remainder dynamic program for wider prime sets, while retaining the distinction between exact finite computations and an unknown uniform asymptotic.
