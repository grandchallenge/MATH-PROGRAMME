## The question in plain language

Let f(n) be the least positive integer such that in the OPEN interval (n,n+f(n)) one can choose n DISTINCT integers a_1,...,a_n satisfying k|a_k for k=1,...,n. Erdős–Pomerance ask for an asymptotic formula for f(n), not merely big-O upper and lower bounds. The maintained problem #710 remains OPEN; the fact that the interval is open at both ends changes finite fixtures by one.

## A small example

For n=1 the interval (1,3) contains integer 2, giving f(1)=2; (1,2) contains none. For n=2, (2,5) contains 3 and 4, allowing a_1=3,a_2=4, so f(2)=3. For n=3, (3,7) contains 4,5,6; assign a_1=5,a_2=4,a_3=6, giving f(3)=4. For n=4, the interval (4,9) contains exactly 5,6,7,8, but a_3 must be 6 and a_4 must be 8, leaving no even unused number for a_2, so f(4)>5; (4,10) gives a_1=5,a_2=6,a_3=9,a_4=8, proving f(4)=6. These examples are exact finite matching certificates.

## What is known

Maintained source attributes to Erdős and Pomerance [ErPo80] the bounds (2/sqrt(e)+o(1))n sqrt(log n/loglog n) ≤ f(n) ≤ (1.7398...+o(1)) n sqrt(log n). These do not match to a single asymptotic leading term. There is a direct relation f(n)=f(n,n) to #711's offset-parameter function, but #711 is not fully resolved. Historical Erdős 1992 prize offer is not a mathematical status. Full original ErPo80 primary proof pages were not independently checked.
