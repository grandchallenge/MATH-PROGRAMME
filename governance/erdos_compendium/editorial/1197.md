## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1197](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1197) is **EXTERNAL DISPROVED** in the source as of 15 October 2026.

For **every Lebesgue-measurable** $E\subset(0,\infty)$ of positive measure, is it true that for **almost all** real $x>0$, and **all sufficiently large integers $n$ depending on $x$**, some integer $r\ge1$ satisfies $nx\in rE$, i.e. $nx/r\in E$? The quantifier 'almost every $x$' is with respect to Lebesgue measure; a failure on a positive-measure interval disproves it. It is not equivalent to existence of infinitely many $n$ with this property.

## A small example

For the interval $E=(1,2)$, choose $x=3/2$, $n=4$ and $r=4$: then $nx/r=3/2\in E$. More generally, the interval of permissible real $r$, $(nx/2,nx)$, has length $nx/2>1$ for all sufficiently large $n$, so it contains an integer and the asserted property actually holds for **every** fixed $x>0$ when $E$ contains a genuine open interval. This positive special case is not a disproof of a counterexample built from a more complicated set.

## What is known

The source https://www.erdosproblems.com/1197 marks **DISPROVED**, attributing a 2026 counterexample to ebarschkis based on Buczolich–Mauldin [BuMa99] and Haight's interval-free constructions. Source proof of the new counterexample has **not independently been verified** by GCL; do not claim BuMa99 by itself establishes every part of the 2026 negative result.
