## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1210](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1210) currently has source status **OPEN**.

For $A\subseteq[1,n)$ of pairwise coprime integers, ask whether there is an **absolute** $O(1)$ such that
$$\sum_{a\in A}\frac1{n-a}\le\sum_{p<n}\frac1p+O(1).$$
Pairwise coprimality concerns the $a$'s, **not** the shifted denominators $n-a$. A sample where the left-hand side exceeds the bare prime harmonic sum does not refute an inequality with an unspecified additive constant uniform in $n$.

## A small example

For each $n=5,\dots,14$, enumerate all $2^{n-1}$ subsets of $\{1,\dots,n-1\}$, impose pairwise $\gcd=1$, and maximize the shifted reciprocal sum as an **exact rational**. For $n=5$ the maximum is $7/4$, attained by $A=\{1,3,4\}$, with denominators $4,2,1$. These bounded maxima supply no general constant and do not disprove the conjecture.
