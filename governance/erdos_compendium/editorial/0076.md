## The question in plain language

Color every edge of the complete graph $K_n$ either red or blue. Erdős #76 asks how many **monochromatic triangles** can always be chosen so that no two chosen triangles share an edge. A triangle is monochromatic if its three edges have the same color; the collection may contain both red and blue triangles.

The conjectured asymptotic guarantee is about $n^2/12$ triangles. The phrase *edge-disjoint* is crucial: many different triangles can exist but compete for the same edges.

## A small example

Divide six vertices into two groups of three. Color the edges *within* each group blue and all crossing edges red. Exactly two triangles are monochromatic, one inside each group; both are blue and they are edge-disjoint. No crossing triangle is monochromatic, since it has a blue internal edge and red crossing edges.

For general even $n$, the analogous balanced complete-bipartite red color class and two blue cliques illustrate an asymptotically extremal configuration. The two cliques support at most $n^2/12+O(n)$ pairwise edge-disjoint blue triangles by counting edges: each triangle consumes three distinct edges. Existence of an almost-maximal packing is a deeper matter.

## What is known

The conjecture is **PROVED** by Vytautas Gruslys and Shoham Letzter in *Monochromatic triangle packings in red-blue graphs*. Their Theorem 1.2 states that every red-blue coloring of $K_n$ has a collection of

$$
\frac{n^2}{12}+o(n^2)
$$

pairwise edge-disjoint monochromatic triangles. The sign of the small error is not specified by the asymptotic notation: the operational lower-bound reading is at least $n^2/12-o(n^2)$. Their Theorem 1.3 gives stability information for colorings near the minimum: extremal configurations have a color class close to bipartite. Both statements are visible in the authors' primary paper.

Their proof uses fractional triangle packing and further structural arguments; counting the triangles alone does not prove the packing result.

GCL inspected Theorems 1.2 and 1.3 in the 37-page primary preprint and checked the six-vertex example. The protected catalogue marks #76 proved. ## What remains

A bounded successor is to formulate the exact finite triangle-packing integer program and verify the two-clique construction for small even $n$, keeping each edge in at most one chosen triangle. A separate source audit can map the integral asymptotic theorem to the fractional relaxation in the paper. Computations on small graphs test semantics only; they cannot certify the asymptotic theorem.
