## The question in plain language

Euler's totient function $\phi(n)$ counts the positive integers up to $n$ which are coprime to $n$. The sum-of-divisors function $\sigma(m)$ adds all positive divisors of $m$. [Erdős Problem #48](https://www.erdosproblems.com/48) asks whether there are **infinitely many pairs of positive integers** with

$$
\phi(n)=\sigma(m).
$$

The answer is **yes**, proved by Kevin Ford, Florian Luca and Carl Pomerance in 2010.

## A small example

There is an exact small equality,

$$
\phi(5)=4=1+3=\sigma(3).
$$

Thus $(n,m)=(5,3)$ works. Checking a handful of examples shows that the two arithmetic functions can agree, but does not prove infinitely many distinct solutions.

## What is known

Ford, Luca and Pomerance, *Common values of the arithmetic functions $\phi$ and $\sigma$*, *Bulletin of the London Mathematical Society* **42** (2010), 478–488, proved infinitely many solutions. Their paper also establishes much stronger multiplicity phenomena for common values. Subsequent work by Garaev sharpened quantitative counts reported in the maintained problem registry.

These results rely on deeper information about primes, not just the multiplicative formulas for the two arithmetic functions.

## GCL's current state

[Source audit #1317](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1317#issuecomment-6133363144) checked the published primary abstract, the finite witness, and the exact pinned Formal Conjectures claim. The frozen research theorem states infinitude of the set of pairs with equal totient and divisor sum, but its own proof body is unfilled. The 2010 analytic proof has not been GCL-replayed or formally certified.

## What remains

The mathematical infinitude question is settled. Formalization work may implement exact finite witnesses and import or reconstruct the published analytic proof with source and axiom controls. It would not be a new solution.
