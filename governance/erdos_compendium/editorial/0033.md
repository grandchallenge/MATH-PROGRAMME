## The question in plain language

An *additive complement to the squares* is a set $A$ such that every sufficiently large integer can be written as $a+m^2$ for some $a\in A$ and integer $m\ge0$. [Erdős Problem #33](https://www.erdosproblems.com/33) asks for the best possible constants governing the size of a very sparse such $A$, expressed by the limsup and liminf of its counting function divided by $\sqrt N$.

The source statement requires representation of **all sufficiently large** integers. The frozen Lean helper instead asks for a representation of **every** nonnegative integer. These properties are not equivalent for a fixed set, although finite changes to $A$ can preserve the asymptotic optimization.

## A small example

If $A$ represents every integer $k\ge K$ as $a+m^2$, adjoin the finite set $F=\{0,1,\ldots,K-1\}$ and put $B=A\cup F$. All previously representable integers remain covered; each smaller $k$ is represented by $a=k$ and $m=0$. Thus $B$ covers every nonnegative integer.

The counting functions of $A$ and $B$ differ by at most $K$, and

$$
\frac{K}{\sqrt N}\longrightarrow0.
$$

So this repair does not alter either asymptotic normalized limsup or liminf. It does **not** mean that the original $A$ already covered every integer.

## What is known

The maintained problem page records an open optimization and attributes finite upper constructions and lower bounds to the literature, including later work by van Doorn. The [GCL audit](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1302#issuecomment-6104178767) did not independently inspect the relevant primary pages, so no optimal numerical constant is asserted here.

## GCL's current state

The frozen source file uses a stronger all-integers predicate than the historical eventual-cover formulation and contains unfilled research proofs. [Audit #1302](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1302#issuecomment-6104178767) proves the finite-augmentation bridge and identifies the asymptotic-normalization assumptions. It supplies no proof of the optimal constant.

## What remains

Determine the optimal normalized counting-function bounds for genuine square-complement sets. Formal source comparisons must keep the fixed-set distinction intact, even though adding finitely many elements leaves the asymptotic constants unchanged.
