## The question in plain language

For a fixed positive integer $N$, choose any subset of the denominators $1,2,\ldots,N$ and add their reciprocals. Which positive integers can occur? Erdős Problem 308 asks for the first positive integer that cannot occur. If

$$
R_N=\left\{k\in\mathbb Z_{>0}: k=\sum_{n\in A}\frac1n\text{ for some }A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}\right\},
$$

then the least missing integer is

$$
f(N)=\min\{k\geq1:k\notin R_N\}.
$$

The second question asks whether $R_N$ is always an initial interval of positive integers: $\{1,\ldots,m\}$ for some $m$. The quantifiers matter. The classical result discussed below establishes this shape for all sufficiently large $N$; it does not, by itself, settle the same assertion for every $N\geq1$.

The natural comparison quantity is the harmonic sum $H_N=\sum_{n=1}^N1/n$ and its integer part $m_N=\lfloor H_N\rfloor$. The problem is about exact sums of distinct unit fractions, not arbitrary fractions, repeated denominators, or representations using denominators larger than $N$.

## A small example

Take $N=6$. Exact arithmetic gives

$$
H_6=1+\frac12+\frac13+\frac14+\frac15+\frac16=\frac{49}{20},
$$

so $m_6=2$. The integers $1$ and $2$ are represented: $1=1/1$, and

$$
2=1+\frac12+\frac13+\frac16.
$$

Because every subset sum is between $0$ and $H_6<3$, no positive integer other than $1$ or $2$ can occur. Thus an exact enumeration of the $2^6=64$ subsets gives $R_6=\{1,2\}$ and $f(6)=3=m_6+1$. A reproducible exact check is:

```python
from fractions import Fraction

N = 6
sums = {Fraction(0)}
for d in range(1, N + 1):
    sums |= {s + Fraction(1, d) for s in list(sums)}
assert {s.numerator for s in sums if s.denominator == 1 and s > 0} == {1, 2}
assert sum((Fraction(1, d) for d in range(1, N + 1)), Fraction(0)) == Fraction(49, 20)
```

This verifies one finite instance and illustrates why the answer can be $m_N+1$. It proves neither the eventual theorem nor the stronger assertion for all values of $N$.

## What is known

The live problem record attributes the essential resolution to Ernest S. Croot III, *On some questions of Erdős and Graham about Egyptian fractions*, *Mathematika* 46 (1999), 359–372. The paper’s main theorem, on the second page of the accessible author copy, uses the largest integer $\eta(N)$ such that every integer from $1$ through $\eta(N)$ is representable. In the notation above, $\eta(N)=f(N)-1$. Croot gives two asymptotic bounds with correction size $(\log\log N)^2/\log N$:

$$
\left\lfloor H_N-\frac92(1+o(1))\frac{(\log\log N)^2}{\log N}\right\rfloor
\leq \eta(N) \leq
\left\lfloor H_N-\frac12(1+o(1))\frac{(\log\log N)^2}{\log N}\right\rfloor.
$$

Translating from $\eta$ to the least missing integer adds one to both bounds. Since the correction tends to zero, the endpoint $\eta(N)$ is eventually either $m_N-1$ or $m_N$, and therefore $f(N)$ is eventually either $m_N$ or $m_N+1$. By the definition of the first missing integer, this also says that $R_N$ is eventually either $\{1,\ldots,m_N-1\}$ or $\{1,\ldots,m_N\}$.

There is a genuine source-normalization issue here. The maintained page and its LaTeX transcription display Croot’s inequalities directly with $f(N)$ and no visible $+1$, while the latest FormalConjectures source documents that Croot bounds $\eta(N)=f(N)-1$ and restores the $+1$. This audit preserves that discrepancy rather than silently treating the two displays as identical. The primary paper is the authority to revisit if a line-by-line semantic adjudication is required.

## What remains

The classical eventual statement has a clear source path, while the variant for every value of $N$ remains a separate boundary. A bounded successor task should first freeze the corrected normalization: define $R_N$, $f(N)$, and $\eta(N)=f(N)-1$ with exact finite sums; then prove the elementary equivalence between an initial segment $R_N=\{1,\ldots,m\}$ and the identity $f(N)=m+1$. The task can use Croot’s published theorem as an external premise and should not imply that its analytic proof has been formalized.

Acceptance should require the exact quantifiers, an explicit comparison with the primary paper’s $\eta$, and finite checks such as $R_6=\{1,2\}$. Falsification would be any counterexample to the stated finite lemma, any missing $+1$ in the translation, or any claim that the eventual result settles all $N$. Stop after the source-normalized interface and finite checks are recorded; do not spend the bounded task on an exhaustive Lean build or on a new mathematical certification claim.
