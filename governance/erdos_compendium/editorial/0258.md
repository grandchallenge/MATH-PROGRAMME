## The question in plain language

Let $a_1,a_2,\ldots$ be positive integers whose values eventually exceed every fixed bound; in symbols, $a_n\to\infty$. Consider the positive series

$$
S(a)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\tau(n)}{a_1a_2\cdots a_n},
$$

where $\tau(n)$ is the number of positive divisors of $n$. The problem asks whether $S(a)$ must always be irrational. The quantifier is important: this is one assertion about **every** positive-integer sequence tending to infinity, not merely about a convenient sequence such as $a_n=n+1$.

The condition $a_n\to\infty$ makes the series converge. After some index all $a_n$ are at least $2$, while $\tau(n)$ grows much more slowly than an exponential; the finite initial segment cannot affect convergence or the rational-versus-irrational nature of the resulting value. The sequence need not be monotone: it may go up and down, provided it eventually dominates every fixed integer.

## A small example

For the simple monotone choice $a_n=n+1$, the first four terms are

$$
\frac{\tau(1)}2+\frac{\tau(2)}{2\cdot3}+\frac{\tau(3)}{2\cdot3\cdot4}+\frac{\tau(4)}{2\cdot3\cdot4\cdot5}
=\frac12+\frac13+\frac1{12}+\frac1{40}.
$$

This is only a beginning of an infinite sum; a decimal approximation or a finite partial sum cannot decide irrationality.

The audited RESULT/1 also records a finite check that illustrates the divisor estimate used by the reported unrestricted argument. At $N=36$, direct factorisation verifies

$$
\tau(36+k)\leq 2^k\qquad(1\leq k\leq30).
$$

For example, $37$ is prime, so $\tau(37)=2$; $38=2\cdot19$, so $\tau(38)=4$; $40=2^3\cdot5$, so $\tau(40)=8$; and $60=2^2\cdot3\cdot5$, so $\tau(60)=12\leq2^{24}$. The whole finite assertion is independently reproducible with:

~~~python
from math import isqrt

def tau(n):
    return sum(1 if d*d == n else 2
               for d in range(1, isqrt(n) + 1) if n % d == 0)

assert all(tau(36 + k) <= 2**k for k in range(1, 31))
~~~

This check is deliberately modest. It does not establish the required assertion for infinitely many starting points $N$, and it is not a substitute for the analytic theorem below.

## What is known

There are three different layers of status.

First, the classical fixed-base case is proved. Erdős showed in 1948 that the Lambert series

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\tau(n)}{t^n}
$$

is irrational for every integer $t$ with $|t|>1$. The positive case $t\geq2$ is the part relevant here.

Second, Erdős and Straus proved the monotone case in Theorem 2.23 of their 1971 paper: if $2\leq a_1\leq a_2\leq\cdots$, then $S(a)$ is irrational. On the following pages they explicitly stated the unrestricted $a_n\to\infty$ assertion as Conjecture 2.24. Thus the 1971 paper is a classical proof of a substantial special case, not a proof of the full quantifier in Problem #258.

Third, the unrestricted case now has an external proof report. Tao and Teräväinen’s Theorem 1.1 gives an absolute constant $C>0$ and infinitely many positive integers $N$ such that

$$
\Omega(N+k)\leq Ck\qquad\text{for every }k\geq1,
$$

where $\Omega$ counts prime factors with multiplicity. Since $\tau(m)\leq2^{\Omega(m)}$, these same $N$ satisfy $\tau(N+k)\leq2^{Ck}$. Chojecki’s April 2026 note observes that this is exactly the tail control needed: if a rational value were assumed, clearing the first $N$ denominators would produce a positive integer smaller than $1/2$ once the later $a_n$ are sufficiently large. The note states the resulting theorem for every positive sequence with $a_n\to\infty$.

Accordingly, the original unrestricted question is not described here as currently unresolved in the external literature. The reported proof is not a GCL-original result. Related Erdős–Straus questions involving $\phi(n)$ or $\sigma(n)$ under monotonicity remain separate conjectural questions and should not be folded into #258.
