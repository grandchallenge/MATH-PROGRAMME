## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1160](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1160). The number of abstract groups of a given finite order can grow enormously. Are powers of two extremal among all smaller orders?

$$
g(n)=\#\{\text{isomorphism classes of groups of order }n\},\quad g(n)\le g(2^m)\quad(n\le2^m)?
$$

Let g(n) be the number of isomorphism classes of groups of EXACT order n (not subgroups of a fixed symmetric group). For every integer m≥0 and every 1≤n≤2^m ask whether g(n)≤g(2^m). A stronger distinct conjecture asks ∑_{1≤n<2^m}g(n)≤g(2^m) for sufficiently large m (perhaps m≥7). Quantifier is for ALL n up to the power of two; proving only odd-order case is not enough.

## A small example

Small group classification gives g(1)=g(2)=g(3)=g(5)=g(7)=1, g(4)=g(6)=2, g(8)=5; hence for 1≤n≤8 all g(n)≤g(8)=5. This finite known-order table is not a new classification nor theorem for arbitrary m. Stronger SUM version does not follow from the pointwise inequality.
