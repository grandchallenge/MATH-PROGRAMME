## The question in plain language

For which positive integers $n$ can one find **$n$ points in the real plane** such that every pairwise distance is an integer, **no three points are collinear**, and **no four lie on one circle**? These are *integral point sets in general position*. The requirement is the integrality of distances, not coordinates.

The original Erdős question asks whether such configurations exist for arbitrarily large $n$. It is closely related to the classical Anning–Erdős theorem, which forbids an **infinite** integral-distance set unless all its points are collinear.

## A small example

The points $(0,0)$, $(3,0)$ and $(0,4)$ have pairwise distances $3,4,5$ and are not collinear. With only three points, the no-four-concyclic condition is vacuous. Adding a fourth point with integral distances is not enough: one must also check that no triple becomes collinear and no four form a cyclic quadrilateral.

The squared-distance test is necessary but insufficient. If a squared distance is an integer that is not a perfect square, the Euclidean distance is irrational, not integral.

## What is known

**OPEN.** Harborth exhibited five points meeting the requirements; Kreisel and Kurz (2008) found an example with **seven** points, the largest cited on the maintained [Erdős #213](https://www.erdosproblems.com/213). Anning and Erdős (1945) ruled out infinite noncollinear sets of integral distances. That theorem does not imply a *uniform bound* on the sizes of **finite** examples.

Ascher, Braune and Turchet (2020) established a uniform upper bound **assuming Bombieri–Lang**. Greenfeld, Iliopoulou and Peluse (2024) proved unconditionally that an integral-distance set $S\subseteq[-N,N]^2$ in general position satisfies

$$
|S|\ll(\log N)^{O(1)}.
$$

This controls cardinality in a **bounded box**, not the overall maximum over all possible scales. Scaling a fixed integral point set to fit a larger box changes $N$, so the result does not settle existence for arbitrarily large $n$.

The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1482#issuecomment-6180319898). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded task takes the seven-point Kreisel–Kurz source coordinates and checks every unordered pair distance, every triple orientation determinant and every four-point incircle determinant with exact arithmetic. Acceptance: all pair distances are positive integers, no triple determinant vanishes, no four-point incircle determinant vanishes, and the coordinate scaling is documented. Do not treat this finite witness as a uniform family.
