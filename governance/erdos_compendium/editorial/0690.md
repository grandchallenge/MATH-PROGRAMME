## The question in plain language

For a fixed index k, let d_k(p) be the natural density of positive integers whose k-th smallest **distinct** prime divisor equals prime p. Erdős asked whether p↦d_k(p) is unimodal: nondecreasing up to one peak, then nonincreasing. The maintained catalogue records the general question as SOLVED in the sense that uniform unimodality is false, while it is true for k=1,2,3. This is an open-ended status, not a proof that every larger k has been classified by the published paper.

## A small example

Exact elementary prime-divisibility density: d_1(2)=1/2, d_1(3)=(1/3)(1−1/2)=1/6 and d_1(5)=(1/5)(1−1/2)(1−1/3)=1/15. This shows the first few values decrease for k=1. For k=2, d_2(3)=1/6 and d_2(5)=1/10, but finite examples alone do not prove unimodality of every prime index.

## What is known

For primes q<p, divisibility indicators are independent in natural density by the Chinese remainder theorem. Thus d_k(p)=p^(−1) times the coefficient of z^(k−1) in ∏_{q<p}[(1−1/q)+z/q]. This exact finite polynomial expression is a useful implementation-independent way to compute rational values. Stijn Cambie's PRIMARY 2026 Journal of Number Theory paper, 'Resolution of Erdős' problems about unimodularity', volume 280, pp.271–277, DOI 10.1016/j.jnt.2025.08.014, says unimodality holds for k=1,2,3 and supplies explicit non-unimodality for k=4,…,20. A separate later preprint claims all k≥4 but was not primary-refereed or independently proof-replayed in this tranche, and should not be silently upgraded into the published result. The single counterexample at k=4 already settles the broad 'for fixed k, is it always unimodal?' question negatively.
