## The question in plain language

Erdős and Szekeres ask whether, whenever 1≤i<j≤n/2, the common divisor of the two binomial coefficients C(n,i) and C(n,j) has a **prime factor at least i**. This is [Erdős Problem #699](https://www.erdosproblems.com/699), listed as **FALSIFIABLE** rather than resolved. The strict condition p>i is a different historical strengthening.

## A small example

For n=28,i=5,j=14, exact arithmetic gives gcd(C(28,5),C(28,14))=1080=2³·3³·5. The largest prime factor is p=5, equal to i. Thus the weak p≥i condition is satisfied, while p>i fails. This is an example of why the inequality sign cannot be changed. A finite check for n≤60 found no violation of the weak condition, but this is not a proof.

## What is known

Sylvester–Schur ensures that C(n,i) has a prime divisor greater than i for i≤n/2. It does not imply that this same prime also divides C(n,j). The [GCL-pinned formal statement](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/699.lean) distinguishes the main weak condition from a strict finite-exception variant. Its main formal proposition still contains a proof hole. No complete formal certificate is available from that pinned file.
