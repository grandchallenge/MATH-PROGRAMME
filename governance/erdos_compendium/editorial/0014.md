## The question in plain language

Given a set $A$ of nonnegative integers, how often can an integer $n$ have **exactly one** representation as a sum $a+b$ with $a,b\in A$? The pair is **unordered**, so $a+b$ and $b+a$ are the same representation, and repeated summands are allowed. Write $r_A(n)$ for the number of such pairs and

$$
F_A(N)=\#\{1\le n\le N:r_A(n)\ne1\}.
$$

[Erdős #14](https://www.erdosproblems.com/14) asks two asymptotic questions about how few exceptions are possible, involving lower bounds near $N^{1/2}$ and whether some single set has $F_A(N)=o(\sqrt N)$. Both remain open in the inspected record. The two possibilities are **not logically contradictory**: a function like $\sqrt N/\log(N+2)$ is simultaneously smaller than $\sqrt N$ in relative terms and larger than $N^{1/2-\varepsilon}$ up to constants for any fixed positive $\varepsilon$.

## A small example

For $A=\{1,2,4\}$, the unordered pair sums with repetition are

$$
1+1=2,\quad1+2=3,\quad2+2=4,\quad
1+4=5,\quad2+4=6,\quad4+4=8.
$$

Each listed integer has exactly one representation; 7 has none and is therefore an exception. In general the exceptions include both **missing sums** and sums with **two or more** different representations.

## What is known

Representations can be computed by a finite convolution. Let $x_j$ be the indicator of $j\in A$, $c_A(n)=\sum_{j=0}^n x_jx_{n-j}$ count **ordered** pairs, and let $d_A(n)=1$ if $n$ is even and $n/2\in A$ (zero otherwise). Then

$$
2r_A(n)=c_A(n)+d_A(n).
$$

Non-diagonal unordered pairs contribute twice to $c_A(n)$, while diagonal pairs contribute once. This gives an exact finite optimization and verification interface, not an asymptotic solution.

The literature includes older Erdős–Freud investigations of Sidon sets and unique sums. Subsequent finite computational reports must be distinguished from a construction of **one infinite set** that meets an asymptotic requirement at every sufficiently large cutoff. Optimizers for separate finite cutoffs need not fit together.

## GCL's current state

[Source audit #1283](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1283#issuecomment-6103876225) checks unordered-pair and diagonal semantics against the pinned formal helper, supplies exact finite convolution and prefix-restriction identities, and records primary-source attribution limits. It also proves that shifting a nonnegative set by one translates pair-sum representations with a controlled finite change in the exception count. These are elementary interfaces, not MATHCERT proofs of either open assertion.

## What remains

One direction requires a genuine universal lower bound for exceptions with its quantifiers and dependence on $A$ stated correctly. The other seeks **one fixed infinite set** with exception count little-oh of $\sqrt N$. Finite searches and convolution identities alone supply neither.
