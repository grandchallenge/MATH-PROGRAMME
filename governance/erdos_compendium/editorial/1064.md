## The question in plain language

Euler's totient function $\phi(n)$ counts the integers from $1$ to $n$ that are relatively prime to $n$. Compare the original value with the totient of the smaller integer $n-\phi(n)$.

[Erdős Problem #1064](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1064) asks for two apparently contrasting facts:

$$
\phi(n)>\phi(n-\phi(n))
\quad\text{for almost all }n,
$$

yet

$$
\phi(n)<\phi(n-\phi(n))
\quad\text{for infinitely many }n.
$$

Here “almost all” means that the proportion of positive integers satisfying the first strict inequality approaches $1$ as the cutoff tends to infinity. A density-one statement can coexist with an infinite exceptional set.

## A small example

For $n=30$, we have $\phi(30)=8$, and $n-\phi(n)=22$, with $\phi(22)=10$. Thus $8<10$: $30$ is an exception to the inequality that normally holds.

There is an explicit **infinite** family of exceptions. For every integer $k\ge1$ take $n=15\cdot2^k$. Then

$$
\phi(15\cdot2^k)=4\cdot2^k,
\qquad
\phi(11\cdot2^k)=5\cdot2^k.
$$

Since $n-\phi(n)=11\cdot2^k$, the reversed strict inequality holds for every member of this unbounded family. These equalities follow directly from the totient formula for coprime prime powers and were independently checked at finite values of $k$.

A complete totient sieve for $1\le n\le200{,}000$ finds $194{,}833$ strict-greater cases, $175$ equalities and $4992$ strict-less cases. This finite frequency is **not** a proof that the limiting density is one.

## What is known

This problem is **proved** in the literature. Luca and Pomerance, *On some problems of Mąkowski–Schinzel and Erdős concerning the arithmetical functions $\phi$ and $\sigma$*, *Colloquium Mathematicum* **92** (2002), 111–130, prove the density-one inequality and a stronger result allowing a lower-order error term. Earlier work by Grytczuk, Luca and Wójtowicz established relevant intermediate results.

The original problem appeared as Erdős's P.294 in *Canadian Mathematical Bulletin* **23** (1980), page 505. Its full original scan was not independently checked in this audit, so historical exact-wording equivalence remains open for editorial review.
