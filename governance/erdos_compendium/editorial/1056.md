## The question in plain language

Suppose we arrange consecutive positive integers in a row and divide them into adjacent, nonempty blocks. Can we choose a prime number $p$ so that the product of the integers in **each** block leaves remainder $1$ on division by $p$?

[Erdős Problem #1056](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1056) asks whether this is possible for **every** number $k\ge 2$ of blocks. We may choose a different prime and different boundaries for each $k$. Knowing how to construct two or three blocks is not enough.

## A small example

For two blocks, take $p=11$ and consecutive intervals $\{3,4\}$ and $\{5,6,7\}$. Their products are

$$
3\cdot4=12\equiv1\pmod{11},
\qquad 5\cdot6\cdot7=210\equiv1\pmod{11}.
$$

For three blocks, a historical example takes $p=17$ and intervals $[2,5]$, $[6,11]$ and $[12,15]$, inclusive of integer endpoints. Their products are $120$, $332640$ and $32760$, each congruent to $1$ modulo $17$. All five congruences were independently recomputed using exact integer arithmetic in the [bounded test program](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/blob/editorial/ec-bulk-007-168/ci/erdos_compendium_007_finite_checks.py). These finite examples do not establish the universal conjecture.

## What is known

Guy's *Unsolved Problems in Number Theory*, problem A15, records Erdős's 1979 example for two blocks. Andrzej Mąkowski published the three-block example in *Elemente der Mathematik* **38** (1983), pp. 101–102. The [primary bibliographic record](https://eudml.org/doc/141318) identifies the paper; its full printed text still requires a page-by-page source check.

The frozen catalogue labels the general problem **open**. Later discussion has reported additional finite cases; a list of isolated instances cannot settle a statement quantified over all $k$.

## GCL's current state

[Source audit #2327](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2327#issuecomment-6144495991) verifies the specific congruences and inspects the [frozen formal statement](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1056.lean). Lean represents adjacent blocks by strictly increasing boundaries and half-open intervals. It provides finite executable examples, but its universal existence theorem still contains an unproved placeholder. Formal statement metadata and these finite computations are not GCL certification.

There is a subtlety: a singleton block containing only $1$ automatically has product $1$. Whether such a trivial block is admissible under the original historical convention affects the counting of particular finite witnesses.

## What remains

The mathematical challenge is to find valid intervals for every $k$, or disprove existence for some $k$. The immediate source obligation is narrower: inspect Guy and Mąkowski in full, compare interval conventions with the Lean definition, and independently replay finite examples before claiming stronger source fidelity.
