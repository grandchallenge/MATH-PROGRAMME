**Editorial status:** UNACCEPTED_DRAFT. Erdős Problem #495 is the **OPEN** Littlewood conjecture. The theorem of Einsiedler, Katok and Lindenstrauss (2006) establishes that possible exceptions form a set of **Hausdorff dimension zero**, not that there are no exceptions.

## The question in plain language

How well can *two* real numbers be approximated by rational numbers sharing the same denominator? To measure nearness to an integer, write

$$
\|t\|=\min_{m\in\mathbb Z}|t-m|,
\qquad 0\le\|t\|\le\tfrac12.
$$

Littlewood's conjecture asserts that **for every pair** $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb R$,

$$
\boxed{\liminf_{n\to\infty} n\|n\alpha\|\|n\beta\|=0.}
$$

The limiting variable $n$ ranges over **positive integers**. The symbol $\liminf$ does not require that the ordinary limit exist or equal zero; it requires the product to become arbitrarily small at arbitrarily large denominators. The same $n$ multiplies $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

The central difficulty is that two individually good rational approximations may occur at *different* denominators. The result demands coordinated approximation in the much stronger product sense. The conjecture was posed by J. E. Littlewood around 1930 and recorded by Erdős in *Some unsolved problems* (1961), printed p.238, item I.34.

## A small example

Suppose $\alpha=\frac25$ and $\beta$ is any real number. Choose $n=5k$ for any positive integer $k$. Then $n\alpha=2k$ is exactly an integer, so

$$
n\|n\alpha\|\|n\beta\|=0
$$

for infinitely many arbitrarily large $n$. Thus the conjecture is immediate if **either** coordinate is rational. Nothing about the other coordinate matters.

More generally, since $\|n\beta\|\le1/2$, one always has the exact inequality

$$
0\le n\|n\alpha\|\|n\beta\|
\le \frac n2\,\|n\alpha\|.
$$

Consequently, if $\liminf_{n\to\infty}n\|n\alpha\|=0$, the Littlewood product also has liminf zero, regardless of $\beta$. The same argument works after swapping $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

A real number $\alpha$ is *badly approximable* precisely when

$$
\inf_{n\ge1}n\|n\alpha\|>0.
$$

Therefore a counterexample to Littlewood must have **both** coordinates badly approximable. Irrational numbers with bounded continued-fraction partial quotients are exactly those with this property.

The original 1961 paper explicitly identifies $(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$ as a difficult example. Numerical exploration of such a pair, even to extremely large $n$, cannot prove that the liminf is either zero or positive.

## What is known

### Littlewood's question in the primary source

The original Erdős (1961) *Some unsolved problems*, p.238, item I.34, states the nearest-integer norm and the two-variable liminf exactly, and identifies the case $\alpha=\sqrt2$, $\beta=\sqrt3$ as exceptionally difficult. The original 34-page paper was retrieved into the authenticated CHAD environment and printed p.238 was text-extracted and checked. The maintained [#495 record](https://www.erdosproblems.com/495) preserves the **OPEN** classification.

### The strong modern partial result

Manfred Einsiedler, Anatole Katok and Elon Lindenstrauss, *Invariant measures and the set of exceptions to Littlewood's conjecture*, *Annals of Mathematics* **164** (2006), 513–560, proved a remarkable theorem. Define

$$
\Xi=\left\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb R^2:
\liminf_{n\to\infty}n\|n\alpha\|\|n\beta\|>0\right\}.
$$

Their Theorem 1.5, on printed p.516, proves

$$
\dim_{\mathrm H}\Xi=0.
$$

The authors strengthen this by showing that $\Xi$ is a **countable union of compact sets of box dimension zero**. These statements were inspected on the actual [journal PDF](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v164-n2-p04.pdf#page=4), including Conjecture 1.2 on p.515 and Theorem 1.5 on p.516.

Their argument combines entropy rigidity and dynamics on the homogeneous space of unimodular lattices, and is an outstanding partial result. It does **not** say that $\Xi$ is empty. Hausdorff dimension zero sets can be nonempty or even infinite. Consequently, the full Littlewood conjecture remains open, despite the very severe restriction on possible counterexamples.

The same journal paper recalls earlier special cases for pairs lying in the same cubic number field and discusses the badly approximable setting. These do not supply a proof for arbitrary pairs of quadratic irrationals.

## What remains

The genuinely unresolved question is whether **every pair of badly approximable real numbers** satisfies the Littlewood liminf relation. An independent resolution would either show $\Xi$ is empty or construct an explicit pair in $\Xi$ and prove a strictly positive lower bound uniformly in all positive integers.

An appropriate bounded next task is to prove the rational-coordinate special case with exact fractions, verify that $\|t\|\le1/2$ gives the one-coordinate squeeze inequality, and produce a theorem-faithful formal statement. The acceptance tests are algebraic; a finite numerical search cannot certify a liminf for an infinite sequence.
