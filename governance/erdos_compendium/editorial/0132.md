## The question in plain language

Choose $n$ distinct planar points. For each positive distance $d$ occurring between pairs of the points, let $m(d)$ count its multiplicity among **unordered pairs**. Call a distance *low-multiplicity* when

$$
1\le m(d)\le n.
$$

Erdős and Pach asked whether there must always be **at least two** such distances, and whether the number of low-multiplicity distances necessarily grows without bound as $n$ tends to infinity. These are different strengths of the same question.

## A small example

For four points forming the union of two equilateral triangles glued along one common side, there is a configuration with only one distance whose multiplicity is at most four. Thus the first question cannot hold without an exceptional small-size restriction. The source conjecture asks about all sufficiently large $n$, and its finite exceptions must not be silently ignored.

The **diameter** is the largest distance between any two points. Hopf and Pannwitz proved that the diameter can occur at most $n$ times, giving at least **one** low-multiplicity distance universally. Their theorem does not guarantee a second.

## What is known

The original universal two-distance assertion remains **OPEN** for general planar sets. Erdős and Fishburn (1995) proved it for $n=5,6$. More recently, Friedrich Clemen, Adrian Dumitrescu and Ding Liu, [*On multiplicities of interpoint distances*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10474-025-01562-y), *Acta Mathematica Hungarica* 177 (2025), 231–245, prove that a second low-multiplicity distance always exists when the points are in convex position or satisfy an explicit weaker “not too convex” condition. The primary publisher abstract directly confirms the two restricted cases.

That theorem is **not** a general solution, nor does it settle the stronger divergence of the number of such distances. The same paper gives examples with highly uneven distance multiplicities, illustrating why arguments from a handful of popular distances alone may fail.

## GCL's current state

The pinned and [maintained #132](https://www.erdosproblems.com/132) keep the universal question **OPEN**. Its original source issue is [#1401](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1401). The 2025 primary result and exact restriction to convex or “not too convex” point sets have been checked at publisher-abstract level; the detailed geometric proof was not independently reconstructed.

The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1401#issuecomment-6165454455). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded fixture should enumerate squared distances for rational point sets of sizes four to eight, grouping unordered pairs by exact squared lengths. Acceptance: the diameter class has multiplicity at most $n$, the program reports the number of distance classes with multiplicities between one and $n$, and a separately constructed four-point example demonstrates why no stronger unconditional four-point assertion is valid. Stop before generalizing a finite pattern or a restricted convex theorem to every planar set.
