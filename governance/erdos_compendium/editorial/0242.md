## The question in plain language

The **Erdős–Straus conjecture** asks whether every integer $n>2$ admits three **distinct positive** denominators $x<y<z$ satisfying

$$
\frac4n=\frac1x+\frac1y+\frac1z.
$$

This is an exact rational identity, not a decimal approximation. The strict order $x<y<z$ is part of the catalogue's statement; allowing repeated denominators changes the formulation.

## A small example

For $n=5$, the denominators $2,5,10$ give

$$
\frac45=\frac12+\frac15+\frac1{10}.
$$

A finite search can select $x$ and $y$, solve algebraically for $z$, then demand a **positive integer** satisfying $z>y$. All identities should be checked by integer cross multiplication. A negative, fractional or repeated denominator is not a valid witness.

## What is known

**OPEN.** The maintained [Erdős #242](https://www.erdosproblems.com/242), updated May 2026, reports computational verification for all $n\le10^{18}$ (Mihăilescu–Du 2025). Even this enormous finite range is **not** a proof for all positive integers. It suffices to prove the conjecture for **prime** denominators $n$, but infinitely many primes remain.

Obláth and Mordell established numerous congruence families with solutions; Mordell reduced possible exceptions to six residue classes modulo 840. Vaughan proved exceptional integers are sparse; Elsholtz–Tao investigated numbers of representations. None of these results eliminates every possible exception.

The pinned descriptor **FALSIFIABLE** does not mean a counterexample has been found. The mathematical conjecture is still open and no certified counterexample was identified.

## What remains

A bounded successor searches every integer $3\le n\le500$, returns exact ordered triples, and checks all identities without floating-point arithmetic. Acceptance: every reported witness has distinct positive denominators and equality under cross multiplication; finite success must be labelled **finite verification only**. The actual research obligation remains the infinite family of uncovered prime cases.
