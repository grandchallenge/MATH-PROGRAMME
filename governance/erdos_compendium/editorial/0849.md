## The question in plain language

Binomial coefficients can occur in multiple rows of Pascal's triangle. Once column symmetry is removed, do any coefficients occur arbitrarily many times? Concrete multiplicities three and four are known; no examples with multiplicity five are known.

For positive integer t ask whether there exists an integer a attained by binomial(n,k) in EXACTLY t ordered (n,k) pairs with 1<=k<=n/2. This symmetry-normalized domain counts each pair in Pascal's triangle only once, and includes the k=1 boundary. The main existential every-t formulation is OPEN; Singmaster conjectured a UNIVERSAL bound on the multiplicity, which would make the original answer NO for sufficiently large t.

## A small example

For a=120 the three counted pairs are (10,3),(16,2),(120,1). For a=3003 the four pairs are (14,6),(15,5),(78,2),(3003,1). These can be verified exactly and show why k=1 boundary matters.

This finite calculation tests definitions, not a result quantified over all large N or infinite sets.

## What is known

Maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/849 read 2026-10-14 credits Erdős/Gordon and Singmaster. Counts t=3 at a=120 and t=4 at a=3003; no t>=5 examples known. Matomäki–Radziwiłł–Shao–Tao–Teräväinen [MRSTT22], *Singmaster's conjecture in the interior of Pascal's triangle*, Quarterly J Math 2022 1137–1177, prove at most two when k>=exp((log n)^(2/3+epsilon)) and a sufficiently large depending on epsilon. Restricted interior theorem does not settle k small/boundary or all t.

The convention counts integer pairs $(n,k)$ satisfying $1\leq k\leq n/2$ and $\binom{n}{k}=a$, including $k=1$. For $a=120$ the three pairs are $(10,3),(16,2),(120,1)$. For $a=3003$ the four are $(14,6),(15,5),(78,2),(3003,1)$. Removing $k=1$ would invalidate the quoted multiplicities. The interior-of-Pascal theorem applies only to its specified lower restriction on k, not to every pair in the triangle.
