## The question in plain language

Colour each integer from $1$ to $N$. Erdős and Freud asked how few colours can suffice if **every four-term arithmetic progression** must use at least **three distinct colours**.

Let $h(N)$ be the minimum number of colours meeting that condition. A progression has the form

$$
a,\ a+d,\ a+2d,\ a+3d,
\qquad d\ge1,\quad a+3d\le N.
$$

The four entries may use three or four colours, but **never only one or two**. This condition is stronger than forbidding monochromatic four-term progressions and should not be confused with van der Waerden's theorem.

## A small example

In $\{1,2,3,4\}$ the only four-term progression is $(1,2,3,4)$. It must have at least three colours. For $N\le3$ no four-term progression exists, so one colour suffices. Therefore $h(4)=3$.

For $N=5$, the progressions include $(1,2,3,4)$ and $(2,3,4,5)$. A colouring assigning three different colours to positions $2,3,4$ and reusing them at the endpoints satisfies both constraints, so $h(5)\le3$. One colour on the entire interval is forbidden as soon as $N\ge4$.

## What is known

The sharp growth remains **OPEN**. The maintained [Erdős #160](https://www.erdosproblems.com/160) attributes the earlier upper estimate $h(N)\ll N^{2/3}$ to a MathOverflow argument, and the stronger bound

$$
h(N)\ll N^{\log 3/\log 22+o(1)}
$$

to Zach Hunter's October 2025 observation, building on the methods of Deng, Tidor and Zhao in [arXiv:2307.06914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06914). Since $\log3/\log22\approx0.3554$, this improves the older exponent.

The same source records the lower bound

$$
h(N)\gg\exp\bigl(c(\log N)^{1/9}\bigr)
$$

for some $c>0$, via Hunter's observation combined with modern results on sets avoiding three-term progressions, including work of Bloom–Sisask and Kelley–Meka. Both bounds tend to infinity but are far apart; they do not establish an exact power law. The reduction details in the external MathOverflow commentary have not been independently written out here.

## GCL's current state

The pinned and current catalogue classify the problem **OPEN**. Original issue: [#1429](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1429). The exact four-term/all-progression quantifier and no-two-colours condition are preserved. The cited construction and modern lower-bound deductions are source-attributed, not new GCL proofs.

The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1429#issuecomment-6167540072). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded task models the colouring condition as a finite constraint satisfaction problem and searches exact minimum colourings for $4\le N\le14$. Acceptance: enumerate every step $d$ and start $a$, enforce at least three distinct colours, prove $h(4)=3$, and use a second checker to validate every purported witness and every impossibility certificate. Stop at finite enumeration and avoid treating finite-size patterns as an asymptotic law.
