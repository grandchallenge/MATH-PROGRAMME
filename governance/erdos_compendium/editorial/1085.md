## The question in plain language

Place $n$ distinct points anywhere in $d$‑dimensional space. How many pairs can be exactly one unit apart? [Erdős Problem #1085](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1085) asks for the maximum possible count $f_d(n)$.

There is **no restriction** that the other distances be at least one. That restriction belongs to the *contact-number* problem [#1084](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1084), not to this problem.

## A small example

Three vertices of an equilateral triangle of side one determine three unit-distance pairs. This is all $\binom32=3$ possible pairs, so $f_d(3)=3$ for every $d\ge2$.

Four corners of a unit square determine four unit-distance sides and two diagonals of length $\sqrt2$, yielding $f_2(4)\ge4$. Showing that four is optimal would require a separate argument; the square alone supplies a lower bound.

## What is known

**The general extremal problem remains open.** For the plane, the famous unit-distance upper bound is

$$
f_2(n)=O(n^{4/3}),
$$

from Spencer, Szemerédi and Trotter (1984). The maintained catalogue reports a new superlinear planar lower result attributed to an internal model at OpenAI, for an infinite sequence of sizes. That unusually attributed claim has **not** been independently source-verified here and should not be treated as a GCL-certified theorem.

In dimension three, the catalogue records estimates between approximately $n^{4/3}\log\log n$ and $n^{3/2}\beta(n)$, with $\beta$ slowly growing. For $d\ge4$, orthogonal-circle constructions give quadratic leading terms governed by $p=\lfloor d/2\rfloor$:

$$
f_d(n)=\frac{p-1}{2p}n^2+o(n^2)
$$

in the regimes covered by the cited extremal results. Even dimensions have sharper established formulas; odd dimensions contain correction terms.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source audit #2356](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2356#issuecomment-6162305404) compared the dimension-dependent statements and inspected the [frozen formal-conjectures definition](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1085.lean). That definition does not impose minimum separation, matching the essential difference from #1084.

Finite triangle and square examples have been checked. The full published incidence proofs, and especially the newer claimed lower result, have not undergone independent mathematical-source verification.

## What remains

Verify the original 1984 upper theorem, the higher-dimensional Lenz–Erdős constructions and the primary proof for any proposed new planar lower bound. Separate dimension-specific resolved cases from the still-open planar and spatial growth questions.
