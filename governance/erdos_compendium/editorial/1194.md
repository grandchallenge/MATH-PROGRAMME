## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1194](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1194) is **OPEN SHARP RATE** in the source as of 15 October 2026.

An infinite **perfect difference set** $A\subseteq\mathbb N$ uniquely represents every integer $n\ge1$ as $a_n-b_n$ for $a_n,b_n\in A$; uniqueness refers to ordered positive differences. Such an $A$ is Sidon, and $a_n$ is the **larger** element of the unique pair representing $n$, not the $n$ th enumerated member of $A$. Ask how fast $a_n/n$ must increase. Bounds asserted **infinitely often** differ sharply from a bound for **every** sufficiently large $n$.

## A small example

The four-point ruler $A=\{0,1,4,6\}$ has all **six** positive differences exactly once: differences $1,2,3,4,5,6$. It also has distinct unordered two-term sums, so is a finite Sidon set. This exact fixture illustrates uniqueness; it is **not** an infinite perfect difference set. The zero element can be shifted to make all elements positive without changing differences.

## What is known

The catalogue https://www.erdosproblems.com/1194 marks the sharp rate **OPEN**, referencing Erdős [Er80,p.100]. Greedy constructions, described by Lev [Le04], produce an infinite perfect difference set with $a_n\ll n^3$. Erdős established $\limsup a_n/n=\infty$ and the site derives $a_n\gg n\log n$ infinitely often from thin subsequences of infinite Sidon sets [HaRo66]. Cilleruelo–Nathanson [CiNa08] transfer denser Sidon constructions. A **2026 GPT-5.4 Pro argument in comments**, reported as $a_n\gg n^{2-o(1)}$ infinitely often (and a parameterized divergent-series variant), is external and requires independent proof verification; it does not settle the optimal achievable rate.
