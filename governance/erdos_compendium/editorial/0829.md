## The question in plain language

For A={positive perfect cubes}, define (1_A * 1_A)(n)=#{(a,b) in A²:a+b=n}; this is the ORDERED number of representations as two positive cubes, and includes a=b where applicable. Is this uniformly <=(log n)^C for some ABSOLUTE C and all sufficiently large n? OPEN.

## A small example

1729=1^3+12^3=9^3+10^3 yields FOUR ordered representations ((1,12),(12,1),(9,10),(10,9)), not two when using convolution. Brute force cubes up to 14 detects precisely this first familiar multiplicity example.

The example can be checked exactly. A finite witness does not replace the universal, asymptotic, or infinitely-often quantifiers in the original question.

## What is known

Maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/829 OPEN. Mordell proved unbounded limsup of representation count. Mahler [Ma35b] infinitely often >= const (log n)^(1/4), Stewart [St08] improves infinitely often to >=const (log n)^(11/13). Lower on a subsequence does not contradict any sufficiently large polylog UPPER. Original proofs not reproduced.

The convolution counts **ordered** pairs:

$$

(1_A*1_A)(N)
=\#\{(u,v)\in\mathbb N^2:u^3+v^3=N\}.

$$

For the taxi-cab integer,

$$

1729=1^3+12^3=9^3+10^3,

$$

the ordered count is four, not two. Known lower bounds showing unbounded representations, even as powers of $\log N$ on infinitely many inputs, do not disprove the desired upper bound by some other fixed logarithmic power.
