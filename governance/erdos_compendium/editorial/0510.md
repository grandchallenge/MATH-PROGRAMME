**Editorial status:** UNACCEPTED_DRAFT. Erdős Problem #510, **Chowla's cosine problem**, remains **OPEN** at its conjectured square-root exponent. Its literature has moved substantially since the pinned 2025 catalogue: Bedert's July 2026 revision improved the guaranteed exponent to $1/5-o(1)$, Abhishek Shankar's September 2026 preprint removes the subpolynomial loss, and a separate October 12, 2026 preprint improves the *construction-side leading constant* in the opposite direction. None of these results proves the proposed $\sqrt N$ lower bound.

## The question in plain language

Choose a set $A$ of $N$ **distinct positive integers**, and form the trigonometric sum

$$
F_A(\theta)=\sum_{a\in A}\cos(a\theta).
$$

At $\theta=0$, every cosine equals $1$, so $F_A(0)=N$. The difficult question is: **how negative must this sum become**, regardless of how cleverly the $N$ frequencies were chosen?

Define the optimal universal depth

$$
K(N)=\inf_{\substack{A\subset\mathbb Z_{>0}\\|A|=N}}
\left(-\min_{\theta\in[0,2\pi]}F_A(\theta)\right).
$$

Chowla conjectured that a universal constant $c>0$ exists such that

$$
\boxed{K(N)\ge c\sqrt N}
$$

for all sufficiently large $N$. Equivalently, each sufficiently large $N$‑element set of distinct positive frequencies has *some* angle for which $F_A(\theta)\le-c\sqrt N$.

The two directions matter. A **lower bound on $K(N)$** guarantees a negative excursion for **every** frequency set. An **upper bound on $K(N)$** constructs some especially well-behaved sets whose sums never become too negative. The latter can show that a conjectured exponent is the best one could hope for, but cannot establish that every other set behaves the same way.

A source-fidelity caveat: the maintained website sometimes phrases the statement with $A\subset\mathbb Z$, while the original and the author proofs work with **distinct positive frequencies**. Allowing both $a$ and $-a$ creates repeated cosine frequencies; allowing zero adds a constant term. These variants should not be identified without a proof of equivalence.

## A small example

For the singleton $A=\{m\}$ with $m>0$, the sum $\cos(m\theta)$ attains $-1$, so $K(1)=1$.

There is a much more informative family based on a **Sidon set**, a collection $B$ of integers with unique nonzero ordered differences. Take

$$
B_k=\{2^j-1:0\le j<k\},
$$

and let $A_k$ consist of all **positive differences** $b-b'$ between two distinct members of $B_k$. These differences are unique, so

$$
|A_k|=\binom{k}{2}.
$$

For example, $B_4=\{0,1,3,7\}$ gives $A_4=\{1,2,3,4,6,7\}$, with six distinct frequencies.

The key exact identity is

$$
\left|\sum_{b\in B_k}e^{ib\theta}\right|^2
=k+2\sum_{a\in A_k}\cos(a\theta).
$$

The expression on the left is **nonnegative** for every angle. Therefore

$$
F_{A_k}(\theta)\ge-\frac{k}{2}
\qquad\text{for every }\theta.
$$

Thus the optimal depth for the cardinality $N=\binom{k}{2}$ obeys

$$
K(N)\le\frac{k}{2}=O(\sqrt N).
$$

For arbitrary $N$, choose $k$ with $\binom{k}{2}\le N<\binom{k+1}{2}$ and add fewer than $k$ unused positive frequencies; each additional cosine is at least $-1$. This extends the constructive upper estimate to $K(N)=O(\sqrt N)$ for **all** $N$. It explains why Chowla's proposed exponent $1/2$ is sharp if it can be proved, without solving the conjecture.

## What is known

### Erdős, Chowla and the first bounds

In *Some unsolved problems* (1961), Section IV.2, [printed p.247](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1961-22.pdf#page=27) and [p.248](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1961-22.pdf#page=28), Erdős discusses a related Littlewood $L^1$ question and then Ankeny and Chowla's conjecture that cosine sums must achieve arbitrarily large negative magnitudes as the number of distinct frequencies grows. He records Chowla's observation that a Sidon-type construction makes the **square-root order** the natural conjectural endpoint. Both original pages were **visually inspected**.

The qualitative $K(N)\to\infty$ statement was established through work on the Littlewood conjecture. Later estimates grew from logarithmic to superlogarithmic scales. Imre Z. Ruzsa, *Negative values of cosine sums*, *Acta Arithmetica* (2004), 179–186, proved an estimate of the form

$$
K(N)\ge \exp(c\sqrt{\log N})
$$

for an absolute $c>0$. The complete Ruzsa original proof was not independently inspected here.

### The 2025–2026 polynomial breakthroughs

Benjamin Bedert, *Polynomial bounds for the Chowla Cosine Problem*, arXiv:2509.05260, introduced the first general polynomial negative-excursion estimates. Crucially, its **versions differ**.

The original [September 2025 version 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.05260v2), Theorem 1.1, establishes

$$
K(N)\ge N^{1/7-o(1)},
$$

with a shorter proof of a weaker $N^{1/12}$ bound. Its first two pages were read directly. The asymptotic $o(1)$ term **cannot be dropped** to obtain a fixed constant times $N^{1/7}$ without further argument.

Bedert's **24 July 2026 version 3**, reflected in the current [arXiv manuscript text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05260), improves this to

$$
K(N)\ge N^{1/5-o(1)}.
$$

The maintained [Erdős #510 catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/510), edited September 2025, still displays an exponent $1/7$ and the stronger-looking fixed-constant wording. The pinned Formal Conjectures file preserves the 2025-era assertion. Neither should replace the dated primary 2026 revision.

Abhishek Shankar, *A Log-Free $n^{1/5}$ Bound for Chowla's Cosine Problem* (4 September 2026), [arXiv:2609.05338](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05338), reports removing the subpolynomial loss:

$$
K(N)\ge cN^{1/5}
$$

for one absolute positive constant $c$. The primary author abstract confirms the exact claim, but the complete proof PDF was unavailable in this audit. This is therefore a **source-reported preprint theorem**, not independently replayed GCL mathematical certification.

### A new upper-bound construction dated October 12, 2026

Bryan Bu, Fayzan Khan, Hyunjun Yi, Atharv Jha, Vihaan Vajpeyi, Ishaan Chandupatla and Madhav Nagpal, *A Half-Constant Upper Bound for Chowla's Cosine Problem*, [arXiv:2610.17160v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.17160), state in Theorem 1.3 that

$$
K(N)\le\frac12\sqrt N+
O\!\left(N^{1/4}\sqrt{\log N}\right).
$$

Its complete preprint is available; the original title, abstract and Theorem 1.3 pages were visually checked. The authors use a **two-sided Sidon-square identity**, arithmetic difference-set constructions and a correction term to improve the leading constant from the earlier Sidon-difference value $1/\sqrt2$ along triangular cardinalities to $1/2$ uniformly up to a smaller-order error.

This October 2026 paper is a **recent preprint**, and GCL has not independently checked its complete construction proof or numerical artifacts. Its result is an **upper bound on $K(N)$**, not the conjectured lower bound, and it does not establish that every set experiences an excursion of order $\sqrt N$. The same paper also develops refinements of Bedert's estimates that recover the exponent $1/5$.

Together, the strongest source-reported 2026 picture is

$$
\boxed{cN^{1/5}\le K(N)\le
\frac12\sqrt N+O\!\left(N^{1/4}\sqrt{\log N}\right).}
$$

The left inequality is Shankar's reported theorem; the right is the newer Bu–Khan–Yi–Jha–Vajpeyi–Chandupatla–Nagpal reported theorem. Their **different directions** and verification levels must remain explicit.

## What remains

The principal open problem is to raise the **universal lower bound** from the current reported $N^{1/5}$ scale to the conjectured $\sqrt N$ scale. Improvements to constructive upper constants, however useful, do not by themselves reduce that exponent gap.

The immediate bounded source and mathematical tasks are to replay the exact finite Sidon construction; retrieve and critically review the complete September 2026 Shankar proof; and independently validate the new October 2026 construction and the claimed $1/2$ leading constant. A separate statement comparison is needed before equating the catalogue's general $A\subset\mathbb Z$ wording with the positive-frequency version used in these original papers.
