## The question in plain language

Let $p_n$ be the $n$‐th prime, indexed by $p_1=2$, and set

$$
u_n=\frac{p_n}{n}.
$$

Erdős and Prachar asked whether the indices where this normalized prime sequence **increases** form a set of **positive lower asymptotic density**:

$$
\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac1N
\#\left\{1\le n\le N:\frac{p_n}{n}<\frac{p_{n+1}}{n+1}\right\}>0.
$$

The original 1961/62 paper asks for *lower density*, not merely infinitely many increases or positive upper density. The maintained [Erdős Problem #968](https://www.erdosproblems.com/968), last edited 31 March 2026, is labeled **OPEN**, but this status now requires a prominent research-source exception: a **25 September 2026 OpenAI mathematics release claims an affirmative answer**, and an independent research corpus reports **replaying its matching Lean theorem with an axiom check**. The release is not known here to be refereed, and GCL has not performed its own pinned replay. The mathematically responsible description is therefore **catalogue OPEN; externally claimed and formally checked resolution, awaiting GCL adjudication**, not an unqualified "unsolved" label and not an automatic GCL certification.

## A small example

*A short exact calculation and the crucial equivalence*

For $n=2$, we have $p_2=3$ and $p_3=5$, so

$$
u_2=\frac32<\frac53=u_3.
$$

More generally write the consecutive prime gap as $g_n=p_{n+1}-p_n$. Clearing the **positive** denominators gives the exact arithmetic equivalence

$$
\frac{p_n}{n}<\frac{p_{n+1}}{n+1}
\iff (n+1)p_n<n p_{n+1}
\iff g_n>\frac{p_n}{n}.
$$

This is a *large-relative-to-average prime-gap* condition. The prime number theorem yields

$$
p_n\sim n\log n,\qquad
\frac{p_n}{n}\sim \log p_n.
$$

Therefore a uniform positive proportion of gaps larger than $2\log p_n$ at all sufficiently large index cutoffs would imply the desired positive lower density. It is **not** enough that arbitrarily large gaps exist, or that a zero-density subsequence has enormously large gaps.

## What is known

*Earlier established partial results*

Erdős and Prachar, *Sätze und Probleme über $p_k/k$*, *Abhandlungen aus dem Mathematischen Seminar der Universität Hamburg* **25** (1961/62), pp.251–256, proved

$$
\sum_{p_n<x}\left|u_{n+1}-u_n\right|\asymp (\log x)^2
$$

and that the *opposite* set $\{n:u_n>u_{n+1}\}$ has positive lower density. Their final-page question about the increasing steps, reported in [Er65b] on pp.203–204, was not thereby settled. A sum of absolute increments can be concentrated on a thin collection of exceptionally large increments, so the total-variation estimate alone does not prove that the count of increasing steps is $\gg N$.

Goldston–Pintz–Yıldırım proved a positive-density theorem for sufficiently small normalized prime gaps. This addresses a different side of the distribution. Terence Tao's September 2025 discussion explains why a first-moment balance argument cannot directly turn many small gaps into many *moderately large* gaps: a very sparse family of extreme gaps could carry the excess. He gives an affirmative *conditional* argument from stronger prime-gap moment/tail information under analytic assumptions. These are historical partial results, separate from the claimed new unconditional theorem. [Problem discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/968).

## A September 2026 claimed resolution

The 25 September 2026 OpenAI preprint, [*Positive lower density of large prime gaps*](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/Positive-lower-density-of-large-prime-gaps-September-25-2026/main.pdf), states its Theorem 1.1 as follows: for **every fixed** real $C>0$, there exist constants $c(C)>0$ and $N_0(C)$ such that

$$
\#\{1\le n\le N:p_{n+1}-p_n>C\log p_n\}
\ \ge\ c(C)N
\quad\text{for every }N\ge N_0(C).
$$

Its Corollary 1.2 then asserts exactly the positive lower density in the original Erdős–Prachar question. The formal deduction is short and transparent: use $C=2$ and the prime number theorem to ensure

$$
p_n/n<2\log p_n
$$

for all sufficiently large $n$. Each index with $g_n>2\log p_n$ then satisfies $g_n>p_n/n$ and hence $u_n<u_{n+1}$. Removing finitely many indices does not change positive lower density.

**The hard theorem is the positive-proportion lower bound for fixed multiples of $\log p_n$**, not the corollary's algebra. The preprint describes a sieve-weight construction comparing primes in two adjacent intervals of logarithmic length, relying on correlations of smooth divisor sums, the Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem and averages of prime-tuple singular series. A counting lemma controls the number of interval starts corresponding to the same long prime gap. These technical lemmas are the appropriate objects of independent proof review.

This preprint's released README describes work produced by an internal OpenAI model and manuscripts at **different verification stages**. No journal or independent referee report is established here. It must not be presented as an uncontested, peer-reviewed resolution.

## What the new claim does and does not settle

The claimed corollary exactly resolves **positive lower density**. It says nothing about whether

$$
\lim_{N\to\infty}
\frac{\#\{n\le N:u_n<u_{n+1}\}}{N}
$$

exists. It also does not by itself settle Erdős's separate questions about infinitely many triples of consecutive **increases**

$$
u_n<u_{n+1}<u_{n+2}
$$

or of consecutive decreases. Positive density of isolated increases alone does not force two adjacent increasing steps.

The stronger prime-gap theorem is itself an interesting independent assertion: it quantifies over **all fixed $C>0$** with a positive density constant that may depend on $C$. An average-gap computation or the existence of infinitely many large gaps is strictly weaker.
