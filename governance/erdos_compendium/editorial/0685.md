## The question in plain language

For an integer n and a binomial index k in the middle range n^ε < k ≤ n^(1−ε), Erdős asks whether the number ω(C(n,k)) of DISTINCT prime divisors is asymptotic to k times the sum of reciprocals of primes p strictly between k and n. The asymptotic must be uniform in this specified range once ε>0 is fixed. The even harder version allows k as small as a power of log n. These remain OPEN questions; no assertion about prime factors counted with multiplicity is intended.

## A small example

For n=20,k=5, C(20,5)=15504=2^4·3·17·19, so ω(C(20,5))=4. For n=30,k=7, C(30,7)=2035800=2^3·3^3·5^2·13·29, with five distinct prime factors. These are exact arithmetic examples, not a test of the proposed asymptotic because ε, n and the uniformity window must be treated asymptotically.

## What is known

For a prime p, Legendre's formula gives v_p(C(n,k))=Σ_{j≥1}(floor(n/p^j)−floor(k/p^j)−floor((n−k)/p^j)). Hence ω(C(n,k)) is exactly the number of primes for which this sum is positive. Kummer's theorem interprets the valuation as the number of base-p carries when k and n−k are added. For p>k with p²>n, there is no higher-power contribution and p divides C(n,k) precisely if the interval n−k+1,…,n crosses a multiple of p. This motivates, but does not prove, a prime-independent heuristic kΣ_{k<p<n}1/p. Higher powers, overlapping divisibilities, and uniformity in both n and k must be controlled. The maintained problem is OPEN; the original [Er79d] source has not been inspected page by page.
