## The question in plain language

Given an alphabet of exactly $k$ permitted symbols, Erdős asked whether **every word of length $2^k-1$** necessarily contains an *abelian square*: two adjacent nonempty blocks $X,Y$ having the same counts of each letter, even if their orders differ.

For example, the word 1221 is an abelian square, divided as 12|21; the halves are permutations of each other rather than identical. A word without abelian squares must avoid **every** adjacent pair of equal-multiset blocks, not merely literal repeated substrings.

## A small example

Over two symbols, the three-letter word 121 has **no abelian square**, disproving the assertion as literally phrased for $k=2$; it has no two adjacent equal letters and is too short for two blocks of length two.

Over three symbols, 1213121 has length seven and is abelian-square-free. For $k=4$ the fifteen-letter word

121312141213212

is abelian-square-free. It is a prefix of the catalogue's longer 16-letter example 1213121412132124, which is also abelian-square-free. Each is directly verifiable by comparing the Parikh vectors of all pairs of adjacent equal-length blocks.

These short explicit words remove any reliance on a speculative search result for the **finite** question.

## What is known

**DISPROVED**, both for the finite universal formulation and the stronger infinite-word issue. Keränen (1992) constructed an **infinite** abelian-square-free word over four letters. Such a word has an abelian-square-free prefix of every finite length, disproving the $2^k-1$ claim for any $k\ge4$ by using only four of the permitted symbols.

The maintained [Erdős #231](https://www.erdosproblems.com/231) acknowledges the Keränen construction and its equivalence to #192, but also questions an earlier claimed counterexample for $k=4$ at length $2^k-1=15$. Its own displayed 16-letter counterexample has a 15-letter prefix, so that doubt is **internally inconsistent**. Finite counterexamples for $k=2$ and $k=3$ similarly follow directly from the words above.

Avoiding abelian squares is stronger than avoiding ordinary squares. Thue's three-letter infinite square-free construction is **not** an abelian-square-free four-letter construction.

## What remains

A bounded task exhaustively checks all adjacent blocks in the three counterexamples, records their Parikh vectors, and independently counts abelian-square-free words of length seven over three symbols. Acceptance: the displayed words pass, the related 1212 fails at 12|12, and finite checking is separated from the infinite Keränen theorem. A proof-level task should replay Keränen's substitution with a fixed original source and prove indefinite avoidance.
