## The question in plain language

Let k≥4 and let F consist of k-element subsets of an n-element set. Erdős–Sós ask whether every family with MORE THAN $\binom{n-2}{k-2}$ sets contains distinct A,B with $|A\cap B|=1$. The maintained catalogue says PROVED. The threshold is strict, and all sets have equal cardinality k; it is not the classical intersecting-family EKR bound.

## A small example

Fix any two coordinates {1,2}. All k-subsets containing both form a family with exactly $\binom{n-2}{k-2}$ members, and every two sets meet in at least those two coordinates. Thus the stated threshold cannot be lowered for n≥k≥4. For n=6,k=4 the family has $\binom{4}{2}$=6 members and no intersection of size one.

## What is known

Peter Frankl, 'On families of finite sets no two of which intersect in a singleton', Bulletin of the Australian Mathematical Society 17 (1977), 125–134, DOI 10.1017/S0004972700025521, is a PRIMARY publisher-level source. Its ABSTRACT says the Erdős–Sós conjecture holds when n>n0(k), not explicitly for every n. The maintained catalogue attributes the FULL n statement to Frankl [Fr77], and Katona earlier established k=4.
