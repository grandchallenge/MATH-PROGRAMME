## The question in plain language

Problem 266 concerns an infinite list of positive integers $a_1,a_2,\ldots$ whose reciprocal sum converges. The Stolarsky conjecture asks whether at least one positive integer shift must make the shifted sum irrational. Written with the quantifiers made explicit, the claim is

$$
\forall (a_n)\ \Bigl[\bigl(a_n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\ \text{for all }n\ \text{ and }\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_n}<\infty\bigr)\Rightarrow\exists t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\ \text{such that}\ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_n+t}\notin\mathbb{Q}\Bigr].
$$

The live maintained entry phrases this as “there exists some integer $t\geq1$” after introducing an arbitrary admissible sequence. It attributes the question to Stolarsky and labels it **DISPROVED**. The positive shift matters: because $a_n+t>0$, no denominator can vanish. The original sources also use the equivalent sign convention $1/(a_n-t)$ for integers $t$ avoiding the poles $t=a_n$; replacing $t$ by $-t$ explains that convention, but it should not be silently conflated with the positive-shift display.

The quantifier order is the heart of the problem. It does not say that one fixed $t$ works for every sequence. It says that each admissible sequence should have at least one successful shift. To disprove it, one sequence must defeat every positive integer $t$ simultaneously.

## A small example

A finite calculation helps separate rational arithmetic from the infinite assertion. Take the increasing list $(2,3,7,43)$ and form the four-term sum

$$
S_t=\frac1{2+t}+\frac1{3+t}+\frac1{7+t}+\frac1{43+t}.
$$

Exact integer arithmetic gives

$$
S_0=\frac{1805}{1806},\qquad S_1=\frac{193}{264},\qquad S_2=\frac7{12},\qquad S_3=\frac{337}{690}.
$$

For example, $S_2=1/4+1/5+1/9+1/45=45/180+36/180+20/180+4/180=105/180=7/12$. Every finite sum of rational terms is rational, so this is only a sanity check; it is not a counterexample to an assertion about an infinite series.

The calculation is independently reproducible with Python’s standard-library `Fraction` implementation:

```python
from fractions import Fraction
a = (2, 3, 7, 43)
for t in range(4):
    print(t, sum((Fraction(1, x + t) for x in a), Fraction()))
```

The output is the four displayed fractions. This exact finite check is also recorded in the authenticated source-audit return [RESULT/1](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1536#issuecomment-6135318296), whose scope is evidence reporting rather than certification.

## What is known

The historical formulation is cited to Erdős and Graham, *Old and New Problems and Results in Combinatorial Number Theory* (1980), p. 64, and to Paul Erdős, “On the irrationality of certain series: problems and results” (1988), p. 104. The 1980 book page was not directly available for inspection in this audit; its wording is therefore retained as a cited historical source, not presented as a fresh transcription. The 1988 author scan was inspected directly. On printed p. 104 Erdős records Stolarsky’s question in the $1/(n_k-t)$ convention, with $t$ excluded from the sequence, and says the “all $n$” version remained open at that time.

Kovač and Tao’s arXiv version 4, dated 14 July 2025, resolves the question negatively. Their Theorem 2.11 states that there exists a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers $(a_n)$ such that

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_n+t}
$$

converges to a rational number for every $t\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{-a_n:n\in\mathbb{N}\}$. This is stronger than required: every positive integer is among those allowed rational shifts, and for positive shifts the pole exclusion is automatic. Hence the existential conclusion in Problem 266 fails for this one sequence, so the status is **disproved**, not open or merely partially settled.

Section 8 of the same paper gives the construction. It enumerates the rationals, builds increasingly large blocks, and uses rational target values plus approximation and perturbation steps so that all shifted sums are rational. The paper’s displayed scale has eventual size comparable to $2^{2^{\sqrt{k}}}$; the proof is an existence construction, not a short closed-form list of the $a_n$.

## What remains

No mathematical question remains at the level of Problem 266’s truth value once Kovač–Tao’s Theorem 2.11 is accepted as the primary proof: the conjecture is disproved. Documentary work does remain. The 1980 p. 64 book page should be inspected if a direct quotation or exact historical typography is needed. The sign and indexing conventions should continue to be reported explicitly: the 1988 source uses $n_k-t$, while the live entry and Kovač–Tao use $a_n+t$; the stronger rational-shift theorem bridges them, but this is a semantic comparison, not permission to rewrite the sources.

A separate formalization project could prove the elementary bridge from a strictly increasing positive sequence to convergence of each shifted tail, and then prove the logical implication from an all-rational counterexample to the negation of the original quantifier statement. That would not formalize Kovač–Tao’s infinite-dimensional construction. Any such work must use a pinned environment and a proof term without `sorry`.

Neither an external proof repository, self-reporting CI, a statement file, nor a Lean declaration containing `sorry` is mathematical certification. This manuscript therefore reports the published result and its boundaries without promoting formalization metadata into a proof claim.
