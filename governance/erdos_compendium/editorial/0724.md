## The question in plain language

Let f(n) be the maximum number of mutually orthogonal Latin squares of order n, where each square has n symbols and no symbol repeats within a row or column, and two squares are orthogonal when their ordered-pair superposition gives each of the n² pairs exactly once. Erdős asks whether f(n)≥c sqrt(n) for a fixed c>0 and all sufficiently large n. This remains OPEN.

## A small example

For n=3, define L_a(i,j)=i+a j modulo 3 for a=1,2. Each L_a is a Latin square. For a≠b, the map (i,j)↦(i+a j,i+b j) is invertible over F_3, so the pair of squares is orthogonal; f(3)≥2. A general upper bound f(n)≤n−1 follows from orthogonal-array counting, so f(3)=2. This finite prime-power construction cannot establish an all-n square-root lower bound.

## What is known

Bose–Parker–Shrikhande disproved Euler's n≡2 mod4 'no orthogonal pair' belief; they proved f(n)≥2 for n≥7. Chowla–Erdős–Straus gave f(n)≫n^(1/91), Wilson improved to n^(1/17), and Beth to n^(1/14.8), as maintained source attributes. Those exponents are strictly below 1/2, so do not settle #724. Prime-power orders have a full family n−1 by finite-field construction; controlling ALL n is the challenge. Original BPS/CES/Wilson/Beth full theorem proof pages not directly inspected.
