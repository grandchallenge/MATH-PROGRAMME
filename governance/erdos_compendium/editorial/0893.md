## The question in plain language

Let $\tau(m)$ be the number of positive divisors of $m$. Mersenne numbers are the integers $2^k-1$. Their divisor counts vary considerably, so Erdős asked about the accumulated sum

$$
f(n)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tau(2^k-1)
$$

and in particular whether the **doubling ratio** $f(2n)/f(n)$ tends to a limit as $n\to\infty$. This is [Erdős Problem #893](https://www.erdosproblems.com/893).

A finite real limit is now ruled out by a published theorem. The remaining natural question is whether the ratio tends to **positive infinity**, rather than taking arbitrarily large values while also returning to more moderate values along other subsequences. The maintained catalogue therefore continues to classify the question as **open**.

## A small example

The initial Mersenne numbers are $1,3,7,15,31,63,\ldots$, with divisor counts

$$
1,2,2,4,2,6,\ldots
$$

(the number 1 has one positive divisor). Consequently $f(1)=1$, $f(2)=3$, $f(3)=5$ and $f(4)=9$. The first doubling ratios are $f(2)/f(1)=3$ and $f(4)/f(2)=3$.

An exact finite factorization program computes every $\tau(2^k-1)$ for $1\le k\le64$, including factor-product and prime checks on each factor, and independently uses trial division for $k\le24$. Among its doubling ratios:

| $n$ | $f(2n)/f(n)$ (approximately) |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | 3.000 |
| 2 | 3.000 |
| 4 | 3.000 |
| 8 | 3.741 |
| 16 | 5.337 |
| 32 | 15.134 |

Increasing finite values are suggestive, but cannot prove divergence for all sufficiently large indices.

## What is known

Vjekoslav Kovač and Florian Luca, [*On the number of divisors of Mersenne numbers*](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04883v4), arXiv:2506.04883, original version June 5, 2025, updated version 4 February 3, 2026, prove in **Theorem 1**

$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{f(2n)}{f(n)}=\infty.
$$

This **unconditional** theorem excludes a finite real limit. It does **not** establish that the full sequence of ratios tends to infinity. The paper's Proposition 2 proves a related divergent ratio for an auxiliary sum of $2^{\tau(k)}$; transferring that conclusion to the actual Mersenne sum requires the authors' further argument, not mere substitution.

Kovač and Luca also prove conditional full divergence under either of two separate hypotheses. One concerns the divisor counts of $2^N+1$ along record indices $N$ for $\tau(2^N-1)$. The other imposes a logarithmic upper bound on the number of distinct prime factors of cyclotomic values $\Phi_d(2)$ for all sufficiently large $d$. The paper provides theoretical and numerical reasons for these hypotheses but does not prove them.

The original problem is from Erdős, [*Some of my new and almost new problems and results in combinatorial number theory*](https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110809794.169), Eger proceedings (1998), printed p.180. He suggested that $f(n)$ may increase too irregularly for a simple asymptotic formula. The maintained catalogue preserves the open question of the ratio's full behavior and credits the Kovač–Luca unconditional limsup theorem, based in part on an independently proposed heuristic of Stijn Cambie.

## GCL's current state

There is a simple **uniform lower bound** that can be verified without reproducing the difficult analytic proof. The factorization

$$
2^{2k}-1=(2^k-1)(2^k+1)
$$

has coprime factors, because both are odd and their difference is 2. Since the divisor-count function is multiplicative for coprime integers,

$$
\tau(2^{2k}-1)=\tau(2^k-1)\tau(2^k+1).
$$

The second factor is always at least 2 for $k\ge1$. Separate even and odd indices in the sum $f(2n)$:

$$
f(2n)=\sum_{k=1}^n\tau(2^{2k}-1)
+\sum_{k=1}^n\tau(2^{2k-1}-1)
>2\sum_{k=1}^n\tau(2^k-1)=2f(n).
$$

Therefore **$f(2n)/f(n)>2$ for every positive integer $n$**. This lemma is much weaker than full divergence: it is compatible with bounded subsequences above 2.

The executable finite research program at governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_892_893.py uses SymPy to factor all Mersenne numbers through exponent 64. It checks that every factor is prime and below $2^{64}$, verifies every product of prime powers reconstructs the original integer, independently computes divisor counts by trial division for the first 24 exponents, and checks the exact coprime-factor identity through $k=32$. Its JSON receipt stores selected prime factorizations and exact rational doubling ratios.

## What remains

A bounded theorem suitable for a direct formal replay is the **Mersenne doubling identity**:

$$
\tau(2^{2k}-1)=\tau(2^k-1)\tau(2^k+1)
$$

for positive $k$, followed by the universal inequality $f(2n)>2f(n)$. Acceptance requires a complete elementary proof or a pinned no-placeholder Lean theorem with actual axiom output, plus exact factorization and divisor-count regression through exponent 64. A factor shared by the two coprime components, or a mismatch in a calculated divisor count, falsifies the reduction.

A separate research obligation is the genuinely open **mass-distribution** step required to promote the published unbounded limsup to divergence along every sufficiently large index. Kovač–Luca's conditional results identify useful hypotheses but do not establish them; any successor must choose one precise sufficient condition and prove it without silently assuming a conjecture on record Mersenne indices or cyclotomic factors.
