## The question in plain language

A *strong $B_3$ set* $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ is a set for which the sum of any three elements determines their **multiset** uniquely. Repeated summands are allowed, so the triples $(a,a,b)$ and $(a,b,b)$ are distinct, while permutations are considered the same. Let $f(N)$ be the maximum size of such a set. [Erdős #241](https://www.erdosproblems.com/241) asks whether

$$
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{f(N)}{N^{1/3}}=1.
$$

A claim merely establishing upper and lower bounds proportional to $N^{1/3}$ would not settle the constant-one limit.

## A small example

The set $A=\{1,5\}$ is strong $B_3$: its possible unordered triple sums are $3,7,11,15$, all distinct. There is a useful exact link to cyclic arithmetic. For $A\subseteq[1,N]$, two triple sums differ in absolute value by at most $3N-3$. Hence reduction modulo $M\ge3N-2$ preserves *exactly* whether triple sums collide. For $N=5$, $M=13$ preserves the four sums, while modulo $12$ the sums 3 and 15 collide. This is a sharp uniform threshold, not an asymptotic result.

## What is known

The Bose–Chowla constructions yield strong $B_3$ sets of size at least $(1-o(1))N^{1/3}$. Ben Green (2001) established an upper bound with constant $(7/2)^{1/3}$.

**The latter is no longer the best published constant.** Ethan Patrick White, *An optimal $L^2$ autoconvolution inequality*, *Canadian Mathematical Bulletin* **67** (2024), 108–121, provides the improved upper constant at most

$$
\left(\frac{2}{0.574636066}\right)^{1/3}\approx1.5155.
$$

A February 2026 Rechnitzer preprint reports tighter rigorous autoconvolution bounds, but its transfer into a published numerical upper constant needs exact hypothesis and endpoint verification. None of these bounds proves the limit equals one.

## GCL's current state

[Source audit #1510](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1510#issuecomment-6098842021) documents the pinned formal statement, the improvement to Green's historical constant, the finite sharp cyclic/no-wrap lemma and a protected adjudication of smaller finite $B_3$ facts. It also identifies an already-existing native mathematical successor, which this documentary article neither duplicates nor authorizes.

## What remains

Proving the conjecture requires tightening the asymptotic upper constant all the way to one, or identifying a counterexample to that limit. Sharp finite modular transport and small-set enumerations aid verification but do not resolve the asymptotics as $N$ grows.
