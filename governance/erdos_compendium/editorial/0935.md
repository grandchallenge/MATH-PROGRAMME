## The question in plain language

Write \(N=\prod_p p^{e_p}\). Its *powerful part* is
\[
Q_2(N)=\prod_{p:e_p\ge2}p^{e_p},
\]
including the **whole** prime power \(p^{e_p}\), not merely \(p^2\). Fix \(\ell\ge1\), and set \(N_\ell(n)=n(n+1)\cdots(n+\ell)\). Erdős asked three related questions:

1. For every \(\varepsilon>0\), is \(Q_2(N_\ell(n))<n^{2+\varepsilon}\) for all sufficiently large \(n\)?
2. For \(\ell\ge2\), is \(\limsup_{n\to\infty} Q_2(N_\ell(n))/n^2=\infty\)?
3. For \(\ell\ge2\), is \(\lim_{n\to\infty}Q_2(N_\ell(n))/n^{\ell+1}=0\)?

The **second question has an affirmative construction**. The first and third remain open unconditionally in the maintained catalogue. It would be incorrect to label the entire three-part problem either fully solved or wholly untouched. [Erdős Problem #935](https://www.erdosproblems.com/935).

## A small example

For \(n=3,\ell=2\), the product is \(3\cdot4\cdot5=60=2^2\cdot3\cdot5\). Consequently \(Q_2(60)=4\), not \(60\) or \(2^2 3^2\). For \(n=4,\ell=2\), the product is \(4\cdot5\cdot6=120=2^3\cdot3\cdot5\), so \(Q_2(120)=8\). These examples isolate the multiplicity threshold from the ordinary radical or square part.

## What is known

The maintained catalogue attributes to Mahler a result implying
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty} \frac{Q_2(N_\ell(n))}{n^2}\ge1
\]
for every fixed \(\ell\ge1\).

A construction based on Pell-equation solutions \(x^2-8y^2=1\), recorded for the related [Problem #367](https://www.erdosproblems.com/367) by Wouter van Doorn and independently obtained by Aletheia [Fe26], establishes
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{Q_2(n(n+1)(n+2))}{n^2}=\infty.
\]
The same conclusion follows for every fixed \(\ell\ge2\): since \(N_2(n)\) divides \(N_\ell(n)\), \(Q_2(N_2(n))\) divides \(Q_2(N_\ell(n))\) prime by prime and is no greater. Thus the **second part is proved for all such \(\ell\)**. This deduction uses the fact that adjoining factors cannot lower any prime valuation.

According to the catalogue, the ABC conjecture implies the third statement; that is a **conditional** result, not an unconditional proof. The discussion emphasizes a provenance distinction: the Pell construction was already present in comments on #367, while the Aletheia work independently rediscovered it. Priority and mathematical correctness are separate questions. Avoid claiming novelty for this route.

## Why the problem is difficult

Overlapping small prime factors of consecutive integers can produce high powers in the product even if they occur in only one or two individual factors. The sought universal bound \(n^{2+\varepsilon}\) is stronger than a direct estimate of the whole product, which grows on the order of \(n^{\ell+1}\). The third part asks for powerful parts to occupy a vanishing fraction of that full product. The Pell construction demonstrates exceptional spikes relative to \(n^2\) without settling either universal upper question.

## GCL's current state

The monotonicity deduction from the three-factor Pell result to all \(\ell\ge2\) is elementary and exact. The deep Pell construction is cited rather than independently proved here. The first and third parts remain open unconditionally. No Lean certification or complete local verification is claimed.
