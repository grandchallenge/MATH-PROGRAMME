## The question in plain language

Choose $n$ distinct points in the real plane, with **no three collinear and no four concyclic**. There are $\binom n2$ unordered pairs of points. Erdős asks for which $n$ it is possible to have precisely $n-1$ distinct pairwise distances, with their multiplicities, in some order, equal to

$$
1,2,\ldots,n-1.
$$

These multiplicities sum to $\binom n2$, so the requirement is numerically possible, but imposes a strong geometric constraint. Distinct distances means **different Euclidean lengths**, not different ordered pairs.

## A small example

At $n=4$, there are six unordered point pairs, and the desired multiplicities are one, two and three. A configuration containing an isosceles triangle and a carefully placed fourth point supplies the known small case. Counting a distance between $p$ and $q$ twice, once for each orientation, would incorrectly double all multiplicities.

No three on a line forbids degenerate triangles. The extra **no four on a circle** restriction rules out certain highly symmetric constructions even when they realize the right number of distances.

## What is known

The existence of examples for **arbitrarily large $n$ remains OPEN**. Pomerance constructed a five-point example. Palásti found examples with seven points in 1987 and eight points later, and in 1989 supplied a six-point configuration with no equilateral triangle to meet a strengthened variant.

Erdős originally thought $n\ge5$ impossible, but those examples refuted that early expectation. He subsequently conjectured that no such configurations exist for **all sufficiently large $n$**. The maintained [Erdős #217](https://www.erdosproblems.com/217), updated April 2026, records these finite examples and the link to a stronger distinct-distance question #98. None of the finite examples resolves the eventual-existence question.

## What remains

A bounded successor obtains the published coordinates of the seven- or eight-point construction and checks squared distances exactly, grouping equal lengths without floating-point error. Acceptance: the multiplicity multiset is precisely $\{1,\ldots,n-1\}$, no orientation determinant for any triple vanishes, and no four-point concyclicity determinant vanishes. Avoid inferring an infinite family from finite examples.
