## The question in plain language

A new term in an increasing integer sequence cannot equal the sum of ANY block of consecutive earlier terms. The old half-density prediction was false, but the original growth and weighted reciprocal sum questions remain unresolved.

Let a_1<a_2<... be positive integers with NO later a_i equal to the sum of any consecutive block of earlier terms a_j+...+a_l (1<=j<=l<i). Original asks if limsup a_n/n=infinity or stronger weighted reciprocal-sum limit zero. An ancillary suspicion that the upper density cannot exceed 1/2 is FALSE. Do not substitute this ancillary disproval for the still OPEN main claims.

## A small example

Sequence 1,2,4,8,16,... is admissible: each new term exceeds sum of all earlier terms. Sequence 1,3,5,7,9 is INVALID: its fifth element 9 equals 1+3+5, a consecutive block of first three earlier elements, even though that block does not end immediately before the fifth element.

This finite calculation is not an asymptotic or all-parameter proof.

## What is known

Maintained /latex/839 read 2026-10-14: Erdős states liminf a_n/n finite is possible, and reciprocal sum can be Omega(loglog x). R. Freud [Fr93], *Adding numbers - on a problem of P. Erdős* (1993), 6199–6202, constructs sequence of upper density 19/36>1/2, disproving only suggested upper density <=1/2. Main limsup and reciprocal sum upper remain OPEN. Full Freud paper not independently inspected.

The forbidden representation is any consecutive block of EARLIER terms, not necessarily a block ending immediately before a later term. The prefix 1,3,5,7,9 fails because 9=1+3+5, although these are not the three immediately prior terms. Powers of two give a simple admissible sequence because each term exceeds the sum of all previous terms. Freud's upper-density 19/36 construction refutes only the side conjecture that density cannot exceed one half.
