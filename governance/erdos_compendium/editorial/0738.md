## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #738](https://www.erdosproblems.com/738), attributed to Gyárfás, asks whether EVERY triangle-free graph with INFINITE chromatic number contains every finite tree as an **induced** subgraph. Induced means that the selected vertices have exactly the edges of the chosen tree, not extra chords. The problem remains OPEN.

## A small example

A five-cycle C₅ is triangle-free and requires three colours. It contains an induced three-vertex path but not an induced four-vertex star K₁,₃, since all its vertices have degree two. This is not a counterexample: C₅ has finite chromatic number.

## What is known

In a triangle-free graph, all neighbors of a fixed vertex are pairwise nonadjacent; hence any vertex of degree at least k generates an induced star K₁,k. Infinite chromatic number forces unbounded finite degrees (otherwise a finite greedy colouring exists, using compactness in the infinite case). This proves all finite **stars** occur as induced subgraphs but does not prove every finite tree occurs. A broad Gyárfás–Sumner tree-free chromatic boundedness conjecture is closely related but must not be assumed established in full.
