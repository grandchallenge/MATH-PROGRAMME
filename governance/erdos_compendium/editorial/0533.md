**Editorial status:** UNACCEPTED_DRAFT. Erdős Problem #533 is **DISPROVED**. The original conjecture predicted that a sufficiently dense graph with no five-vertex clique must contain a **linearly large set of vertices spanning no triangle**. Balogh and Lenz disproved this in 2013, and Liu, Reiher, Sharifzadeh and Staden subsequently established the exact Ramsey–Turán density $1/12$ (when the number of edges is divided by $n^2$). The protected Formal Conjectures file records the negative answer but its main proof and quantitative variants still contain placeholders.

## The question in plain language

Take a graph $G$ with $n$ vertices. Suppose it has **no complete graph on five vertices**, written $K_5$. Assume that it has at least $\delta n^2$ edges for some fixed $\delta>0$.

Must $G$ contain a set of at least $c(\delta)n$ vertices such that **no three of those vertices form a triangle**?

In symbols, the conjecture asks whether

$$
\boxed{
\forall\delta>0,\ \exists c(\delta)>0,\
\text{every sufficiently large }K_5\text{-free }G
\text{ with }e(G)\ge\delta n^2
\text{ has }\alpha_3(G)\ge c(\delta)n,
}
$$

where

$$
\alpha_3(G)=\max\{|S|:S\subseteq V(G),\ G[S]\text{ contains no triangle}\}.
$$

The requirement is stronger than having no five-clique, but **weaker than finding an independent set**: the selected vertices may contain edges and even four-cycles, just not any triangle.

The answer is **no**. There are sequences of $K_5$‑free graphs with a positive proportion of the maximum possible number of edges, yet their largest triangle-free induced vertex subsets occupy an arbitrarily small fraction of all vertices.

## A small example

Consider the complete four-partite graph with four classes of two vertices, denoted $K_{2,2,2,2}$. Any two vertices in different classes are joined by an edge; vertices in the same class are not adjacent.

It has eight vertices. Choosing one vertex from each of four classes forms a $K_4$, but **there is no $K_5$**, since a clique can contain at most one vertex from each class. The total number of edges is

$$
e(K_{2,2,2,2})=\binom82-4\binom22=28-4=24.
$$

Any set meeting **three different classes** contains a triangle. Therefore a triangle-free vertex subset can meet at most two classes, each of size two, so

$$
\boxed{\alpha_3(K_{2,2,2,2})=4.}
$$

This small graph illustrates the difference between the forbidden $K_5$ and the desired triangle-free induced subset. It is **not** a counterexample to the asymptotic conjecture: its triangle-free set has **half** of all vertices, which is still a positive linear fraction.

In fact, a balanced complete four-partite graph on arbitrarily many vertices always has a triangle-free subset consisting of two whole classes. To disprove Erdős's conjecture one needs far subtler constructions with $\alpha_3(G)=o(n)$ while keeping $e(G)$ of order $n^2$.

## What is known

### The original Erdős–Hajnal–Simonovits–Sós–Szemerédi question

The question appears in the work of P. Erdős, A. Hajnal, M. Simonovits, V. T. Sós and E. Szemerédi, *Turán–Ramsey theorems and $K_p$‑independence numbers*, *Combinatorics, Probability and Computing* **3** (1994), 297–325, notably p.306 as cited by the [maintained problem](https://www.erdosproblems.com/533). The complete original 1994 paper was not obtained; its known results were checked against the later full papers.

The originators established a density **upper bound** of $1/12$ for the $K_5$ problem and proved the related positive statement for $K_4$‑free graphs. These are *not* proofs that the $K_5$ version is true.

To formulate the extremal quantity, let $\mathrm{RT}_3(n,K_5,m)$ denote the maximum number of edges of an $n$‑vertex graph with no $K_5$ and with no triangle-free induced subset larger than $m$. The relevant limiting edge density, in the **$n^2$ denominator convention**, is

$$
\boxed{
\delta_3(5)=
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\lim_{n\to\infty}
\frac{\mathrm{RT}_3(n,K_5,\varepsilon n)}{n^2}.
}
$$

The conjecture would have forced $\delta_3(5)=0$. Its disproof establishes a **positive** density instead.

### Balogh and Lenz's 2013 counterexample

József Balogh and John Lenz, *On the Ramsey–Turán numbers of graphs and hypergraphs*, *Israel Journal of Mathematics* **194** (2013), 45–68, [DOI 10.1007/s11856-012-0076-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11856-012-0076-2), disproved the conjecture.

The [original complete preprint, arXiv:1109.4428v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1109.4428v2), was retrieved. **Theorem 3 on PDF p.3** states a positive Ramsey–Turán density bound for complete graphs $K_{t+\ell}$ when $2\le\ell\le t$. Specializing to $t=3$, $\ell=2$, and $u=\lceil t/2\rceil=2$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta_3(5)&\ge
\frac12\left(1-\frac12\right)2^{-2^2}\\
&=\frac14\cdot\frac1{16}
=\boxed{\frac1{64}>0}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Their p.4 displays the same $1/64$ bound among concrete cases. It already suffices to refute Erdős's assertion. Indeed, pick a density $\delta$ strictly below $1/64$, for instance $1/128$. For all large $n$, suitable $K_5$‑free examples have at least $\delta n^2$ edges while their maximum triangle-free induced subsets have size **$o(n)$**. No positive fixed $c(\delta)$ can work for those graphs.

Their proof constructs high-dimensional geometric configurations; the finite complete multipartite example above is only an educational contrast, **not** their counterexample.

### The sharp value established by Liu, Reiher, Sharifzadeh and Staden

Hong Liu, Christian Reiher, Maryam Sharifzadeh and Katherine Staden, *Geometric constructions for Ramsey–Turán theory*, [arXiv:2103.10423v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.10423v2), revised August 18, 2025, constructed the matching density using high-dimensional **complex spheres**. Their paper appeared in the *Journal of the European Mathematical Society*, [DOI 10.4171/JEMS/1712](https://doi.org/10.4171/JEMS/1712).

The **complete original 27-page preprint** was retrieved. Its **Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2 on PDF p.4** imply

$$
\boxed{\varrho_3(5)=\frac16}.
$$

This is not a contradiction of the answer $1/12$. The authors use the normalization

$$
\varrho_3(5)=
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\lim_{n\to\infty}
\frac{\mathrm{RT}_3(n,K_5,\varepsilon n)}
{\binom n2},
$$

whereas the original problem page and Balogh–Lenz use the denominator $n^2$. Since $\binom n2/n^2\to1/2$,

$$
\boxed{
\delta_3(5)=\frac12\varrho_3(5)=\frac1{12}.
}
$$

Combined with the original upper bound, this gives the **exact threshold**. It shows the original assertion fails for every fixed $\delta<1/12$, while the original upper-bound theory provides the asserted large triangle-free subsets when $\delta>1/12$. The limiting-density equality alone does **not** settle the exact borderline case $\delta=1/12$.
