## The question in plain language

Pairwise-intersecting hypergraphs requiring three vertex colors can have exponentially many vertices, refuting the hoped-for quadratic vertex bound. A different overlap question asks whether two edges must intersect in a fixed positive fraction of their lengths and remains open.

Let $G$ be a hypergraph of uniformity $r$ with vertex chromatic number 3 and any two distinct edges intersect. Two questions: must the number of vertices be $O(r^2)$? NO; must some pair of edges intersect in $\Omega(r)$ vertices? OPEN. Require all vertices of G to be incident to an edge when interpreting vertex bound; arbitrary isolated vertices already make literal first question false.

## A small example

At $r=3$, take $X=\{0,1,2,3\}$; $Y$ has three partition vertices, for seven vertices in total. There are four 3-subsets of X and six triples consisting of two vertices from $X$ and one from $Y$, ten edges. Every pair of edges intersects; exhaustive 2-colorability fails, and a 3-coloring exists. This finite fixture validates combinatorial definition, not exponential asymptotic alone.

This finite calculation is not an asymptotic or all-parameter proof.

## What is known

Maintained /latex/836 read 2026-10-14. Erdős–Lovász [ErLo75] show a pair of edges intersects in at least $\Omega(r/\log r)$ vertices, weaker than the target of $\Omega(r)$ vertices. In Alon's construction, $X$ has $2r-2$ vertices and $Y$ has one vertex for each unordered balanced bipartition of $X$ into sets of size $r-1$, giving $|Y|=\tfrac12\binom{2r-2}{r-1}$. Hyperedges comprise all subsets of size $r$ of $X$ and one subset of size $r-1$ plus its associated $Y$ vertex for every bipartition class. It is intersecting, non-2-colorable but 3-colorable, and $|V|\sim 4^r/\sqrt r$. The $r=3$ finite construction has been independently checked using all ten explicit hyperedges, verifying pairwise intersections and the absence of a proper two-colouring. The infinite-r theorem remains attributed to Alon, not certified afresh by that fixture.

Alon's construction has a part $X$ with $2r-2$ vertices and a part $Y$ indexing unordered balanced bipartitions of $X$. Therefore

$$
|V|=2r-2+\frac12\binom{2r-2}{r-1}.
$$

The binomial coefficient grows exponentially, so this grows faster than $O(r^2)$. Hyperedges consist of all subsets of $X$ of size $r$, plus a subset of $X$ of size $r-1$ with its associated vertex from $Y$. These edges intersect pairwise and the hypergraph requires three colors. It does not settle whether two edges must intersect in $\Omega(r)$ vertices.
