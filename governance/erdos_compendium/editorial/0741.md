## The question in plain language

Burr–Erdős #741 comprises two questions: (i) when A+A has positive density, can A be split into disjoint A_1,A_2 whose self-sumsets both have positive density? (ii) is there an additive basis A of order two such that no partition has BOTH self-sumsets syndetic (bounded gaps)? The current maintained source is SOLVED (LEAN) by a mixture of proofs, but the outcome of (i) depends critically on which notion of density is meant: strict existing natural density, positive UPPER density, or positive LOWER density. These MUST NOT be treated as equivalent.

## A small example

As a simple positive instance, A=all nonnegative integers, partition into even A_1 and odd A_2. Then A+A=A; A_1+A_1 is the set of nonnegative even integers, and A_2+A_2 is the set of even integers at least 2. Both have natural density 1/2, so the upper and lower density versions succeed for this A. One example is not a universal theorem for arbitrary A.
