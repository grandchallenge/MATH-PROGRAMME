## The question in plain language

Erdős Problem 332 asks how much regularity a set of recurring differences must have. Start with a set $A$ of natural numbers. A number $d$ is a recurring difference when there are infinitely many pairs of elements of $A$ whose difference is $d$. The question is: what assumptions on $A$ guarantee that these recurring differences do not leave arbitrarily long gaps?

For comparison with the original literature, use nonnegative differences:

$$
D_\infty(A)=\{d\in\mathbb N_0:|\{a\in A:a+d\in A\}|=\infty\}.
$$

Here “bounded gaps” means that some fixed $M$ works so that every interval of $M$ consecutive nonnegative integers contains an element of $D_\infty(A)$. This is the usual syndeticity condition. The live page phrases the problem with $A\subseteq\mathbb N$ and $a_1-a_2$, without spelling out whether negative differences are included. Erdős and Graham’s cited source, and Stewart and Tijdeman’s primary paper, use nonnegative differences and include $0$.

The distinction matters for formalization. The pinned Lean file instead defines $D_A(A)$ as a subset of $\mathbb Z$, allowing both signs, and states bounded gaps on all of $\mathbb Z$. That is a reasonable symmetric variant, but it is not automatically identical to the original nonnegative formulation.

## A small example

Take the even numbers, $A=2\mathbb N_0$. Every even $d$ occurs infinitely often: for each $n\geq 0$, the pair $2(n+d/2)$ and $2n$ has difference $d$. No odd number is a difference of two even numbers. Thus

$$
D_\infty(2\mathbb N_0)=2\mathbb N_0,
$$

which has gaps of at most $2$.

There is also a finite exact check behind this example. For $A_N=\{0,2,4,\ldots,2(N-1)\}$, direct integer enumeration gives

$$
\#\{(a,b)\in A_N^2:a-b=2d\}=N-d\qquad(0\leq d<N).
$$

For $N=5$, the multiplicities for differences $0,2,4,6,8$ are respectively $5,4,3,2,1$. This is reproducible by counting the pairs $(2i,2j)$ with $i-j=d$; there are exactly $N-d$ choices of $j$. The finite calculation checks the pattern and illustrates recurring differences, but it does not prove infinitude for an arbitrary set, a density theorem, or the Erdős question itself.

## What is known

The original catalogue entry points to P. Erdős and R. L. Graham, *Old and New Problems and Results in Combinatorial Number Theory* (1980), p. 50. The maintained page reports that Prikry, Tijdeman, Stewart, and others showed positive density is sufficient, and it points to C. L. Stewart’s 1978 survey and R. Tijdeman’s 1979 survey. It also asks related questions about positive density of $D_\infty(A)$, divergence of $\sum_{d\in D_\infty(A)}1/d$, and mere nonemptiness.

A directly inspectable primary source is C. L. Stewart and R. Tijdeman, “On Infinite-Difference Sets,” *Canadian Journal of Mathematics* 31 (1979), 897–910, DOI [10.4153/CJM-1979-085-6](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1979-085-6). On p. 897 they define the infinite-difference set for an infinite increasing sequence of nonnegative integers. On p. 898, Theorem 2 says that if the sequence has positive upper asymptotic density, finitely many translates of its infinite-difference set cover $\mathbb N_0$. The paper then explains that such a finite covering prevents arbitrarily long gaps. Its Corollary 1, proved on p. 902, gives the further density inequality $\underline d(D_\infty(A))\geq\overline d(A)$ in the relevant notation.

This is a verified sufficient condition, not a complete characterization. The primary paper also shows that the resulting difference sets can have more complicated density behavior, so “positive density of $A$” should not be silently upgraded to a precise best-possible description of all sets with bounded-gap difference sets. The maintained page currently displays the problem as open, with one unverified discussion comment, zero proof claims, and no proof exposition. The comment sketches a possible strengthening from positive asymptotic density to positive upper Banach density; it is explicitly user-generated and has not been treated here as established evidence.
