A 2026 project by Elliot Glazer and collaborators reports the stronger conclusion that the unrestricted infinite-independent-set assertion is **independent of ZFC**. ## The question in plain language

For **every** real number $y$, choose a bounded subset $A_y$ of the real line, whose **Lebesgue outer measure** satisfies

$$
m^*(A_y)<1.
$$

An outer measure can be assigned even if a set is not Lebesgue measurable. We want an infinite set $X\subseteq\mathbb R$ such that

$$
x\notin A_y\qquad
\text{for every pair of distinct }x,y\in X.
$$

Such an $X$ is called **independent** or **free** for the set mapping. The condition is directed: for each different pair, we must check **both** $x\notin A_y$ and $y\notin A_x$. Self-membership is *not* prohibited. This makes the problem different from ordinary independence in a symmetric undirected graph.

A second, distinct question asks: suppose every $A_y$ is **closed** and has Lebesgue measure below one. Must there be three mutually independent points? The answer to this closed-set question is **yes**. In fact an infinite independent set exists under those conditions.

The unrestricted version raises a deeper issue. Its answer can depend on additional axioms of set theory. Under the **continuum hypothesis (CH)** there is a counterexample. On the other hand, a 2026 formalization project reports a model of ZFC in which the assertion holds. Thus the recorded special status is about **logical consequence**, not a finite mathematical search.

## A small example

Take $A_y=\varnothing$ for every real $y$. Each assigned set is bounded and has outer measure zero. Every subset of $\mathbb R$ is independent: the forbidden membership relation never occurs.

A subtler example uses $A_y=\{y\}$. Every assigned set is again bounded and has outer measure zero. The **whole real line** is still independent, because the condition checks only $x\ne y$. An implementation that forbids $y\in A_y$ would wrongly reject this valid example.

For contrast, on a *finite* universe $U=\{0,1,2\}$ define $A_y=U\setminus\{y\}$. Every two distinct points mutually forbid one another, so independent sets have size at most one. This demonstrates the direction and logic of the condition, but is **not** a counterexample to Erdős #501: assigning these finite sets inside the real line for only three indices is not a family satisfying the original global hypotheses. Finite directed graphs alone cannot settle a theorem quantified over all real numbers and all small-outer-measure sets.

## What is known

### From the original 1961 paper to the closed-set theorem

Erdős, *Some unsolved problems* (1961), printed p.243, Problem II.9, [original scan](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1961-22.pdf#page=23), was visually inspected. Erdős and Hajnal had already proved the existence of independent sets of **arbitrarily large finite size** under the bounded outer-measure hypotheses. Erdős asked whether these finite witnesses could be extended to an infinite independent set, and separately asked for a three-point set when the forbidden sets are closed.

Arbitrarily large finite independent sets do not automatically imply an infinite one. That would require a suitable compactness principle and the underlying set-mapping constraints do not supply one for free.

The historical negative direction is attributed to S. H. Hechler, *On two problems in combinatorial set theory* (1972): assuming the continuum hypothesis $2^{\aleph_0}=\aleph_1$, there is a family satisfying the general hypotheses **without** an infinite independent set. This is a conditional counterexample, not a theorem that ZFC alone disproves the universal assertion.

The closed-set question was settled by Ludomir Newelski, Janusz Pawlikowski and Witold Seredyński, *Infinite free set for small measure set mappings*, *Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society* **100** (1987), 335–339, [DOI 10.1090/S0002-9939-1987-0884475-3](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1987-0884475-3). Their theorem provides an **infinite** independent set if every $A_y$ is closed of measure below one; boundedness is unnecessary in this stronger version. Therefore the requested triple exists. The complete 1987 original proof body has not been re-read in this editorial audit, so the attribution is literature-grounded rather than independently re-proved.

### The reported 2026 independence result

A 2026 [primary Lean project by Elliot Glazer and collaborators](https://github.com/elliotglazer/erdos501) describes two forcing constructions: Hechler's counterexample in a model satisfying CH, and a positive instance in a random-real extension. It states the targets

$$
\mathrm{ZFC}\not\models P,
\qquad
\mathrm{ZFC}\not\models\neg P,
$$

where $P$ is the first question of #501. The project also states a semantic bridge showing that its formal first-order set-theory sentence matches the Mathlib formulation of the original real-line question. Its README identifies the mathematical forcing argument and associated Lean comparator files, exact compiler/toolchain versions, and an author-run audit listing only standard Lean logical axioms.

These are **author-reported, source-inspected results**, not independently executed GCL receipts. An external source's successful CI and claimed axiom audit do not substitute for verifying the exact source head, compiling under a pinned environment, auditing its statement and inspecting its forcing prerequisites.

There is another, weaker positive consistency route: Sungchul Lee's [2026 source draft](https://github.com/lsngchl/Erdos-501) argues for an infinite independent set assuming a countably additive extension of Lebesgue measure to every subset of the line. That **additional measure-extension assumption** has consistency-strength implications. It is not a proof of $P$ from bare ZFC. The Glazer project reports that its random-real route removes the stronger large-cardinal consistency premise.

**Logical distinctions:** a statement may be consistent with ZFC without being provable from ZFC. “Not disprovable” means that its negation is not derivable from the specified base theory, under the relevant consistency assumptions. “Independent” means neither the statement nor its negation follows from that theory. Neither label means that a finite sample, an arbitrary model, or every intended mathematical universe has the same truth value.
