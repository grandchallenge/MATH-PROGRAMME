## The question in plain language

Fix k>=3. For sufficiently large n, does every n-vertex graph with 2n-2 edges and with every proper INDUCED subgraph having minimum degree <=2 necessarily contain a cycle of length exactly k? These are degree-3-critical graphs. The universal all-k claim is DISPROVED, but the restriction to even k remains OPEN.

## A small example

For K4, every proper induced subgraph is K3 or smaller and therefore has minimum degree <=2, but K4 has 6 edges while 2n-2=6 and cycles of length 3 and 4, not 23. Small K4 does NOT test the 'sufficiently large n' counterexample; use only as exact structural fixture.

## What is known

Maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/815, read 2026-10-14: Erdős–Faudree–Gyárfás–Schelp [EFGS88] prove C3,C4,C5 present for all such n>=5, plus cycle of length at least floor(log_2 n); Erdős 1991 attributes cycles 3..6 to himself and Hajnal. Narins–Pokrovskiy–Szabó [NPS17], *Graphs without proper subgraphs of minimum degree 3 and short cycles*, Combinatorica 2017, 495–519, construct arbitrarily large examples with no C23. The all-even-k strengthening remains an open separate question. Full original papers not replayed.

The counterexample excludes the specific odd cycle $C_{23}$ for arbitrarily large admissible orders. This is consistent with the historical guarantee of **some** cycle of length at least $\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor$: a long cycle need not have length 23. The restriction to every fixed EVEN $k$ is a distinct open claim, not an automatic consequence of the published negative theorem.

The extremal hypotheses require exactly $2n-2$ edges, and the induced subgraph condition concerns every proper vertex subset. A statement that some cycle has length at least $\lfloor\log_2n\rfloor$ does not imply existence of a cycle of any prescribed length. The known forbidden $C_{23}$ examples are consistent with that logarithmic-long-cycle theorem; the even-length restriction remains open.
