## The question in plain language

An *antichain* is a family of subsets of ${1,\ldots,n}$ in which no two different members contain one another. Ordinarily, Sperner's theorem asks how many members an antichain can have. Erdős and Trotter asked a different question: how many **different sizes** can occur if every occurring size must appear at least $r$ times?

Write $g(n,r)$ for the largest number of occupied sizes. For every fixed $r\geq2$, large enough $n$ permits $n-3$ sizes, but not $n-2$. What is the threshold $n_0(r)$ after which that is always possible? A 2026 paper settles its asymptotic behavior and two exact cases, but leaves the precise threshold open in general.

## A small example

When $n=4$ and $r=2$, take the two sets $\{1,2\}$ and $\{3,4\}$. They are incomparable, both have size two, and so form a valid antichain with one occupied level of multiplicity two.

In contrast, $\{1\}$ and $\{1,3\}$ are comparable and cannot belong to the same antichain, even though their sizes differ. The desired construction must occupy many levels *without* creating any inclusion between members on those levels. This is why choosing many subsets of each cardinality is not enough.

## What is known

Yixin He and Quanyu Tang, [*An Erdős–Trotter problem on antichains with multiplicity $r$ on each occurring level*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09803), arXiv:2602.09803v2 (21 March 2026), state on pp. 1–2 that

$$

n_0(2)=3,\qquad n_0(3)=8,

$$

and prove in Theorems 1.4 and 1.5, for $r\geq4$,

$$

2r+2\leq n_0(r)\leq 2r+2\log_2 r+O(\log_2\log_2 r).

$$

In particular,

$$

\frac{n_0(r)}{r}\longrightarrow 2.

$$

This answers the **leading-order growth** question while not determining each exact integer $n_0(r)$ for $r\geq4$. A crucial point is the definition of threshold: $n_0(r)$ is the smallest integer such that **every** larger $n$ admits the target family, not merely one favorable value of $n$.

The paper records a further source distinction on p. 2, Remark 1.2. The historical Erdős–Trotter formulation required exactly $r$ sets at each occupied level, whereas the Guy/maintained-catalogue version says at least $r$. These yield the same extremal $g(n,r)$: from an antichain with at least $r$ sets at a level, select any $r$ of them and discard the rest. Removing sets cannot create an inclusion relation, and no occupied size disappears. This proves equivalence without guessing.

## What remains

Build a bounded SAT or backtracking oracle for $g(n,r)$, checking the antichain condition and cardinality multiplicities, and compare small cases with the paper's Appendix A. Verify the equivalence of exactly and at least $r$ independently. Audit the full 1981 original and the computation behind $n_0(3)=8$. No finite list of successful $n$ establishes the eventual-for-every $n$ threshold.
