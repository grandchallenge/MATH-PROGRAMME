## The question in plain language

An infinite increasing set $A=\{a_1<a_2<\cdots\}$ of positive integers is **admissible** if, whenever two finite subsets have the same sum, they also have the same cardinality. Equivalently, the restricted sumsets $S_r(A)$ formed by adding exactly $r$ **distinct** members are disjoint for distinct values of $r$.

[Erdős Problem #875](https://www.erdosproblems.com/875) asks how densely such an infinite sequence can be packed, particularly for which exponents $c$ it is possible to achieve

$$
a_{n+1}-a_n\le n^c.
$$

A polynomial upper bound on *individual gaps* is stronger than a bound on the sequence's average growth. Whether the inequality must hold for every positive index or only sufficiently large indices is an important source-fidelity distinction.

## A small example

For finite sets, a surprisingly dense example is the terminal interval $T_{N,k}=\{N-k+1,\ldots,N\}$. It is admissible exactly when $(k+1)^2\le4N+1$, giving a terminal size of $\lfloor\sqrt{4N+1}\rfloor-1$. For $N=20$ the eight integers $13,\ldots,20$ satisfy the rule.

However, an infinite admissible sequence cannot simply be made by joining independently admissible finite terminal blocks: an equality involving numbers from different blocks might use different numbers of summands on its two sides. Controlling all such cross-block equations is the central construction problem.

Our finite verification fixture, governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_875_876.py, independently checks the terminal criterion and the finite extremal values through $N=36$. Finite checks do not certify the infinite construction.

## What is known

Erdős, Nicolas and Sárközy, [*Sommes de sous-ensembles*](https://www.numdam.org/article/JTNB_1991__3_1_55_0.pdf), *Journal de Théorie des Nombres de Bordeaux* **3** (1991), pp.55–72, Theorem 2, printed p.65, constructed an infinite admissible set satisfying

$$
A(x)=\bigl|\{a\in A:a\le x\}\bigr|\gg x^{5-2\sqrt6}.
$$

This is a proven polynomial-density result. It does not, by itself, give a uniform bound on every individual gap. The printed primary page was inspected directly.

Deshouillers and Freiman, [*On an Additive Problem of Erdős and Straus, 2*](https://www.numdam.org/item/AST_1999__258__141_0.pdf), *Astérisque* **258** (1999), Theorem 1, show that, for sufficiently large $N$, every finite admissible subset of $[1,N]$ has at most $\sqrt{4N+1}-1$ members. Applying this to initial segments of an infinite admissible set yields

$$
a_n\ge\left(\frac14-o(1)\right)n^2.
$$

A May 2026 [Erdős Problems #875 discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/875) reports a constructed infinite admissible set with absolute gaps $o(n^{3+2\sqrt2})$ and a claimed Lean formalization, including a later update addressing the all-index pointwise statement. A [July 2026 working report](https://www.erdosproblemaday.com/report/875) analyzes optimality **within the particular carry-block construction framework**. These reports are not an independent GCL build or a universal lower bound on the possible exponent. The general problem remains open.

## GCL's current state

The finite 1999 theorem yields a useful elementary restriction on possible gaps. Suppose, for some $\varepsilon>0$, that the gaps were eventually bounded by $(1/2-\varepsilon)n$. Summing the gaps would give

$$
a_n\le\left(\frac14-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+o(1)\right)n^2,
$$

contradicting the lower bound obtained above. Therefore every infinite admissible sequence satisfies

$$
\boxed{\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n+1}-a_n}{n}\ge\frac12.}
$$

In particular, no inequality $a_{n+1}-a_n\le n^c$ with $c<1$ can hold eventually. The endpoint exponent $c=1$ is **not** excluded by this argument, because $n$ is larger than the demonstrated obstruction $n/2$.

This deduction is a rigorous consequence of published finite extremal theory, not a resolution of the best possible gap exponent. The exact finite fixture also verifies that the construction in #874 and the weaker subset-sum-free condition in #876 must not be confused.

## What remains

A bounded successor is to verify the 2026 *carry-block* construction, not to infer its claimed exponent as the universal optimum. Inputs: the exact block and carry invariants from the posted construction, a pinned source and Lean dependency revision, and the original distinct-subset-sum admissibility predicate. Target: prove that the union of all stages remains admissible, and that the gaps meet the stated eventual little-o bound and separately asserted all-index bound.

Acceptance requires a source-locked construction, an independently rerun Lean build with a literal axiom report, and exact finite regressions of the first several stages. A counterexample consisting of two finite subsets of different cardinalities with equal sums invalidates admissibility; a single index violating the claimed pointwise gap bound invalidates that stronger quantifier. Stop at the specific construction's certified exponent. A claim of optimality for all possible admissible sequences requires a new general obstruction theorem.
