## The question in plain language

Write the primes in increasing order, $p_1=2,p_2=3,p_3=5,\ldots$. Choose an integer $n$ between two consecutive primes,

$$
p_{k-1}\le n<p_k,
$$

and inspect the prime factors of $n!+1$. Could it happen infinitely often that **all** these prime factors belong to the very short list $\{p_k,p_{k+1}\}$?

The Erdős–Stewart question, [Erdős Problem #1058](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1058), asks whether only finitely many such $n$ exist. A permitted prime need not actually occur: equivalently one allows

$$
n!+1=p_k^a p_{k+1}^b,\qquad a,b\ge0.
$$

## A small example

For the first few integers,

$$
\begin{aligned}
1!+1&=2,\\
2!+1&=3,\\
3!+1&=7,\\
4!+1&=25=5^2,\\
5!+1&=121=11^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

These factorizations were independently verified. For $n=2,3,4,5$, each $n!+1$ uses only the two primes allowed by the relevant consecutive-prime interval. The $n=1$ entry requires an explicit indexing convention, such as $p_0=1$, because the usual prime sequence starts at $p_1=2$. A rigorous statement must not silently overlook that endpoint.

## What is known

**The question is solved in the affirmative.** Florian Luca's paper *On a Conjecture of Erdős and Stewart*, *Mathematics of Computation* **70** (2001), 893–896, proves that there are no solutions with $n\ge6$ under the stated adjacent-prime-factor restrictions. The main result is stated on printed page 893.

The proof uses more than elementary factorization. It includes constraints from valuations and lower bounds for linear forms, together with finite computational exclusions. The existence and scope of the published result are documented; GCL has not independently replayed its difficult analytic or computational components.
