## The question in plain language

How far after an integer $n$ must we look to encounter another integer having **no small prime divisor relative to the distance traveled**? Write $p(r)$ for the least prime factor of $r\geq2$. The first part of [Erdős #680](https://www.erdosproblems.com/680) asks whether every sufficiently large $n$ has some positive $k$ for which $p(n+k)>k^2+1$. Here $n+k$ may itself be prime. A second, sharper question asks whether a threshold of the form $\exp((1+\varepsilon)\sqrt{k})+C_{\varepsilon}$ is too demanding in a precise quantified sense. Both questions are OPEN.

## A small example

Let $n=10$ and $k=1$. Then $n+k=11$ is prime, so its least prime factor is $11$, larger than $1^2+1=2$. A composite example is $n=8,k=1$, where $n+k=9$ has least prime factor $3>2$. A finite witness is easy to inspect but tells us nothing about **every sufficiently large** starting integer.

## What is known

For a prime number $r$, $p(r)=r$. For a composite $r$, one always has $p(r)\leq\sqrt r$. Thus searching for a witness can use prime occurrences or much rarer *rough composites*. Erdős noted an implication of strong conjectures about prime gaps, attributed on the maintained page to Cramér's heuristic, but these gap models and Granville's refined correction are not unconditional proofs. The second part of #680 is a proposed upper boundary for the possible least prime factors. Its logical negation of an eventual existential assertion cannot be replaced by a single small numerical counterexample. **Problem #681 adds the explicit requirement that $n+k$ be composite**, and must not be substituted for this problem.

## What remains

A bounded next task is to turn one explicitly stated hypothesis on gaps between consecutive primes into a correct implication for the first #680 question, keeping track of the choice of $k$ and its relation to $n$. Exact trial-division searches can validate finite witnesses and falsify a proposed short-range lemma; they cannot establish asymptotic universality. Critical source review, protected publication, deployed MathJax inspection and exact-hash V2 admission are outstanding.
