## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1145](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1145) concerns the following mathematical question and its exact quantifiers:

$$
r_{A,B}(n)=\#\{(a,b)\in A\times B:a+b=n\},\qquad \frac{a_n}{b_n}\to1.
$$

Two infinite positive-integer sets A={a_1<a_2<...}, B={b_1<b_2<...} with a_n/b_n->1 form an ASYMPTOTIC additive basis A+B (all sufficiently large n are representable). Erdős–Sárközy ask if r(n)=(1_A*1_B)(n), the count of ordered pairs (a,b) from A×B with a+b=n, must be unbounded, i.e. limsup_n r(n)=infinity. Requiring ALL positive n is impossible with A,B positive because 1 not in A+B; site corrected to eventually.

## A small example

For A=B=positive integers, a_n/b_n=1 and r(n)=n-1 for all n>=2. Conversely A0={nonnegative numbers with only even-position binary digits}, B0={nonnegative numbers with only odd-position digits}: every n from 0..63 has EXACTLY ONE representation A0+B0, verified exhaustively via six binary digits; A0/B0 do not have matching index asymptotics. Thus neither example disproves the conjecture.
