## The question in plain language

An **ordinary line** of a finite set of distinct planar points \(A\subset\mathbb R^2\) is a line containing **exactly two points of the entire set \(A\)**. Fix integers \(r,k\ge2\). Suppose \(|A|=n\), with no \(k\) points of \(A\) on one line. How many ordinary lines force an \(r\)-point subset \(A'\subseteq A\) such that **every line joining two points of \(A'\)** is ordinary with respect to the original set \(A\)?

Erdős asked whether the smallest forcing threshold \(f_{r,k}(n)\) is \(o(n^2)\), perhaps even \(O(n)\), for fixed \(r,k\) as \(n\to\infty\). **The proposed subquadratic behavior is false for every \(r\ge3,\ k\ge4\)**. A 2026 construction gives a quadratic lower bound, while Turán's theorem gives a quadratic upper bound. The resulting order is \(\Theta_{r,k}(n^2)\) in this nontrivial parameter range. [Erdős Problem #960](https://www.erdosproblems.com/960).

## A small concrete example

Take
\[
A=\{(0,0),(1,0),(2,0),(0,1)\}.
\]
Exactly three points lie on the horizontal line \(y=0\), so the set satisfies the hypothesis of **no four collinear points**. The horizontal line is not ordinary. The three lines connecting \((0,1)\) to the three horizontal points are all ordinary: each contains exactly the stated two points of \(A\).

Thus the associated ordinary-line graph is the star \(K_{1,3}\). It has three edges and **no triangle**, so no triple of points has all three joining lines ordinary in the original set. This checks the combinatorial mechanism but is **not** an asymptotic counterexample. The 2026 theorem supplies configurations for arbitrarily large \(n\).

## The exact ordinary-line graph reduction

Define a simple graph \(G_A\) on vertex set \(A\) by
\[
\{p,q\}\in E(G_A)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{the complete line through \(p,q\) contains exactly two points of \(A\).}
\]
Each ordinary line corresponds to one unordered pair and hence exactly one graph edge. A subset of \(r\) vertices determines only ordinary lines **with respect to all of \(A\)** exactly when it spans a \(K_r\) in \(G_A\). The condition cannot be checked merely inside \(A'\): any \(r\) generic noncollinear points have pair-lines ordinary relative to themselves, which would make the original question trivial.

For \(n\ge r\), define the integer forcing threshold precisely by
\[
f_{r,k}(n)=1+\max\bigl\{|E(G_A)|:|A|=n,\
A\text{ has no \(k\) collinear points},\
G_A\text{ contains no }K_r\bigr\}
\]
whenever the maximization class is nonempty. If it is empty, the forcing property is vacuous and the threshold requires a separate convention. This is the useful asymptotic formulation.

## The general upper bound

By Turán's theorem, a \(K_r\)-free graph on \(n\) vertices has at most
\[
\left(1-\frac{1}{r-1}\right)\frac{n^2}{2}
\]
edges. Applied to \(G_A\), this yields
\[
f_{r,k}(n)\le
\left\lfloor\left(1-\frac{1}{r-1}\right)\frac{n^2}{2}\right\rfloor+1.
\]
The estimate is universal and requires no special planar geometry beyond the graph interpretation. For \(r=3\), it is at most \(\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor+1\).

This does **not** alone settle Erdős's question: an ordinary-line graph is a very special geometric graph, and might in principle have substantially fewer edges when \(K_r\)-free than an arbitrary extremal graph.

## The decisive 2026 construction

Boris Alexeev, Moe Putterman, Mehtaab Sawhney, Mark Sellke and Gregory Valiant, *Short proofs in combinatorics, probability and number theory II*, [arXiv:2604.06609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06609), Theorem 2.1 and its subsequent propositions, provide the missing geometric lower bound. Their construction, for \(n\ge72\), uses a structured subset of points of a nonsingular real elliptic curve. It has **no four collinear points**, but its ordinary-line graph is bipartite (hence triangle-free) and has at least
\[
\frac{n^2}{12}-\frac{10}{3}n
\]
edges. In particular, for every fixed \(r\ge3\) and \(k\ge4\), the configuration satisfies the forbidden-\(K_r\) condition and the no-\(k\)-collinear restriction, and therefore
\[
f_{r,k}(n)\ge \frac{n^2}{12}-O(n).
\]
Combined with Turán's estimate, this proves \(f_{r,k}(n)=\Theta(n^2)\) throughout the nontrivial range.

The proof builds a cyclic finite subgroup of an elliptic curve, uses the group law to control collinear triples, removes one congruence class modulo seven, and adjusts the cardinality to reach the desired \(n\). It is not legitimate to assert that **every** elliptic-curve point configuration works: the subgroup, chosen deletions, incidence and edge-count lemmas are central. The author-hosted preprint attributes discovery of the five proofs to an internal OpenAI model, with the named authors responsible for the mathematical write-up. This is provenance information, **not** an independent GCL proof receipt. [Secondary theorem index](https://erdosproblems.ai/wiki/library/discrete_geometry/alexeev_2026_short_proofs_combinatorics_probability_number_theory).

## Exceptional parameter ranges

When \(k=3\), no three points are collinear, so **every** line through two points of \(A\) is ordinary. For \(n\ge r\), every \(r\)-point subset works, and the lower construction requiring three-point collinearity does not apply. For \(r=2\), a single ordinary line supplies the desired pair, so this is a qualitatively different trivial threshold question. Therefore the headline \(\Theta(n^2)\) conclusion is deliberately restricted to \(r\ge3,k\ge4\).
