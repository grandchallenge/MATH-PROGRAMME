## The question in plain language

A strongly sum-free set has no element equal to the sum of any collection of distinct other members. Avoiding ordinary three-term Schur relations alone is not sufficient.

Let l(n) guarantee a subset B of any n-element A subset Z with no equality a1=a2+...+ar where ALL participating terms are DISTINCT members of B. This is the strong subset-sum-free property; ordinary Schur sum-free sets only rule out r=2, not arbitrary r. The original text explicitly requires distinctness.

## A small example

The set {1,2,4} satisfies the condition, but {1,2,3} does not because 1+2=3. The set {1,2,4,7} also fails due to 1+2+4=7, a relation missed by a two-term-only test.

Finite example B={1,2,4}: 1+2=3 not in B, so the three-element set meets the strong property. B={1,2,3}: 1+2=3 with three distinct terms violates it. B={1,2,4,7}: 1+2+4=7 violates even though its pairwise sums may miss B. All three are exact fixtures and must not be conflated with sum-free in the two-summand sense.
