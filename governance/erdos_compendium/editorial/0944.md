## The question in plain language

Let \(k\ge4\) and \(r\ge1\). A graph is **vertex-critical** of chromatic number \(k\) if \(\chi(G)=k\) and deleting any vertex lowers its chromatic number. A *critical set of edges* is a set whose deletion lowers \(\chi(G)\). Erdős asked whether, for each \(k,r\), some vertex-critical graph has **no critical edge set of size at most \(r\)**. Equivalently, the graph remains \(k\)-chromatic after deletion of any at most \(r\) edges.

**Current boundary:** Skottova and Steiner (2025) proved existence for every \(k\ge5\) and \(r\ge1\). The remaining open case is \(k=4\), even for \(r=1\). [Source statement and research history](https://www.erdosproblems.com/944).

## A simple illustration

The complete graph \(K_k\) is \(k\)-vertex-critical. However deleting even one edge permits the two endpoints to share a color, so \(\chi(K_k-e)=k-1\). Consequently \(K_k\) **fails** the required edge robustness for every \(r\ge1\). Vertex-critical does not mean edge-robust: the problem asks for a graph that is simultaneously maximally sensitive to vertex deletion and resistant to many edge deletions.

For the quantifiers it is enough to test deletion sets of **exactly** \(r\) edges when a graph has at least \(r\) edges: if removing a smaller set lowers chromatic number, then removing any superset also does. Nevertheless the original definition includes all sizes at most \(r\), which is safer for finite verifiers and small exceptional graphs.

## What is known

This extends a 1970 conjecture of Dirac, corresponding to \(r=1\). Brown [Br92] gave an example for \(k=5\). Lattanzio [La02] established many more \(k\), and Jensen [Je02] gave a construction for all \(k\ge5\) in the \(r=1\) case.

Martinsson and Steiner [MaSt25] established the general \(r\) assertion when \(k\) is sufficiently large as a function of \(r\). Skottova and Steiner [SkSt25], *Critical edge sets in vertex-critical graphs*, arXiv:2508.08703, strengthened this to all \(k\ge5\), with no restriction on \(r\). Thus a manuscript which merely labels all \(k\ge4\) cases open would be obsolete.

Erdős also asked a quantitative question. Let \(f_k(n)\) be the largest \(r\) for which an \(n\)-vertex \(k\)-vertex-critical graph resists deletion of every edge set of size at most \(r\). The precise existence of \(f_k(n)\) at each \(n\) needs the definition to account for the possibility of no eligible graph; take the maximum over the admissible class where nonempty. Skottova and Steiner proved for \(k\ge5\) bounds of the form
\[
n^{1/3}\ll_k f_k(n)\ll_k \frac{n}{(\log n)^C}
\]
for an absolute \(C>0\), according to the maintained problem record. The corresponding \(k=4\) question remains open.

## Why the problem is difficult

Vertex-criticality requires that *every* vertex contributes essentially to the chromatic obstruction. Edge robustness requires that this obstruction persists under many different deletions. Naively gluing several \(k\)-chromatic subgraphs creates redundant vertices, often destroying vertex-criticality; taking complete graphs preserves vertex-criticality but destroys edge robustness.

## GCL's current state

This is a literature-grounded article, not a new construction or mathematical certification of [SkSt25]. The exact residual obligation is the \(k=4\) case. A bounded verifier should implement exact chromatic-number search for small graphs, confirm vertex-criticality after all single-vertex deletions, and test every edge-deletion set up to an explicit \(r\). Exhaustion at finite order cannot prove nonexistence for all graph orders.
