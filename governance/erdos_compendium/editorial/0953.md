## The question in plain language

Let $D_r=\{x\in\mathbb R^2:|x|<r\}$. What is the largest planar Lebesgue measure of a **measurable** set $A\subset D_r$ such that the Euclidean distance between any two **distinct** points of $A$ is never a positive integer? Define

$$
M(r)=\sup\{|A|: A\subset D_r\text{ measurable},\ 
 |a-b|\notin\mathbb Z \text{ for all distinct }a,b\in A\}.
$$

The catalogue attributes this problem jointly to **Erdős and Sárközy**. It labels the sharp growth question open, with an established upper estimate $M(r)=O(r)$ and a lower estimate $M(r)\gg_\varepsilon r^{1/2-\varepsilon}$, the latter attributed to adapting Sárközy's work on the related #466. [Erdős Problem #953](https://www.erdosproblems.com/953).

## A small example

*A simple admissible construction*

Any open disk of radius strictly smaller than $1/2$ has diameter less than one, so no two points in it are an integer distance apart. For $r>0$, take a centered disk of radius $s=\min(r/2,1/4)$. It lies inside $D_r$ and has area $\pi s^2>0$. This gives an elementary admissible example but only a bounded area as $r$ grows, far short of the known power-law lower bounds.

The exclusion includes distance **one** and every larger integer up to $2r$; checking only unit distances is too weak. Since the points are distinct, the zero distance between a point and itself is irrelevant.

## Scaling and an upper-bound caution

The full ambient disk has area $\pi r^2$, which is a trivial quadratic bound. The much stronger recorded $O(r)$ upper estimate is a mathematical result, not the area of the ambient disk. Any further claimed $O(\sqrt r)$ estimate would be stronger still and would essentially match the known lower exponent up to subpolynomial factors.

In a May 2026 discussion, Allen Hart reported a **Lean formalization** of an $O(\sqrt r)$ upper bound, pointing to a public repository. The maintained catalogue nevertheless still describes the trivial upper bound as $O(r)$ and classifies the question as open. Accordingly, the stronger claim is **unadjudicated source evidence**, not a GCL-verified theorem. Before updating the editorial status one must independently inspect the exact theorem, underlying geometric lemmas, permitted axioms, Mathlib pin, and a reproducible Lean build. [Discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/953).

## Why the problem is difficult

Forbidden distances constrain every pair of points, while area measures a continuum set. One can spread small disconnected components far apart, but their **cross-distances** must also avoid every integer. Local packing arguments alone do not ensure those cross-distance constraints. Conversely, proving upper bounds requires understanding restrictions at many radial scales; a bound derived only from one forbidden distance need not be sharp.

The size is a supremum over measurable sets, so a finite sample of points carries measure zero and cannot directly witness a positive lower bound. A numerical visualization of a finite configuration needs positive-radius neighborhoods with analytically verified cross-distance intervals before it implies any area estimate.

## What is known

The primary catalogue records $M(r)=O(r)$. Koizumi and Kovac observed in its discussion that Sárközy's lower construction, associated with [Problem #466](https://www.erdosproblems.com/466), yields

$$
M(r)\gg_\varepsilon r^{1/2-\varepsilon}\qquad(\varepsilon>0).
$$

The constants may depend on $\varepsilon$, and the bound is asymptotic. The strongest published and independently certified upper estimate has not been established here beyond the catalogue's $O(r)$ statement.

## GCL's current state

The small-disc example is proved from the diameter bound. The literature lower estimate and $O(r)$ bound are attributed. The later reported $O(\sqrt r)$ theorem and formalization remain an **explicit review exception**, not silently accepted mathematical certification.
