## The question in plain language

Write a positive integer as a sum of *unit fractions*, such as $1/2+1/3+1/6=1$. [Erdős Problem #45](https://www.erdosproblems.com/45) asks whether, for every number of colours $k\ge2$, there is one integer $n_k$ such that every colouring of its **nontrivial proper divisors** into $k$ colours contains a monochromatic collection with reciprocal sum exactly one.

The mathematical answer is **yes**, following from a theorem of Ernest Croot. The integer $n_k$ must be chosen before seeing the colouring.

## A small example

For $n_k=12$, the nontrivial proper divisors are $2,3,4,6$. If 2, 3 and 6 happen to share a colour, then

$$
\frac12+\frac13+\frac16=1.
$$

This verifies one colour configuration, not every possible colouring; the full theorem requires a special sufficiently large integer chosen as a function of $k$.

## What is known

Croot, *On a coloring conjecture about unit fractions*, *Annals of Mathematics* **157** (2003), 545–556, proved that for any colouring of a suitable finite interval of denominators $[2,b^k]$, one colour contains distinct denominators whose reciprocals sum to one. Choose $n_k$ divisible by every integer in that interval, and large enough that each is a **proper** divisor. Any colouring of the appropriate divisors restricts to a colouring of the interval, giving #45.

The maintained registry reports a doubly exponential scale for the optimal $n_k$, with lower estimates discussed by Mehtaab Sawhney. Those sharper estimates have not been independently reconstructed here.

## What remains

Nothing remains to settle the existence question. Formalizing the exact interval-to-divisor reduction and verifying a cited external proof are separate documentary or certification tasks, not a new solution.
