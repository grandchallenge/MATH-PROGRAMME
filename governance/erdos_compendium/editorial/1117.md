## The question in plain language

An entire function $f$ reaches its greatest absolute value somewhere on each circle $|z|=r$. Usually only a few points on the circle achieve that maximum.

[Erdős Problem #1117](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1117) asks how many such maximizing points there can be for an entire function that is **not a monomial**. Let

$$
\nu(r)=\#\{z:|z|=r,\ |f(z)|=M(r)\},
\qquad M(r)=\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|.
$$

Two questions must be distinguished: can $\limsup_{r\to\infty}\nu(r)$ be infinite, and can the stronger $\liminf_{r\to\infty}\nu(r)$ be infinite?

## A small example

For $f(z)=1+z^2$, write $z=re^{it}$. The triangle inequality gives

$$
|1+r^2e^{2it}|\le1+r^2,
$$

with equality precisely when $e^{2it}=1$. Thus for **every** $r>0$ the maximizing points are $z=r$ and $z=-r$, and

$$
\nu(r)=2.
$$

A function with a constant number of maximizers is a useful check of the definition, but neither confirms nor refutes possible unbounded growth for other functions.

## What is known

The first question has a **positive answer**, attributed to **Herzog and Piranian (1968)**.

The second, stronger question remains **open**. The maintained catalogue describes a 2024 result of **Glücksam and Pardo-Simón** as an *approximate* affirmative answer, but that does not automatically imply the exact liminf assertion.

The logical difference is substantial. An unbounded limsup only requires arbitrarily large peaks in $\nu(r)$; unbounded liminf requires the number of maximizers to remain arbitrarily large at **every sufficiently large radius**.

## What remains

Review Herzog–Piranian's construction and the precise theorem established in 2024. Determine exactly what “approximate” means, without converting an approximation into the unresolved liminf theorem. Formalize and test maximizing-point counts for selected entire functions.
