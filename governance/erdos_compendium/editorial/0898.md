## The question in plain language

Draw any nondegenerate triangle with vertices $A,B,C$, and choose a point $P$ strictly inside it. Compare the sum of the three distances from $P$ to the *vertices* with twice the sum of its perpendicular distances to the *three side lines*:

$$
PA+PB+PC\ \ge\ 2(d_A+d_B+d_C).
$$

Here $d_A$ is the perpendicular distance from $P$ to the **line containing** side $BC$, with $d_B$ and $d_C$ defined cyclically. This is the celebrated **Erdős–Mordell inequality**, [Erdős Problem #898](https://www.erdosproblems.com/898). The distinction between a supporting line and a line segment matters for obtuse triangles: a perpendicular foot may lie outside the side segment, although the corresponding distance to the supporting line is unambiguous.

The inequality is **proved**. Equality occurs exactly when the triangle is equilateral and $P$ is its centre. The problem is not an open geometry conjecture.

## A small example

Let $A=(0,0)$, $B=(3,0)$, $C=(0,4)$ and $P=(1,1)$. Since $1/3+1/4<1$, $P$ lies strictly inside the right triangle. The three vertex distances are

$$
PA=\sqrt2,\qquad PB=\sqrt5,\qquad PC=\sqrt{10}.
$$

The two perpendicular distances to the coordinate-axis sides are $1$ each. The remaining side line has equation $4x+3y=12$, so its distance from $P$ is $|4+3-12|/5=1$. The inequality becomes

$$
\sqrt2+\sqrt5+\sqrt{10}\ge6,
$$

which holds strictly.

For an equilateral triangle of side length $2$, the centre is at distance $2/\sqrt3$ from each vertex and $1/\sqrt3$ from each side line. The two sides of the inequality are then equal, both being $2\sqrt3$.

## What is known

The problem was proposed by Erdős and solved by Louis J. Mordell, in work also associated with Donald F. Barrow. A version appeared as American Mathematical Monthly **Problem 3740**, proposed in 1935, with a published Mordell–Barrow solution in 1937. The historical source cited by the maintained catalogue, [Er82e, p.61], reports an earlier 1932 conjecture date; this date should not be conflated with the documented 1935 *Monthly* proposal.

A standard detailed mathematical reference is [*Notes on Euclidean Geometry*, §9.4, Theorem 9.3](https://people.math.rochester.edu/faculty/dangeba/geom-080399.pdf). It proves a stronger *side-weighted* set of inequalities and identifies the exact equality conditions. Numerous later elementary proofs exist. The maintained [Erdős catalogue #898](https://www.erdosproblems.com/898) classifies the classical inequality as **PROVED (LEAN)**.

## GCL's current state

There is an efficient route from a stronger vertex-wise lemma to the Erdős–Mordell inequality. Write

$$
a=|BC|,\qquad b=|CA|,\qquad c=|AB|
$$

and $p=PA$, $q=PB$, $r=PC$. The following side-weighted inequalities hold for every interior $P$:

$$
ap\ge b\,d_C+c\,d_B,\qquad
bq\ge a\,d_C+c\,d_A,\qquad
cr\ge a\,d_B+b\,d_A.
$$

One can establish the first by comparing the segment joining the perpendicular feet from $P$ to $AB$ and $AC$ with its projection onto the direction of $BC$, and obtain the other two cyclically. Dividing by the corresponding side lengths and adding gives

$$
p+q+r\ge
d_A\left(\frac bc+\frac cb\right)
+d_B\left(\frac ca+\frac ac\right)
+d_C\left(\frac ab+\frac ba\right).
$$

Each parenthesized ratio sum is at least $2$ by AM–GM, proving the desired result. This intermediate inequality explains *why* the factor two is universal and why an equilateral triangle is necessary for equality.

The executable exact fixture at governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_898_899.py checks a squared, denominator-cleared form of each side-weighted lemma for 2,148 noncollinear integer-coordinate triangles and all 27 positive barycentric weight triples drawn from $\{1,2,3\}^3$. This comprises **173,988 exact rational-geometry cases**, using only integer dot/cross products and exact inequalities. It also records the algebraic equilateral equality example. These checks are regressions for the lemma, not a substitute for a proof over all real triangles.
