## The question in plain language

A Sidon set has no repeated unordered sums. Erdős and Freud considered an unusual relaxation: allow **at most one particular integer** to have multiple representations as $a+b$, with $a\le b$ taken from a set $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$. That exceptional sum may have more than two representations. Every other sum must be represented at most once.

How large can $A$ be? [Erdős Problem #864](https://www.erdosproblems.com/864) asks whether the maximum has asymptotic size

$$
F(N)\sim\frac{2}{\sqrt3}\sqrt N.
$$

The hypothesis does not bound the *number of collisions* at the single exceptional sum; it bounds the **number of distinct sums at which collisions occur**. Confusing those restrictions changes the problem.

## A small example

The eight-element set

$$
A=\{1,2,4,8,13,17,19,20\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,20\}
$$

satisfies the condition. Its only sum with multiple unordered representations is $21$, represented in four ways:

$$
21=1+20=2+19=4+17=8+13.
$$

All its other unordered pair sums, including doubles such as $1+1$ and $20+20$, are unique. An exhaustive search finds $F(20)=8$. In particular, all $\binom{20}{9}=167{,}960$ nine-element subsets were independently checked and none satisfied the one-exception condition.

This is a finite reproduction of known calculations, not a resolution of the asymptotic conjecture.

## What is known

The construction behind the proposed constant comes from [Erdős and Freud, *On sums of a Sidon-sequence*, *Journal of Number Theory* **38** (1991), 196–205](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-314X(91)90083-N). Choose a large Sidon set $B\subseteq[1,N/3]$ with

$$
|B|=\left(\frac1{\sqrt3}+o(1)\right)\sqrt N,
$$

and form $A=B\cup(N-B)$, with $N-B=\{N-b:b\in B\}$. The two parts are disjoint for large $N$. Sums within one part are unique by the Sidon property, and cross sums equal $N+(b_i-b_j)$.

For distinct $i,j$, differences of a Sidon set are unique; when $i=j$, however, every pair $(b_i,N-b_i)$ gives the **same exceptional sum** $N$. Hence the construction is admissible and proves

$$
F(N)\ge\left(\frac2{\sqrt3}+o(1)\right)\sqrt N.
$$

The original problem is restated in [Erdős (1992), pp.39–40](https://hrj.episciences.org/125/pdf).

An elementary upper bound is $F(N)\le(\sqrt2+o(1))\sqrt N$. If $\sigma$ is the exceptional repeated sum, elements below $\sigma/2$ form a Sidon set: sums of such elements are less than $\sigma$. Likewise elements above $\sigma/2$ form a Sidon set. After assigning or deleting at most one midpoint element, each side has Sidon cardinality bounded by the square root of its interval length, up to lower-order terms. The two square roots sum to at most $\sqrt{2N}$, which is weaker than the proposed $2\sqrt{N/3}$.

The maintained catalogue continues to mark #864 **open**. No published proof of the proposed sharp coefficient has been identified in the checked primary sources.

## GCL's current state

The frozen source packet and the maintained [#864 statement](https://www.erdosproblems.com/864) agree on the single exceptional sum condition. The 1991 primary paper supplies the mirrored Sidon lower construction; the 1992 paper explicitly asks whether it is asymptotically optimal.

The reproducible program governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_863_864.py counts unordered pairs with $a\le b$, including diagonals. A monotone backtracking search evaluates $F(N)$ for $1\le N\le20$, and a separate combinations search checks the values for $N\le12$ as well as **every** nine-element subset of $[20]$. It confirms the witness above and that the sole repeated sum $21$ has four representations. The finite extremal sequence is not a new result; stronger finite enumerations are publicly recorded.

The best source-grounded research boundary is therefore to improve the asymptotic upper bound, not to infer the asymptotic constant from small $N$.

## What remains

A bounded successor is the *exceptional-sum splitting lemma*: prove rigorously that if $\sigma$ is the only repeated unordered sum, then the sets below and above $\sigma/2$ are ordinary Sidon sets, apart from the one midpoint convention, and derive

$$
F(N)\le(\sqrt2+o(1))\sqrt N.
$$

Input: the exact inclusive-diagonal representation predicate and a standard finite Sidon upper bound. Acceptance: a complete proof or Lean formalization without placeholders, plus independent agreement on $F(20)=8$ and the four-representation witness. Falsification: any admissible $A$ whose lower or upper half contains a repeated sum other than $\sigma$, or a violated finite extremal value. Stop before promoting the unproved coefficient $2/\sqrt3$ to a theorem. Any sharper successor would need a genuine argument linking the two Sidon halves rather than treating them independently.
