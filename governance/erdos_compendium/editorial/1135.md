## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1135](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1135) is the famous **Collatz conjecture**. For any positive integer $n$, apply

$$
f(n)=
\begin{cases}
n/2,&n\text{ even},\\
(3n+1)/2,&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
$$

Does every starting integer $m\ge1$ eventually reach $1$ after some **positive** number of iterations?

No general proof is known. The conjecture remains **open**.

## A small example

The normalized steps from seven begin

$$
7\to11\to17\to26\to13\to20\to10\to5\to8\to4\to2\to1.
$$

An exact integer-arithmetic program verifies that every seed from $1$ through $10,000$ reaches $1$. The largest **first-positive hitting time** observed in that range is 165 steps, for seed $6171$.

The positive-step convention matters for seed $1$: $1\to2\to1$, so $k=2$ is its first positive return. A sweep of finitely many integers, however large, **cannot prove** convergence of every positive integer.

## What is known

The problem originated with **Lothar Collatz** before 1952, rather than with Erdős. Erdős discussed its difficulty, which explains its inclusion in this catalogue.

Substantial mathematical work concerns typical orbits, density estimates and huge computational ranges. In particular, Tao's partial result about almost-all orbits in logarithmic density does **not** establish the universal assertion for every integer.

The commonly repeated claim that Erdős formally offered a $500 prize is historically imprecise: Lagarias records that Erdős **valued** the problem at that amount during conversation with Graham. A prize valuation is not necessarily a formally offered award.

The [frozen formal-conjectures source](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1135.lean) contains an unproved theorem placeholder, **not** a verified proof.
