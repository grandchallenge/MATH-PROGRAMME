## The question in plain language

Let $G$ be a graph on the positive integers $1,2,\ldots,n$ with **no triangle**: there are no three different vertices all pairwise adjacent. Erdős and Hajnal asked whether every sufficiently large such graph must have an **independent** triple of the special arithmetic form

$$
\{a,b,a+b\},
$$

where $a$ and $b$ are **different positive integers**, $a+b\le n$, and all three pairs among $a,b,a+b$ are **nonedges** of $G$.

[Erdős Problem #895](https://www.erdosproblems.com/895) has an affirmative answer. The maintained catalogue attributes the finite threshold $n\ge18$ to a SAT verification by Ben Barber, reported in personal communication. A separate, stronger question attributed to Hajnal asks for larger independent sets of *all nonempty subset sums* (Hindman sets); that generalization remains **open**.

## A small example

Consider the graph on the six integers $1,\ldots,6$ with just five edges

$$
\{1,5\},\quad\{2,3\},\quad\{2,5\},\quad
\{2,6\},\quad\{3,4\}.
$$

This graph contains no triangle. Yet it has **no** independent triple $\{a,b,a+b\}$ with $1\le a<b$ and $a+b\le6$. For each possible arithmetic triple, at least one of its three pairs is an edge. The executable finite verifier checks every triangle and every admissible additive triple to confirm these facts exactly.

This does **not** contradict the proved statement, which applies only when $n$ is sufficiently large. Independent integer-programming runs produce further small counterexample graphs for $n=9$, 12 and 15. The bounded runs at 17 and 18 were not conclusive: both hit a time limit. They provide **no new proof** of the claimed threshold 18.

## What is known

The original problem appears in Erdős, [*On some problems in combinatorial set theory*](https://www.erdosproblems.com/895), *Publications de l'Institut Mathématique (Beograd)*, new series **57**(71) (1995), pp.61–65. The maintained problem entry says Barber established the $n=18$ instance using a SAT solver. That is a computational proof claim, reported by the curator, and the original SAT run and machine-verifiable certificate were not obtained in this GCL execution.

The reduction from a fixed finite threshold to all greater sizes is rigorous and simple. If every triangle-free graph on $\{1,\ldots,18\}$ has a suitable independent arithmetic triple, then every triangle-free graph on $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ for $n\ge18$ does too: restrict to the first 18 vertices and use its triple unchanged. Thus a **certificate of the single finite case $n=18$** suffices.

Since September 2026, external contributors have published Lean and LRAT reconstructions of the finite SAT argument, including a development linked from Formal Conjectures and credited to Boris Alexeev. These are stronger provenance signals than the original personal communication, but GCL has **not** independently fetched, compiled and checked the exact Lean dependencies, theorem statement and axiom output. The pinned protected GCL provider snapshot contains no admitted #895 formal-solution source. Do not confuse an upstream formal proof claim with a protected local certification.

Hajnal's stronger proposal seeks a vertex set containing all the **nonempty subset sums** of a prescribed number of generators and forming an independent set. The affirmative result for only three vertices $a,b,a+b$ does not settle that infinite family of stronger finite-configuration questions.

## GCL's current state

The finite question has a clean **Boolean encoding**. For every unordered pair of distinct vertices introduce a binary variable $e_{u,v}$ indicating whether it is an edge. The triangle-free condition is exactly

$$
e_{a,b}+e_{a,c}+e_{b,c}\le2
\quad\text{for all }a<b<c.
$$

To express the **negation** of the desired result, require at least one graph edge for every admissible arithmetic triple:

$$
e_{a,b}+e_{a,a+b}+e_{b,a+b}\ge1
\quad\text{whenever }1\le a<b,\ a+b\le n.
$$

A satisfying assignment is exactly a **counterexample**: a triangle-free graph with no independent positive distinct-summand additive triple. Proving that this system is **infeasible at $n=18$** settles the general problem by the restriction argument.

The bounded finite program at governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_894_895.py generates this encoding and uses SciPy HiGHS mixed-integer optimization to search for a satisfying assignment. For $n=6,9,12,15$ the returned counterexample edge sets were checked again using exact Python graph predicates. Runs for 17 and 18 reached the solver time limit **without a feasibility or infeasibility certificate**. A solver status alone, even a reported infeasible status, would still need a reproducible SAT proof and a trusted checker to support a formal GCL claim.

## What remains

The bounded successor is a **source-pinned SAT/LRAT proof audit for $n=18$**. Inputs are the exact graph encoding above, a fixed DIMACS CNF with a SHA-256 digest, an UNSAT proof in LRAT or another supported certificate format, an independently trusted checker, and the external Lean theorem's exact commit and compiler/dependency pins.

Acceptance requires an exact source-to-CNF semantic comparison, complete certificate check with deterministic readback, independent reconstruction that the asserted Lean theorem really quantifies over **every** triangle-free graph on 18 labelled vertices, and a no-placeholder compiler run with literal axiom output. Any satisfying 18-vertex graph, a nonmatching encoding of repeated summands, an LRAT verifier failure, or untrusted extra axioms invalidates the proposed certification. The purely mathematical extension to all $n\ge18$ should be proved separately and is elementary.

Preserve the stronger independent-Hindman-set problem as a distinct open successor. Stop before claiming that proof for it from the three-vertex theorem.
