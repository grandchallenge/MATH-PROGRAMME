## The question in plain language

Suppose we know the **largest prime gap** whose preceding prime is below a large number $x$. Is an interval of a few times that length, starting at any nearby point $y$, guaranteed to contain approximately the expected number of primes?

[Erdős Problem #1138](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1138) asks, for $x/2<y<x$, $C>1$ and

$$
d(x)=\max_{p_n<x}(p_{n+1}-p_n),
$$

whether the uniform asymptotic

$$
\pi(y+Cd(x))-\pi(y)\sim\frac{Cd(x)}{\log y}
$$

holds as $x\to\infty$.

The maintained catalogue now labels this statement **DISPROVED (LEAN)**, although its older explanation was not updated to reflect the new result.

## A small example

For $x=5000$, $y=4000$ and $C=2$, direct primality checks yield $d(x)=34$. The actual number of primes between $4000$ and $4068$ is $10$, while the proposed main term is

$$
\frac{68}{\log4000}.
$$

A finite discrepancy does **not** disprove an asymptotic. A disproof must identify an obstruction persisting along arbitrarily large choices of $x$, with the correct quantifiers over $y$ and $C$.

## What is known

A 2026 disproof by **Sunder, Kumrawat and Cheri**, reported in the [problem discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/1138), uses **strict record prime gaps** and a contradiction from two nearby constants $1<C_1<C_2$. The source discussion reports a Lean 4 verification of the central contradiction and a separate Aristotle formalization.

The [current formal-conjectures source](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/main/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1138.lean) labels the original conjecture false and points to an external proof commit. However, the local declaration ends in a proof placeholder, so its linked implementation and dependencies must be checked before GCL can claim a kernel replay.

The older catalogue explanation correctly observes that the proposed interval length is far shorter than ranges accessible using traditional prime-counting estimates. That background does not supersede the newer reported counterexample.
