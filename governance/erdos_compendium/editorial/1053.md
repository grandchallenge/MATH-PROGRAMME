## The question in plain language

For a positive integer $n$, let $\sigma(n)$ denote the sum of its positive divisors. A *multiply-perfect* number satisfies

$$
\sigma(n)=kn
$$

for an integer multiplier $k\ge1$. Ordinary perfect numbers have $k=2$.

[Erdős Problem #1053](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1053) asks whether, among all multiply-perfect integers tending to infinity, the multiplier must satisfy

$$
k=o(\log\log n).
$$

In plain language, $k$ must become negligible compared with the slowly growing quantity $\log\log n$. A constant upper bound on $k$ would be sufficient, but is not known in the required generality. Standard upper bounds of order $\log\log n$ are not strong enough to settle the question.

## A small example

The divisors of $6$ sum to $1+2+3+6=12=2\cdot6$; likewise, $\sigma(28)=56=2\cdot28$. A different multiplier occurs for $120=2^3\cdot3\cdot5$:

$$
\sigma(120)=(1+2+4+8)(1+3)(1+5)
=15\cdot4\cdot6=360=3\cdot120.
$$

We independently verified these divisor sums, cross-checked two divisor-sum implementations for all integers from 1 through 1,000, and enumerated multiply-perfect examples through 10,000. They show that $k$ is not fixed at $2$.

There is also a useful elementary restriction: **the only squarefree multiply-perfect positive integers are $1$ and $6$.** Indeed, for squarefree $n>1$, write $P$ for its largest prime factor. The Euler-product upper bound gives $k=\sigma(n)/n<P$. As $P$ divides $\sigma(n)=\prod_{q\mid n}(q+1)$ but cannot divide $k$, some smaller prime divisor $q$ must satisfy $P\mid q+1$. Necessarily $q+1=P$, which is possible for primes only at $q=2$, $P=3$. Thus $n=6$.

The inequality $k<P$ follows by comparing $\sigma(n)/n$ with the product of $p/(p-1)$ over primes $p\le P$: that product is at most $P$. This classification is a **restricted lemma**, not a resolution of the open problem for integers with repeated prime factors.

## What is known

The canonical [Erdős Problems page](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1053) lists the question as **open** and attributes it to Erdős through Richard Guy's *Unsolved Problems in Number Theory*, problem B2.

Erdős's 1956 paper *On Perfect and Multiply Perfect Numbers* established results about how sparse multiply-perfect numbers are. A bound on the **number** of eligible integers below $x$ does not by itself give an improved bound on the multiplier of each one. The paper's exact comparison with Guy's statement remains subject to primary-source review.

These results support a source-bounded explanation, not a mathematical resolution or formal proof certification.

## What remains

An actual resolution must control multipliers uniformly along arbitrary large multiply-perfect integers, including those with repeated prime factors. The near-term research target is a reproducible divisor-sum enumeration and a primary-source comparison of the 1956 counting theorem, with its quantifiers kept distinct from the desired pointwise bound. Neither computation nor the squarefree lemma can prove the general asymptotic statement.
