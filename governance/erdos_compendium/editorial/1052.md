## The question in plain language

A divisor $d$ of $n$ is *unitary* when $\gcd(d,n/d)=1$. An integer is *unitary perfect* if the sum of its **proper** unitary divisors equals $n$. If $\sigma^*(n)$ is the sum of *all* unitary divisors (including $n$), the condition is

$$
\sigma^*(n)=2n.
$$

[Erdős #1052](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1052) asks whether only finitely many unitary perfect numbers exist. A list of five examples and extensive bounded searches cannot decide a global finiteness assertion.

## A small example

The unitary divisors of 6 are $1,2,3,6$: all satisfy the coprimality requirement. The proper ones sum to $1+2+3=6$, so 6 is unitary perfect.

If $n=\prod_i p_i^{e_i}$ with distinct primes $p_i$, a unitary divisor must take either **all** or **none** of each prime-power component. Consequently,

$$
\sigma^*(n)=\prod_i(1+p_i^{e_i}),
\qquad
n\text{ unitary perfect}\iff
\prod_i(1+p_i^{e_i})=2\prod_i p_i^{e_i}.
$$

It is essential that each factor really be a power of a *single distinct prime*. Arbitrarily grouping composite factors can create false numerical matches.

## What is known

Subbarao and Warren (1966) documented the early unitary perfect numbers; Wall (1975) established the fifth, which does not prove that it is the last. The source record lists $6,60,90,87360$ and $146361946186458562560000$ as five historically known examples. Later Wall papers bound the structures of any further examples, including restrictions on odd prime-power components.


## GCL's current state

The [authenticated source audit](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2323#issuecomment-6099076202) reconciles the frozen statement, primary literature and bounded elementary checks. These observations are not a new GCL mathematical certification.

## What remains

To prove finiteness, all sufficiently complicated canonical prime-power factorizations must be excluded by an unbounded argument. To prove infinitude, one needs an infinite valid family. Relaxed multiplicative identities without genuine prime-power conditions are not evidence of additional unitary perfect numbers.
