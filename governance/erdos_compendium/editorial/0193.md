## The question in plain language

An **$S$ walk** in the integer lattice $\mathbb Z^3$ is an infinite sequence of distinct lattice points $P_0,P_1,\ldots$ with every step $P_{n+1}-P_n$ in a **fixed finite** set $S\subseteq\mathbb Z^3$. Gerver and Ramsey asked whether **every** such walk contains three distinct collinear points.

The bounded-step condition is essential: allowing arbitrary jumps permits many easy examples, whereas requiring the same finite step set throughout looks restrictive. The question concerns collinearity of any three points in the walk, not only three consecutive ones.

## A small example

On a two-dimensional integer grid, an infinite walk with bounded steps has unavoidable collinear triples, as shown by Gerver and Ramsey. Three consecutive points forming a corner are not collinear, but this says nothing about a much later point on the same line. In $\mathbb Z^3$, test three lattice points $a,b,c$ by checking whether $(b-a)\times(c-a)=0$; this is an exact integer condition.

A lattice walk without three collinear points cannot repeat a vertex, since the problem asks for a set of distinct visited points. Bounding the coordinate sizes of steps alone is not enough: the **finite list** of possible displacement vectors must also be fixed.

## What is known

**DISPROVED.** Stijn Cambie and Erik Kalviainen, [*An infinite small-step $\mathbb Z^3$ walk with no collinear triple*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01766), 1 September 2026, construct an infinite walk using **at most 16 distinct step vectors** and avoiding every collinear triple. Their Theorem 1 uses a Gaussian-integer walk, four directional states, and a height coordinate that increases strictly.

A reusable step in their proof is a 2-adic valuation invariant for the squared lengths of planar chords and differences of height coordinates. If three chosen points were collinear, the valuation equality applied to the three chords would force an impossible parity relation. The primary preprint includes an explicit attribution of AI-assisted drafting and externally maintained Lean verification. This does not make GCL a proof certifier.

The earlier Gerver–Ramsey 1979 theorem proves the **positive** two-dimensional case and bounded finite no-three-collinear runs in three dimensions. The newer infinite construction genuinely changes the dimension-three answer.

## What remains

A bounded task implements the published recurrence for the first 256 points, enumerates its distinct displacement vectors and checks every collinearity determinant over all point triples. Acceptance: no triple is collinear, height is strictly increasing, and the step set has cardinality at most 16. Then prove the author's exact 2-adic chord invariant separately; the finite check alone cannot certify the infinite theorem.
