## The question in plain language

Suppose graph G has ordinary chromatic number χ(G)=m. Erdős and Gimbel ask if G must contain a (not necessarily induced) subgraph H whose cochromatic number satisfies ζ(H)≥c m/log m for an absolute c>0 and sufficiently large m. This is PROVED by Alon, Krivelevich and Sudakov [AKS97], improving Erdős–Gimbel's earlier bound c√(m/log m). The qualifier 'subgraph' is essential.

## A small example

The complete graph K3 has χ=3 but ζ(K3)=1, because its entire vertex set forms a clique class. By DELETE ONE EDGE, obtain the three-vertex path P3 as an ordinary subgraph, which has ζ(P3)=2. Thus passing to a noninduced subgraph can increase cochromatic number; one cannot simply take H=G and assert the theorem.

## What is known

Alon–Krivelevich–Sudakov [AKS97] proved the lower order m/log m for the maximal ζ of a subgraph of any χ=m graph. The maintained source calls this best possible up to constants, citing complete-graph cases. ζ(G)≤χ(G) always, since a proper independent-set coloring is a cochromatic coloring, but this upper alone does not yield the lower for a specific G.
