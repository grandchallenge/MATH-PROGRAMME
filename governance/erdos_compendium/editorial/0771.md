## The question in plain language

For each n define f(n) to be the largest integer r such that for EVERY positive m one can choose a subset S⊆{1,...,n} of cardinality r whose subset sums do NOT include m. Erdős and Graham conjectured f(n)=(1/2+o(1))n/log n; the asymptotic is PROVED by combining their lower construction with Alon–Freiman [AlFr88] upper bound. The set S may depend on m; one single S avoiding ALL positive m is not the question.

## A small example

For n=10 and m=9, choose S={2,4,6,8,10}, size five: all subset sums of S are even, hence none equals 9. More generally if prime p does NOT divide m, choosing all multiples of p up to n gives floor(n/p) elements with every subset sum divisible by p. This is an exact elementary lower-bound mechanism, but it does not show the sharp worst-case asymptotic by itself.

## What is known

Erdős–Graham showed that when m can matter, it can be restricted to m<n(n+1)/2 and the least prime p not dividing m satisfies p<(2+o(1))log n, providing f(n)≥(1/2+o(1))n/log n. Alon and Freiman [AlFr88] built a hard target m (a suitable lcm of initial integers) proving the complementary upper f(n)≤(1/2+o(1))n/log n. The upper is not a theorem that every S avoids no sums: it says there exists a difficult m for which no larger S can avoid representing m. Full 1988 paper details not independently rederived.
