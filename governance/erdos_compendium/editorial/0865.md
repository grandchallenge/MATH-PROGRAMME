## The question in plain language

Take a large subset $A$ of the first $N$ positive integers. Must it contain three **distinct** elements $a,b,c$ such that *all three pairwise sums*, $a+b$, $a+c$, and $b+c$, also belong to $A$?

The original density threshold was $5N/8$. [Erdős Problem #865](https://www.erdosproblems.com/865) asks whether every set slightly larger than that threshold must contain the configuration.

The distinction is not just a numerical density calculation. The same set $A$ must contain all three original numbers **and** their pairwise sums. The sums do not have to be pairwise different by definition, although for distinct integers they can be analyzed separately.

## A small example

For any integer $M\ge1$, consider

$$
A_M=[M,2M]\cup[4M,8M],
$$

where the intervals contain integers. There are $M+1$ integers in the first block and $4M+1$ in the second, so $|A_M|=5M+2$ inside $[8M]$. This is the standard construction establishing the sharpness of the coefficient $5/8$.

The two widely separated blocks prevent three distinct members from having all three pairwise sums back in $A_M$. A local exact test independently enumerated every distinct triple in this construction for $M=1,2,3,4,5,8,12,20,40$, finding no forbidden configuration.

The local fixture does not establish the full upper theorem; it verifies the sharpness examples only.

## What is known

Ricky Cipollini, [*A sharp 5/8 bound for an Erdős-Sós pairwise-sums problem*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29361), June 28, 2026, gives a self-contained mathematical proof claiming

$$
f_3(N)\le\frac58N+O(1),
$$

where $f_3(N)$ is the least size that forces the pairwise-sums configuration. Together with the construction above, this yields

$$
f_3(N)=\frac58N+O(1).
$$

This is the sharp asymptotic density prediction and is the result behind the maintained problem's **proved** status.

Cipollini's abstract makes a precise distinction about formalization: an **earlier conditional reduction** was formalized in Lean, while the paper claims an unconditional proof. It is incorrect to infer from this abstract alone that the entire final unconditional proof was Lean-certified.

A later public standalone Lean development, [Jayyhk/erdos-lean](https://github.com/Jayyhk/erdos-lean), has source-level evidence for a theorem of the shape $8|A|\le5N+53$ for triple-free sets, alongside a density-threshold theorem. That development uses a separately pinned toolchain and imports. GCL has not independently rebuilt the exact dependency versions or inspected the actual compiler's axiom report. This is therefore a **proof-provenance obligation**, not a completed GCL Lean certification.

## GCL's current state

The frozen catalogue packet and the maintained [#865 page](https://www.erdosproblems.com/865) record a solved claim. The current article distinguishes a published unconditional mathematical argument, an earlier conditional Lean reduction, and a later standalone formal proof source whose exact dependency closure still needs independent examination.

The reproducible verification fixture governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_865_866.py checks the standard two-block sharpness sets with exact integer arithmetic. Each triple is tested against membership of all three sums in the *same* set. The fixture also covers #866, but those are different problems and no proof transfers merely because both involve pairwise sums.

The explicit bound $8|A|\le5N+53$ would imply $|A|\le5N/8+53/8$ for every triple-free set, matching the required asymptotic. The next formal question is whether the external source proves **exactly** this theorem, with the intended distinctness and membership conventions.
