## The question in plain language

Pack $n$ Euclidean squares inside a square of side one, requiring that no two squares share an **interior** point. The smaller squares may be rotated. Let $f(n)$ be the supremum of the sum of their side lengths. Erdős #106 conjectured that, for every positive integer $k$,

$$
f(k^2+1)=k.
$$

The condition permits boundary contacts and rotations. Replacing it by a restriction that all squares have sides parallel to the container changes the problem.

## A small example

Divide the unit square into a $k\times k$ array of squares of side $1/k$. Their total side length is exactly $k$. Replacing one square by two smaller squares of side $1/(2k)$ preserves that sum while increasing the number of pieces from $k^2$ to $k^2+1$. Hence $f(k^2+1)\ge k$.

For the axis-parallel variant, Baek, Koizumi and Ueoro proved equality $g(k^2+1)=k$ in [*A note on the Erdős conjecture about square packing*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07274). Their restriction is substantive, not merely a coordinate choice.

## What is known

**DISPROVED for unrestricted orientations, by an explicit 2026 rational construction.** The maintained [Erdős #106](https://www.erdosproblems.com/106), edited March 2026, still labels the unrestricted problem **FALSIFIABLE/OPEN**. It predates the public August 2026 counterexamples and must not control the mathematical conclusion.

In [Sprite143's primary construction](https://github.com/Sprite143/erdos-106-counterexample), 17 squares, 16 axis-parallel and one rotated through an angle with cosine $255/257$ and sine $32/257$, fit inside the unit square with pairwise disjoint interiors and total side length

$$
\frac{2190452873}{547596200}
=4+\frac{68073}{547596200}>4.
$$

The corresponding conjecture predicts $f(17)=4$ at $k=4$. This explicit strict inequality refutes its universal assertion. The repository's accompanying exact rational checkers are not the only evidence: GCL independently reconstructed each square's four vertices from the publicly specified closed-form configuration, then tested all 68 corners for containment and all 136 unordered pairs using a **separate** exact-rational separating-axis implementation. The results were 100 strictly separated pairs, 36 contacting pairs and **zero interior overlaps**. No floating-point tolerance was used.

## What remains

A bounded successor is to preserve a minimal, separately implemented exact-rational verifier as a reproducible project fixture. Its acceptance test must reconstruct all corners, verify containment, enumerate all 136 pairs, permit boundary contact, and independently sum 17 positive side lengths to the rational above. A future referee can replay the certificate without trusting a geometric optimization package. Stop short of claiming the optimal value of $f(17)$ or any result about packings of exactly $k^2$ squares.
