## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1199](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1199) is **OPEN — DIAGONAL CRUCIAL** in the source as of 15 October 2026.

Owings' conjecture asks whether for **every two-coloring** of the positive integers there exists an **infinite set** $A$ such that its full algebraic sumset

$$
A+A=\{a+b:a,b\in A\}
$$

is monochromatic. The diagonal sums $2a=a+a$ for every $a\in A$ **must be included**. If sums are required only for **distinct** $a\ne b$, an infinite monochromatic restricted sumset exists by Hindman's finite-sums theorem; that is a weaker, **proved** statement. The all-pairs conjecture remains open for two colors despite a known **three-color counterexample**.

## A small example

For the finite set $A=\{1,3,5,7\}$, direct enumeration **with repetition** gives $A+A=\{2,4,6,8,10,12,14\}$. Its diagonal subset is $\{2,6,10,14\}$. Under the simple parity coloring all these sums have the even color, so this is a finite positive example. It does **not** prove the property for **every** two-coloring or any infinite $A$, and emphasizes why the diagonals cannot be forgotten.

## What is known

The maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/1199 records **OPEN** in two colors, original Owings [Ow74], Erdős [Er80,p.104]. Hindman [Hi79] constructed a 3-coloring for which the full sumset conclusion fails. Hindman [Hi74] proves the infinite **distinct-index finite-sums** theorem, and so settles the version excluding diagonal pairs, but it does **not** provide the sums $a+a$ needed here. A 3-coloring disproof does **not** imply a two-coloring disproof, and neither the site's 'formalized' badge nor finite arithmetic examples establish a resolution.
