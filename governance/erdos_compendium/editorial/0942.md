## The question in plain language

An integer \(m\ge1\) is **powerful** if each prime factor \(p\) of \(m\) occurs with exponent at least two. Write
\[
h(n)=\#\{m:n^2\le m<(n+1)^2,\ m\text{ powerful}\}.
\]
Erdős asked how large \(h(n)\) can be. More specifically, does some \(c>0\) give an upper bound \(h(n)<(\log n)^{c+o(1)}\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), alongside a matching lower bound \(h(n)>(\log n)^{c-o(1)}\) infinitely often? The matching-exponent question is open. [Erdős Problem #942](https://www.erdosproblems.com/942).

## A basic example and exact enumeration

The left endpoint \(n^2\) is always powerful, including when \(n=1\). Thus \(h(n)\ge1\). For \(n=2\), the half-open interval is \([4,9)\); its powerful integers are \(4=2^2\) and \(8=2^3\), so \(h(2)=2\). The endpoint \(9\) belongs to the **next** interval, not this one.

Each powerful positive integer has a unique representation \(a^2b^3\) with \(b\) squarefree: write each prime exponent \(e\ge2\) as \(2u+3v\), choosing \(v\in\{0,1\}\) to match parity. Accordingly the exact finite count is
\[
h(n)=\sum_{\substack{b\ge1\\ b\ {\rm squarefree}}}
\#\{a\ge1:n^2\le a^2b^3<(n+1)^2\}.
\]
This is a correct enumerator, not by itself an asymptotic estimate.

## What is known

De Koninck and Luca [DeLu04], *Sur la proximité des nombres puissants*, proved that infinitely many \(n\) satisfy
\[
h(n)\gg\left(\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\right)^{1/3}.
\]
Their work also determines the natural density of \(n\) with \(h(n)=1\), approximately \(0.275\), as summarized in the catalogue. This is compatible with unbounded \(h(n)\): even if most windows are sparse, exceptionally rich windows may occur.

A June 2026 refinement attributed by the maintained catalogue to Scott Hughes observes that optimizing the De Koninck–Luca construction yields infinitely many \(n\) with
\[
h(n)\gg\frac{\log n}{\log\log n\,\log\log\log n}.
\]
The discussion reports a Lean 4 formalization of a generalized theorem for fixed powers. Its repository, pinned Mathlib version, theorem statements and kernel/axiom report require independent source and build replay before GCL can certify that formal verification. A discussion report is **not** a GCL proof receipt. [Catalogue discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/942).

The improved lower bound is almost logarithmic, but does not settle the full question: one still requires the proposed *uniform upper bound* and a matching exponent \(c\). The catalogue marks #942 open.

## Why the problem is difficult

The interval \([n^2,(n+1)^2)\) has length \(2n+1\), much shorter than the scale \(n^2\) of its elements. A global asymptotic for the number of powerful integers below \(x\) does not automatically control fluctuations in these individual short intervals. Special Diophantine constructions can pack powerful integers together but do not supply a bound for every \(n\).
