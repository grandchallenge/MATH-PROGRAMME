## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1188](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1188) is **OPEN — MANDATORY CRITICAL** for the *modern maintained-catalogue formulation*. **This is different from Erdős's original 1980 question on printed p. 95**, which asks how many distinct covering systems can be packed with moduli mutually disjoint **even across different systems**, rather than counting individual minimal systems.

A **distinct covering system** consists of one congruence $a_i\bmod n_i$ for each distinct modulus $1<n_1<\dots<n_k$ whose union covers every integer. It is **minimal** if removing any of its actual congruences destroys coverage. Let $F(x)$ count such minimal **congruence assignments** with all moduli $\le x$; estimate its asymptotic growth. Crucially, a minimal **system of congruences** differs from an **irreducible covering set of moduli**: after deleting a modulus from an irreducible set, **no replacement residue choices** may restore coverage. Counting unrestricted nonminimal systems is a different, much easier, exponentially abundant problem.

## A small example

The exact finite fixture considers **only** the allowed moduli $\{2,3,4,6,8,12\}$, choosing either no congruence or one residue per modulus. It enumerates all **49,140** assignments over a period of 24 and finds **216** covering systems, of which **24** are minimal under removal of any *original* congruence. A specific minimal witness is $(0\bmod2),(0\bmod3),(1\bmod4),(1\bmod6),(11\bmod12)$. These are counts **restricted to the chosen modulus family**, emphatically **not** the global value $F(12)$.

## What is known

Maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/1188 is **OPEN for sharp $F(x)$** [Er80,p.95]. Hough [Ho15] proved Erdős #2, implying $F(x)\to\infty$. Balister–Bollobás–Morris–Sahasrabudhe–Tiba [BBMST24] yield $F(x)\ge\exp((\log x)^{3-o(1)})$, versus trivial upper $\exp(O(x\log x))$. This lower is much faster than the original 'very slowly' expectation. The site distinguishes $G(x)$ counting **irreducible modulus sets**, with $G(x)\le F(x)$. The BBMST source proof and correspondence between its counted objects and these minimal covering assignments need independent review.

**Original-source discrepancy (critical correction, 2026-10-17).** In *A survey of problems in combinatorial number theory* (1980), printed p. 95, Erdős asks for the maximum number $j_x$ of distinct covering systems **with no modulus repeated anywhere across the different systems**, subject to a cutoff $x$. That is a *packing* problem, not the modern catalogue's count $F(x)$ of individual minimal covering systems. The [maintained site's discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/1188) explicitly acknowledges this difference and retains the adapted modern problem as an interesting question. By Hough's minimum-modulus theorem, every distinct covering system has at least one modulus at most a universal constant $M$. Thus any family with pairwise-disjoint moduli across systems has at most $M-1$ systems, since their small moduli must all differ and lie among $\{2,\ldots,M\}$. The historic packing parameter is therefore uniformly bounded in $x$; this **does not** bound modern $F(x)$, which counts different minimal systems even when their moduli overlap. Exact parameter matching, the Hough citation and original wording remain subject to a distinct independent source-critical review.
