## The question in plain language

Choose a set $A$ of positive integers. For each $n$, count how many **ordered pairs** of elements of $A$ add up to $n$. Erdős asked whether one can choose $A$ so that this count approaches a fixed positive multiple of $\log n$ for every sufficiently large $n$, with the ratio tending to a positive constant.

Write

$$
r_A(n)=\#\{(a,b)\in A^2:a+b=n\}.
$$

The question is whether $r_A(n)/\log n$ can have a finite, nonzero limit. The maintained catalogue records this as **open**.

## A small example

For $A=\{1,2,3\}$, the number $4$ has three ordered representations: $(1,3)$, $(2,2)$, and $(3,1)$. Thus $r_A(4)=3$. The distinction between ordered and unordered representations matters when translating source statements.

A useful general identity is

$$
\sum_{n\le x}r_A(n)
=\#\{(a,b)\in A^2:a+b\le x\}.
$$

Let $A(x)=|A\cap[1,x]|$. Every pair with $a,b\le x/2$ contributes to this sum, while every contributing pair has $a,b\le x$. Consequently,

$$
A(x/2)^2\le\sum_{n\le x}r_A(n)\le A(x)^2.
$$

## What is known

If the desired limit existed and equaled $c>0$, then summing $r_A(n)\sim c\log n$ would give

$$
\sum_{n\le x}r_A(n)\sim c\,x\log x.
$$

The elementary pair-counting bounds would then force

$$
(c+o(1))x\log x\le A(x)^2
\le(2c+o(1))x\log x.
$$

So such a set would necessarily have about $\sqrt{x\log x}$ elements up to $x$, within constant factors. This is a necessary density constraint, not an existence construction.

The maintained catalogue reports that Erdős and Sárközy ruled out an extremely precise approximation to $\log n$, and that Horváth proved a related restriction. It also describes random constructions that work after discarding an exceptional set of density zero. Neither statement establishes the required limit for **every** sufficiently large integer. The exact historical theorems still require primary-page verification.

## GCL's current state

The pinned catalogue and maintained [Erdős #66](https://www.erdosproblems.com/66) classify the problem as **OPEN**. The present GCL work derives the necessary counting-scale lemma from the exact ordered-pair convention. The original assignment is [#1335](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1335).

This is an unaccepted local draft. No source lock or mathematical certification is claimed for the historical impossibility theorems.

## What remains

A bounded task is to formalize the ordered additive convolution and the pair-counting inequalities, then prove the necessary $\Theta(\sqrt{x\log x})$ scale under the assumed limit. The real open difficulty is obtaining or excluding such precise representation counts without exceptional integers. A necessary counting scale alone does not settle existence.
