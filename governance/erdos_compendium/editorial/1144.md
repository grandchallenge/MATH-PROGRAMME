## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1144](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1144) concerns the following mathematical question and its exact quantifiers:

$$\mathbb P\left(\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{m\le N}f(m)}{\sqrt N}=\infty\right)=1.$$

Choose independent fair random signs f(p) in {-1,1} at primes and extend COMPLETELY multiplicatively f(p^a)=f(p)^a. With S_N=sum_{n<=N} f(n), ask whether almost surely limsup_{N->infinity} S_N/sqrt(N)=infinity. Do not substitute squarefree-supported Rademacher f(n) or Steinhaus signs; distinguish an almost-sure lower-tail statement from moment identities.

## A small example

For N=16 the six prime signs admit exactly 64 assignments. Exact enumeration yields E S_16=4 and E S_16²=32. In general E f(n)=1 if n is a perfect square and 0 otherwise, while E f(a)f(b)=1 iff ab is a perfect square. Hence E S_N=floor sqrt(N) and E S_N² counts ordered square-product pairs. These are rigorously exact moment identities, NOT a proof of almost-sure positive limsup.
