## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1209](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1209) currently has source status **MIXED — GENERAL TRANSLATION FALSE**.

For an arbitrarily rapidly growing integer sequence $A=(a_k)$, does existence of **one** translation $n$ with every $n+a_k$ prime (or squarefree) imply infinitely many translations? Without restrictions on $A$ this is **false by diagonalization**: at stage $k$ arrange $a_k+k$ to be divisible by a nontrivial prime (or a prime square), eliminating $n=k$. Separately ask about the highly structured shifts $n+2^{2^k}$, with 'all terms prime', 'all squarefree', and 'infinitely many prime/squarefree' different questions.

## A small example

For $1\le k\le7$, phase25 constructs increasing primes $a_k$, and verifies selected $q_k\nmid k$ dividing $a_k+k$; for instance $(k,a_k,q_k)=(1,3,2),(2,7,3)$. The finite diagonal prefix rules out the corresponding seven translations only. Extending to every positive translation requires the **infinite** Dirichlet construction, not just these examples.

## What is known

The maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/1209 explains the unrestricted diagonal construction: choose primes $a_k\equiv-k\bmod q_k$ where $q_k$ is a prime not dividing $k$. Dirichlet's theorem permits arbitrarily fast growth; replacing $q_k$ by $q_k^2$ provides a squarefree obstruction. An ebarschkis/GPT argument from 2026 reportedly proves no integer shift makes **all** $n+2^{2^k}$ prime, by recurring divisibility using multiplicative orders. The infinitely-often and squarefree variants are not thereby resolved; source review remains pending.
