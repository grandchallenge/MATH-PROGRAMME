## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1211](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1211) currently has source status **PROVED SHARP LOGARITHMIC DENSITY**.

Partition positive integers $\mathbb N=A\sqcup B$. Let $S(A)$ and $S(B)$ consist of **finite sums of distinct elements** in the respective color classes. For $X\subseteq\mathbb N$ define upper **logarithmic** density $\overline\delta(X)=\limsup_{x\to\infty}(\log x)^{-1}\sum_{n\in X,n<x}1/n$. Determine the optimal universal constant $c$ such that $\max(\overline\delta(S(A)),\overline\delta(S(B)))\ge c$ for every partition. Ordinary upper natural density is not interchangeable with upper logarithmic density.

## A small example

Partition $[1,30]$ into odds and evens. Enumerating subsets and truncating sums at 30 gives **29** positive sums for odd elements and **15** for even elements; even-element sums remain even, while odd sums include most but not all integers in this short range. The exact constant $(2+\sqrt3)/4$ is a published **infinite logarithmic-density** value, not extracted from this finite partition.

## What is known

Erdős [Er80,p.113],[Er82c],[Er82d] observed one of the two subset-sum sets must have **upper natural density 1**, although both may have lower density 0. The maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/1211 reports Conlon–Fox–Pham [CFP22] **PROVED**
$$c=\frac{2+\sqrt3}{4}\approx0.9330127.$$
An extremizing construction partitions integers according to parity of $\lfloor\log_b\log n\rfloor$, with $b=2+\sqrt3$. A base-4 example yields logarithmic density $14/15$ for both colors, which is larger than the optimal $c$. Original theorem and density-limsup arguments remain to be independently replayed.
