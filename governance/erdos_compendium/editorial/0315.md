## The question in plain language

An Egyptian fraction writes a number as a sum of reciprocals of positive integers. Problem 315 starts with the greedy decomposition of $1$, whose denominators are

$$
2,3,7,43,1807,3263443,….
$$

The shifted sequence in the live statement is $u_1=1$ and $u_{n+1}=u_n(u_n+1)$, so the actual denominators are $u_n+1$. Its growth is roughly doubly exponential, and the associated scale is the Vardi constant

$$
c_0=lim_{n	oinfty}u_n^{1/2^n}=1.264085….
$$

The question asks whether every other strictly increasing sequence of positive integers $a_1<a_2<cdots$ satisfying

$$
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_k}=1
$$

must have

$$
\liminf_{n\to\infty}a_n^{1/2^n}<c_0.
$$

“Other” means that the sequence is not exactly the Sylvester denominator sequence. The strict inequality and the quantifiers matter: this is not merely a comparison of a few initial denominators, and it does not ask for a uniform numerical gap below $c_0$.

## A small example

The recurrence gives $u_1=1$, $u_2=2$, $u_3=6$, $u_4=42$, and $u_5=1806$. Direct exact-arithmetic verification gives

$$
\frac12=\frac12,
$$

$$
\frac12+\frac13=\frac56,
$$

$$
\frac12+\frac13+\frac17=\frac{41}{42},
$$

$$
\frac12+\frac13+\frac17+\frac1{43}=\frac{1805}{1806}.
$$

Thus, for each displayed partial sum, the residual is respectively $1/u_2$, $1/u_3$, $1/u_4$, and $1/u_5$. Equivalently, the checked identity is

$$
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{u_k+1}+\frac{1}{u_{n+1}}=1
$$

for $n=1,2,3,4$. This is a finite telescoping check: from $u_{k+1}=u_k(u_k+1)$, one has $1/(u_k+1)=1/u_k-1/u_{k+1}$. It explains why the Sylvester denominators sum to $1$, but it does not establish the asymptotic liminf comparison for an arbitrary sequence.

## What is known

The live [Erdős Problems entry for #315](https://www.erdosproblems.com/315) gives the strict-increasing formulation, says that it is true, and cites Yuhi Kamio and Zheng Li with Quanyu Tang. It also records the earlier indexing convention and identifies $c_0$ as the Vardi constant.

The principal primary source checked here is [Li and Tang, *On a conjecture of Erdős and Graham about the Sylvester's sequence*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12277), arXiv:2503.12277v4 (21 March 2025), published in *Acta Mathematica Hungarica* 177 (2025), 41–63, DOI [10.1007/s10474-025-01566-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10474-025-01566-8). Their Conjecture 1.3, p. 3, matches the strict sequence and the liminf inequality. Theorem 1.5 and Theorem 1.6, pp. 3–4, establish the two constructive ingredients; Corollary 1.7, p. 4, states the conclusion, and the constructive proof is developed in Sections 3.1–3.3, pp. 13–19. The paper presents this as a proof of the original conjecture.

Kamio’s independent [*Asymptotic Analysis of Infinite Decompositions of a Unit Fraction into Unit Fractions*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02317), arXiv:2503.02317v1 (4 March 2025), states a weakly increasing version in Problem 1, p. 1, and proves the generalized result as Theorem 8, pp. 3–4. Since strict increase implies weak increase, this is relevant evidence, but the two papers should not be treated as byte-identical statements. Both correct the apparent indexing/recurrence error inherited from the 1980 source [Erdős and Graham, *Old and New Problems and Results in Combinatorial Number Theory*, p. 41](https://www.erdosproblems.com/bibs/ErGr80).

## What remains

The external proof provenance is strong enough for a source-locked documentary account, but the mathematical certification boundary remains separate. A useful bounded successor would compare the live strict statement, Li–Tang’s Conjecture 1.3, Kamio’s weak statement, and the FC theorem line by line, including the index shift from $u_1=1$ to Sylvester denominators beginning at $2$. It should also record whether each hosted formal proof uses `StrictMono` or only `Monotone` and whether that difference is an implication or an unintended statement change.

That comparison should not silently promote the broader generalization in Li–Tang, whose non-constructive route assumes an unproved claim about eventually greedy best Egyptian underapproximations. Nor should the finite telescoping calculation be presented as evidence for the limiting theorem. A source mismatch, an unverified dependency, or failure to align the sum and indexing conventions is a valid stopping point.

The most useful output of that successor is a reproducible normalization record: each source should name the same objects, quantify over the same class of sequences, and expose every assumption needed by its limiting expression.
