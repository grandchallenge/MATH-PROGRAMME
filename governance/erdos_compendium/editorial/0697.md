## The question in plain language

Let δ(m,α) denote the natural density of positive integers divisible by some d≡1 mod m with 1<d<exp(m^α). Erdős asked whether a sharp critical exponent β>1 separates density tending to ZERO for α<β from density tending to ONE for α>β as m→∞. R. R. Hall's 1992 Journal of Number Theory paper resolves it with β=1/log2. The mathematical problem is PROVED in the published source. Crucially, the pinned Formal Conjectures 697.lean reverses the inequality hypotheses of BOTH limit theorems, making its precise targets false.

## A small example

A direct finite fixture: with m=2, α=1, eligible d≡1 mod2 are d=3,5,7 because 1<d<exp(2). The density of integers divisible by at least one is 1/3+1/5+1/7−1/15−1/21−1/35+1/105=19/35. This is a finite-modulus illustration, not a threshold theorem. A stronger elementary contradiction to the pinned formal theorem is α=1/2: the union bound δ(m,1/2) ≤ Σ_{1<d<exp(sqrt m),d≡1 mod m}1/d ≪ m^{-1/2}, hence δ→0, while pinned part ii, whose hypothesis α<β includes 1/2, falsely claims δ→1.
