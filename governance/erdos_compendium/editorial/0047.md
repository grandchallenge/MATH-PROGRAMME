## The question in plain language

The reciprocal sum of a finite set $A$ is the sum of $1/a$ over its positive elements. [Erdős Problem #47](https://www.erdosproblems.com/47) asks whether, for each fixed $\delta>0$, every sufficiently large $N$ and every $A\subseteq[1,N]$ satisfying

$$
\sum_{a\in A}\frac1a>\delta\log N
$$

must contain a subset $S$ whose reciprocal sum is **exactly one**. The answer is **yes**, proved in published mathematical work.

## A small example

For $A=\{2,3,6\}$, the reciprocal sum is one and the entire set is a suitable witness. For $N=6$ this sum exceeds $\frac12\log6$. One finite witness gives no information about every qualifying set for arbitrarily large $N$.

## What is known

Thomas Bloom, *On a density conjecture about unit fractions* (2021), proved a sufficient reciprocal-sum threshold of smaller order than $\log N$. Yang Liu and Mehtaab Sawhney, *On further questions regarding unit fractions* (2024), improved it further to a bound on the scale $(\log N)^{4/5+o(1)}$.

For each fixed positive $\delta$, those thresholds are eventually smaller than $\delta\log N$. Hence the stronger published results imply the original Erdős assertion. The deep source papers establish the required combinatorial number-theory estimates; the comparison between thresholds is elementary only **given** their precise hypotheses.
