## The question in plain language

Let

$$
1<a_1<a_2<\cdots
$$

be positive real numbers such that distinct finite products

$$
\prod_i a_i^{k_i},\qquad k_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}
$$

are always separated by a distance of at least one on the real line. Here exponent tuples have finite support and the empty product equals one. Erdős asked whether

$$
\#\{i:a_i\le x\}\le\pi(x),
$$

where $\pi(x)$ is the number of ordinary primes at most $x$. This is a question about *Beurling generalized primes*, not necessarily ordinary integer primes. The [maintained catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/951) marks it open and notes an important quantifier ambiguity: the inequality may be intended for all $x$ or only for sufficiently large $x$.

## A small example

*The integer-prime example*

Take $a_i=p_i$, the ordinary primes. Unique factorization says distinct exponent tuples produce distinct positive integers, so their associated products differ by at least one. The count is exactly $\pi(x)$. This shows that the proposed bound, if valid in its intended asymptotic sense, would be sharp.

The separation requirement is much stronger than merely requiring the $a_i$ to be distinct: it applies to **all** monomials in any finite number of generators, with arbitrarily large nonnegative exponents. Testing just the generators or their pairwise products is insufficient.

## A rigorous counting observation

All distinct generalized integers $b=\prod_i a_i^{k_i}$ with $1\le b\le x$ are separated from one another by at least one. Since the empty product supplies $b=1$, there can be at most $\lfloor x\rfloor$ of them. Thus

$$
\#\left\{\prod_i a_i^{k_i}\le x\right\}\le \lfloor x\rfloor.
$$

This elementary packing bound is exact but does **not** imply $\#\{a_i\le x\}\le\pi(x)$. Indeed $\pi(x)\ll x$, and the gap between those bounds is enormous.

The statement also relates to Beurling's conjecture that if the generalized-integer counting function has the particularly precise form $x+o(\log x)$, then the generalized primes must coincide with the ordinary primes. This hypothesis concerns the **whole** monoid of products, not just the number of generators.

## The finite-cutoff caveat

The source revision history states that interpreting Erdős's inequality as holding for **every** $x$ is false. It reports a counterexample at $x=10$ and explains that a finite prefix of generators meeting suitable finite constraints can be extended to an infinite separated system by a greedy method. Consequently the universally quantified version must not be presented as an open conjecture. The interpretation for **all sufficiently large $x$** remains the substantive unresolved formulation in the maintained catalogue.

This is a subtle logical distinction. An infinite family with one small-cutoff violation does not refute a statement that permits finitely many exceptional values of $x$. Conversely, a finite search for apparently dense generalized primes cannot prove or disprove the eventual upper bound.

## What is known

The problem originates with Erdős [Er77c]; his later remarks [Er80] associate it with a Queens College audience question, possibly by S. Shapiro, and consider rigidity of the equality case. The catalogue includes substantial discussion of finite counterexamples and the intended cutoff quantifier, none of which resolves the sufficiently-large‐$x$ question. An ordinary prime sequence satisfies the complete separation condition by the fundamental theorem of arithmetic.

## GCL's current state

The elementary generalized-integer packing bound is proved above. The reported $x=10$ counterexample and greedy-extension argument are explicitly attributed to the source and remain candidates for exact independent reconstruction. No new proof of the asymptotic prime-count bound or uniqueness-of-equality assertion is claimed.
