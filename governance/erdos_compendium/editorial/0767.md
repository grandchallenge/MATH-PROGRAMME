## The question in plain language

Fix k≥1 and let g_k(n) be the largest number of edges of an n-vertex graph containing no cycle with k DISTINCT chords sharing one vertex on that cycle. Erdős conjectured that g_k(n)=(k+1)n−(k+1)² for sufficiently large n. Jiang [Ji04] proved the formula for n≥3k+3. The important threshold is n≥3k+3, not every n.

## A small example

For k=1, a 4-cycle WITH a diagonal has one chord incident to each of its diagonal endpoints, and so is forbidden. The complete bipartite graph K_{2,n−2} has 2(n−2)=2n−4 edges and all cycles alternate between the two sides; each such cycle is a 4-cycle without chords (the bipartite graph has no within-side edges). Thus K_{2,n−2} realizes the exact lower 2n−4 for k=1. More generally K_{k+1,n−k−1} has (k+1)(n−k−1) edges and is a source-proposed extremal family; its full forbidden-configuration check needs clear cycle/chord convention.

## What is known

Pósa proved g₁(n)=2n−4 for n≥4, Erdős established k=2,3 for n≥2k+2, and Jiang [Ji04] proved the general n≥3k+3 formula. Historic Erdős [Er69b] mentions a communicated Lewin counterexample 'for general k', apparently conflicting with the later theorem; possible small-n or statement-difference explanations require primary source inspection, not silent deletion of the historical note. The maintained 2025 #767 marks PROVED and recounts this conflict.
