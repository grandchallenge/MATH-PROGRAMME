## The question in plain language

A graph has **chromatic number** $k$ if its vertices admit a proper coloring with $k$ colors but not fewer. An **edge-critical graph** loses chromatic number whenever any edge is deleted. For fixed $k\ge4$, let $f_k(n)$ be the largest possible number of edges in an edge-critical graph with $n$ vertices and chromatic number $k$.

[Erdős Problem #917](https://www.erdosproblems.com/917) asks three questions: Is $f_k(n)\gg_k n^2$? Is $f_6(n)\sim n^2/4$? More generally, for fixed $k\ge6$, is the leading coefficient

$$
\frac12\left(1-\frac1{\lfloor k/3\rfloor}\right)?
$$

**The components have different statuses.** Toft established a positive quadratic lower bound. Stiebitz constructions refute the proposed general coefficient when $k$ is not divisible by three. The precise $k=6$ asymptotic remains **open**, despite improved published upper bounds.

## A small example

Take two disjoint odd cycles, each of length $2q+1$, and add **all** edges between their vertex sets. This *join* is six-chromatic: each odd cycle needs three colors, and no color can be reused across the complete join.

Its vertex and edge counts are

$$
N=4q+2,\qquad
e=(2q+1)^2+2(2q+1)
=\frac{N^2}{4}+N.
$$

It is also **edge-critical**. Deleting an edge inside an odd cycle leaves a bipartite path, allowing that part to use two colors and the other part three. Deleting a cross-edge $ab$ permits a three-coloring in each odd cycle with $a$ and $b$ each uniquely bearing one shared color; the other four colors remain distinct. Both cases give a proper five-coloring.

This is Dirac's 1952 construction. It establishes a quadratic lower bound for $k=6$, not the unproved assertion that coefficient $1/4$ is optimal. The exact finite verifier constructs the joins for $q=1,2,3,4$ on 6, 10, 14, 18 vertices, with 15, 35, 63, 99 edges respectively, and checks a five-coloring after **each** individual edge deletion.

## What is known

Dirac, [*A property of 4-chromatic graphs and some remarks on critical graphs*](https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/917), *Journal of the London Mathematical Society* **27** (1952), pp.85–92, supplied the dense six-critical join construction. Erdős returned to the problem in [Er69b] and [Er93, p.341]. Toft, [*On the maximal number of edges of critical k-chromatic graphs*](https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/917), *Studia Scientiarum Mathematicarum Hungarica* **5** (1970), pp.461–470, proved the positive quadratic lower bound for fixed $k\ge4$, apart from exceptional vertex counts where fully critical graphs do not exist.

Stiebitz, [*Subgraphs of colour-critical graphs*](https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/917), *Combinatorica* **7** (1987), pp.303–312, produced constructions and upper bounds. For infinitely many vertex counts and $k\ge6$, the constructions yield the coefficient

$$
\frac12\left(1-\frac1{\lfloor k/3\rfloor+\delta_k}\right)
$$

with $\delta_k=0$ for $k\equiv0\pmod3$, $\delta_k=1/7$ for $k\equiv1\pmod3$ and $\delta_k>0$ for $k\equiv2\pmod3$. In particular, for $k=7$ this gives $4/15>1/4$, contradicting Erdős's general proposed asymptotic constant $1/4$. It does not resolve the separate $k=6$ equality question.

Luo, Ma and Yang, [*On the maximum number of edges in k-critical graphs*](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0963548323000238), *Combinatorics, Probability and Computing* **32** (2023), pp.900–911, established the improved upper bound: for fixed $k\ge4$ and sufficiently large $n$,

$$
f_k(n)\le e(T_{k-2}(n))-c_kn^2,
\qquad c_k\ge\frac1{36(k-1)^2},
$$

where $T_{k-2}(n)$ is the balanced complete multipartite Turán graph. Their Theorem 1.2 also proves $f_4(n)<0.164n^2$ for large $n$. Neither bound proves that $f_6(n)\sim n^2/4$.

A vital definition issue: Luo–Ma–Yang use **fully critical** graphs, where every proper subgraph lowers chromatic number. The catalogue explicitly requires only **edge-criticality**, which permits adding isolated vertices. For any edge-critical graph, removing all isolates leaves a fully critical core: another nontrivial component would have edges whose deletion cannot lower the overall chromatic number. Conversely an ordinary critical graph remains edge-critical after adding isolates. Exact maximization at fixed $n$ therefore requires accounting for isolate padding. Asymptotic upper bounds can be transferred by examining the core size, not by pretending the definitions coincide verbatim.

The [maintained catalogue #917](https://www.erdosproblems.com/917) still marks the combined question OPEN. A [July–August 2026 working report](https://www.erdosproblemaday.com/report/917) develops a join-indecomposable reduction but labels its progress partial; this is not GCL-certified proof.

## GCL's current state

The Dirac join construction yields a **reusable elementary proof** of six-chromatic edge-criticality, with two cases for deleted edges. For an internal edge, the odd cycle becomes a path and becomes two-colorable. For a cross-edge, both odd cycles can be three-colored with the chosen endpoints uniquely bearing one shared color, making the join five-colorable after that cross-edge is deleted.

The exact program at governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_916_917.py constructs four examples, verifies their edges and counts, and checks all **212** explicit five-coloring certificates, one for every edge (15+35+63+99). It also checks the quadratic edge formula. The six-color lower bound follows independently from the fact each joined odd cycle requires three colors and no color can be shared between the two original parts.

This is a concrete infinite-family proof with finite executable regressions, but it supplies only a lower bound. The program cannot infer the maximum edge count over all critical graphs at large orders.

## What remains

The bounded formalizable task is the **join-of-odd-cycles lemma**: for every $q\ge1$, show the join of two cycles of length $2q+1$ is six-chromatic and deleting any edge leaves a five-colorable graph. Acceptance requires a complete proof or a pinned, no-placeholder Lean theorem with literal axiom reporting, including source-normalized graph joins, the two edge types and all 212 finite coloring certificates. A single edge deletion that still requires six colors would falsify the construction.

The next source task is the exact relation between **edge-critical** and **fully critical**, including isolated vertices and exceptional orders. For the remaining open problem, the required new theorem is an upper bound

$$
f_6(n)\le\left(\frac14+o(1)\right)n^2.
$$

Neither Toft's lower bound nor the Stiebitz and Luo–Ma–Yang upper results provide it. Independently source-audit the 1987 residue-class constructions before claiming an exact optimal coefficient. Stop short of a new asymptotic claim until this gap is closed.
