## The question in plain language

Define ε_n as the supremum of exponents ε for which one can choose one residue class a_p modulo each prime with n^ε<p≤n and cover all integers 1,…,n. Erdős asks if ε_n→0. The interval of available prime moduli shrinks as ε grows; so the claim is that every successful covering must eventually employ very small primes relative to n. The maintained status is OPEN.

## A small example

For n=10, excluding p=2 leaves 3,5,7. Their chosen classes meet at most 4+2+2=8 elements of 1,…,10, so those primes alone cannot cover the interval. In fact an exhaustive enumeration of all 2·3·5·7=210 residue choices shows that even the FULL set of primes 2,3,5,7 cannot cover the first ten integers. Consequently there is no feasible ε at n=10, and a finite-n definition as a *maximum over successful choices* is undefined here. The formal supremum-of-empty-set convention requires its own explanation. This is not evidence for or against ε_n→0 when n is sufficiently large.

## What is known

The maintained source attributes the lower estimate ε_n ≫ logloglog n / loglog n to Erdős. It does not settle the requested upper decay. Direct inspection of the pinned FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/688.lean (commit 85f8637..., blob d7d04f26c5b2f47c0023013fe4d961fc93062a1b) shows a predicate requiring, for every m∈[1,n], some prime p with n^ε<p≤n and matching residue; epsilonFunction is the supremum of such ε. All displayed theorem bodies, including the classical attributed lower-bound variant, contain sorry, so the snapshot is not a proof certificate. A supremum need not be attained, and its defining set can be empty at small n; treating it as a finite maximum for every n requires justification.
