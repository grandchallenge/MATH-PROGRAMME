## The question in plain language

An arithmetic function $f$ is **additive** if

$$
f(ab)=f(a)+f(b)\qquad\text{whenever }\gcd(a,b)=1.
$$

It need not satisfy the same identity for noncoprime integers. Every positive integer decomposes uniquely into powers of **different primes**, so additive functions are determined by their freely specified values on prime powers.

Erdős and Wirsing asked what follows if these prime-power values can become arbitrarily large **relative to the logarithm**:

$$
\limsup_{\substack{p\ \mathrm{prime}\\k\ge1}}
\frac{f(p^k)}{\log(p^k)}=\infty.
$$

Do the normalized adjacent increments $(f(n+1)-f(n))/\log n$ necessarily have unbounded limsup? Or, in a separate proposal, must the adjacent ratios $f(n+1)/f(n)$ have unbounded limsup? [Erdős Problem #897](https://www.erdosproblems.com/897) asks both questions.

**Both proposed implications are false**, by a construction attributed to Wirsing. The maintained catalogue also records an external Lean verification. This article distinguishes that published counterexample from elementary facts about additive functions and from GCL's finite checks.

## A small example

For a simple additive function, set $f(1)=0$ and define the value on a prime power by

$$
f(p^e)=e(e+1).
$$

Extend this to arbitrary positive integers by summing over their distinct maximal prime-power factors:

$$
f(n)=\sum_{p^e\parallel n}e(e+1).
$$

If $\gcd(a,b)=1$, their prime supports are disjoint, so $f(ab)=f(a)+f(b)$. In particular, $f(2^k)=k(k+1)$ and therefore

$$
\frac{f(2^k)}{\log(2^k)}
=\frac{k+1}{\log2}\longrightarrow\infty.
$$

Thus this **elementary example satisfies the hypothesis** of #897. However, it is **not** the Wirsing counterexample and does not refute either proposed conclusion. Its adjacent differences can be large in either direction; the exact finite verifier finds differences ranging from $-102$ to $104$ for $2\le n\le2000$.

This example explains why unbounded growth on prime powers, by itself, is insufficient information to infer any particular neighboring-integer behavior. A genuine disproof requires an additive function that meets the premise **and** defeats the stated global limsup conclusion.

## What is known

The problem is indexed [Er72, p.85] and described as a conjecture of Erdős and Wirsing. Wirsing had proved, in [*A characterization of $\log n$ as an additive arithmetic function*](https://www.erdosproblems.com/897), *Symposia Mathematica* (1970), pp.45–57, that if adjacent differences are **uniformly bounded in absolute value**, then an additive function is essentially a constant multiple of $\log n$ up to a bounded error. The conjecture in #897 attempted to replace that strong premise by weaker information about prime-power growth.

Wirsing's [*Additive and completely additive functions with restricted growth*](https://www.erdosproblems.com/897), in *Recent Progress in Analytic Number Theory*, volume 2 (Durham 1979 proceedings, published 1981), pp.231–280, contains the negative construction, attributed by Wirsing to an observation of Erdős. The maintained catalogue describes it by assigning

$$
f(q)=g(q)\log q
$$

at prime powers $q$, for a **sufficiently slowly growing** function $g$, and extending additively. The choices and proof inequalities governing the slowness of $g$ are part of the original argument, not arbitrary consequences of the displayed formula.

The [maintained #897](https://www.erdosproblems.com/897) marks both questions **DISPROVED (LEAN)**. It also states that the limsup of the *normalized difference* can be zero. This extra zero-limsup assertion applies to the **difference expression**; it must not be casually transferred to the positive adjacent ratio, for which positivity can impose additional restrictions.

A public [Formal Conjectures #897 file](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/main/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/897.lean) points to an external Lean development by Aristotle, operated largely by L. Wu, and to the Archivara 2025 rediscovery. Its own upstream theorem bodies are marked with the Lean placeholder term sorry, despite metadata linking to external proof code. The linked external development is evidence to check, not a kernel-certified proof within GCL's admitted provider snapshot.

## GCL's current state

The basic prime-power extension lemma is elementary. Every integer $n\ge1$ has a unique factorization $n=\prod_p p^{e_p(n)}$. For any assignment of real weights $w(p,e)$ to positive prime-power exponents, define

$$
f(n)=\sum_{p:e_p(n)>0}w(p,e_p(n)),
\qquad f(1)=0.
$$

Then coprimality of $a,b$ ensures their positive-exponent prime sets are disjoint, proving $f(ab)=f(a)+f(b)$. This statement does **not** require the weights to depend linearly on $e$, and consequently distinguishes ordinary additivity from complete additivity.

The exact executable program at governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_896_897.py implements the weight $w(p,e)=e(e+1)$. It independently checks coprime additivity for every $1\le a,b\le150$, verifies the growth formula on $2^k$ for $1\le k\le32$, and computes adjacent differences through $n=2000$ without floating-point factorization.

These finite checks **do not reproduce Wirsing's crucial slow-growth construction** and cannot establish a limiting counterexample. That proof requires control of *all sufficiently large neighboring integers*, not a finite set of prime powers or a table of their values.

## What remains

A bounded first task is the **prime-power specification lemma**: formalize unique factorization and prove that arbitrary real-valued prime-power weights extend uniquely to an additive function, with $f(1)=0$. Acceptance requires a complete paper proof or a pinned Lean build without placeholders and with a literal axiom report, and reproduction of all finite coprime tests and the growth formula for powers of two. A coprime pair violating additivity falsifies the lemma.

The substantive source-replay task is to retrieve the exact 1981 Wirsing construction, pin the allowed function $g$, and independently derive the inequalities that make the difference limsup nonpositive while preserving the unbounded prime-power normalized values. In parallel, inspect the exact external 2025 Lean theorem, its dependencies, its ratio denominator hypotheses, and its actual compiler/axiom output. Acceptance demands a literal semantic match to **both** original implications and a no-placeholder kernel check. Stop before treating the upstream placeholder statements or a metadata link as GCL certification.
