## The question in plain language

Arrange $d\ge4$ distinct, pairwise nonparallel lines in the real plane, with **no four concurrent**. A *Gallai triangle* is formed by three of the lines whose pairwise intersections are three distinct points, **each incident to exactly two lines in the entire arrangement**. Erdős asked whether every such arrangement must contain one. An ordinary intersection on one side is not enough: all three triangle vertices must be ordinary.

By projective duality there is a related question about noncollinear triples of a planar point configuration whose three connecting lines each contain exactly two of the original points.

## A small example

Take the four lines $x=0$, $y=0$, $x+y=1$ and $x+2y=3$. They are not pairwise nonparallel as a family because the first two are perpendicular and the last two have different slopes, so they satisfy the required **nonparallel** condition. The first three have pairwise distinct intersections and no fourth line through any of their three vertices; hence those three lines form a Gallai triangle. Checking only that the candidate triangle is nondegenerate is insufficient, since an extra fourth line could pass through a vertex and destroy ordinary status.

The term *ordinary* means exactly two incident lines **among the full arrangement**, not merely among the three selected lines.

## What is known

**DISPROVED.** Füredi and Palásti (1984) constructed counterexamples for a broad range of cardinalities. Juan García Escudero, [*Gallai triangles in configurations of lines in the projective plane*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crma.2016.03.003), *Comptes Rendus Mathématique* 354 (2016), 551–554, supplied counterexamples for **every $d>3$**. These are projective-plane arrangements with at most triple concurrency and no Gallai triangle. The passage between the projective and affine formulations must preserve which intersections lie at infinity; the current source presents them as equivalent.

The Sylvester–Gallai theorem guarantees an *individual* ordinary intersection, but does not guarantee three that form a triangle. This is the precise failed strengthening.

## GCL's current state

The pinned and maintained [Erdős #209](https://www.erdosproblems.com/209) classify the problem **DISPROVED**. Original source issue: [#1478](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1478). Escudero's published abstract verifies the negative answer for all $d>3$; the complete configuration proof and any external formalization were not independently reconstructed.

The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1478#issuecomment-6180318321). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded successor stores rational-coordinate line arrangements as equations $a x+b y=c$, checks every pair intersection, detects three- or four-fold concurrence exactly, and searches for a Gallai triangle. Acceptance: a hand-built four-line positive example is recognized and the search never counts a vertex shared by three lines as ordinary. Next replay Escudero's finite arrangements at a named source revision; finite success cannot certify the construction for all $d$.
