## The question in plain language

Let $A$ be a set of positive integers, and let $R_A(n)$ count the representations

$$
n=p+a,\qquad p\text{ prime},\ a\in A.
$$

Erdős asked whether $R_A(n)$ is **unbounded** when $A$ contains at least a constant multiple of $\log N$ elements up to $N$ for all sufficiently large $N$. Formally, the target is

$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty}R_A(n)=\infty.
$$

The representation count is ordered only by the choice of $a\in A$: each such $a$ determines the unique candidate prime $n-a$.

## A small example

Take $A=\{1,2,4,8,\ldots\}$. A particular integer $n$ is represented once for every exponent $k$ for which $n-2^k$ is prime. This specializes the question to #236, but **unboundedness** of that count does not assert the stronger estimate $o(\log n)$ or its negation.

A finite set $A$ yields $R_A(n)\le |A|$ uniformly, so the assumption that $A$ is infinite is essential.

## What is known

**PROVED, under a stronger conclusion about the hypothesis.** Erdős (1950) handled the powers-of-two set. Chen and Ding (2022), as recorded in [Erdős #237](https://www.erdosproblems.com/237), established the desired unboundedness for **every infinite set $A\subseteq\mathbb N$**, without any logarithmic lower bound for its counting function.

Thus the historical density condition can be discarded entirely, but the conclusion remains a **limsup** rather than a claim that every sufficiently large $n$ has many representations. The proof is a result about prime shifts of an arbitrary infinite sequence, not merely an application of the prime number theorem to a fixed finite list.

## What remains

A bounded successor enumerates $R_A(n)$ for sample infinite sets truncated at a cutoff, including powers of two and sparse squares, using exact primality checks. Acceptance: a finite experiment is not offered as proof of an infinite limsup; the statement requires merely $A$ infinite, not positive density or even logarithmic growth.
