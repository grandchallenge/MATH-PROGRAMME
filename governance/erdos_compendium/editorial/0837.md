## The question in plain language

Some hypergraph edge-density thresholds force a substantial density improvement in a growing subgraph. Graph thresholds are classical, but determining the three-uniform hypergraph density-jump thresholds is an open extremal problem.

Let $A_k$ be the set of threshold densities alpha in [0,1] with a positive DENSITY JUMP: for any sequence of growing k-uniform hypergraphs whose normalized edge density has liminf >alpha, there exist subhypergraphs on vertex orders tending to infinity with liminf density exceeding some beta>alpha; additionally the implication need not remain true on replacing >alpha by >=alpha. Determine $A_3$. Do not confuse asymptotic density with a single finite graph.

## A small example

The balanced complete bipartite $K_{3,3}$ has 9 edges on 6 vertices and density $9/\binom{6}{2}=3/5$; a sequence $K_{m,m}$ has density $m^2/\binom{2m}{2}=m/(2m-1)\to1/2$. This illustrates graph-Turán threshold 1/2 (j=2), NOT a 3-uniform theorem.

This finite calculation is not an asymptotic or all-parameter proof.

## What is known

Maintained /latex/837 read 2026-10-14. For ordinary graphs (uniformity 2), $A_2=\{1-1/j:j\ge1\}$, the Turán density thresholds. No classification of $A_3$ is provided; overall OPEN. Source originally attributed to Erdős–Simonovits. Full historical proof not independently read. Exact equality case is a necessary part of definition.

For an s-uniform hypergraph, normalize the edge density as

$$
d_s(G)=\frac{e(G)}{\binom{|V(G)|}{s}}.
$$

An alpha jump asks for a beta>alpha working uniformly across qualifying sequences of graphs with growing subgraphs, and requires checking the non-strict equality threshold separately. The known graph jump set is $A_2=\{1-1/j:j\geq1\}$. That does not classify $A_3$.
