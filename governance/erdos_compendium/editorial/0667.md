## The question in plain language

Suppose a large graph is locally dense: **every group of $p$ vertices spans at least $q$ edges**. How large a complete subgraph, or *clique*, must the whole graph contain? [Erdős Problem #667](https://www.erdosproblems.com/667), attributed to Erdős, Faudree, Rousseau and Schelp, asks whether stronger local density forces a strictly larger polynomial clique exponent.

For fixed $p,q$, write $H(n;p,q)$ for the largest integer $m$ such that every qualifying graph on $n$ vertices contains a clique with $m$ vertices, and set

$$

c(p,q)=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log H(n;p,q)}{\log n}.

$$

The question is whether $c(p,q)$ increases **strictly** as the allowed integer $q$ grows from $1$ to $\binom{p-1}{2}+1$. The maintained catalogue records **OPEN**.

## A small example

Take $p=3$ and $q=2$. Every triple must contain two edges or more. In the complementary graph, every triple therefore contains at most one edge. Two missing edges sharing a vertex would themselves form two edges inside some triple, so the complement is a **matching**.

Deleting a maximum matching from the complete graph leaves a graph satisfying the condition, with maximum clique of size about $n/2$. Conversely, a complement consisting of a matching allows us to select one vertex from each missing edge and every unmatched vertex, giving a clique with at least $\lceil n/2\rceil$ vertices. Consequently

$$

H(n;3,2)=\lceil n/2\rceil,\qquad c(3,2)=1.

$$

For $p=3,q=1$, the complement only needs to be triangle-free. The classical triangle-free Ramsey estimates give $c(3,1)=1/2$. Thus strictness holds in this small endpoint comparison, without settling the question for all $p$.

## What is known

**Nondecreasing is easy.** A graph satisfying every $p$ subset edge lower bound $q+1$ also satisfies the weaker bound $q$. Hence the family of admissible graphs shrinks, and

$$

H(n;p,q+1)\geq H(n;p,q).

$$

Taking logarithms and lower limits gives $c(p,q+1)\geq c(p,q)$. This says **nondecreasing**, which is weaker than the conjectured **strictly increasing**. One cannot infer a strict inequality without a new estimate.

The maintained [#667 statement](https://www.erdosproblems.com/667) reports the Ramsey-type benchmark $1/(p-1)\leq c(p,1)\leq2/(p+1)$, the top-endpoint value $c(p,\binom{p-1}{2}+1)=1$, and a bound $c(p,\binom{p-1}{2})\leq1/2$. These are catalogued prior results, not proofs recovered from the original Erdős–Faudree–Rousseau–Schelp publication in this audit.

A useful dual view is the complement: at least $q$ edges on every $p$ vertices in $G$ is exactly **at most $\binom p2-q$ edges** on every $p$ vertices in its complement. This translates forced-clique questions into independence-number questions under forbidden local edge counts.

## What remains

A bounded follow-up should choose $p=4$ and one adjacent pair of $q$ values, then derive **upper and lower polynomial exponents** for $H(n;4,q)$ under the exact induced-subgraph conditions. A finite graph search may disprove a proposed local lemma, but it cannot establish asymptotic strictness.

The original historical source and cited benchmarks still need direct review. Later editorial admission requires critical mathematics/source checks, a protected production merge and a browser rendering receipt, followed by a separate V2 exact-hash record.
