## The question in plain language

An integer is *squarefree* when no prime square divides it. [Erdős Problem #11](https://www.erdosproblems.com/11) asks whether **every sufficiently large odd integer** can be written

$$
n=s+2^e,\qquad s>0\text{ squarefree},\quad e\ge0.
$$

An older, stronger formulation asks whether **every odd integer greater than one** has this form. It would be an error to treat the two quantified statements as equivalent merely because many small integers have been checked. The large-integer assertion is still open in the reviewed source record.

## A small example

The odd integer $11$ can be written as $7+2^2$: the remainder 7 is squarefree. Exponent 1 instead gives $11-2=9$, not squarefree because $3^2$ divides 9. Since exponent zero is also allowed in the formal statement, $11=10+2^0$ is another valid representation. Thus the smallest **positive** exponent and the smallest **nonnegative** exponent can differ.

A squarefreeness test checks whether any prime square divides the remainder. A finite search over exponents and odd integers can verify examples, but cannot establish a claim for all sufficiently large odd integers.

## What is known

Computational work reported by the maintained problem registry includes a check by Hercher through $2^{50}$, far beyond the smaller illustrative [GCL finite replay](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1280#issuecomment-6103789133). The large finite threshold does **not** turn the universal problem into a theorem.

Granville and Soundararajan, *A binary additive problem of Erdős and the order of 2 mod $p^2$*, *The Ramanujan Journal* **2** (1998), 283–298, relate stronger representation hypotheses to the distribution of non-Wieferich primes. Their implications are **conditional**. In particular an implication about the older all-odd hypothesis must not be stated as a consequence of the weaker eventual question without further proof.

## GCL's current state

The [source audit #1280](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1280#issuecomment-6103789133) identifies the old global versus current eventual quantifier difference, the exponent-zero distinction, and the frozen Lean statement's unfilled proof. It reports an independent integer-only replay for all 499,999 odd numbers from 3 through 999,999 using **positive** exponents, with exact witness hashes and histogram, plus a separate exponent-zero analysis. That is useful reproducibility evidence but not an asymptotic proof or an independent replay of Hercher's much larger computation.

## What remains

An affirmative proof must handle every odd integer beyond a single fixed threshold, not merely a finite prefix or most odd integers. A refutation needs arbitrarily large odd exceptions. The source work also leaves a formalization decision: whether to encode the current eventual question or separately preserve the older stronger variant.
