## The question in plain language

Choose a set $A$ of integers from $1$ through $n$. Its **coprime graph** $G(A)$ has the numbers in $A$ as vertices and joins two **distinct** numbers precisely when their greatest common divisor is $1$. How large must $A$ be before this graph necessarily contains odd cycles of prescribed lengths?

Erdős and Sárközy identify the exact natural threshold

$$
b(n)=\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac n3\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac n6\right\rfloor.
$$

[Erdős Problem #883](https://www.erdosproblems.com/883) asks whether every set with $|A|>b(n)$ contains every odd cycle of length at most $n/3+1$. The phrase *every odd cycle length* means that, for each eligible odd integer $q$, there must be a **simple** cycle of length $q$, not that a single cycle simultaneously has multiple lengths. A second question asks for a complete tripartite subgraph with part sizes $(1,\ell,\ell)$ for every fixed positive integer $\ell$ when $n$ is sufficiently large.

## A small example

Let $B_n$ be the integers at most $n$ divisible by $2$ or $3$. Inclusion–exclusion gives $|B_n|=b(n)$. Its coprime graph is bipartite: put the even elements in one part, and the odd multiples of $3$ in the other. Two distinct even numbers share factor $2$, and two odd multiples of $3$ share factor $3$. Therefore **no odd cycle occurs**, even though $B_n$ has exactly the threshold number of vertices. This demonstrates why the strict inequality $|A|>b(n)$ is necessary.

For $n=12$, the threshold is $b(12)=8$. An exhaustive graph search of **all 299 subsets of $[12]$ containing at least nine elements** finds that each coprime graph contains both a triangle and a simple cycle of length $5$. Finite data are informative examples, not a proof for arbitrary $n$ or of the proposed sharp $n/3+1$ length bound.

## What is known

Erdős and Sárközy, [*On cycles in the coprime graph of integers*](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v4i2r8/pdf/), *Electronic Journal of Combinatorics* **4**(2) (1997), #R8, prove Theorem 1: there exist absolute constants $c>0$ and $n_0$ such that if $n\ge n_0$ and $|A|>b(n)$, then $G(A)$ contains **every** cycle of length $2j+1$ for $1\le j\le cn$. This is a linear range of odd cycle lengths. The theorem does **not** identify $c=1/6$, which would give the conjectured maximum length of approximately $n/3$.

Sárközy, [*Complete tripartite subgraphs in the coprime graph of integers*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0012-365X(98)00359-8), *Discrete Mathematics* **202** (1999), pp.227–238, answers the **second** question affirmatively. More strongly, a complete $(1,\ell,\ell)$ subgraph exists for $\ell$ increasing at least at a logarithmic scale with $n$ under the same density threshold, so every fixed $\ell$ eventually works. The maintained catalogue states a conservative growing bound $\ell\gg\log n/\log\log n$. The precise iterated-logarithm improvement reported in the 1999 article should be source-locked before being used as a formal theorem.

Thus **the fixed-tripartite question is solved**, but the *sharp long-odd-cycle question remains open*. The catalogue's single overall “OPEN” label applies to this surviving part, not to both statements. No independent GCL reproduction of either long proof is asserted.

## GCL's current state

The mathematical threshold and the associated bipartite obstruction have short independent proofs: counting multiples of $2$ or $3$ gives $b(n)$, and the partition by parity gives bipartiteness. These are exact for every positive $n$. The open issue starts immediately above this extremal cardinality and concerns **how long** the guaranteed odd cycles must be.

The reproducible program governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_883_884.py constructs coprime graphs using exact integer GCD arithmetic. It independently verifies the triangle predicate with two algorithms, checks the bipartite construction for $3\le n\le12$, and exhaustively tests the demanded odd lengths through $n=12$. In particular, every one of the 299 strict-superthreshold sets for $n=12$ contains a triangle and cycle of length $5$. It does not test the asymptotic tripartite theorem or certify the optimum possible cycle length in general.

Source-fidelity details matter: all graph vertices must be **distinct**, the proposed cycle is simple, the threshold contains three floor functions, and the Erdős–Sárközy 1997 result has an unspecified positive $c$, not the conjectured value $1/6$. Replacing the threshold with $2n/3$ without integer corrections is unsafe when $6$ does not divide $n$.

## What remains

A bounded useful successor is the **bipartite extremal certificate plus fixed-length graph interface**. The input is an integer $n\ge3$, the set $B_n$ of multiples of $2$ or $3$, and the coprime graph on any finite vertex set. Prove formally that $|B_n|=b(n)$ and $G(B_n)$ is bipartite. Then define a simple odd-cycle predicate and establish its agreement with the finite adjacency-based fixture for $n\le12$.

Acceptance is a complete elementary proof or compiled Lean development without placeholders, the exact floor-count identity, and matching two-method triangle and simple-cycle results. A contrary odd cycle entirely inside $B_n$, or a mismatch in GCD adjacency, falsifies the task. Stop at this finite structural interface. Moving from the 1997 linear range to the conjectured $n/3+1$ requires a genuinely new large values of $n$ argument, not extrapolation from the finite table.
