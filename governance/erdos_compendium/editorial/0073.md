## The question in plain language

An independent set in a graph contains no adjacent pair of vertices. Every bipartite graph has an independent set of at least half its vertices, namely the larger side of its two-coloring. Erdős #73 asks whether a graph is *almost bipartite* if every subgraph has an independent set of size at least half its vertices, apart from a fixed additive defect.

More precisely, for a fixed $k\ge0$, assume every finite subgraph $H$ on $n$ vertices satisfies

$$
\alpha(H)\ge\frac{n-k}{2}.
$$

Must deleting at most $f(k)$ vertices make the whole graph bipartite? The constant must depend only on $k$, not on the number of vertices.

## A small example

An odd cycle $C_{2t+1}$ has independence number exactly $t$, because one cannot choose adjacent vertices. For $k=0$, its required lower bound would be $t+1/2$, impossible. Thus any graph satisfying the $k=0$ condition contains no odd cycle and is bipartite. This elementary case is a complete proof only when $k=0$.

For $k>0$ an odd cycle may fit within the allowed defect. Counting odd cycles individually does not establish that all obstructions are contained in a *bounded set of vertices*.

## What is known

The problem is **PROVED** by Bruce Reed. The primary publication is *Mangoes and Blueberries*, *Combinatorica* 19 (1999), pages 267–296, DOI 10.1007/s004930050056. Its bibliographic entry is recorded by the publisher and the Combinatorica volume index. The maintained Erdős problem explicitly attributes the general statement to Reed.

An older graph-problem list described an unpublished solution. The existence of Reed's 1999 article corrects the historical provenance, but its full text was not available for a theorem-by-theorem check in this pass. In particular, GCL has not extracted an explicit bound on $f(k)$ or certified whether additional finiteness conventions are present in the primary theorem. The source classification is not a claim that GCL has reconstructed the deep proof.

## What remains

A bounded source-fidelity task is to inspect Reed's printed proof, identify the exact finite-versus-infinite graph hypothesis, and extract or bound $f(k)$. A separate formal exercise establishes $\alpha(C_{2t+1})=t$ and “a graph is bipartite if and only if it contains no odd cycle.” Neither exercise resolves the general $k>0$ case without Reed's deeper structural machinery.
