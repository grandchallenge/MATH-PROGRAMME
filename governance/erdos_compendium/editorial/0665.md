## The question in plain language

How nearly equal in size can the blocks of a **pairwise balanced design** be? In [Erdős Problem #665](https://www.erdosproblems.com/665), a design on $n$ points is a family of proper blocks $A_1,\ldots,A_m$ satisfying $2\leq |A_i|<n$, with every unordered pair of different ground points contained in **exactly one** block. The question is whether a constant $C>0$ suffices to guarantee a design for every sufficiently large $n$ with all block sizes greater than $\sqrt n-C$.

Importantly, $C$ must not depend on $n$; and the requirement $|A_i|<n$ excludes a meaningless one-block construction. The maintained status is **OPEN**.

## A small example

On the seven residues modulo seven, let $D=\{0,1,3\}$, and take the seven translated blocks $D+i$ ($i=0,\ldots,6$). Their six nonzero oriented differences cover all six nonzero residues exactly once, so every pair of distinct points lies in exactly one three-point block. This is the Fano plane of order two.

Every block has size three and all blocks are proper. The exact pair-counting identity is $7\binom32=\binom72=21$. Since $3>\sqrt7$, this tiny example satisfies the proposed lower bound with $C=0$; it does not solve the problem for every sufficiently large $n$.

## What is known

Each unordered pair appears in one and only one block, so every design satisfies the fundamental counting identity

$$
\sum_{i=1}^m\binom{|A_i|}{2}=\binom n2.
$$

An entire projective plane of order $q$ supplies $n=q^2+q+1$ points and the same number of lines, each with $q+1$ points. Since $q+1>\sqrt{q^2+q+1}$, this produces excellent designs for **special** sizes $n$. The issue is that the assertion concerns **all sufficiently large integers** $n$, not only those admitting known projective planes.

The [original 1982 Erdős–Larson journal chapter](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-0208(08)73259-6), *Annals of Discrete Mathematics* **15**, 129–134, has a publisher summary giving uniform constructions with block sizes $\sqrt n+O(n^{1/2-c})$ for some positive $c$. Its abstract notes a conditional refinement to error $O((\log n)^2)$ under strong conjectures on gaps between consecutive primes. Neither estimate supplies an unconditional constant error.

Shrikhande and Singhi's [1985 paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02579251), *Combinatorica* **5**, 351–358, gives an embedding obstruction for near $\sqrt n$ blocks. As reported by its author abstract and the maintained problem source, a negative solution follows **assuming** that every finite projective plane has prime-power order. This conjectural restriction must not be treated as a proved classification of finite projective planes.
