## The question in plain language

Disjoint triangles are connected by a Hamiltonian cycle whose edges are all new. Coloring every triangle alone is straightforward, but it is not obvious that the cycle can be accommodated with just three vertex colors. Fleischner and Stiebitz proved that it can.

Take n disjoint triangles on 3n vertices and add a Hamiltonian cycle all of whose edges are NEW (distinct from triangle edges). Must the resulting simple graph be 3-vertex-colorable? Yes for every admissible graph, not only a particular cycle. For n=1, no Hamilton cycle exists with all its edges new, so the domain is configurations satisfying that condition.

## A small example

For n=2 take vertex triples (0,1,2), (3,4,5). The Hamilton cycle 0–3–1–4–2–5–0 uses only cross-triangle edges. Proper three-coloring, for example assign 0,1,2 colors 0,1,2 and 3,4,5 colors 2,0,1, so all six cross-cycle edges receive different endpoint colors.

A finite instance tests the definition, not a claim quantified over every large graph, infinite set or arbitrary coloring.

## What is known

Maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/842, read 2026-10-14, reports Fleischner–Stiebitz [FlSt92], *A solution to a colouring problem of P. Erdős*, Discrete Mathematics (1992), 39–48 proved YES. Their full primary proof not independently replayed. This is the famous cycle-plus-triangles coloring problem; do not generalize arbitrary union of 3-regular graphs or edge-overlapping cycles.

Three colors are necessary because each original triangle is a clique of order three. The Fleischner–Stiebitz theorem says they are always sufficient. For a six-vertex example, two triangles (0,1,2) and (3,4,5) with cross-triangle cycle 0-3-1-4-2-5-0 admit colors (0,1,2) and (2,0,1) respectively. That verifies one configuration, not the global theorem.
