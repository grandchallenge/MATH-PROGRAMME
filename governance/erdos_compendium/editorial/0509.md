**Editorial status:** UNACCEPTED_DRAFT. Erdős Problem #509 is **OPEN**. It asks whether every monic nonconstant complex polynomial has a closed unit sublevel set coverable by disks whose radii sum to at most two. Cartan obtained a larger universal constant, Pommerenke improved it to 2.59, and the desired constant two is known when the lemniscate is connected under specified hypotheses. A covering theorem for all possible disconnected configurations is still missing.

## The question in plain language

Let

$$
f(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-a_j),\qquad n\ge1,
$$

be a monic complex polynomial, with repeated roots allowed. Consider its **filled lemniscate**

$$
E_f=\{z\in\mathbb C:|f(z)|\le1\}.
$$

Can we cover every point of $E_f$ by closed Euclidean **disks**

$$
D_j=\{z:|z-c_j|\le r_j\}
$$

whose radii satisfy

$$
\boxed{\sum_j r_j\le2?}
$$

The word *circles* in Erdős's 1961 wording refers to the filled covering disks, not just their circumferences. This is a sum of **radii**, not diameters, squared radii or areas. A finite cover suffices if one exists; the pinned formal statement also permits a summable indexed family of positive-radius disks.

The polynomial must be **monic**. Multiplying a polynomial by a non-unit constant changes the set $|f(z)|\le1$, and the same numerical question is not invariant under that change. The boundary case $|f(z)|=1$ is included in the set.

## A small example

For a linear polynomial $f(z)=z-a$, we have $E_f=D(a,1)$, so one disk of radius one covers the entire set.

The complete **quadratic case** also has an elementary proof. Let

$$
f(z)=(z-a)(z-b).
$$

If $z\in E_f$, then $|z-a||z-b|\le1$. It is impossible for both factors to be strictly greater than one. Consequently,

$$
E_f\subseteq D(a,1)\cup D(b,1).
$$

The two radii sum to **exactly two**. If $a=b$, the lemniscate is itself the unit disk about $a$, so one disk of radius one suffices.

For a general degree-$n$ monic polynomial, the same product argument gives

$$
E_f\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}D(a_j,1).
$$

Unfortunately those $n$ disks have total radius $n$, which is not useful for proving the fixed target **two** when $n$ is large. This demonstrates the core challenge: how can one exploit clusters and geometric dependence among roots to compress the *sum* of covering radii?

A second useful family is $f(z)=(z-a)^n$. Its lemniscate is again just $D(a,1)$, regardless of the degree. High degree alone does not force a difficult covering; the geometry of **several components** is the issue.

## What is known

### The original 1961 question and universal bounds

Erdős's *Some unsolved problems* (1961), printed p.246, first problem of Section IV, [original scanned page](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1961-22.pdf#page=26), was visually inspected. He reports that **Henri Cartan** proved a universal cover with sum of radii less than $2e$, and that **Christian Pommerenke** reduced the constant to **2.59**. Erdős then asks whether the optimal bound of **two** always holds.

Cartan's original source is *Sur les systèmes de fonctions holomorphes à variétés linéaires lacunaires et leurs applications*, *Annales scientifiques de l'École Normale Supérieure* (3) **45** (1928), 255–346, [publisher record](https://www.numdam.org/item/ASENS_1928_3_45__255_0/). The historical claim is attributed to the published theorem. The complete 1928 proof was not reconstructed in this audit.

Pommerenke's *On metric properties of complex polynomials*, *Michigan Mathematical Journal* **8** (1961), 97–115, [DOI 10.1307/mmj/1028998561](https://doi.org/10.1307/mmj/1028998561), is the source credited for the 2.59 universal result. Again, that number is a **known upper bound** on the required radii sum, not a discovered counterexample to the conjectured value two. A covering of total radius 2.59 does not demonstrate that every smaller cover is impossible.

### The connected-set theorem

Pommerenke's earlier *On some problems by Erdős, Herzog and Piranian*, *Michigan Mathematical Journal* **6** (1959), 221–225, [DOI 10.1307/mmj/1028998227](https://doi.org/10.1307/mmj/1028998227), Theorem 3, gives the connected-set special case.

More precisely, when the **open** set $\{z:|f(z)|<1\}$ is connected, his result places the lemniscate inside a disk of radius two about the **mean of the zeros**,

$$
\zeta=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n a_j.
$$

The later 1961 paper treats the slightly different assumption that the **closed filled lemniscate** $E_f$ is connected, in its discussion and proof of Theorem 10(b). Connectedness of the open strict sublevel set is not interchangeable without argument with connectedness of the closed sublevel set.

These theorem locations were cross-checked against the primary publication metadata and indexed descriptions, but their full original proof bodies were **not** independently retrieved. The maintained [Erdős #509 page](https://www.erdosproblems.com/509) retains the general **OPEN** status, and references both results and the related Erdős #1046.

## GCL's current state

The [authenticated original #509 source return](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1780#issuecomment-6191274667) records the exact hypotheses and one self-contained bounded mathematical task. The source packet and the dated catalogue both classify the general constant-two problem as **OPEN**.

The pinned [Formal Conjectures 509.lean](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/509.lean), Git blob a893d7bb685858fa183145abbef928a2d1d7c554, defines a summable indexed cover by closed balls with positive radii. It separately records the universal constant-two question, Cartan's bound, the universal Pommerenke bound and the connected case. The proof bodies remain placeholders; this source is **not** a proof or a GCL Lean kernel certificate.

The exact arithmetic fixture verifies degree-one and degree-two root-disk covers with Gaussian rational roots, including points on the boundary. A further explicit quadratic family

$$
f(z)=z^2-R^2,\qquad R\ge1,
$$

satisfies

$$
E_f\subseteq D(R,1/R)\cup D(-R,1/R).
$$

To see this, in the right half-plane $|z+R|\ge R$, so $|z-R|\le1/R$ whenever $|f(z)|\le1$; the left half-plane is symmetric. The covering radii sum to $2/R\le2$. This is a proof for a special family, not a global conjecture solution.

## What remains

The genuine unsolved step is a uniform radius-sum **compression theorem for disconnected lemniscates**, without a dependence on the degree or number of roots. The source-fidelity prerequisite is to inspect Pommerenke's 1959 Theorem 3 and the 1961 Theorem 10(b) directly, to pin down exactly which connectedness conditions yield a single radius-two disk.

A practical bounded task is to check the universal degree-two argument symbolically and then exhaustively evaluate it on rational Gaussian grids. Accept only coverings that include the closed boundary, use positive radii and sum **radii** rather than diameters. Finite grids do not prove arbitrary high-degree coverings.
