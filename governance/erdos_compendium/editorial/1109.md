## The question in plain language

Choose some integers between $1$ and $N$. Can we arrange that **every sum of two chosen numbers is squarefree**, meaning no prime square divides it?

[Erdős Problem #1109](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1109) asks how large such a set $A$ can be. Writing the maximum size as $f(N)$, the conjecture is that it grows extraordinarily slowly, perhaps no faster than a fixed power of $\log N$.

The condition applies to $a+a$ as well as to sums of *different* elements. In particular, each $2a$ must be squarefree.

## A small example

For $N=16$, exact exhaustive search shows

$$
f(16)=2.
$$

One optimal set is $A=\{11,15\}$, whose distinct two-term sums are

$$
A+A=\{22,26,30\}.
$$

All three are squarefree. The computation first filters the seven numbers with squarefree self-sums, then checks **every** subset for all pairwise sums. No three-element subset qualifies.

This is an exact result for $N=16$, not an estimate of the growth of $f(N)$ for arbitrarily large $N$.

## What is known

Erdős and Sárközy (1987) studied the problem and established the rough bounds

$$
\log N\ll f(N)\ll N^{3/4}\log N.
$$

The maintained catalogue reports Konyagin's (2004) improvements:

$$
(\log\log N)(\log N)^2
\ll f(N)\ll N^{11/15+o(1)}.
$$

The conjectured $f(N)\le N^{o(1)}$, let alone polylogarithmic growth, is still **open**.

The infinite analogue is [Problem #1103](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1103): construct an infinite increasing sequence with squarefree pair sums while making its terms grow slowly. Bounds on $f(N)$ yield restrictions on the growth of such an infinite sequence, but the two problems must retain their different quantifiers.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source audit #2380](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2380#issuecomment-6168833257) reproduces the complete bounded $N=16$ search and maintains the diagonal $a=a$ constraint.

The current catalogue indicates a formalized statement, but the older pinned formal-conjectures snapshot used by GCL has no corresponding #1109 file. That requires version reconciliation, not an inference that there is no current formalization.

## What remains

Independently inspect the 1987 and 2004 extremal arguments, confirm all exponent losses and squarefree-sum conventions, and compare the finite and infinite versions without conflating them. An independent modular formulation could verify finite maxima for larger $N$.
