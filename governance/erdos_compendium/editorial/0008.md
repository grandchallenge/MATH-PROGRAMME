## The question in plain language

A **covering system** is a finite collection of congruences whose union contains every integer. Its positive moduli are required to be distinct and greater than one. [Erdős Problem #8](https://www.erdosproblems.com/8) asked whether every finite colouring of the positive integers has a colour containing **all the moduli of some covering system**.

The answer is **no**. This is a historical problem already disproved using Bob Hough's theorem on the smallest modulus of a distinct covering system. The word *finite* permits a very large number of colours, not just two.

## A small example

Suppose there were a universal bound $B$ such that every distinct covering system had at least one modulus at most $B$. Colour the numbers $1,2,\ldots,B$ using different colours, and colour all numbers greater than $B$ with one new colour.

A monochromatic covering system cannot use the last colour, because every one of its moduli would exceed $B$. It cannot use an earlier colour: that colour contains only one possible modulus $m$, and a single congruence modulo $m>1$ misses infinitely many integers. This is a complete elementary contradiction, conditional on the bound $B$.

## What is known

Hough, *Solution of the minimum modulus problem for covering systems*, *Annals of Mathematics* **181** (2015), 361–382, proved that the smallest modulus of any distinct covering system is **at most** $10^{16}$. Thus the colouring construction with $B=10^{16}$ settles Erdős #8 negatively. The public problem page illustrates the same argument with the looser cutoff $10^{18}$; that is not a discrepancy in the theorem.

A related density formulation is also described as disproved by the registry, but its separate quantified premise is not reproduced or independently verified by this finite-colouring argument.

The admitted frozen formal-provider snapshot had no file for this problem; the external theorem's complete proof was not replayed inside GCL.

## What remains

Nothing remains to settle the original finite-colouring question. Further documentary or formal work should focus on explicitly importing Hough's theorem, checking the definition of a covering system, and separately documenting the density variant—not reopening the solved assertion as a conjecture.
