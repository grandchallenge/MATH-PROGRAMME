## The question in plain language

Start with a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers $a_1<a_2<\cdots$. Call it a Type-3 irrationality sequence if every bounded integer perturbation still has an irrational reciprocal sum. Precisely, the requirement is

$$
\forall b:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{Z},\quad \left(\exists C\in\mathbb{N}\;\forall n,\ |b_n|\le C\right)\land\left(\forall n,\ b_n\ne0\ \land\ a_n+b_n\ne0\right)
$$

implies

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_n+b_n}\notin\mathbb{Q}.
$$

The boundedness quantifier is uniform: one single $C$ must work for all indices. The perturbations may vary arbitrarily from term to term, but they may not be zero, and no denominator may vanish. For $a_n=2^n$ and $a_n=n!$, every permitted perturbed series converges absolutely, so the question is genuinely about rationality rather than convergence.

There is an important historical normalization issue. Erdős and Graham’s 1980 book and Erdős’s 1988 survey describe bounded integer shifts without explicitly writing $b_n\ne0$. Kovač and Tao add that condition so that the $2^n$ question is meaningful: if zero shifts were allowed, the rational identity $\sum_{n\ge1}2^{-n}=1$ would immediately defeat the property. The modern website statement and the pinned Lean declaration use the added nonzero-shift convention. These are related formulations, but they must not be silently treated as identical.

## A small example

For $a_n=2^n$, the basic series is geometric:

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^n}=1.
$$

The relevant Kovač–Tao test does not inspect this sum alone. It measures the squared tail by

$$
4^n\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}4^{-k}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}4^{-j}=\frac{1}{3}.
$$

Thus the lower-tail quantity is uniformly positive, exactly the shape required by their theorem. A finite, independently reproducible check is

$$
4^n\sum_{k=n+1}^{n+6}4^{-k}=\sum_{j=1}^{6}4^{-j}=\frac{1365}{4096},
$$

for every $n$. Exact rational Python arithmetic reproduced this value for $n=1,3,8$; the omitted positive tail is what changes the finite value into $1/3$.

The same calculation also shows why a finite experiment cannot settle the problem. A finite list of chosen shifts only produces a rational partial sum. The theorem must control an infinite continuation and show that some bounded continuation lands exactly on a rational number.

For comparison, factorials behave differently under this test. Writing $k=n+j$ and using

$$
\frac{n!}{(n+j)!}=\frac{1}{(n+1)(n+2)\cdots(n+j)}\le\frac{1}{(n+1)^j},
$$

gives

$$
(n!)^2\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k!)^2}\le\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+1)^{2j}}=\frac{1}{n(n+2)}\longrightarrow0.
$$

This is a check on a hypothesis, not a solution of the factorial problem.

## What is known

Kovač and Tao prove the decisive negative result for the exponential branch. Their Theorem 2.5 in arXiv version 4, §2.1.3 (reported as Theorem 5 in the published numbering) says that if $a_n$ is strictly increasing and positive, $\sum_n1/a_n$ converges, and

$$
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(a_n^2\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a_k^2}\right)>0,
$$

then $a$ is not a Type-3 irrationality sequence. Their Corollary 2.6 further covers every strictly increasing sequence with bounded successive ratio in the limsup sense. Since $2^n$ has ratio $2$ and the calculation above gives the required tail value $1/3$, the theorem supplies a bounded nonzero integer perturbation whose reciprocal sum is rational. The source’s proof interface is existential, not an explicit formula for the perturbation.

The proof works by an interval-subsums lemma. After choosing a constant $C$ from the lower-tail hypothesis, it allows positive shifts from a finite interval such as $1,2,\ldots,4C+1$. The possible tail sums overlap enough that a rational target is hit. Positive shifts automatically satisfy the modern $b_n\ne0$ condition, and the construction keeps denominators positive. This proves that the modern $2^n$ branch is answered negatively.

The factorial branch is not answered by that theorem. The exact estimate above shows that its lower-tail hypothesis fails strongly, tending to zero. That only says that this proof interface cannot be reused directly. It does not produce an irrationality proof, a rational perturbation, or a counterexample. Kovač and Tao also show that Type-3 sequences can exist with prescribed sufficiently super-exponential growth, and explicitly note that the factorial variant is probably more difficult; growth rate alone therefore cannot settle the issue.

## What remains

The $2^n$ subproblem needs no claim of a new GCL proof: the external Kovač–Tao theorem is the relevant primary proof source, and its hypotheses can be checked exactly. What remains is to preserve the definition boundary, reconcile historical wording, and avoid presenting the existence argument as an explicit computable perturbation.

The $n!$ subproblem remains genuinely open in the source packet. A finite search over bounded perturbation prefixes can test particular rational targets, but failure to find a target proves nothing about all infinite perturbations. Conversely, one exact infinite perturbation with a rational sum would disprove the factorial Type-3 property. Any future formal work must first prove semantic equivalence between its indexing, boundedness, convergence, and nonzero-shift conventions and the modern statement. A Lean declaration, an external repository, or an unreviewed formalization claim cannot be promoted to an accepted proof merely because it compiles or is labelled solved.
