## The question in plain language

A finite graph G on n vertices is diameter-two EDGE-CRITICAL if diam(G)=2 and deleting ANY one edge increases its diameter (possibly to infinity). Murty–Simon ask whether |E(G)|≤floor(n²/4) for every such graph. The maintained catalogue says DECIDABLE rather than PROVED: Füredi establishes the inequality for all sufficiently large n, leaving finitely many n to check. The full all-n statement must NOT be promoted to a theorem merely from the sufficiently-large result.

## A small example

For n=5, K_{2,3} has 6 edges, diameter 2, and deleting any cross-edge makes its endpoints distance 3. Thus it is diameter-two edge-critical and has 6=floor(25/4) edges. In general K_{a,b} with a+b=n, a,b≥2, gives ab≤floor(n²/4), with equality for balanced parts.

## What is known

Füredi [Fu92] proves the Murty–Simon inequality for n≥n0.
