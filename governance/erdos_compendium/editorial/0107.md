## The question in plain language

Place $N$ points in the Euclidean plane, with **no three collinear**. How large must $N$ be to guarantee that some $n$ of the points form the vertices of a strictly convex $n$ gon, in some order? Write $f(n)$ for the minimum such number.

Erdős #107, the Erdős–Szekeres **Happy Ending problem**, conjectures

$$
f(n)=2^{n-2}+1
$$

for every $n\ge3$. The extremal configuration may have no three collinear but still contain many points in the interior of its convex hull.

## A small example

Four points in general position need not make a convex quadrilateral: one can lie strictly inside the triangle formed by the other three. But Klein observed that any five points with no three collinear contain four in convex position. Hence $f(4)=5=2^{4-2}+1$.

The five-point theorem is a finite geometric result, not a proof of the conjecture for arbitrarily large $n$. Similarly, the sharp value $f(5)=9$ established by Makai and Turán supplies one more case, not a general construction.

## What is known

The exact conjecture is **OPEN**. Erdős and Szekeres established the classic estimates

$$
2^{n-2}+1\le f(n)\le\binom{2n-4}{n-2}+1.
$$

The left inequality comes from arrangements avoiding a large convex polygon; the right from their order-type combinatorial argument. Suk's 2017 work substantially improved the upper growth rate to $2^{n+o(n)}$. The maintained [Erdős #107](https://www.erdosproblems.com/107) attributes the strongest recorded upper bound to Holmsen, Mojarrad, Pach and Tardos:

$$
f(n)\le2^{n+O(\sqrt{n\log n})}.
$$

The primary 2020 publisher source [*Two extensions of the Erdős–Szekeres problem*](https://doi.org/10.4171/JEMS/1000) was checked for its authors and scope. Its main abstract treats extensions to convex bodies, rather than serving as an independent verification of every constant in the **point-set** estimate cited by the catalogue. Those two settings must not be conflated.

The maintained entry labels #107 **FALSIFIABLE** because a finite counterexample to the exact equality would refute it, even though proving the equality for all $n$ would require more than finite enumeration.

## GCL's current state

The pinned registry and maintained page, last edited April 2026, both mark #107 falsifiable/open. The original source task is [#1376](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1376). No full proof of the upper estimate, exhaustive verification of $f(5)=9$, or new extremal value is certified by this editorial audit.

The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1376#issuecomment-6162330163). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded check is to formalize the five-point quadrilateral theorem using orientation signs of triples, and verify explicit four-point configurations with one interior point. Acceptance: in every realizable order type of five noncollinear points, a four-point subset has each vertex on its convex hull. Ensure configurations with collinear triples are rejected. Stop at this exact finite statement without extrapolating an infinite formula.
