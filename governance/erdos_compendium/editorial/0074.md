## The question in plain language

Could a graph require infinitely many vertex colors while every finite part becomes bipartite after deleting only a few edges? Erdős #74 asked whether this is possible *for every prescribed function* $f(n)\to\infty$, however slowly it grows. The graph may depend on $f$; each finite $n$‑vertex subgraph must need at most $f(n)$ edge deletions.

## A small example

A triangle becomes bipartite after deleting one edge, but the disjoint union of arbitrarily many triangles is only three-colorable. Few edge deletions in each small subgraph do not automatically produce high chromatic number. Quantifiers over *all* finite subgraphs matter.

For a graph $G$ write $b_G(n)$ for the maximum, over finite $n$‑vertex subgraphs $H$, of the minimum number of edges whose removal makes $H$ bipartite. The conjecture asserts the existence of infinite-chromatic $G$ with $b_G(n)\le f(n)$ for every divergent $f$.

## What is known

The universal assertion is classified **DISPROVED**. A 2026 Lean proof package associated with Tom Adamczewski's FrontierMath Erdős benchmark has a compared statement showing

$$
\neg\bigl[\forall f\to\infty,\;\exists G:\chi(G)=\infty
\text{ and }\forall n,\;b_G(n)\le f(n)\bigr].
$$

Equivalently, there is at least one divergent rate $f$ for which all qualifying graphs have finite chromatic number. The source package describes proofs of a stronger three-colorability result, but the theorem compared in Challenge.lean asserts only *finite* chromatic number, and the proposed explicit asymptotic rate is not part of its formal target.

The bounded verification target is to run the pinned Lean build and statement comparator, inspect axiom dependencies, and check semantics against the original graph-theoretic formulation. In particular, the helper definitions take minima over edge deletions and maxima over all finite subgraph candidates (not only induced ones); finite/infinite vertex types and the quantifier order must match precisely.
