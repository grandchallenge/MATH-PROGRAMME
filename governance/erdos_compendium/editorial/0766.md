## The question in plain language

For k-vertex graphs F with exactly l edges, define f(n;k,l)=min_F ex(n,F), where ex(n,F) is the greatest edge count of an n-vertex graph with no copy of F. Erdős asks for estimates when k<l≤k²/4 and whether, for fixed k and large n, f is STRICTLY increasing as l increases. This is OPEN. An elementary monotonic nondecreasing inequality DOES hold and must not be misreported as strict monotonicity.

## A small example

If H has l+1 edges on a fixed k-vertex set, delete any one edge to form F with l edges. Every F-free graph is H-free; hence ex(n,F)≤ex(n,H). Minimizing over all H gives f(n;k,l)≤f(n;k,l+1), for feasible l. The inequality need not be strict from this argument. For k=6 the target range is 6<l≤9, so compare l=7,8,9 rather than choosing an empty range such as k=4.

## What is known

Dirac and Erdős separately proved that just outside the target upper range, l=floor(k²/4)+1, one has f(n;k,l)≤floor(n²/4)+1. This boundary result does not answer the strict increase across every adjacent integer l IN the interval. The maintained #766 OPEN status and graph conventions are source-based; the complete Erdős 1964 statement and extremal proof not independently read.
