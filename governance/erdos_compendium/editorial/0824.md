## The question in plain language

Count h(x)=#{(a,b):1<=a<b<x, gcd(a,b)=1, sigma(a)=sigma(b)}. Does h(x)>x^(2-o(1)) as x grows? This is stronger than proving h(x)/x tends to infinity, and strict b<x is part of the count. A secondary squarefree variant and a no-trivial-factor variant are not identical to gcd(a,b)=1.

## A small example

(a,b)=(6,11) has gcd=1 and sigma=12 on both sides, contributing to h(x) for every x>11. (14,15) similarly are coprime with sigma=24. By contrast sigma(20)=sigma(26)=42 but gcd(20,26)=2, so this pair must be EXCLUDED.

The example can be checked exactly. A finite witness does not replace the universal, asymptotic, or infinitely-often quantifiers in the original question.

## What is known

Maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/824 read 2026-10-14 OPEN. Erdős [Er74b] established limsup h(x)/x=infinity; Pollack–Pomerance [PoPo16] *Some problems of Erdős on the sum-of-divisors function*, Trans AMS Ser B (2016), 1–26, established the stronger h(x)/x -> infinity. This does NOT imply the proposed exponent 2-o(1). Source exact proof not replayed; Weisenberg variant is separate.

The counting function records strictly ordered, coprime pairs with equal divisor sums:

$$

h(x)=\#\{(a,b):1\leq a<b<x,\ \gcd(a,b)=1,\ \sigma(a)=\sigma(b)\}.

$$

A proven $h(x)/x\to\infty$ is **far weaker** than the conjectured $h(x)>x^{2-o(1)}$. Indeed, functions like $x\log x$ satisfy the former growth but not the latter. The arithmetic coprimality restriction must not be dropped.
