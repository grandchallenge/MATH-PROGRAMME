## The question in plain language

Fix an integer \(k\ge2\). Let \(f_k(N)\) be the number of **unordered multisets of exactly \(k\) positive primes**, allowing repeated primes, satisfying
\[
N=p_1^k+\cdots+p_k^k.
\]
The **same** integer \(k\) is the number of prime summands **and** their common exponent. Erdős asked whether, for **each fixed** \(k\ge2\), the representation count is unbounded:
\[
\boxed{\limsup_{N\to\infty}f_k(N)=+\infty.}
\]
Equivalently, for each \(k\ge2\) and each integer \(R>0\), there must be **arbitrarily large** integers \(N\) having at least \(R\) essentially different representations. Here *essentially different* means different **multisets**, not different permutations of the same primes.

The maintained [Erdős Problem #979](https://www.erdosproblems.com/979) is correctly classified **OPEN in general**. Erdős proved the case **\(k=2\)** in a refereed 1937 paper and reported that he had also proved **\(k=3\)**, though that proof appears to be unpublished. There are now **two distinct 2026 affirmative \(k=3\) claims**, an August 17 Lean 4 development and an August 18 paper on arXiv. Neither addresses **all \(k\ge4\)**. An additional \(k=4\) result merely gives a **two-representation lower bound**, not an unbounded limsup.

## Why counting conventions matter

For fixed \(N,k\) define the ordered representation count
\[
R_k(N)=\#\{(p_1,\ldots,p_k):
p_j\text{ positive prime},\
p_1^k+\cdots+p_k^k=N\}.
\]
If all the primes in a representation are different, its unordered multiset contributes **\(k!\)** ordered tuples. If some primes repeat with multiplicities \(m_1,\ldots,m_r\), it contributes only
\[
\frac{k!}{m_1!\cdots m_r!}
\]
ordered tuples. Thus one always has
\[
f_k(N)\ \le\ R_k(N)\ \le\ k!\,f_k(N).
\]
For each **fixed \(k\)**, unboundedness of one count is equivalent to unboundedness of the other. Their actual **numerical values are not equal**, however, and comparing a computational table of ordered tuples directly against a theorem counting multisets can create a spurious factor of \(k!\).

The [Google DeepMind Formal Conjectures reference file](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/main/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/979.lean) models a solution as a **multiset of \(k\) natural primes** with sum of \(k\)-th powers equal to \(N\). That representation count agrees with the unordered interpretation adopted here, and permits repeated primes. The required \(\limsup=\infty\) quantifies **over all large represented integers \(N\)**, not merely all primes \(N\).

## Exact small witnesses

For \(k=2\), the integer
\[
2210=19^2+43^2=23^2+41^2=29^2+37^2
\]
has **three** unordered prime-square representations. All six numbers \(19,23,29,37,41,43\) are prime. Hence
\[
f_2(2210)\ge3.
\]
An exact enumeration over unordered prime pairs with total at most \(12000\) found exactly these three representations at \(2210\); this bounded calculation does **not** establish \(f_2(N)\to\infty\) or its limsup.

For \(k=3\), an exact collision is
\[
\begin{aligned}
185527&=13^3+43^3+47^3\\
      &=19^3+31^3+53^3.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(f_3(185527)\ge2\). This witness was independently enumerated using all unordered triples of primes below \(220\); the equalities are directly checkable with integer arithmetic.

For \(k=4\), an analogous, independent finite calculation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
726724&=7^4+7^4+11^4+29^4\\
      &=17^4+17^4+23^4+23^4.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore \(f_4(726724)\ge2\). Repetitions within the prime multiset are **permitted**, and the two representations are genuinely different. This does **not** imply \(f_4\) is unbounded.

The finite examples make clear the gap between finding isolated collisions and proving **arbitrarily many** collisions at arbitrarily high integers.

## The established \(k=2\) theorem

In *On the Sum and Difference of Squares of Primes*, *Journal of the London Mathematical Society* **12** (1937), pp.133–136, Erdős proved the limsup assertion for \(k=2\). A companion 1937 part in the same volume, pp.168–171, gives an improved quantitative lower bound for the number of representations along an infinite sequence of integers.

As summarized by the independent primary-source audit, the first paper yields for infinitely many \(N\) a representation count greater than roughly
\[
N^{c/(\log\log N)^2}
\]
for a positive absolute constant \(c\) in the appropriate asymptotic range; the second paper improves the scale to roughly
\[
N^{c/\log\log N}.
\]
These expressions diverge as \(N\to\infty\) and imply the desired qualitative unboundedness. The precise constant and exact normalization depend on the source version; this manuscript **does not** rederive the sieve and additive-number-theory machinery that produces them. The established conclusion is **unconditional for \(k=2\)**. [Source and claim review](https://erdosproblems.ai/wiki/problems/diophantine_problems/E0979).

## The \(k=3\) status, updated in August 2026

Erdős wrote in [Er65b, p.224] that he could prove the \(k=3\) result, but its proof seems not to have been published. That historical attribution is **not** an independently inspectable primary argument.

On **August 17, 2026**, Kenta Kitamura published [KitaKen1/erdos-979-k3](https://github.com/KitaKen1/erdos-979-k3), a **claimed complete, kernel-checked Lean 4 proof** of the multiset statement for \(k=3\). The accompanying README expressly says it addresses **only** the \(k=3\) case, not the higher-\(k\) conjecture or any quantitative rate. The research index records that this proof was subsequently named as the source for the \(\texttt{erdos\_979.variants.k3}\) declaration in the Formal Conjectures catalogue. The index **has not independently built that Lean file**, and GCL has not pinned its full dependency graph, checked the theorem's closed axiom set, or validated the absence of admitted assumptions. Thus it is a **reported formal proof**, not yet an independent GCL formal verification.

On **August 18, 2026**, Yukai Wang and Xu Zhang submitted the unconditional mathematical preprint [*Many Representations as Sums of Three Prime Cubes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19262). It states that
\[
\limsup_{N\to\infty} f_3(N)=\infty
\]
and identifies the classical **Hecke equidistribution theorem for the CM Fermat cubic** as its principal input, together with primes-in-arithmetic-progressions estimates and elementary counting. The paper is a **specific public mathematical proof claim**, not a verified reconstruction by GCL. It is also not shown here to have passed journal refereeing.

These two routes make the \(k=3\) case substantially better documented than Erdős's earlier unpublished assertion. They must nevertheless be distinguished from the still-open **all-\(k\)** problem. [Independent \(k=3\) claim inventory](https://erdosproblems.ai/wiki/problems/diophantine_problems/E0979).

## Why the \(k=4\) partial result does not finish a case

The same Wang–Zhang paper proves, by an argument involving **Green–Tao–Ziegler** theory and an admissible binary quartic identity, that
\[
\limsup_{N\to\infty} f_4(N)\ge2.
\]
This guarantees that two representations occur for arbitrarily large \(N\), which is much stronger than an isolated numerical identity but **far weaker** than
\[
\limsup_{N\to\infty} f_4(N)=+\infty.
\]
A finite positive lower bound (even if achieved infinitely often) never implies an unbounded count. The catalogue and independent research corpus list **no** result settling the unboundedness conjecture for any \(k\ge4\).

The detailed arithmetic of the quartic construction is an appropriate subject for a separate audit; it cannot be advertised as an affirmative answer to Erdős #979 for \(k=4\).

## Why the general problem is difficult

The number of primes up to \(X^{1/k}\) is about \(kX^{1/k}/\log X\). Thus an entirely naive count of all possible \(k\)-element prime multisets with each prime bounded by \(X^{1/k}\) gives on the order of
\[
\frac{X}{(\log X)^k}
\]
candidate representations for values \(N\le kX\). Averages across the **full** integer interval tend to be small, so a pigeonhole argument distributing all tuples evenly among all \(N\) will not produce unbounded multiplicities.

The core requirement is highly structured **collisions** of \(k\)-fold sums of prime \(k\)-th powers, even though the set of represented integers is sparse. Fermat-type identities, automorphisms and prime-point distribution on associated algebraic varieties may permit such collisions, but the needed arithmetic geometry becomes considerably harder as \(k\) rises. The special CM Fermat cubic route in the 2026 \(k=3\) preprint is not a general theorem for Fermat forms of every degree.

One must also control the positivity and primality of **each** summand. A Diophantine identity between unrestricted integer powers, or even between powers of composite numbers, is not a certificate of the required representation count.
