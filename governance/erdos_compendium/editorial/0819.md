## The question in plain language

Let k=floor(sqrt N). Among all A subset {1,...,N} of EXACT size k, define f(N) as the MAXIMUM cardinality of (A+A)∩[1,N]; summands may be equal. Estimate f(N)/N, not the full (untruncated) sumset size.

## A small example

Exact exhaustive finite checks: N=5, k=2 gives f(5)=3, attained by A={1,2}; N=9, k=3 gives f(9)=6, for example A={1,3,4}; N=16, k=4 gives f(16)=10, A={2,4,7,8}. The finite ratios need not lie between asymptotic 3/8 and 1/2 due to o(1) terms.

## What is known

Maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/819, read 2026-10-14, credits Erdős–Freud [ErFr91], *On sums of a Sidon-sequence*, Journal of Number Theory (1991), 196–205, with (3/8-o(1))N<=f(N)<=(1/2+o(1))N. Sharp constant remains OPEN and connected to quasi-Sidon sets (#840). Original 1991 proof uninspected.

The exact parameter is $k=\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor$. Counting unordered pairs of selected elements, WITH repetitions, gives $k(k+1)/2$ candidate sums; collisions and sums exceeding $N$ can only reduce the number counted. The published bounds are asymptotic, $(3/8-o(1))N\leq f(N)\leq(1/2+o(1))N$. The finite value $f(9)=6$ does not contradict the asymptotic upper coefficient because the unspecified $o(1)$ term may be substantial for small $N$.

The optimization must retain the truncation:

$$

f(N)=\max_{\substack{A\subseteq[1,N]\\ |A|=\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor}}
\left|(A+A)\cap[1,N]\right|.

$$

The elementary pair-count upper bound is $\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor(\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor+1)/2$, before subtracting coincident or oversized sums. Erdős–Freud's asymptotic half-interval upper bound is more delicate, and its unspecified error does not conflict with the exact small value $f(9)=6$.
