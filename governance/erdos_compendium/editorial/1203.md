## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1203](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1203) has maintained status **OPEN — DOMAIN NORMALIZATION REQUIRED** at this audit.

Let $\omega(m)$ count **distinct** prime divisors. Erdős asks whether $$F(n)=\max_{k}\omega(n+k)\frac{\log\log k}{\log k}\longrightarrow\infty\qquad(n\to\infty).$$ The domain over which the maximum is taken is omitted; **$k=1$ is undefined** because $\log1=0$, and for $k=2$, $\log\log2<0$. The meaningful study should restrict to integers $k\ge3$ (or larger) and source-verify whether max or supremum is intended. Because large $k$ may dominate, maximizing over a **bounded** finite window does not compute actual $F(n)$.

## A small example

The deterministic fixture computes the **partial** maxima for $3\le k\le100$ at $n=100,1000,10000$, obtaining respectively approximately **1.103145926829**, **1.465002343568**, and **1.811078443497**. Exact $\omega(n+k)$ values are calculated by trial factorization and only the logarithmic ratios are floating-point approximations. These numbers are **lower bounds on** $F(n)$ for each sampled $n$, not the full maximum, and cannot prove $F(n)\to\infty$.

## What is known

The maintained source https://www.erdosproblems.com/1203 lists **OPEN**, Erdős [Er80,p.107]. Erdős reports the lower estimate $F(n)\ge1-o(1)$, not unbounded growth; related questions #248,#679,#890 concern concentrations of prime factors in shifts. One can construct integers with many distinct prime divisors by congruence methods, but controlling the size of the positive **shift** relative to its maximal divisor count and proving a lower bound diverging uniformly in $n$ is the actual obligation.
