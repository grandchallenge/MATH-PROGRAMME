## The question in plain language

An *almost covering system* consists of finitely many congruences with **distinct moduli** that collectively miss only a small proportion of integers. [Erdős Problem #27](https://www.erdosproblems.com/27) asked whether there is one constant $C>1$ such that, for every desired uncovered proportion $\varepsilon>0$ and every lower cutoff $N$, one can choose all moduli between $N$ and $CN$ and still leave at most an $\varepsilon$ proportion uncovered.

The answer is **no**. Requiring one fixed multiplicative interval is too restrictive.

## A small example

For distinct moduli $m_1,\ldots,m_k$, independently choose one residue uniformly modulo each $m_i$. For any fixed integer $x$, the probability that none of the random congruences contains $x$ is

$$
\prod_{i=1}^k\left(1-\frac1{m_i}\right).
$$

Averaging over one period of the combined congruences shows there is some residue selection whose uncovered density is **at most** that product. This is a probability calculation about independently chosen residues, not an assumption that the moduli themselves are coprime.

For all integer moduli from $N$ through $M$, the product telescopes to

$$
\prod_{m=N}^{M}\left(1-\frac1m\right)=\frac{N-1}{M}.
$$

When $M$ is approximately $CN$, the product approaches $1/C$, not zero.

## What is known

Filaseta, Ford, Konyagin, Pomerance and Yu, *Sieving by large integers and covering systems of congruences*, *Journal of the American Mathematical Society* **20** (2007), 495–517, prove a strong uncovered-density lower bound for large distinct moduli lying in a fixed multiplicative interval. In particular, for any fixed $C>1$, one cannot make the uncovered proportion arbitrarily small merely by choosing residues and moduli within $[N,CN]$ as $N$ grows.

The hard theorem supplies the **lower** bound for all residue choices. The elementary averaging calculation above supplies a useful upper choice but does not, by itself, prove the negative answer.

[Source audit #1296](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1296#issuecomment-6121316701) records the exact quantifier order and links to the published paper and author manuscript. It derives the independent-residue averaging identity and distinguishes an almost covering from an exact covering. No complete replay of the deep sieve argument or GCL mathematical certificate is claimed.

## What remains

The historical question has been answered negatively. Further work would concern faithful formalization of the quantitative lower bound, its uniform dependence on the interval width, or strengthened covering-system results—not a fresh solution to the original conjecture.
