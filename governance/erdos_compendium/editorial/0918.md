## The question in plain language

Graphs may have infinitely many vertices, and their **chromatic number** is the smallest cardinal number of colors needed so that adjacent vertices receive different colors. Countably infinitely many colors are represented by $\aleph_0$; the first and second uncountable cardinal numbers are $\aleph_1$ and $\aleph_2$.

[Erdős Problem #918](https://www.erdosproblems.com/918) asks two existence questions. First, can a graph on exactly $\aleph_2$ vertices require $\aleph_2$ colors, even though **every subgraph on $\aleph_1$ vertices is countably colorable**? Second, can a graph on $\aleph_{\omega+1}$ vertices require $\aleph_1$ colors, while every subgraph on $\aleph_\omega$ vertices remains countably colorable?

The word **every** is essential: isolated examples of countably colorable large subgraphs are not sufficient. Both questions remain open in the maintained catalogue. These are problems about infinite cardinal arithmetic and chromatic **reflection**, not questions a finite graph search can decide.

There is also a genuine historical hypothesis issue. The original Erdős–Hajnal 1968 **Problem 2** explicitly assumes the **generalized continuum hypothesis** (GCH), while the maintained catalogue states the first question with no GCH qualifier. Removing GCH strengthens the source problem and must be treated as a distinct version rather than an invisible correction.

## A small example

A complete graph on three vertices needs three colors; any subgraph on two vertices needs at most two. More generally, chromatic number is **monotone under deleting edges or vertices**: a proper coloring of a graph remains proper on any subgraph.

This finite analogy explains the direction of the desired inequality but not the extraordinary jump in cardinality asked by Erdős and Hajnal. For an infinite graph, they seek very large overall chromatic number while every smaller-cardinality sample remains at most **countably** chromatic.

It matters whether we quantify over arbitrary subgraphs or **induced** subgraphs. For a vertex subset $W$, the induced graph $G[W]$ contains all edges inherited from $G$ between vertices of $W$. Any other graph $H$ obtained from $G$ on that same $W$ by removing some edges has

$$
\chi(H)\le\chi(G[W]).
$$

Thus, when the requirement is an **upper bound** such as $\chi\le\aleph_0$, testing every induced subgraph is equivalent to testing every subgraph on those vertex sets. The equivalence fails for **equality** $\chi=\aleph_0$: an edgeless spanning subgraph has chromatic number one, regardless of its number of vertices.

## What is known

Paul Erdős and András Hajnal, [*On chromatic number of infinite graphs*](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1968-04.pdf), *Theory of Graphs* (Tihany conference proceedings, 1968), pp.83–98, give a family of **partial** infinite-graph constructions. Printed p.86, Theorem 2, builds graphs on successor cardinalities using a partition-theoretic method, showing chromatic number greater than a lower cardinal while vertex sets below a specified threshold admit colorings with that many colors.

Printed p.86, **Corollary 1 explicitly invokes GCH** to produce, for each finite integer $k\ge1$, a graph on $\aleph_{\xi+k}$ vertices with chromatic number $\aleph_{\xi+1}$ and all subgraphs on at most $\aleph_{\xi+k-1}$ vertices of chromatic number at most $\aleph_\xi$. At $\xi=0$ this gives a family of countably chromatic smaller subgraphs and overall chromatic number $\aleph_1$. It does **not** solve either of the two requested limit-level jumps.

Printed p.87, **Problem 2**, directly asks, **under GCH**, whether a graph on $\aleph_2$ vertices can have chromatic number $\aleph_2$ while all its subgraphs on fewer than $\aleph_2$ vertices have chromatic number at most $\aleph_0$. The same 1968 paper distinguishes the conditional partial results from unresolved statements.

Erdős's later [*Problems and results in chromatic graph theory*](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1969-13.pdf), *Proof Techniques in Graph Theory* (1969), pp.27–35, printed p.28, lists both questions. Its printed wording uses chromatic number **equal to** $\aleph_0$ for every smaller subgraph, which is not equivalent to the intended **at most** $\aleph_0$. The [maintained catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/918), revised October 24, 2025, repairs that reading on the authority of the original 1968 question and comments identifying the trivial obstruction for arbitrary edgeless subgraphs.

The [protected Formal Conjectures snapshot](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/918.lean), Git blob **d3d9b34b11e0903ca735b8e95190de565ba883fe**, contains an additional problem. Part ii quantifies over **every ordinal** $\omega$, rather than fixing $\omega$ as the **first infinite ordinal**, which is what $\aleph_{\omega+1}$ and $\aleph_\omega$ mean in Erdős's question. It also omits the original GCH assumption. Both parts retain placeholders, so this is **a protected formal-statement source requiring semantic reconciliation**, not a Lean proof. The newer public upstream [918.lean](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/main/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/918.lean) has corrected the ordinal binding and explicitly includes GCH in its finite-step historical theorem, but it does not retroactively alter the pinned provider.

## GCL's current state

There are two elementary source-normalization lemmas that can be independently justified. First, the colorability of an induced subgraph bounds the colorability of **every** subgraph on the same vertex set, because edge deletion cannot increase chromatic number. Conversely, induced subgraphs themselves belong to the quantified family of all subgraphs. This proves an exact equivalence between those quantifiers when the predicate is $\chi\le\aleph_0$.

Second, if the ambient vertex set has cardinality at least $\lambda$ and **all** subsets of exactly $\lambda$ vertices induce graphs of chromatic number at most $\aleph_0$, then the same holds for vertex subsets of cardinality **at most** $\lambda$: use choice to extend any smaller vertex set to one of cardinality exactly $\lambda$, and restrict the proper coloring. These lemmas do not require GCH and do not construct an uncountably chromatic graph.

The executable fixture governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_918_919.py exhaustively checks **32,767** graph-and-vertex-subset instances over all labelled graphs on at most five vertices and **174,929** arbitrary edge-subgraph restrictions. Every such restriction satisfies the exact chromatic monotonicity inequality. The finite computation also distinguishes equality from upper bounds using edgeless spanning graphs.

The GCL research boundary is therefore **semantic reconciliation**, not an infinite existence proof. The protected provider blob, original 1968 page and contemporary catalogue are retained as different, dated sources with their non-equivalent hypotheses made explicit.

## What remains

The immediate bounded formalizable task is a **source-equivalence theorem** for chromatic-cardinal bounds: prove the equivalence between all induced and all arbitrary subgraphs on a fixed vertex cardinality when the requirement is $\chi\le\aleph_0$. Separately prove exact-cardinality-to-at-most-cardinality padding and the failure of the corresponding equality statement. Acceptance requires complete proofs or pinned, no-placeholder Lean theorems with literal axiom reporting, plus the 174,929 exact finite graph restrictions. A supposed proper coloring violated on an edge, or an unaddressed edgeless spanning subgraph under equality, invalidates the certificate.

A distinct mandatory source-lock task is to reconcile the **1968 GCH hypothesis** and the **fixed** ordinal $\omega$ in part ii against the admitted Formal Conjectures blob. Any proposed amendment should be a versioned, independently reviewed formal-statement change, not a silent replacement of the protected snapshot. Independently reconstruct the precise assumptions of Erdős–Hajnal Theorem 2 and Corollary 1 before using either as a mathematical prerequisite for a new infinite graph construction.

The genuinely open mathematical targets are the two stated **existence problems** under their chosen set-theoretic hypotheses. A finite search, a proof of chromatic monotonicity or a Lean statement containing an admitted placeholder does not decide either. Stop the bounded task at validated semantics and a source-locked, falsifiable successor obligation rather than claiming a new uncountable graph.
