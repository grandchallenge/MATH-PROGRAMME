## The question in plain language

Let $r_3(N)$ be the maximum size of a set of integers from $1$ through $N$ with no three distinct numbers in arithmetic progression. Erdős #140 asks for **every** fixed $C>0$ an upper bound

$$
r_3(N)=O_C\!\left(\frac{N}{(\log N)^C}\right).
$$

The constant may depend on $C$, but the exponent must be arbitrarily large and fixed before $N\to\infty$. Merely proving $r_3(N)=o(N)$ does not suffice.

## A small example

If $a,b,c$ are distinct and $a+c=2b$, they form a nontrivial three-term progression. For example $2,5,8$ is forbidden. A finite optimizer can encode one linear forbidden triple for each integer pair $a<c$ with even $a+c$, allowing at most two selected vertices from that triple.

## What is known

**PROVED.** Kelley and Meka's [*Strong Bounds for 3-Progressions*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05537) proves a density bound of the form $r_3(N)/N\le\exp(-c(\log N)^\beta)$ for absolute $c,\beta>0$. Since $(\log N)^\beta$ grows faster than $\log\log N$, this exponential factor is eventually smaller than $(\log N)^{-C}$ for **every fixed** $C>0$. A 2023 exposition by Bloom and Sisask simplifies portions of the proof. The corresponding conjectures for longer progressions must be checked separately; this theorem is specifically for $k=3$.

## What remains

Prove the elementary consequence from an assumed $\exp(-c(\log N)^\beta)$ bound to $(\log N)^{-C}$, with quantified constants and every fixed $C$. Acceptance: compare logarithms rigorously and keep $k=3$ fixed. A separate source-proof replay would need to audit Bohr-set and additive-energy estimates from the 2023 paper.
