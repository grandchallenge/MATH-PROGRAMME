## The question in plain language

List the primes as $p_1<p_2<\cdots$ and write their consecutive gaps as $d_n=p_{n+1}-p_n$. Erdős conjectured that each set of indices with

$$
d_{n+1}\ge d_n\qquad\text{and}\qquad d_{n+1}\le d_n
$$

has natural density $1/2$. He also conjectured there are **infinitely many equal adjacent gaps**, meaning $d_{n+1}=d_n$. An equality here is equivalent to three **consecutive primes** forming an arithmetic progression.

## A small example

The primes $3,5,7$ are consecutive, and their gaps are $2,2$. They witness one occurrence of equal adjacent gaps, but say nothing about **infinitely many** such occurrences. The primes $5,7,11$ have successive gaps $2,4$, so the first gap is no larger than the next.

A useful distinction concerns the density statements: if the equality indices had positive natural density, the two sets defined by the weak inequalities would overlap with positive density and **could not both have density $1/2$** while their union is all positive indices. The conjectured densities therefore imply zero density for equality indices, but zero density is compatible with infinitely many equal gaps.

## What is known

The claims remain **OPEN unconditionally**. Banks (2023) offered a heuristic based on a quantitative version of the prime-tuples conjecture. For fixed $c\ge0$ and $\varepsilon>0$ the predicted count for $p_n\le x$ with $d_{n+1}\ge c d_n$ is

$$
\frac{\pi(x)}{c+1}
+O\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{3/2+\varepsilon}}\right).
$$

Taking $c=1$ suggests the half-density assertion for the nondecreasing comparison. This is **conditional heuristic evidence**, not a theorem resolving prime-gap comparison.

Erdős also conjectured arbitrarily long strings of equal consecutive gaps, which is equivalent to arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions of **consecutive** primes; see #141. Green–Tao's theorem on arbitrarily long prime progressions does not establish consecutiveness.

Pinned and current [Erdős #218](https://www.erdosproblems.com/218) classify the questions **OPEN**. Original issue [#1487](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1487). The modern formal provider contains a statement file, but its translation of **density** and infinitude has not been independently proved equivalent to these natural-language claims.

The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1487#issuecomment-6180799079). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded task generates consecutive prime gaps through a specified cutoff, computes indices satisfying each comparison, explicitly identifies ties, and checks that the counts satisfy $A+B=N+T$, where $T$ counts ties. Acceptance: the $3,5,7$ tie is found, every comparison uses adjacent prime gaps, and empirical proportions are not called asymptotic densities.
