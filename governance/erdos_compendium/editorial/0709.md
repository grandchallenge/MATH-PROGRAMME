## The question in plain language

For n distinct positive integers a_i≥2, let M=max a_i. In EVERY block I of f(n)M consecutive integers, can we choose n DISTINCT representatives x_i∈I with a_i|x_i, each a_i matched to its own x_i? Erdős–Surányi ask for good upper and lower growth bounds on the smallest such f(n). The main sharp-order question is OPEN. It differs from #708, which only asks for product divisibility.

## A small example

For n=1, any a consecutive integers contain a multiple of a, so f(1)=1. More generally an interval of nM consecutive integers contains at least floor(nM/a_i)≥n multiples of each a_i because a_i≤M. Assign x_1,...,x_n greedily; at step i fewer than n previously selected values are excluded, so some unused multiple of a_i remains. Thus the elementary universal bound is f(n)≤n. This exact argument does not attain the much sharper known O(√n) bound.

## What is known

The maintained problem attributes (log n)^c≪f(n)≪n^(1/2) to Erdős–Surányi [ErSu59]. Its March 2026 revision attributes the improved lower f(n)≫log n/loglog n to van Doorn [vD26], through the offset-sensitive #711. Hall's marriage theorem gives a necessary and sufficient finite matching criterion: for each subcollection S of indices, the union of their allowable multiples in I must contain at least |S| integers. A bound only on total multiplicity is insufficient. Complete primary authors' papers [ErSu59] and [vD26] not independently inspected.
