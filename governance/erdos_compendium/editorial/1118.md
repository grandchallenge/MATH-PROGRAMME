## The question in plain language

Consider an entire function that is very large only on a set of **finite planar area**. How slowly can such a function grow overall? And if the region where $|f(z)|>c$ has finite area for one height $c$, must it also have finite area for some **slightly lower** height?

[Erdős Problem #1118](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1118) writes

$$
E(c)=\{z\in\mathbb C:|f(z)|>c\}
$$

and studies the relation between the area of $E(c)$ and the maximum modulus $M(r)=\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|$.

## A small example

For $f(z)=e^z$ and $c>0$,

$$
E(c)=\{z:\operatorname{Re}z>\log c\}.
$$

This is an open half-plane with **infinite area** for every $c>0$, so it does not satisfy the motivating finite-area hypothesis.

The sets $E(c)$ shrink as $c$ increases. Therefore

$$
T_f=\{c>0:|E(c)|<\infty\}
$$

is upward closed. That elementary observation does **not** imply that, when $c$ belongs to $T_f$, a slightly smaller threshold also does.

## What is known

Hayman proposed a sharp growth restriction on such entire functions involving an integral of the form

$$
\int^\infty\frac{r}{\log\log M(r)}\,dr<\infty.
$$

The maintained catalogue credits **Camera (1977)** and **Gol'dberg (1979)** with establishing the precise conjecture. Its small-radius cutoff and regularity requirements require verification in the original papers.

The second question has a **negative answer**. Gol'dberg proved that, for any $m>0$, entire functions can have finite-area threshold sets equal to either

$$
T_f=[m,\infty)\quad\text{or}\quad T_f=(m,\infty).
$$

Thus finite area above one threshold does not guarantee finite area at any smaller threshold.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source audit #2389](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2389#issuecomment-6169268596) verifies the half-plane example and the upward-closed nature of $T_f$.

The statements were recovered from the indexed [catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1118) and [history](https://www.erdosproblems.com/history/1118). The published growth proofs and examples realizing the threshold sets remain externally reported, not independently replayed.

## What remains

Read the original Camera and Gol'dberg arguments, establish the exact range of the growth integral, and verify the constructions realizing both threshold endpoint types.
