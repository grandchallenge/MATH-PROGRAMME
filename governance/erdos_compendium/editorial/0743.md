## The question in plain language

Given any collection of trees T_2,...,T_n with T_k having exactly k vertices, can the complete graph K_n have its edges partitioned into edge-disjoint embeddings of all the T_k? This is Gyárfás's tree packing conjecture, maintained FALSIFIABLE and OPEN. The edge count matches exactly, because Σ_{k=2}^n(k−1)=binom(n,2), but equality of counts alone never guarantees a packing.

## A small example

For n=4, take T_4 as the path 0–1–2–3 (3 edges), T_3 as the star with edges {0–2,0–3} (2 edges), and T_2 as the edge {1–3}. These are exactly the six edges of K_4, pairwise edge-disjoint. A particular feasible tuple does not certify every collection of trees at any n.

## What is known

Gyárfás–Lehel proved the all-stars/paths subcases, Fishburn checked n≤9, and Joos–Kim–Kühn–Osthus 2019 proved the bounded-maximum-degree variant. Allen et al. 2021 and Janzer–Montgomery 2024 advanced other restricted cases. An arXiv:2410.13840 preprint (Chalise–Clark–Gnang) CLAIMS a complete proof using a polynomial nonvanishing/composition lemma; the maintained site still marks the conjecture FALSIFIABLE and its forum labels the preprint claim, not an accepted proof. Without independent scrutiny of the composition lemma, do NOT mark the universal statement PROVED.
