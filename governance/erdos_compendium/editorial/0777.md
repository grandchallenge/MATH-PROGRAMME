## The question in plain language

Given a family of distinct subsets of ${1,\ldots,n}$, draw a graph joining two family members whenever one is contained in the other. Erdős Problem #777 asks how dense this *comparability graph* can be, especially for families with size near $2^{n/2}$.

The registered problem contains **three separate conjectural implications**, not a single yes/no assertion. According to the maintained catalogue, the first has an affirmative answer, the second a negative answer, and the third an affirmative answer. The status label SOLVED must not conceal these opposite outcomes.

## A small example

For $n=2$, consider the family

$$

\mathcal F=\{\varnothing,\{1\},\{2\},\{1,2\}\}.

$$

There are six unordered pairs of its four members. Every pair is comparable except $\{1\}$ and $\{2\}$, so the comparability graph has exactly **five edges**. No self-comparisons are counted.

A chain of $m$ distinct nested sets has all $\binom m2$ possible edges. But in an $n$ element Boolean lattice, a strict chain has at most $n+1$ members, so this elementary example cannot establish density for exponential-size families.

## What is known

Let $m=|\mathcal F|$ and let $c(\mathcal F)$ denote the number of strict comparable pairs. The three parts ask, roughly, whether (1) if $m\leq(2-\varepsilon)2^{n/2}$ then $c(\mathcal F)<2^n$ for large $n$, (2) if $c(\mathcal F)\geq cm^2$ then necessarily $m=O_c(2^{n/2})$, and (3) if $c(\mathcal F)>m^{2-\delta}$ then $m<(2+\varepsilon)^{n/2}$ for some $\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)>0$.

In [*Comparable pairs in families of sets*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.4196), Noga Alon, Shagnik Das, Roman Glebov and Benny Sudakov study the maximal possible comparable-pair count. Their paper appeared in *Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B* **115** (2015), 164–185. The authors' abstract establishes, among other things, that $c(n,m)=o(m^2)$ when $m=n^{\omega(1)}2^{n/2}$. The catalogue more specifically attributes an affirmative answer to (1) to their Theorem 1.4 and Corollary 1.5; the inference requires their precise hypotheses, not the abstract statement alone.

The maintained catalogue attributes the affirmative third part to Alon and Frankl (1985), and reports their construction disproving the second: families can have $m\gg n2^{n/2}$ yet a positive proportion of all $\binom m2$ pairs comparable. Thus **positive density of comparabilities does not force the claimed scale**.

The threshold constants $2-\varepsilon$ and $2+\varepsilon$ occur in different implications. Confusing the base $2$ with a multiplicative constant would change their substance.
