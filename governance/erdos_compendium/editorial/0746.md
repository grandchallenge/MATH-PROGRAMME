## The question in plain language

For fixed ε>0, consider the uniformly random graph on n vertices with at least (1/2+ε)n log n edges. Erdős–Rényi asked whether it has a Hamiltonian cycle with probability tending to one. It does: the maintained catalogue says PROVED. The probability model is G(n,m), edges chosen uniformly with a specified total, not an arbitrary deterministic graph with that many edges.

## A small example

The cycle C_5 is a five-vertex graph with exactly five edges and a Hamiltonian cycle. K_5 also has a Hamiltonian cycle, but examples do not establish a high-probability theorem for random graphs; graphs with a large number of edges can be disconnected when edge placement is adversarial.

## What is known

Pósa 1976 gave an upper with sufficiently large C n log n edges. Korshunov 1977 established a near-threshold upper m≥(n/2)log n+(n/2)loglog n+ω(n)n. Komlós–Szemerédi 1983 proved a sharper window law: if m=(n/2)log n+(n/2)loglog n+c n, the Hamiltonicity probability tends to exp(−exp(−2c)). The maintained source's 2025 correction credits Korshunov, not Komlós–Szemerédi, for the ω(n) margin theorem. These are source-proven classical results, not a new GCL derivation. The integer rounding of m and finite-n disconnectedness must not be conflated with asymptotic probability.
