## The question in plain language

Let $k\ge3$ be fixed. Among all sets $A$ of positive integers containing **no $k$ term arithmetic progression**, consider the largest possible sum of their reciprocals:

$$
f(k)=\sup_{\substack{A\subseteq\mathbb N_{>0}\\A\text{ contains no }k\text{-term AP}}}\ \sum_{a\in A}\frac1a.
$$

Erdős asked for the growth of $f(k)$ as $k$ grows, especially whether

$$
\frac{f(k)}{\log W(k)}\longrightarrow\infty,
$$

where $W(k)$ is the ordinary two-colour van der Waerden number. A finite large initial sum is not enough: the supremum ranges over **all**, possibly infinite, progression-free sets.

## A small example

The finite set $A=\{1,2,4\}$ has no three-term arithmetic progression: neither $1,2,4$ nor any of its reorderings has a common nonzero step. Its reciprocal sum equals $1+1/2+1/4=7/4$. Adding $3$ would create the progression $1,2,3$ and invalidate the set for $k=3$.

A set that avoids three-term progressions also avoids every longer arithmetic progression, so $f(k)$ is nondecreasing in $k$. This elementary relation does **not** determine its order of growth.

## What is known

The conjectured divergence is **OPEN**. Berlekamp (1968) established $f(k)\ge(\log2)k/2$. Gerver (1977) improved this to

$$
f(k)\ge(1-o(1))k\log k.
$$

The maintained [Erdős #169](https://www.erdosproblems.com/169), updated 4 April 2026, records the elementary comparison $f(k)/\log W(k)\ge1/2$, but even a uniform improvement of the constant beyond $1/2$ remains open.

For individual parameters, Wróblewski obtained $f(3)\ge3.00849$ and Walker (2025) obtained $f(4)\ge4.43975$. Walker also shows that progression-free *Kempner sets*, specified by permitted digits in a fixed base, approximate the supremum arbitrarily closely. This is a structural reduction, not a proof of the conjectured van der Waerden-normalized limit.

## What remains

A bounded task enumerates subsets of $[1,18]$ without nonconstant three- or four-term arithmetic progressions, computes exact reciprocal sums, and verifies monotonicity in $k$. Acceptance: the fixture $\{1,2,4\}$ passes with reciprocal sum $7/4$, no duplicate or zero-step progressions are counted, and finite partial optima are not confused with $f(k)$.
