## The question in plain language

Imagine coloring every connection between $n$ people red or blue. How large must $n$ be to guarantee a group of $k$ people all of whose mutual connections have one color? The smallest such $n$ is the **diagonal Ramsey number** $R(k,k)$, written $R(k)$ in Erdős #77.

The question asks whether the exponential growth rate stabilizes:

$$
\lim_{k\to\infty}R(k)^{1/k}.
$$

Taking the $k$th root removes the dominant exponential scale. Even the *existence* of this limit remains open. An upper bound and a lower bound do not imply convergence.

## A small example

The smallest nontrivial case is $R(3)=6$. Given a red-blue coloring of the $15$ edges of $K_6$, choose a vertex: at least three of its five incident edges share a color. If an edge joining two of those three neighbors has that same color, it closes a monochromatic triangle. Otherwise all three edges among the neighbors have the opposite color, also giving a monochromatic triangle.

Five vertices do not suffice: color the edges of a $5$-cycle red and its five complementary edges blue. Both color classes form $5$-cycles, so neither contains a triangle. This elementary calculation concerns $k=3$, not the limiting root.

## What is known

The classical probabilistic lower bound yields

$$
\sqrt2\le\liminf_{k\to\infty}R(k)^{1/k}.
$$

An elementary recurrence formerly gave the upper bound $4$ for the limsup. Campos, Griffiths, Morris and Sahasrabudhe broke this decades-old barrier. Gupta, Ndiaye, Norin and Wei sharpened the exponential bound; their 2024 primary preprint explicitly proves

$$
R(k,k)\le 3.8^{\,k+o(k)}.
$$

The maintained problem page records a refined numerical constant of approximately $3.7992$ from this optimization. To avoid overstating what was checked directly in the primary abstract, the conservative value $3.8$ is used for the rigorous interface here. Therefore

$$
\sqrt2\le\liminf R(k)^{1/k}
\le\limsup R(k)^{1/k}\le3.8.
$$

These inequalities leave both existence and value of the limit unresolved.

## What remains

A bounded computational successor is to enumerate all red-blue colorings of $K_5$ and $K_6$, independently classify those containing monochromatic triangles, and certify $R(3)=6$ with exact combinatorial witnesses. A separate analytic exercise proves that if $R(k)\le C^{k+o(k)}$, then $\limsup R(k)^{1/k}\le C$; it must *not* claim existence of the root limit. Source auditing should pin the exact optimization version before quoting the refined decimal constant.
