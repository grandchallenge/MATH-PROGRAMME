## The question in plain language

Let $A$ be the set of positive powerful integers, meaning that every prime factor has exponent at least two. The **additive convolution**

$$
r_A(n)=(1_A*1_A)(n)
=\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}1_A(a)\,1_A(n-a)
$$

counts **ordered** pairs $(a,b)$ of positive powerful integers satisfying $a+b=n$. Erdős asked whether

$$
r_A(n)=n^{o(1)}.
$$

Equivalently, for every $\varepsilon>0$, is there a constant $C_\varepsilon$ such that $r_A(n)\le C_\varepsilon n^\varepsilon$ for all positive $n$? The question is open. This is additive convolution, not Dirichlet convolution. [Erdős Problem #943](https://www.erdosproblems.com/943).

## A small example

For $n=13$, $4=2^2$ and $9=3^2$ are powerful. Hence the ordered pairs $(4,9)$ and $(9,4)$ both contribute, so $r_A(13)\ge2$. If one instead counts unordered representations, the answer for this witness is one. These conventions differ by a factor of two away from diagonal representations, but the distinction matters in exact computational evidence.

## Why a simple sparsity argument is insufficient

A powerful integer has the unique representation $x^2y^3$, with $y$ squarefree. In particular the counting function $A(X)$ grows on the order of $\sqrt X$. The crude upper bound $r_A(n)\le A(n)=O(\sqrt n)$ is far larger than the desired $n^{o(1)}$. Replacing $A$ by the squares yields a **lower** bound on representations, and therefore cannot directly prove the required upper bound.

Writing powerful summands as $a=x^2u^3$ and $b=y^2v^3$ transforms the relation into

$$
u^3x^2+v^3y^2=n
$$

with $u,v$ squarefree. For fixed $u,v$ this resembles a quadratic-form representation problem, but the coefficients vary over many possible pairs. Pointwise control must remain uniform as these coefficients vary.

## What is known

The maintained problem catalogue labels #943 open and records the original reference [Er76d]. A March 2026 discussion proposes an unconditional bound $r_A(n)\ll_\varepsilon n^{2/5+\varepsilon}$, together with geometric and square-sieve approaches. That exponent is **not** $o(1)$. More importantly, the posted write-up is unrefereed commentary and must not be promoted to an independently verified theorem without proof audit. The discussion also describes conditional Campana–Manin geometry and fiberwise thin-set estimates; such a package would require additional unproved uniformity hypotheses. [Problem discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/943).

The distribution of *integers representable at least once* as sums of two powerful numbers is a different aggregate question (related to [Problem #1081](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1081)). An average count or size estimate for that representable set does not imply a uniform bound for every individual $r_A(n)$.

## GCL's current state

The formula for ordered representations and the $(4,9)$ example follow directly from definitions. The key uniform bound $n^{o(1)}$ remains open. Neither the discussion's claimed $2/5+\varepsilon$ estimate nor its conditional geometric framework is accepted here as a GCL-certified proof.
