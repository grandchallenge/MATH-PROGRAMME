## The question in plain language

How can a large collection of points create **very few different distances overall**, while still having a surprising amount of distance variety inside every small group of four?

[Erdős Problem #659](https://www.erdosproblems.com/659) asks for arbitrarily large $n$-point sets in the Euclidean plane such that **every four-point subset determines at least three different positive distances**, but the total number of distinct distances is at most a constant multiple of

$$
\frac{n}{\sqrt{\log n}}.
$$

The answer is **yes**, using a carefully stretched version of an integer lattice. Earlier work by Moree and Osburn established the few-distances behavior of such lattices; Benjamin Grayzel's 2026 paper completes the necessary four-point local obstruction using a classification of exceptional distance patterns.

## A small example

Take the four points

$$
P_2=\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,\sqrt2),(1,\sqrt2)\}.
$$

They form a rectangle of side lengths $1$ and $\sqrt2$. Its squared pairwise distances are precisely

$$
1,\qquad 2,\qquad 3.
$$

There are already **three** different distances, so this four-point set passes the local requirement. More generally, place points on the stretched lattice

$$
L=\{(i,\sqrt2\,j):i,j\in\mathbb Z\},
$$

and consider its finite square-indexed box

$$
P_m=\{(i,\sqrt2\,j):0\leq i,j<m\}.
$$

It contains $m^2$ points. Distances between lattice points are encoded by the **integer quadratic form**

$$
\lVert(i,\sqrt2 j)-(k,\sqrt2\ell)\rVert^2
=(i-k)^2+2(j-\ell)^2.
$$

Using integer squared distances makes equalities exact: there is no floating-point uncertainty about whether two distances coincide.

An independent complete enumeration of the four-subsets of these boxes for $m=2,3,4,5,6$ checked respectively 1, 126, 1,820, 12,650 and 58,905 subsets. Every examined subset determined at least **three** distinct squared distances; none violated the local condition. The global numbers of distinct distances were respectively 3, 8, 14, 22 and 31. This finite experiment supports the construction but cannot establish the infinite theorem.

## What is known

**Why the global number is small.** Each positive squared distance in $P_m$ has the form

$$
q=u^2+2v^2,\qquad
|u|,|v|\leq m-1.
$$

Consequently $1\leq q\leq3(m-1)^2$. The relevant theorem about how many integers are represented by the positive-definite binary quadratic form $u^2+2v^2$ is due to **Bernays**. In the required case, it supplies the estimate that the number of represented integers up to a large cutoff $T$ is

$$
O\left(\frac{T}{\sqrt{\log T}}\right).
$$

Taking $T$ proportional to $m^2$ proves the **published, Bernays-dependent** global estimate

$$
D(P_m)=O\left(\frac{m^2}{\sqrt{\log m}}\right)
=O\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{\log n}}\right),
\qquad n=m^2.
$$

To treat every sufficiently large $n$, choose $m=\lceil\sqrt n\rceil$ and retain any $n$ of the $m^2$ points. Taking a subset cannot increase the global number of distances or introduce a forbidden four-point configuration. Since $m^2=O(n)$, the same asymptotic upper bound survives.

**Why every four-point subset has enough distances.** A crucial fact is the classification of planar sets of four distinct points that determine only two distances, described by Antonella Perucca and used by Grayzel. Of the six similarity classes, every one either contains an equilateral triangle, is a square, or is similar to the four-vertex isosceles trapezoid of a regular pentagon.

The stretched lattice excludes all three possibilities:

1. **No square.** A quarter-turn of the lattice vector $(a,b\sqrt2)$ would be $(-b\sqrt2,a)$. To remain in the lattice, its first coordinate would have to be an integer and its second coordinate an integer multiple of $\sqrt2$. For integers $a,b$, this forces $a=b=0$, excluding a nondegenerate square.
2. **No equilateral triangle.** Every nonzero squared lattice distance is an integer $q$, while the squared area of an equilateral triangle of side length squared $q$ is $3q^2/16$. But any triangle formed by lattice vectors has squared area $d^2/2$ for an integer determinant $d$. Equality would require $8d^2=3q^2$, impossible for positive $q$ by comparing the parity of powers of the prime $3$.
3. **No regular-pentagon trapezoid.** Its ratio of squared long to short distances is the irrational number $(3+\sqrt5)/2$. Every ratio of two nonzero squared distances in our lattice is rational, as each is a positive integer.

The classification therefore rules out all two-distance four-point configurations. The elementary three exclusions have been reconstructed here; the *completeness of Perucca's classification* is still an external published theorem, not a newly proved classification by GCL.

The complete argument is attributed to Benjamin Grayzel, [*Solution to a Problem of Erdős Concerning Distances and Points*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09102v2), January 2026, together with the earlier lattice-distance analysis of Pieter Moree and Robert Osburn, [*Two-dimensional lattices with few distances*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0604163), 2006. It uses both analytic number theory and four-point Euclidean geometry. Neither ingredient can simply be omitted.

## GCL's current state

The maintained Erdős catalogue reports **PROVED (LEAN)**. The [GCL-pinned Formal Conjectures file](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/659.lean) was inspected directly; it includes the mathematical statement and a link to an [external Lean formalization](https://github.com/plby/lean-proofs/blob/226d5fad7143dcebea2bbb5ec87f18a3a1dcea69/src/v4.24.0/ErdosProblems/Erdos659.lean). Crucially, the pinned wrapper itself contains a proof hole, and its commentary says that the external formal proof **assumes Bernays' theorem as an axiom** to obtain the global distance bound.

This is an important distinction: an external Lean verification of a theorem **conditional on Bernays as a named axiom** is not an internally replayed foundational proof of Bernays' analytic result. The published Bernays theorem is a legitimate mathematical dependency, but its exact formal authority must be described truthfully. GCL has not independently compiled the external proof, audited its transitive axioms or certified it.

The [authenticated GCL source return](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1930#issuecomment-6144372149) records the construction, elementary lattice checks, bounded finite enumeration and exact certification limitations. The draft is **UNACCEPTED**, not a protected publication or editorially admitted document.
