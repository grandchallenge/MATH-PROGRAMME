## The question in plain language

A collection of pairwise coprime integers acts as a sieve: strike out all integers divisible by at least one member. Under a budget on the sum of reciprocal divisors, how few survivors are possible? Exact minimizers and asymptotic minimum counts are different questions.

Fix C>0 and N large. Take pairwise coprime A subset {2,...,N} with sum_{a in A}1/a <= C; minimize uncovered integers m<=N not divisible by any a. The historical proposal is an interval of consecutive large primes, whereas an ASYMPTOTIC minimum and the EXACT extremal set are different questions.

## A small example

For N=10, the pairwise coprime sieving set {2,3} has reciprocal mass 5/6 and leaves exactly 1,5,7 uncovered. Including divisor 1 would destroy the meaningful minimization.

Finite model: N=10, A={2,3} gives reciprocal sum 5/6 and uncovered integers 1,5,7 (3 total), since multiples of 2 or 3 are removed; pairwise gcd(2,3)=1. A={2,4} is NOT admissible due to gcd 2. The 1 divisor is excluded; otherwise no uncovered numbers. The asymptotic theorem is not an exact optimizer for every finite N,C.

## What is known

Maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/783, SOLVED: attributes the asymptotic minimum (rho(exp C)+o(1))N to Terence Tao and an exact prime-tail minimizer for C<=log 2 to Chojecki. Hunter's finite perturbations refute unrestricted literal exact-optimality. Here rho denotes the Dickman–de Bruijn function, not Euler's totient. Tao's original proof and Chojecki's full primary argument were not independently fetched; treat attribution as secondary catalogue evidence.
