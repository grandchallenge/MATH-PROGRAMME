## The question in plain language

List the elements of a set $A$ in increasing order,

$$
a_1<a_2<\cdots<a_t,
$$

and suppose that their Euler totient values also strictly increase,

$$
\phi(a_1)<\phi(a_2)<\cdots<\phi(a_t).
$$

[Erdős Problem #49](https://www.erdosproblems.com/49) asks whether this forces $A\subseteq[1,N]$ to contain asymptotically no more elements than the primes up to $N$. The answer is **yes**, established by Terence Tao.

## A small example

If $p$ is prime, then $\phi(p)=p-1$. Thus as the primes increase, their totient values increase. For $N\ge5$, the set $\{2,3,5\}$ has totient values $\{1,2,4\}$, an immediate example. The primes provide a lower construction of size $\pi(N)$.

## What is known

Tao, *Monotone Nondecreasing Sequences of the Euler Totient Function*, *La Matematica* **3** (2024), 793–820, proved a stronger result: even if the totient values are merely **nondecreasing**, the largest possible sequence has cardinality

$$
M(N)=\left(1+O\left(\frac{(\log\log N)^5}{\log N}\right)\right)\pi(N)
$$

for sufficiently large $N$. A strictly increasing totient sequence is automatically nondecreasing. Therefore Tao's theorem applies to Erdős's narrower problem, and the prime example matches its main term.

The related nondecreasing-variant question and analogous sums-of-divisors results should retain their own theorem boundaries.
