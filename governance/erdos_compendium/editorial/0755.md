## The question in plain language

For n points in Euclidean R^6, what is the maximum number of equilateral triangles of unit side length? Erdős conjectured T₆ᵘ(n)≤(1/27+o(1))n³. The maintained problem is PROVED: Clemen, Dumitrescu and Liu [CDL25b] established the stronger asymptotic T₆(n)=(1/27+o(1))n³ even counting triangles of ALL side lengths, not just unit triangles.

## A small example

Split R^6 into three mutually orthogonal 2-dimensional planes through the origin. Put a points on a circle of radius 1/√2 in the first plane, b on the same-radius circle in the second, and c in the third, where a+b+c=n. Every point from different circles is at squared distance 1/2+1/2=1; hence every triple with one from each circle is a unit equilateral triangle. Its count is abc, which equals n³/27 when a=b=c=n/3, up to rounding O(n²). This is a constructive lower bound, not the 2025 upper proof.

## What is known

Erdős–Purdy [ErPu75] first gave the three mutually orthogonal circle construction; later historical sources attributed it to Lenz, so provenance must be preserved. Clemen–Dumitrescu–Liu (2025) proved a stronger any-size extremal asymptotic and reportedly exact formulas for even dimensions d≥6 for large n. The original 2025 theorem's exact qualifiers and triangle counting convention (unordered triples) are not independently reread in full.
