## The question in plain language

A triangle-free graph has no three vertices that are pairwise adjacent. Erdős and Gyárfás asked whether every sufficiently large triangle-free graph $G$ on $n$ vertices with maximum degree less than

$$
n^{1/2-\varepsilon}
$$

can be enlarged **by adding edges only** to a triangle-free graph on the same vertices with **diameter two**, using at most $\delta n^2$ new edges, for any fixed positive $\varepsilon,\delta$.

The graph must remain triangle-free. No original edges may be deleted. The degree exponent $1/2-\varepsilon$ is crucial: allowing a large constant times $\sqrt n$ is insufficient.

## A small example

A star with at least three vertices is triangle-free and has diameter two already; no new edges are needed. An edgeless graph has infinite diameter and must acquire edges. A naive method adding every missing edge creates triangles and is invalid.

An edge between two nonadjacent vertices may be added without creating a triangle only when the endpoints have **no common neighbour** in the current graph. A finite checker must enforce this invariant.

## What is known

**PROVED.** Noga Alon, [*Triangle-free graphs of diameter 2*](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nalon/PDFS/remark1901.pdf), establishes a stronger result: for every $0<\varepsilon<1/6$ and sufficiently large $n$, any triangle-free graph with maximum degree at most $n^{1/2-\varepsilon}$ can be extended, while remaining triangle-free, to diameter two by adding fewer than

$$
3n^{2-\varepsilon}
$$

edges. Since $3n^{-\varepsilon}\to0$, this is below $\delta n^2$ for all sufficiently large $n$ and any fixed $\delta>0$.

For an original parameter $\varepsilon\ge1/6$, choose a smaller positive exponent below $1/6$; the original degree assumption then implies the weaker one, and the extra-edge count remains $o(n^2)$. Erdős and Gyárfás previously proved a case with maximum degree of order $\log n/\log\log n$. The author's exact primary result was checked at theorem-summary level, not fully reproved.

## What remains

A bounded proof task is to derive the conclusion for fixed positive $\delta$ from Alon's $3n^{2-\varepsilon}$ estimate with fully explicit quantifiers and the large-exponent reduction. A second finite fixture should verify that an edge-addition candidate preserves original edges, has no triangle, and has diameter exactly two. Acceptance: the asymptotic arithmetic is checked independently and finite checks do not masquerade as a new construction theorem.
