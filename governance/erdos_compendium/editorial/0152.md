## The question in plain language

A finite set $A$ of natural numbers is a *Sidon set* if each sum $a+b$ with $a,b\in A$ and $a\le b$ identifies the unordered pair uniquely. Form the sumset $S=A+A$ and call $s\in S$ **isolated** when neither $s-1$ nor $s+1$ belongs to $S$.

Erdős, Sárközy and Sós asked whether, for **every** fixed positive integer $M$, every sufficiently large finite Sidon set has at least $M$ isolated elements in $A+A$. The stronger conjecture asks whether the number of isolated sums is bounded below by a constant times $|A|^2$.

## A small example

The set $A=\{1,2,4\}$ is Sidon: its unordered sums (allowing equal terms) are exactly $2,3,4,5,6,8$, all distinct. Among these, $8$ is isolated because neither $7$ nor $9$ is a sum; the other five sums have a neighbouring element of the sumset. Thus this example has one isolated sum.

An isolated **element** is counted once, not once for each representation as $a+b$. For a Sidon set, sums with $a\ne b$ have a unique unordered representation, but $a+a$ is still part of $A+A$. Omitting diagonal sums would produce the wrong isolation test.

## What is known

**PROVED, including a quadratic lower bound.** A Google DeepMind prover agent produced an external formal proof of the original assertion and a stronger numerical inequality. As recorded in the [maintained Erdős #152 discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/152) and [upstream Formal Conjectures entry](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/main/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/152.lean), if $I(A+A)$ is the number of isolated sums and $n=|A|$, then

$$
16I(A+A)+100n+16\ge n^2,
$$

so for large $n$, $I(A+A)\gg n^2$. This explicitly implies the original question for arbitrarily large fixed $M$ by taking $n$ sufficiently large.

The source records two exact third-party formal proof revisions: [the original positive-growth proof](https://github.com/mo271/formal-conjectures/blob/29c60aa79729701905cf9e92517af23f588971f2/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/152.lean#L485) and [the stronger quadratic variant](https://github.com/mo271/formal-conjectures/blob/ff58c933d53bb807bf85d98a47402703f9f14ed3/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/152.lean#L496). GCL has **not** independently executed those exact Lean toolchains or audited their complete axiom dependencies. A related extension to truncations of infinite Sidon sets is a separate assertion requiring its own hypotheses.
