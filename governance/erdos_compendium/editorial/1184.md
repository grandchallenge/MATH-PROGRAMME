## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1184](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1184) is **OPEN** in the maintained catalogue.

For integers $n,k$ let $P(m)$ be the largest prime factor and $f(n,k)=\#\{1\le i\le k:P(n+i)>k\}$. With fixed $\alpha>1$ and $n=k^{\alpha+o(1)}$, the conjectured short-interval smooth-number asymptotic is
$$f(n,k)=(1-\rho(\alpha)+o(1))k,$$
where $\rho$ is the Dickman function. This is **not** the usual density of smooth numbers over the entire range $[1,n]$; the interval has only $k$ points near $n$.

## A small example

Exact prime factorization on short intervals gives large-prime-factor counts: $(n,k)=(100,10)$ yields 8; $(1000,20)$ yields 16; $(10000,40)$ yields 36. The corresponding smooth counts are 2, 4 and 4. These are bounded and do not certify any limiting formula for $\rho(\alpha)$.

## What is known

https://www.erdosproblems.com/1184 remains **OPEN**, citing Erdős [Er76e,p.272]. His lower bound when $n>k^\alpha-k$ is $f(n,k)>(1-1/\alpha+c_\alpha)k$ for $k$ large, and a separate upper bound $(\alpha-1+o(1))k$ for $1<\alpha<2$ and $n\le k^\alpha-k$. Ramachandra–Shorey–Tijdeman [RST75b] establish $f(n,k)\ge k-\pi(k)$ only when $n>\exp(c(\log k)^2)$. None supplies the claimed Dickman asymptotic.
