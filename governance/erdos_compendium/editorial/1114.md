## The question in plain language

Suppose a real polynomial has distinct real roots equally spaced along the number line. Its derivative also has real roots, lying between consecutive roots of the original polynomial.

[Erdős Problem #1114](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1114) concerns the **spacing between derivative roots**. Starting near the center and moving out toward either end, must the gaps grow monotonically?

The answer was **proved by Bálint (1960)**.

A source-indexing issue deserves attention: the catalogue says the polynomial has degree $n$ but lists roots $a_0,\ldots,a_n$, which are $n+1$ distinct roots. The coherent mathematical statement must instead pair degree $m$ with **exactly $m$ distinct roots**, or degree $n+1$ with the listed $n+1$ roots.

## A small example

Take the five equally spaced roots $-2,-1,0,1,2$:

$$
f(x)=x(x^2-1)(x^2-4)
=x^5-5x^3+4x.
$$

Its derivative is

$$
f'(x)=5x^4-15x^2+4.
$$

The four derivative roots are $-s_2,-s_1,s_1,s_2$, where

$$
s_1^2=\frac{15-\sqrt{145}}{10},
\qquad
s_2^2=\frac{15+\sqrt{145}}{10}.
$$

The central gap is $2s_1$. Each outer gap is $s_2-s_1$, which is **slightly larger**. The exact comparison is

$$
s_2-s_1>2s_1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sqrt{145}>12,
$$

true because $145>144$.

Thus this concrete polynomial exhibits strictly increasing consecutive derivative-root gaps away from the center.

## What is known

The catalogue credits Bálint (1960) with the general theorem and notes generalizations by Lorch (1976). It also flags uncertain attribution to Erdős, possibly through personal communication.

The result is an example of the rigid geometry of polynomial roots and their critical points: equal spacing of original roots forces a specific pattern of interlacing and gap inequalities for the derivative.
