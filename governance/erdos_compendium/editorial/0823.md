## The question in plain language

For EVERY real alpha>=1 there must exist infinite sequences of positive integers n_k,m_k with n_k/m_k -> alpha and sigma(n_k)=sigma(m_k) for all k. One finite coincidence of divisor sums does not prove arbitrary prescribed limiting ratios; alpha is real, not assumed rational.

## A small example

For n=6,m=11, sigma(6)=12=sigma(11). The ratio 11/6 is a single value and is not evidence for realizing every alpha. Also sigma(14)=24=sigma(15), with ratio 15/14.

The example can be checked exactly. A finite witness does not replace the universal, asymptotic, or infinitely-often quantifiers in the original question.

## What is known

Maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/823 read 2026-10-14, PROVED attributed to Paul Pollack [Po15b], *Remarks on fibers of the sum-of-divisors function* (2015), 305–320. Erdős [Er74b] reported analogous result for phi as easy. Full Pollack primary proof and quantitative choice of sequences not independently checked.

The quantification is stronger than displaying one equal-sum pair: **for every** real $\alpha\geq1$, one must find pairs with the exact divisor-sum coincidence and

$$

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{n_k}{m_k}=\alpha.

$$

The finite equalities $\sigma(6)=\sigma(11)=12$ and $\sigma(14)=\sigma(15)=24$ are useful regression cases, not proof of the arbitrary-real limiting ratio theorem.
