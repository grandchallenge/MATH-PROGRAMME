## The question in plain language

Let $\phi(n)$ be Euler's totient function, the number of integers from $1$ through $n$ that share no factor with $n$ other than $1$. Define $g(n)=n+\phi(n)$ and repeatedly apply it: $g_1(n)=g(n)$ and $g_k(n)=g(g_{k-1}(n))$. Erdős and Graham ask for which starting values $n$ and positive shifts $r$ the orbit eventually obeys

$$
g_{k+r}(n)=2g_k(n)
$$

for every sufficiently large $k$. “Eventually” matters: early terms may not fit the pattern, while later terms may repeat a fixed multiplicative rhythm. The question asks for all such $n$ and $r$, not merely for examples.

The maintained problem page gives the original reference as Erdős and Graham, *Old and New Problems and Results in Combinatorial Number Theory* (1980), p. 81. The exact wording and notation are also reproduced in Steinerberger's primary 2025 paper, §1. This article follows the live statement rather than silently replacing “for which $n$ and $r$” by the narrower question of classifying points already on a cycle.

## A small example

The classical example $n=10$ can be checked with exact integer arithmetic. Since $\phi(10)=4$, $g(10)=14$; then $\phi(14)=6$, $g(14)=20$; next $\phi(20)=8$, $g(20)=28$; and $\phi(28)=12$, $g(28)=40$. Thus the beginning of the orbit is

$$
10\longmapsto14\longmapsto20\longmapsto28\longmapsto40.
$$

The three displayed comparisons are $20=2\cdot10$, $28=2\cdot14$, and $40=2\cdot20$. A trial-division totient calculation reproduced this orbit and returned the exact checks `(20,20)`, `(28,28)`, and `(40,40)` for the two-step value and its doubled predecessor. This verifies a finite instance of the claimed pattern; it does not classify all starts, prove that the equality persists for every later index, or say anything about shifts $r\ne2$.

The persistence mechanism is elementary once an even term satisfies the two-step equation. For even $m$, $\phi(2m)=2\phi(m)$, so $g(2m)=2g(m)$. Steinerberger uses this to show that the $r=2$ question reduces to the arithmetic equation

$$
\phi(m)+\phi(m+\phi(m))=m.
$$

This reduction is a useful normalization, not a solution of the original problem for all values of $r$.

## What is known

Steinerberger's arXiv:2504.08023v1 (submitted 10 April 2025) proves the $r=2$ reduction and gives six explicit infinite families for the auxiliary equation: $m=2^\ell u$ with $u$ in $\{1,3,5,7,35,47\}$, subject to the lower exponent needed for the cycle. The paper's Theorem on p. 1, the equivalence argument in §§2.1–2.2, and the 2-adic analysis in §2 are the relevant primary locations. It further reduces any additional branch to a prime $p=8m+7\ge10^{10}$ together with $\phi(6m+5)=4m+4$, equivalently to the related equation $\phi(t)=\frac23(t+1)$ with an additional primality condition. The paper presents completeness of the six families as conditional or possible, not as a finished theorem.

Hercher's arXiv:2504.19915v1 (submitted 28 April 2025) studies that related totient equation. Its abstract and Theorem 1 state that every solution is square-free; Corollary 2 gives a prime-factor nondivisibility condition; later results bound any additional solution below neither $10^{14}$ nor six prime factors. These are results about the auxiliary equation, not a complete answer to Erdős Problem 411. The live page's discussion also contains a typographical report of $1195$ where the factored value is $1295$; the paper's displayed factorization and later discussion use $1295$. That discrepancy is retained as source hygiene rather than repaired into a new theorem.

The live page records examples with other multipliers, including $g_{k+4}(738)=3g_k(738)$ and the reported $3114$ example with multiplier $729$. They illustrate richer dynamics but do not answer the requested multiplier $2$ classification.

## What remains

The main mathematical gap is global: classify all transient starts and all shifts $r$, while deciding whether the exceptional prime/totient branch contains anything beyond the displayed families. Even for $r=2$, a cycle classification is not automatically a basin classification, because a starting value may enter a known cycle later. The live discussion's proposed square-free recurrence is explicitly challenged in a later comment: a forward extension rule does not prove that every solution can be reduced by deleting its largest prime factor.

A genuinely bounded next task is to capture a source-normalized theorem interface. Its input should be a Lean-free statement record defining $\phi$, $g_0(n)=n$, and $g_{j+1}(n)=g(g_j(n))$, with the live quantifiers and the auxiliary $r=2$ equation recorded separately. Its target should be the exact lemma: if $m\ge3$ satisfies $\phi(m)+\phi(m+\phi(m))=m$, then $g_2(m)=2m$ and $g_{j+2}(m)=2g_j(m)$ for every $j\ge0$. Acceptance is a checked proof or a line-by-line mathematical proof of that lemma plus exact checks for the six listed base families; falsification is a counterexample satisfying the hypothesis but not the conclusion, or a detected indexing mismatch. Stop after that interface and its finite checks; do not attempt a full classification or a large unbounded search.
