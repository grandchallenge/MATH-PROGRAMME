## The question in plain language

A graph's edges can be partitioned into **edge-disjoint simple cycles and single edges**: every original edge must appear in exactly one piece. Erdős and Gallai conjectured that there is an absolute constant $C$ such that **every** graph with $n$ vertices has such a decomposition using at most $Cn$ pieces.

The word *decomposition* means every edge is covered, not merely that some cycles and edges can be chosen. Cycles may share vertices but not edges, and an isolated unused vertex needs no piece.

## A small example

A triangle $K_3$ has three edges and can be covered by **one** three-edge cycle, rather than three separate edge pieces. A tree contains no cycles, so each edge must remain a singleton; a tree on $n$ vertices has $n-1$ pieces. These show how cycles can save pieces and why linear order is the natural target.

A graph with no cycles has no choice about the decomposition. A graph with cycles can always remove a simple cycle and repeat until no cycle remains, but a naive removal argument alone does not guarantee at most $Cn$ pieces.

## What is known

The conjectured universal **linear** upper bound remains **OPEN**. Erdős, Goodman and Pósa (1966) proved that $O(n\log n)$ edge-disjoint cycles and edges suffice. Bucić and Montgomery, [*Towards the Erdős–Gallai cycle decomposition conjecture*](https://www.erdosproblems.com/184), improved the upper bound to

$$
O(n\log^*n)
$$

pieces, where $\log^*$ is the iterated logarithm function. Although $\log^*n$ grows extraordinarily slowly, it is **unbounded**, so this is not a proof of $O(n)$.

Conlon, Fox and Sudakov proved a positive linear bound $O_\varepsilon(n)$ when the graph has minimum degree at least $\varepsilon n$. That restricted minimum-degree result does not cover arbitrary sparse graphs.

The maintained [Erdős #184](https://www.erdosproblems.com/184), updated April 2026, records that $K_{3,n-3}$ requires at least $(1+c)n$ pieces for some absolute $c>0$, showing that the optimal universal constant cannot simply be one. Dropping *edge-disjointness* produces a different and potentially weaker covering question.

## GCL's current state

Pinned and maintained status: **OPEN**. Original issue: [#1453](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1453). The $O(n\log^*n)$ result is attributed to Bucić–Montgomery (2022) from the source catalogue, and no full original proof was independently recreated. A formal statement in newer upstream sources is not by itself a protected proof certificate.

The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1453#issuecomment-6168977306). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded successor enumerates small graphs and computes the minimum partition into simple cycles and remaining singleton edges using exact edge-bitmask dynamic programming. Acceptance: a triangle needs one piece, a tree needs exactly its edge count, and no edge is duplicated or omitted. Report numerical values for small complete bipartite graphs, while separating finite checks from the conjectured universal linear bound.
