## The question in plain language

A positive integer is *squarefree* if no prime square divides it. List the squarefree integers in increasing order as $s_1<s_2<\cdots$, and denote consecutive gaps by $g_j=s_{j+1}-s_j$. Erdős #145 asks whether, for every **fixed real exponent** $\alpha\ge0$, the limit

$$
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac1x
\sum_{s_j\le x}g_j^\alpha
$$

exists. The normalization is by the real cutoff $x$, not by the number of gaps. The sum uses gaps with their **left endpoint** $s_j\le x$, so the final right endpoint may exceed $x$.

## A small example

The initial squarefree integers are $1,2,3,5,6,7,10,11,13,14,15$. Their early gaps are $1,1,2,1,1,3,1,2,1,1$. For $\alpha=0$, every gap contributes one, and the limit equals the natural density of squarefree integers, $6/\pi^2$. For $\alpha=1$, the sum telescopes:

$$
\sum_{s_j\le x}(s_{j+1}-s_j)=s_{J+1}-s_1,
$$

where $s_J$ is the last squarefree number at most $x$. The terminal gap must be handled to recover a limiting value one.

## What is known

The unrestricted assertion is **OPEN**. Erdős proved existence for $0\le\alpha\le2$. Hooley extended it to $\alpha\le3$, and Greaves–Harman–Huxley to $\alpha\le11/3$. Tsz Ho Chan, [*On moments of gaps between consecutive square-free numbers*](https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/145), *Moscow Journal of Combinatorics and Number Theory* (2023), 287–295, reaches $\alpha\le3.75$ (i.e. $15/4$).

Granville, [*ABC allows us to count squarefrees*](https://doi.org/10.1155/S1073792898000583), *International Mathematics Research Notices* (1998), 991–1009, obtains the **conditional** all-moments conclusion from the ABC conjecture. A conditional consequence is not a proof of unconditional convergence. The indexed gaps are highly dependent, so a numerical histogram is not a substitute for uniform control of large gaps.

## What remains

A bounded successor generates all squarefree integers up to $10^5$ by an exact prime-square sieve, computes the left-endpoint-normalized sums for exponents $0,1,2,3$, and independently verifies the $\alpha=1$ telescoping identity at every tested cutoff. Acceptance: do not omit the final gap crossing $x$; report the finite-cutoff values without interpreting convergence numerically as proof for $\alpha>15/4$.
