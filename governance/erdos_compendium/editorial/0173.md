## The question in plain language

Colour **every point** of the Euclidean plane red or blue. A triangle $T$ is *monochromatically realizable* if the plane contains three points all of one colour forming a triangle **congruent** to $T$, meaning all side lengths match.

Erdős #173 asks whether every two-colouring of the plane realizes **all but at most one triangular shape** in this way. The possible exceptional triangle may depend on the colouring, and “one triangle” means one congruence class of nondegenerate triangles, not one placement or one scale of all equilateral triangles.

## A small example

Partition the plane into alternating red and blue parallel strips. Some prescribed equilateral triangles can fail to have a monochromatic congruent copy, which explains why the conjecture allows a single exceptional shape. The strip width and fixed side length matter: scaling the triangle changes the question, because *congruence* does **not** permit dilation.

A triangle is described up to congruence by its three side lengths, subject to the strict triangle inequalities. Reflecting or rotating it produces a congruent triangle.

## What is known

The universal “at most one exception” assertion remains **OPEN**. The maintained [Erdős #173](https://www.erdosproblems.com/173) attributes to Shader (1976), [*All right triangles are Ramsey in $E^2$!*](https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/173), the result that any **fixed right triangle** must have a monochromatic congruent copy in every two-colouring of the plane. It is therefore not permissible to call an arbitrary right triangle a potential exception.

The site also cites the original 1975 Euclidean Ramsey theorem programme. Recent work on monochromatic collinear configurations or computational searches for plane colourings is related but does not certify this full geometric classification. The source page was last edited October 2025 and still reports the main problem as open.

## What remains

A bounded geometric-semantics task formalizes **congruence**, **two-colouring of all of $\mathbb R^2$**, and **at most one excluded congruence class**. A finite fixture should search a specified rational grid for congruent triangular point triples using squared distances, while expressly noting that finite-grid absence cannot disprove a plane-wide statement. Acceptance: no scaling is mistaken for congruence, and any full-plane proof remains outside the finite fixture.
