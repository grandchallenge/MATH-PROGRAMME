## The question in plain language

Let $1\le a_1<a_2<\cdots$ be increasing integers growing approximately by repeated squaring:

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n^2}=1.
$$

Erdős asked whether rationality of the reciprocal series $\sum_{n\ge1}1/a_n$ forces the eventual recurrence

$$
a_{n+1}=a_n^2-a_n+1.
$$

The ratio limit is a **termwise asymptotic** condition. It does not imply that the deviations from repeated squaring form a convergent series.

## A small example

Starting at $a_1=2$, the recurrence gives the Sylvester sequence $2,3,7,43,1807,\ldots$. The exact identity

$$
\frac1{a_n}=\frac1{a_n-1}-\frac1{a_{n+1}-1}
$$

makes the series telescope to one. Thus rationality occurs in the predicted family. The example does not establish uniqueness of that family under the weaker asymptotic hypothesis.

## What is known

**OPEN in the original form.** Erdős and Straus (1964) proved a partial irrationality criterion involving least common multiples and the growth deviations. Duverney (2001) proved an equivalence under the **stronger assumption** that

$$
\sum_{n\ge1}\left(\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n^2}-1\right)
$$

converges. The original hypothesis only states that the individual summands tend to zero. Since termwise convergence to zero need not imply convergence of a sum, Duverney's theorem is not an unconditional solution. The maintained [Erdős #243](https://www.erdosproblems.com/243) retains the open status.

A bounded task generates exact Sylvester terms and rational partial sums, verifies the telescoping identity and contrasts a convergent sequence of deviations with a **nonsummable** one. Acceptance: no unproved implication from termwise convergence to series convergence; no finite rational partial sum is described as the value of the infinite series.
