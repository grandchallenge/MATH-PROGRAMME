## The question in plain language

Take $n$ distinct points in Euclidean space and list all their pairwise distances. Suppose no two of those lengths differ by less than one. How wide must the configuration be?

[Erdős Problem #670](https://www.erdosproblems.com/670) asks whether the **diameter**, meaning the longest distance between any two points, is at least $(1+o(1))n^2$. The crucial issue is **dimension**. The maintained catalogue still regards the conjecture as OPEN when the dimension $d$ is **fixed** as $n$ grows. A 2026 construction by Boon Suan Ho refutes the version where $d$ can grow with $n$.

## A small example

Four points on the real line at coordinates $0,1,4,6$ determine the six distances

$$
1,\quad2,\quad3,\quad4,\quad5,\quad6.
$$

Every unordered point-pair has a different length and all six lengths are at least one apart. The diameter is six. This is a small **Golomb-ruler** fixture, not evidence that diameter six is optimal for an asymptotic quadratic conjecture.

## What is known

There are exactly $N=\binom n2$ unordered point pairs, hence $N$ different positive distances under the assumed separation condition. Sort them as $r_1<r_2<\cdots<r_N$. Since $r_{i+1}-r_i\geq1$,

$$
\mathrm{diam}(A)=r_N\geq r_1+N-1>N-1.
$$

Thus elementary counting gives a quadratic lower bound with leading constant **one-half**, rather than the conjectured **one**. Improving the coefficient requires geometric restrictions among triangle lengths, not only the fact that the distances occupy distinct unit-spaced intervals.

**Fixed dimension versus growing dimension.** The source attributes the desired bound for points on the line, $d=1$, to Erdős's original discussion [Er97f, p.6]. Its exact original proof passage has not been inspected in this audit. For fixed higher dimensions, the source retains OPEN.

In contrast, the primary author preprint by [Boon Suan Ho, *Erdős's diameter conjecture for separated distances fails in high dimensions*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15305), submitted April 16, 2026, gives for every prime power $q$ a set of $n=q+1$ points in

$$
\mathbb R^{q^2+q}=\mathbb R^{n^2-n}
$$

whose pairwise distances are all at least one apart from one another, yet whose diameter obeys

$$
\mathrm{diam}(A)\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{\pi^2}+o(1)\right)n^2.
$$

The leading coefficient is approximately $0.8987$, strictly below one. That disproves any **dimension-uniform** version of the conjecture. It does **not** disprove a claim where $d=2$, or another fixed $d$, must be chosen before sending $n\to\infty$.

The author reports a Lean formalization. This is an *external* proof-status claim; the GCL cohort has not independently compiled or audited the formal source.

## What remains

Read the original [Er97f, p.6] passage to determine whether Erdős intended a fixed ambient dimension. For a mathematical successor, first work in exactly two dimensions and seek an improvement beyond the universal counting bound $\binom n2-1$. Any proposed bound must keep the minimum distance separation and dimension quantifiers explicit.

The Ho construction is relevant for a comparative source audit, including its exact source and optional Lean dependency check, but must not be treated as a planar counterexample.
