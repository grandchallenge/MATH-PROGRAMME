## The question in plain language

Let $\mu(n)$ be the Möbius function. Its square is especially simple: $\mu(n)^2$ is $1$ when $n$ is squarefree and $0$ when $n$ has a squared prime factor. Problem 259 asks whether the positive-term series

$$
S=\sum_{n\ge 1}\mu(n)^2\frac{n}{2^n}
$$

is irrational. In other words, after throwing away every integer containing a factor $p^2$ with $p$ prime, weight each remaining integer $n$ by $n/2^n$, and ask whether the resulting real number can equal a ratio of two integers.

The original problem page writes the sum without an explicit lower limit, as is usual in this catalogue. The pinned Lean declaration sums over natural numbers, including $n=0$; its $n=0$ term is $0$, so this convention does not change the value. The question is therefore about one fixed number, not about an unspecified family of bases or coefficients.

The maintained problem page records the classical status as proved by Chen and Ruzsa. That status is mathematically distinct from the GCL status reported below: this manuscript is a documentary draft, and neither a catalogue label nor an external formalization is itself a GCL-certified proof.

## A small example

The squarefree positive integers up to $20$ are

$$
1,2,3,5,6,7,10,11,13,14,15,17,19.
$$

Thus the corresponding finite approximation is

$$
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2}{2^2}+\frac{3}{2^3}+\frac{5}{2^5}+\cdots+\frac{19}{2^{19}}.
$$

Writing $Q$ for the set of positive squarefree integers, reducing the terms exactly gives

$$
\sum_{n\in Q,\ n\le20}\frac{n}{2^n}=\frac{890183}{524288}=1.6978893280029296875.
$$

This is a reproducible check of the statement's normalization, not a test for irrationality. Since the squarefree indicator is at most $1$, the omitted positive tail is bounded by the full geometric-derivative tail:

$$
0<S-\frac{890183}{524288}\le\sum_{n>20}\frac{n}{2^n}=\frac{20+2}{2^{20}}=\frac{11}{524288}<2.1\times10^{-5}.
$$

The finite calculation and the bound can be checked using exact integer fractions; they do not establish that the limiting value is irrational. They only show how quickly the sparse squarefree series begins to settle numerically.

## What is known

The useful change of viewpoint is to list the squarefree numbers in increasing order, say $a_1<a_2<\cdots$. Reindexing the nonzero terms gives

$$
S=\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{a_j}{2^{a_j}}.
$$

Erdős's 1981 paper proves an earlier gap criterion: if a sequence of positive integers satisfies $a_{j+1}-a_j\to\infty$, then the corresponding series $\sum_j a_j2^{-a_j}$ is irrational. That interface does not directly settle Problem 259, because the squarefree sequence is not a sequence whose successive gaps tend to infinity. Erdős later stated a stronger squarefree-subseries conjecture, cited on the problem page at page 103 of his 1988 chapter.

Chen and Ruzsa's 1999 result is the decisive strengthening. In the theorem interface identified by the source audit, let $r\ge2$ and $\ell\ge2$. If $(a_j)$ is an injective sequence of positive integers and, for every $j$, $a_j$ is either r-free or squarefull, then

$$
\sum_{j\ge1}a_j\ell^{-a_j}
$$

is irrational. Here “r-free” means that no rth power of an integer greater than $1$ divides the term; “squarefull” means that every prime divisor occurs with exponent at least two. For Problem 259 take $r=2$, $\ell=2$, and let $(a_j)$ enumerate the squarefree integers. Every $a_j$ is 2-free, so the theorem's disjunction is satisfied and the displayed series is exactly $S$.

The proof interface is a rationality contradiction. Assuming the series is a rational number $u/v$, one forms carefully shifted and rescaled tails. Rationality forces a certain correction expression, after multiplication by the fixed denominator $v$, to be an integer. The number-theoretic construction supplies forbidden intervals containing no terms of the allowed type; those gaps make the correction expression positive but drive it to $0$ by geometric-tail estimates. An integer that is eventually strictly between $0$ and $1$ cannot exist. This explains why the theorem needs both an injective sequence and structural restrictions on its values: they control the finite correction and the tail separately.
