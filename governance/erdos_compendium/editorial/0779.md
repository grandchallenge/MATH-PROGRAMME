## The question in plain language

Multiply the first $n$ primes together, obtaining the primorial $P$. Erdős Problem #779 asks whether there is always a *later* prime $q$ which is smaller than $P$ but has the property that $P+q$ is also prime.

This is a quantified conjecture about every integer $n>1$. It is labelled **FALSIFIABLE**: a single fully checked value of $n$ for which every candidate fails would disprove it. No such counterexample is established in the sources checked. Falsifiable is a logical classification, **not** the same thing as disproved.

## A small example

For $n=2$, the first two primes are 2 and 3, so $P=6$. The candidate $q=5$ satisfies $3<5<6$, and $P+q=11$ is prime. Thus the conjecture holds for this one case.

The next checks are equally concrete: $n=3$ gives $P=30$, candidate $q=7$, and $P+q=37$; $n=4$ gives $P=210$, candidate $q=13$, and $P+q=223$. In each example **both** $q$ and $P+q$ must be checked for primality.

The simple fact that $gcd(P,P+q)=1$ follows whenever $q$ is a later prime, since it is coprime to every factor of $P$. But coprimality with $P$ does not make $P+q$ prime. This is precisely the gap between Euclid-style arguments and the desired result.

## What is known

Let $p_1<p_2<cdots<p_n$ be the first $n$ primes, and write

$$

P_n=prod_{i=1}^n p_i.

$$

The assertion is

$$

orall n>1quad exists q	ext{ prime}:
quad p_n<q<P_n,qquad P_n+q	ext{ prime}.

$$

A witness is admissible only if the strict inequalities hold. The maintained [problem #779](https://www.erdosproblems.com/779) attributes the conjecture to Deaconescu and reports a finite verification for $nleq1000$, but this audit did not inspect the original computational record or its primality certificates. It separately attributes a stronger prediction that a witness can be chosen with $q$ polynomially bounded in $n$. That is conjectural, not established by the reported finite tests.

A heuristic treats the primality of $P+q$ for candidate primes as roughly random with chance $1/log P$. Such estimates can help prioritize numerical experiments. However, the events share strong arithmetic structure, so a naive independence approximation is not a proof.

The pinned FormalConjectures provider includes a file for #779, but the presence of a Lean statement is not evidence that its definition matches every quantifier above, nor that a proof has been formally checked.
