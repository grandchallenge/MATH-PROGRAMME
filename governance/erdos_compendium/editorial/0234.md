## The question in plain language

With consecutive prime gaps $d_n=p_{n+1}-p_n$, consider for $c\ge0$ the proportion of indices $n\le N$ for which

$$
\frac{d_n}{\log n}<c.
$$

Erdős conjectured that for every $c$ this proportion has a limit $F(c)$ as $N\to\infty$, and that the resulting function of $c$ is **continuous**. The normalization uses $\log n$, not the gap index's prime $p_n$ or an arbitrary parameter.

## A small example

For small indices, the first prime gaps are $1,2,2,4,\ldots$. Evaluating their quotients by $\log n$ requires starting at $n\ge2$, because $\log1=0$. That single undefined initial term has no effect on an asymptotic density, but must be excluded in a finite implementation.

The threshold is **strict**: ratios exactly equal to $c$ do not count. The conjecture does not assert that the limiting function is exponential or any other named distribution.

## What is known

**OPEN.** This is a distributional prime-gap question, considerably stronger than knowing the mean gap. Even a prime-gap distribution along a selected subsequence or a conditional heuristic under strengthened prime-tuples conjectures would not prove the requested **unconditional all-index** limit.

The maintained [Erdős #234](https://www.erdosproblems.com/234) refers to earlier Erdős 1955 and 1961 questions and to #5. There are strong connections to the adjacent-gap comparison problem #218, but that problem concerns the signs of *successive differences* between gaps rather than the distribution of normalized gap magnitudes.

## What remains

A bounded task computes the empirical cumulative distributions for prime-gap ratios at increasing cutoffs and tests the monotonicity expected of any cumulative distribution. Acceptance: omit the $n=1$ denominator, preserve the strict inequality, compare several cutoffs without assigning a speculative limiting formula, and show that finite empirical continuity does not imply continuity of the unknown limit.
