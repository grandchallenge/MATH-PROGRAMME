## The question in plain language

An infinite graph may require uncountably many colours, yet each of its finite subgraphs can be only moderately far from bipartite. A graph is **bipartite** if its vertices can be coloured with two colours without a monochromatic edge, equivalently if it has no odd cycle.

For an infinite graph $G$, define $h_G(n)$ to be the smallest integer such that **every** subgraph of $G$ on $n$ vertices becomes bipartite after deleting at most $h_G(n)$ edges. Erdős, Hajnal and Szemerédi ask whether, whenever $\chi(G)=\aleph_1$,

$$
\frac{h_G(n)}n\longrightarrow\infty.
$$

The quantifier fixes one host graph $G$ first, then lets $n$ tend to infinity. Deleting edges does not mean deleting vertices or considering only induced subgraphs.

## A small example

For a triangle $K_3$, deleting any one edge makes the graph bipartite, whereas deleting zero does not. Thus its edge-bipartization number is one. In a disjoint union of $t$ triangles, at least one edge from each triangle must be removed, so exactly $t$ deletions are needed. This makes $h_G(n)$ linear in the number of chosen vertices for those examples, rather than superlinear.

More generally, for a finite graph $H$, the minimum number of edges to delete before it becomes bipartite equals $|E(H)|-\mathrm{maxcut}(H)$: choose a bipartition keeping all crossing edges, and delete the others. This identity supplies an exact computational interface.

## What is known

The conjecture remains **OPEN**. The maintained [Erdős #111](https://www.erdosproblems.com/111) states that every graph of chromatic number $\aleph_1$ satisfies a universal lower bound $h_G(n)\gg n$. A core combinatorial input is the existence of uncountably many vertex-disjoint odd cycles of some fixed length in such a graph. That lower bound is **not** the conjectured superlinear behaviour.

Erdős, Hajnal and Szemerédi constructed a graph with chromatic number $\aleph_1$ for which

$$
h_G(n)\ll n^{3/2}.
$$

An earlier Erdős conjecture proposed an improvement to $h_G(n)\ll_\epsilon n^{1+\epsilon}$ for each $\epsilon>0$ in a suitable construction. This **upper** bound would still be compatible with $h_G(n)/n\to\infty$; the two directions are easily confused.

The primary mathematical bibliography includes the 1982 Erdős–Hajnal–Szemerédi work and Erdős's subsequent 1987 and 1997 problem lists. The precise published derivation of the fixed-length odd-cycle property and the host-graph construction have not been independently page-audited here.

## What remains

A bounded task is to enumerate finite graphs up to six vertices, calculate the largest edge cut exactly, and compare it with the minimum size of an odd-cycle edge-transversal. Acceptance: equality for every graph, one deletion for a triangle, $t$ deletions for $t$ disjoint triangles when represented, and a check that forests require zero. Stop at these finite semantics. They cannot establish universal asymptotic behaviour for $\aleph_1$ chromatic hosts.
