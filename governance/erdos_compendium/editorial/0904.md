## The question in plain language

Every vertex $x$ of a finite simple graph has a **degree** $d(x)$: the number of other vertices adjacent to it. The *average degree* in a graph with $n$ vertices and $m$ edges is $2m/n$.

Choose an integer $r\ge2$. An **clique of size $r$** is a collection of $r$ vertices all pairwise adjacent. The *Turán number* $t_r(n)$ is the largest edge count among graph with $n$ vertices graphs with **no clique of size $r+1$**.

Bollobás and Erdős conjectured that whenever $G$ has at least $t_r(n)$ edges, it contains an clique of size $r$ $S$ whose **average vertex degree is at least the graph's average degree**:

$$
\sum_{x\in S}d(x)\ge \frac{2rm}{n}.
$$

[Erdős Problem #904](https://www.erdosproblems.com/904) is **proved**, with the full result established by Bollobás and Nikiforov in 2005. The assertion is about **one clique with a sufficiently large total degree**, not a clique each of whose vertices individually has degree at least $2m/n$.

## A small example

Consider the complete bipartite graph $K_{3,3}$, on six vertices divided into two groups of three. It contains all nine edges between the groups and none within either group. Every vertex has degree three.

For $r=2$, the Turán threshold is

$$
t_2(6)=\left\lfloor\frac{6^2}{4}\right\rfloor=9.
$$

Any cross-group edge is a two-vertex clique. The sum of its endpoint degrees is $3+3=6$, while the required right-hand side is

$$
\frac{2\cdot2\cdot9}{6}=6.
$$

Thus **equality holds**. The numerical constant cannot be increased in general.

An independent exact program enumerates **every simple graph on 2 through 6 labelled vertices**, totaling 33,866 graphs. Whenever a graph meets the appropriate Turán threshold for an integer $2\le r\le n$, it verifies that some clique of size $r$ has degree sum at least $2rm/n$ using an integer inequality with no floating-point rounding. This is a finite regression, not a proof for all $n$ and $r$.

## What is known

The original conjecture was recorded by Bollobás and Erdős in [*Unsolved Problems*](https://www.erdosproblems.com/904), *Proceedings of the Fifth British Combinatorial Conference* (1975), pp.678–680. Erdős also discussed a special case $r=3$ under a slightly stronger edge-density hypothesis in *Some recent progress on extremal problems in graph theory*, *Congressus Numerantium* (1975), pp.3–14.

Edwards (1978) proved the inequality for $2\le r\le8$, subject to $n\ge r^2$. Faudree, [*Complete subgraphs with large degree sums*](https://www.erdosproblems.com/904), *Journal of Graph Theory* (1992), pp.327–334, proved it for all $r\ge2$ when $n>(r-1)r^2/4$. These were substantial partial results, but neither removed all size restrictions.

Béla Bollobás and Vladimir Nikiforov, [*The Sum of Degrees in Cliques*](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v12i1n21), *Electronic Journal of Combinatorics* **12** (2005), Article N21, DOI [10.37236/1988](https://doi.org/10.37236/1988), prove in their main theorem that for every integer $r\ge2$ and graph with $m\ge t_r(n)$,

$$
\max_{S\text{ an }r\text{-clique}}\sum_{x\in S}d(x)\ge\frac{2rm}{n}.
$$

The same paper studies a near-threshold stability statement: for every fixed tolerance, some smaller edge shortfall still guarantees a nearly comparable clique degree sum. Its hypotheses must not be confused with the exact-threshold theorem.

The maintained [Erdős Problem #904](https://www.erdosproblems.com/904) reports **PROVED (LEAN)**. Its public Formal Conjectures source links a separate Lean implementation attributed to Aristotle, but the upstream theorem body itself contains a placeholder. GCL did **not** independently build that external implementation or inspect its literal axiom report.

## GCL's current state

The threshold can be computed directly from the balanced complete multipartite Turán graph. Divide $n$ vertices into $r$ classes whose sizes differ by at most one, and join every pair in different classes. If their sizes are $s_1,\ldots,s_r$, then

$$
t_r(n)=\frac12\left(n^2-\sum_{i=1}^r s_i^2\right).
$$

An exact finite representation uses

$$
q=\lfloor n/r\rfloor,\qquad h=n-rq;
$$

there are $h$ classes of size $q+1$ and $r-h$ of size $q$. This prevents the common mistake of using the continuous approximation $(1-1/r)n^2/2$ as if it were the exact integer threshold for every $n$.

For any graph meeting the threshold, the desired inequality is equivalent to the pure-integer comparison

$$
n\max_{S\text{ an }r\text{-clique}}\sum_{x\in S}d(x)\ge2rm.
$$

The exact executable fixture at governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_903_904.py enumerates every simple undirected graph for $2\le n\le6$, computes its degrees, compares the true edge count with the exact multipartite Turán threshold and checks all candidate cliques. It reports all 33,866 graphs and separately verifies the equality case $K_{3,3}$.

The general Bollobás–Nikiforov argument is substantially more than this finite inequality test. Their theorem is imported from the published primary source; no independent reproduction of the full extremal proof or its external Lean formalization has been completed here.

## What remains

A bounded theorem is the **exact finite Turán threshold and degree-sum interface**: construct the balanced multipartite with $r$ classes graph with its correct class sizes, prove its edge-count formula, and establish the integer form of the balanced-clique inequality for each source-locked finite graph.

Acceptance requires a complete elementary proof or a pinned, no-placeholder Lean theorem with literal axiom readback, replaying all 33,866 graph checks and equality for $K_{3,3}$. A false claimed clique, wrong forbidden clique of size $r+1$ convention, off-by-one Turán edge count or floating-point comparison invalidates the finite certificate.

The substantive source task is to reconstruct the 2005 proof for arbitrary $n,r$, pin the exact source article and compare the external Lean theorem statement against its hypotheses. In particular, a published conjecture stated for $r\ge2$ and an upstream formal theorem quantified over $r\ge1$ must be assessed as **distinct semantic interfaces**, even when the extra $r=1$ case is elementary.
