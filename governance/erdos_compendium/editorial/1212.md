## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1212](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1212) currently has source status **OPEN — EXTRA COMPOSITE CONDITION**.

On the graph of visible lattice points $(x,y)\in\mathbb N^2$ with $\gcd(x,y)=1$, connect pairs differing by exactly $\pm1$ in **one coordinate**. Does there exist an **unbounded path** all of whose vertices satisfy $\min(x,y)>1$ and **at least one coordinate composite**? A known path with both coordinates prime at many vertices does not meet the extra composite requirement. Likewise, finding a connected finite cluster or a path in the full graph does not establish an infinite path inside the induced restricted subgraph.

## A small example

Restrict to integer points $2\le x,y\le17$ satisfying gcd one and at least one composite coordinate. The phase25 finite graph contains **116** vertices. Breadth-first search from $(4,3)$ finds only that vertex within the **restricted** graph because all its nearest visible neighbors violate one of the additional conditions. An isolated seed does not establish that every component is finite, and therefore is not a counterexample to an unbounded path elsewhere.

## What is known

Erdős [Er80,p.114] and the maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/1212 say Stewart proved an infinite path with $\min(x,y)>1$ through pairs of consecutive primes, using a walk $(p_k,p_{k+1})\to(p_k,p_{k+2})\to(p_{k+1},p_{k+2})$ for sufficiently large $k$. But this path includes points with **both coordinates prime**, hence does NOT solve the current strengthened question. The page notes the claimed Herzog–Stewart infinite-component theorem cannot be located in their [HeSt71] paper, and records further monotone/bounded-turn questions. Thus the strengthened composite-coordinate variant remains **OPEN**.
