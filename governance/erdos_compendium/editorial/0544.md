**Editorial status: UNACCEPTED_DRAFT.** Erdős Problem #544 contains **two OPEN questions** about the differences between consecutive triangle Ramsey numbers. Erdős and Sós asked whether these differences eventually grow without bound and whether, at the same time, they become negligible compared with the parameter $k$. Neither statement follows from the familiar asymptotic estimate $R(3,k)\asymp k^2/\log k$.

## The question in plain language

Color every edge of a complete graph red or blue. The **Ramsey number** $R(3,k)=R(K_3,K_k)$ is the smallest $N$ for which every such coloring on $N$ vertices contains either a red triangle or a blue complete graph on $k$ vertices.

Equivalently, $R(3,k)$ is the least number of vertices for which every **triangle-free graph** has an **independent set** of size $k$: consider the graph whose edges are colored red.

Write

$$
d_k=R(3,k+1)-R(3,k).
$$

The two conjectural assertions are

$$
\boxed{d_k\longrightarrow\infty\qquad(k\longrightarrow\infty)}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{\frac{d_k}{k}\longrightarrow0\qquad(k\longrightarrow\infty).}
$$

The first asks that, eventually, each step in the Ramsey sequence grows by an arbitrarily large number of vertices. The second asks that each step be **sublinear** in $k$. Both can be true simultaneously; neither is proved by the other.

The historical problems were posed by Erdős and Vera T. Sós. The [Erdős Problems #544 catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/544) still labels both **OPEN**.

## A small example

The first known exact values are

| $k$ | $R(3,k)$ | Increase from the previous $k$ |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 3 | — |
| 3 | 6 | 3 |
| 4 | 9 | 3 |
| 5 | 14 | 5 |
| 6 | 18 | 4 |
| 7 | 23 | 5 |
| 8 | 28 | 5 |
| 9 | 36 | 8 |

These values are useful reference points, **not evidence of either infinite-limit theorem**. A finite table, even one with increasing differences, cannot rule out infinitely many later small gaps, nor infinitely many gaps of linear size.

For the smallest nontrivial case, a cycle of length five, $C_5$, contains no triangle and has no independent set of size three. This proves $R(3,3)>5$. Conversely, every graph on six vertices has a triangle or an independent three-set, the classical identity $R(3,3)=6$.

An eight-vertex Möbius ladder, obtained from an eight-cycle by adding its four antipodal chords, is triangle-free and has independence number three. This witnesses $R(3,4)>8$; the known upper result is $R(3,4)=9$.

An exact bounded computational task can check the triangle and independence predicates for these examples and compare two independent independence-number algorithms. It should **not** claim to have recomputed the nontrivial upper bounds for all $k\le9$ without the corresponding exhaustive certificates.

### Why the overall Ramsey asymptotic does not settle differences

Jeong Han Kim's 1995 paper, *The Ramsey number R(3,t) has order of magnitude t²/log t*, *Random Structures & Algorithms* **7**, 173–207, combined with earlier results establishes

$$
\boxed{R(3,k)\asymp\frac{k^2}{\log k}.}
$$

This confines the overall size of $R(3,k)$ between constant multiples of $k^2/\log k$, but **does not** constrain successive differences tightly enough to answer either question. A monotone function can satisfy coarse global bounds and have occasional tiny jumps or very large jumps.

The elementary Erdős–Szekeres two-color recurrence gives

$$
R(s,t)\le R(s-1,t)+R(s,t-1).
$$

Apply it with $s=3$ and $t=k+1$, using $R(2,k+1)=k+1$:

$$
R(3,k+1)\le R(3,k)+k+1.
$$

For the lower bound, let $G$ be a triangle-free graph on $R(3,k)-1$ vertices with no independent set of size $k$. Adjoin an **isolated vertex**. The resulting graph remains triangle-free, and its independence number is at most $k$. Thus

$$
R(3,k+1)\ge R(3,k)+1.
$$

Together,

$$
\boxed{1\le d_k\le k+1.}
$$

The upper estimate is only $O(k)$, **not** $o(k)$. This distinction was clarified in April 2026 comments on the maintained page.

### Later quantitative research

The maintained #544 remarks cite an improvement connected with Erdős Problem #1014, asserting for some $c>0$ that

$$
d_k\ll k^{-c}R(3,k).
$$

This is a **source-attributed result**, not an independently rerun GCL proof. Substituting $R(3,k)\asymp k^2/\log k$ yields a relative power saving but does not alone provide the desired $o(k)$ conclusion.

A July 26, 2026 [independent finite working report](https://erdosproblemaday.com/report/544) explores nested triangle-free Ramsey-critical graphs through $k=8$ and a 35-vertex critical graph obstruction at $k=9$. Neither its computation nor verification script was independently replayed here. Its claims concern bounded mathematical experiments, not either infinite limit.

## What remains

For the first question, the missing theorem is a **growing lower bound** on $d_k$, holding for every sufficiently large $k$. For the second, one needs a **sublinear upper bound** valid uniformly at large $k$, or an infinite family with gaps comparable to $k$ to disprove it.

A practical bounded successor is to design a rigorously controlled triangle-free extension operation that forces independent-set capacity across critical instances. Finite graphs can falsify proposed local lemmas, but cannot by themselves certify an asymptotic theorem.
