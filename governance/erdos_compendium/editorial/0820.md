## The question in plain language

For n>=1 let H(n)=least l>=3 for which some integers 2<=k<l satisfy gcd(k^n-1,l^n-1)=1. The restriction k>=2 is IMPLICIT in the maintained statement: allowing k=1 makes its reported H(1)=3 false (k=1,l=2 gives gcd(0,1)=1). Ask whether H(n)=3 infinitely often and obtain fine two-sided growth estimates with exponents near c/loglog n.

## A small example

An independently checked exact search 2<=k<l for n=1..10 gives H values [3,3,3,6,3,18,3,6,3,12]. For n=4, the least witness at l=6 is k=5: gcd(5^4-1,6^4-1)=gcd(624,1295)=1; no smaller l works. This finite computation cannot establish infinitely many n.

## What is known

Maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/820, read 2026-10-14; original Erdős [Er74b] proves infinitely-often lower H(n)>exp(n^{c/(loglog n)^2}); van Doorn public comment SKETCHES a stronger infinitely-often lower exp(n^{c/loglog n}), not an independently verified theorem. The catalogue's proposed matching upper/lower logarithmic exponents and whether gcd(2^n-1,3^n-1)=1 infinitely often remain OPEN. Historical 1974 source not independently read.

The restriction $2\leq k<l$ is necessary to match the maintained sequence. Without it, at $n=1$, the pair $(k,l)=(1,2)$ gives $\gcd(0,1)=1$ and would make $H(1)=2$ instead of the reported 3. With positive bases at least two, $H(n)=3$ is equivalent to $\gcd(2^n-1,3^n-1)=1$. The infinitely-often lower and eventual upper growth questions have different quantifiers and remain OPEN.

The normalized definition consistent with the maintained numerical sequence is

$$

H(n)=\min\left\{l\geq3:\exists\,2\leq k<l,\
\gcd(k^n-1,l^n-1)=1\right\}.

$$

For $l=3$ only $k=2$ is possible, so $H(n)=3$ exactly when $\gcd(2^n-1,3^n-1)=1$. The growth proposal has two different quantifiers: an infinitely-often lower bound and an eventual upper bound.
