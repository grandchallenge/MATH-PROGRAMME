## The question in plain language

For two graphs $H_1,H_2$, their Ramsey number $R(H_1,H_2)$ is the smallest integer $N$ such that every red-blue colouring of all edges of $K_N$ gives a red copy of $H_1$ or a blue copy of $H_2$.

Erdős #159 asks whether there exists an absolute constant $c>0$ such that

$$
R(C_4,K_n)=O(n^{2-c}).
$$

The first forbidden graph is the **four-cycle** $C_4$, not the complete graph $K_4$. A logarithmic saving such as $O(n^2/(\log n)^2)$ is **not** a positive-power saving $O(n^{2-c})$.

## A small example

In a red-blue colouring of $K_N$, the red graph avoids $C_4$ precisely when no two distinct vertices have two different common red neighbours. In that situation, the blue graph is the complement of the red graph. A blue $K_n$ is an independent set of size $n$ in the red graph.

This rewrites $R(C_4,K_n)$ as a graph extremal question: how many vertices can a $C_4$ free graph have while its independence number is less than $n$? The reformulation is elementary but does not supply the required exponent.

## What is known

The conjectured power saving remains **OPEN**. As recorded in the maintained [Erdős #159](https://www.erdosproblems.com/159), the classical estimates are

$$
\frac{n^{3/2}}{(\log n)^{3/2}}
\ll R(C_4,K_n)
\ll\frac{n^2}{(\log n)^2}.
$$

The lower bound is attributed to Joel Spencer [Sp77], and the upper to Endre Szemerédi, cited in the 1978 paper by Erdős, Faudree, Rousseau and Schelp [EFRS78]. Both are source-attributed established estimates. The logarithms leave a gap between a lower power near $3/2$ and an upper power near two.

The original 1978 problem carried a historical prize of USD 100. That historical prize metadata does not imply any award status, new research campaign authorization or permission to replace the protected source statement. The current catalogue expressly labels the question OPEN.

## What remains

A bounded successor formalizes the equivalence between red $C_4$ free or blue $K_n$ free colourings and graphs without a $C_4$ with independence number less than $n$. Acceptance: exhaustive enumeration for $N\le5$ agrees under both formulations and explicitly distinguishes $C_4$ from $K_4$.
