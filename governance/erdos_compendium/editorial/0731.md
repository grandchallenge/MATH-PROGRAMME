## The question in plain language

For n≥1 write B(n)=$\binom{2n}{n}$. Let m(n) be the LEAST positive integer m with m NOT dividing B(n). Erdős–Graham–Ruzsa–Straus ask for an asymptotic equivalent $m(n)\sim f(n)$ for almost all n. The maintained problem is OPEN: an order-of-magnitude statement on a logarithmic scale does not provide a specific asymptotic f(n).

## A small example

For n=1, B(1)=2 and the least positive nondivisor is m(1)=3; n=2 B(2)=6 and m(2)=4; n=3 B(3)=20 and m(3)=3; n=4 B(4)=70 and m(4)=3. These are exact finite values, not evidence for a normal-order theorem. Note the least nondivisor is not necessarily prime: m(2)=4.

## What is known

The maintained source attributes to original EGRS75 the almost-all estimate $m(n)=\exp((\log n)^{1/2+o(1)})$. This quantifies the approximate logarithmic GROWTH exponent for a density-one set of n; it does NOT provide a function f(n) with ratio $m(n)/f(n)\to1$. The OEIS A006197 describes the sequence but cannot prove a nearly-all asymptotic. Exact computation can use binomial valuations: m divides B(n) iff v_p(m)≤v_p(B(n)) for every prime p|m. That is a deterministic algorithm, not a solution of the distribution of m(n).
