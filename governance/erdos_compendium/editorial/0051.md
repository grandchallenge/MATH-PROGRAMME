## The question in plain language

Euler's totient function $\phi(n)$ counts the positive integers up to $n$ that are relatively prime to $n$. Many different integers have the same totient value. If a number $a$ occurs as such a value, let $n_a$ be the **smallest** positive integer with $\phi(n_a)=a$.

[Erdős Problem #51](https://www.erdosproblems.com/51) asks whether there are infinitely many different totient values $a$ for which the ratio of this smallest preimage to $a$ becomes arbitrarily large, in the precise sense

$$
\lim_{\substack{a\to\infty\\ a\in A}}\frac{n_a}{a}=\infty
$$

for some infinite set $A$ of totient values. This remains **OPEN**. The problem concerns the least preimage, not just the existence of integers with a large ratio $n/\phi(n)$.

## A small example

The smallest integer with totient value 8 is 15, since $\phi(15)=8$. This gives $n_8/8=15/8$. Similarly,

$$
\phi(25)=20,\qquad \phi(35)=24,\qquad \phi(51)=32.
$$

Their least preimages are respectively $25$, $35$ and $51$. An independently checked enumeration of all $1\le n\le100$ confirms these values, along with $\phi(69)=44$ and the fact that 69 is its least preimage.

For any positive integer $n$, Euler's product identity gives

$$
\frac{n}{\phi(n)}=\prod_{p\mid n}\frac{p}{p-1}.
$$

Thus a large ratio requires the least preimage $n_a$ to have a product of prime factors with a sufficiently large cumulative effect. It is not enough to construct *some* integer $n$ with large $n/\phi(n)$; another, smaller integer may have the same totient value.

## What is known

The maintained problem record links this question to earlier work of Erdős and to Carmichael's conjecture about whether a totient value can have exactly one preimage. It reports an Erdős theorem that a single uniquely represented totient value, if one exists, would force infinitely many. That conditional result does not settle the question about divergent ratios of **least** preimages.

The historical reference [Er95] is Erdős, *Some of my favourite problems in number theory, combinatorics, and geometry*, *Resenhas* **2** (1995), 165–186. Guy's *Unsolved Problems in Number Theory* discusses related totient questions (B36 and B39). The original source pages and the separate [Er98] citation have not been independently checked line by line here.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated GCL source audit #1320](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1320#issuecomment-6134233239) records the exact infinite-set quantifiers, a 21-value finite computation independently checked against direct coprimality counts, and a proposed larger but bounded totient sieve. The pinned [Formal Conjectures source for #51](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/51.lean) encodes a least preimage for each member of an infinite set, with convergence of the quotient to infinity. Its research theorem is unfilled, not a GCL proof certificate.

## What remains

To answer Erdős's question, one must prove that some infinite sequence of totient values has least-preimage ratios tending to infinity, or rule out this possibility. The proposed bounded computation to $10^6$ would check exact first occurrences and numerical ratios. It cannot decide an assertion about every sufficiently large member of an infinite set.
