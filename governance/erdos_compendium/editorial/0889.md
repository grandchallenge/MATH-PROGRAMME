## The question in plain language

Imagine reading the consecutive integers $n,n+1,n+2,\ldots$. At step $j$, count the **distinct prime divisors** of $n+j$ that have not appeared in an earlier member of that consecutive run. Call that number $v(n,j)$.

Erdős and Selfridge asked whether, however large a target count $M$ is specified, every sufficiently large starting integer $n$ must have some step introducing at least $M$ new distinct prime divisors. Equivalently, defining

$$
v_0(n)=\max_{j\ge0}v(n,j),
$$

is it true that $v_0(n)\to\infty$ as $n\to\infty$? This is [Erdős Problem #889](https://www.erdosproblems.com/889), which remains **open**. The stronger version restricts the search to steps $j\ge\ell$, for each fixed nonnegative $\ell$.

## A small example

The first crucial simplification is exact:

$$
v(n,j)=\#\{p:p\text{ prime},\ p\mid n+j,\ p>j\}.
$$

Indeed, if a prime $p\le j$ divides $n+j$, it also divides the earlier number $n+j-p$, whereas a prime $p>j$ cannot divide two numbers separated by a positive distance at most $j$. Thus the apparently historical *previously unseen prime* question reduces to a factorization and threshold comparison.

For $n=16$, the number at step zero is $16$, with only one distinct prime factor, and the finite calculation gives $v_0(16)=1$. At $n=17$, one has $v_0(17)\ge2$: at step $j=1$, the integer $18$ has the distinct prime factors 2 and 3, both greater than 1.

An exact finite verifier checked every starting $1\le n\le2000$. The complete list with $v_0(n)=1$ is

$$
\{1,2,3,4,7,8,16\}.
$$

The list is consistent with, but not a replacement for, the published theorem.

## What is known

Erdős and Selfridge, [*Some problems on the prime factors of consecutive integers*](https://combinatorica.hu/~p_erdos/1967-21.pdf), *Illinois Journal of Mathematics* **11** (1967), pp.428–430, DOI 10.1215/ijm/1256054564, prove on printed page 428 that $v_0(n)>1$ for every positive integer not among the seven exceptional values above. In particular,

$$
v_0(n)\ge2\qquad(n\ge17).
$$

This establishes a fixed lower bound of two, not divergence to infinity.

The original authors also listed the values of $n<2500$ for which $v_1(n)=1$, ending in $330$. They expressly did **not** have a proof that no larger examples exist. On printed page 429 they introduce a different quantity, traditionally denoted $V(n,j)$, which counts certain **prime powers** rather than newly occurring prime bases. Neither that modified function nor its numerical table should be conflated with $v(n,j)$.

A [January 2026 maintained discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/889) proposes a reduction from the $\ell=0$ divergence conjecture to fixed positive $\ell$, using a shift inequality, but does not supply an independently GCL-verified proof of the limit. The main and generalized questions remain open. The corresponding public Formal Conjectures file labels the seven-exception theorem *solved*, while the divergent-limit theorem bodies contain placeholders; these are different statements.

## GCL's current state

The arithmetic reduction permits an **exact finite cutoff** in each fixed starting integer $n$. If $j>n$, then $n+j<2j$. This integer cannot have two different prime divisors larger than $j$, because their product would exceed $j^2\ge2j$ for such $j$. Thus $v(n,j)\le1$ whenever $j>n$.

Consequently the infinite maximum $v_0(n)$ is computed exactly by considering only $0\le j\le n$, with no heuristic stopping horizon. Similarly, $v_1(n)$ can be obtained from $1\le j\le n$ together with the bound for later steps.

The reproducible finite_889_890.py script computes distinct prime divisors by a smallest-prime-factor sieve, directly compares the earlier-seen-primes definition with the threshold form for small $n$, and exactly determines $v_0$ and $v_1$ for $1\le n\le2000$. It reproduces all seven exceptions and the 29 starting integers below 2500 reported in 1967 to have $v_1(n)=1$. This numerical agreement provides a source-fidelity check, **not** an asymptotic result.

## What remains

A bounded mathematical successor is to formalize the *new-prime factor equivalence* and the finite-cutoff lemma. Inputs are integers $n\ge1$, $j\ge0$, distinct prime-factor support, and earlier members $n,\ldots,n+j-1$. Target:

$$
\text{newPrimeCount}(n,j)=\#\{p\mid n+j:p>j\}
$$

and the inequality $v(n,j)\le1$ for $j>n$. Acceptance is a complete elementary proof or a pinned Lean development with no placeholders, a literal axiom report, and a byte-identical replay of all exact finite counts. A prime missed or double-counted under either definition falsifies the equivalence.

A separate independent source-fidelity task is to reconstruct the exact 1967 proof of the seven-exception theorem, including its treatment of $n=1$, and compare the frozen formal statement. Stop before declaring the conjectured divergence, or eventual behavior of $v_1$, established without a genuine asymptotic argument.
