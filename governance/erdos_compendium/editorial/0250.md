## The question in plain language

For a positive integer $n$, let

$$
\sigma_1(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}d
$$

be the sum of its positive divisors. Erdős Problem 250 asks whether the real number

$$
S=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma_1(n)}{2^n}
$$

is irrational; equivalently, whether $S\notin\mathbb Q$. This is an ordinary convergent real series, not a question about a finite decimal approximation. Indeed, there are at most $n$ divisors of $n$, each at most $n$, so $\sigma_1(n)\leq n^2$. Comparison with $\sum_{n\geq1}n^2/2^n$ proves convergence.

The quantifiers matter. The classical statement concerns the one real number defined by the displayed series. The pinned Lean declaration instead says, in effect,

$$
\forall x\;\bigl(\mathrm{HasSum}(n\mapsto \sigma_1(n)/2^n,x)\Longrightarrow \mathrm{Irrational}(x)\bigr).
$$

That implication does not itself assert that a sum exists; it asserts irrationality for every real $x$ which is a sum of the specified sequence. In standard analysis, convergence gives existence and uniqueness, so the two formulations align after that bridge is supplied. The indexing convention also needs care: the registered sum starts at $n=1$, whereas a formal Lambert-series development may include an $n=0$ term and rely on its coefficient being zero.

## A small example

The first divisor sums are

$$
\sigma_1(1),\ldots,\sigma_1(9)=1,3,4,7,6,12,8,15,13.
$$

Using the first eight terms gives the exact rational partial sum

$$
S_8=\sum_{n=1}^{8}\frac{\sigma_1(n)}{2^n}=\frac{687}{256}.
$$

The corresponding finite version of Nesterenko's normalization is

$$
1-24S_8=-\frac{2029}{32},
$$

and the next omitted summand is $\sigma_1(9)/2^9=13/512$. These figures were independently recomputed with exact rational arithmetic. A minimal reproduction is:

```python
from fractions import Fraction

def sigma(n):
    return sum(d for d in range(1, n + 1) if n % d == 0)

s8 = sum((Fraction(sigma(n), 2**n) for n in range(1, 9)), Fraction(0))
assert [sigma(n) for n in range(1, 10)] == [1, 3, 4, 7, 6, 12, 8, 15, 13]
assert s8 == Fraction(687, 256)
assert 1 - 24*s8 == Fraction(-2029, 32)
```

This checks normalization and arithmetic only. No finite list of partial sums can prove irrationality of the limiting value, and the displayed negative rational comes with no certified error estimate for $1-24S$.

## What is known

The answer is affirmative, and the strongest source-backed statement here is stronger than irrationality: $S$ is transcendental. The key published result is Yuri V. Nesterenko, “Modular functions and transcendence questions,” *Sbornik: Mathematics* 187(9) (1996), 1319–1348. On English p. 1319 he defines Ramanujan's function

$$
P(q)=1-24\sum_{n\geq1}\sigma_1(n)q^n.
$$

Theorem 1 on English p. 1320 states that for every complex $q$ with $0<|q|<1$, at least three of $q,P(q),Q(q),R(q)$ are algebraically independent over $\mathbb Q$. Corollary 2 on the same page states that if $q$ is algebraic and $0<|q|<1$, then $P(q),Q(q),R(q)$ are algebraically independent over $\mathbb Q$, hence each is transcendental. Taking $q=1/2$ gives

$$
P(1/2)=1-24S.
$$

Because $1/2$ is algebraic and lies strictly between $0$ and $1$, Corollary 2 makes $P(1/2)$ transcendental. The nonzero rational affine change

$$
S=\frac{1-P(1/2)}{24}
$$

therefore makes $S$ transcendental, and consequently irrational. This is a borrowed deep lemma from Nesterenko's published paper; this article does not claim to reprove it.

The original source packet records the problem in P. Erdős and R. L. Graham, *Old and New Problems and Results in Combinatorial Number Theory* (1980), p. 61, and in P. Erdős, “On the irrationality of certain series: problems and results,” in *New Advances in Transcendence Theory* (1988), p. 102. The maintained page cites these as [ErGr80] and [Er88c], and attributes the affirmative solution to Nesterenko. Its bibliography points to Nesterenko's 1996 C. R. Acad. Sci. announcement, pp. 909–914; the Sbornik article above is the detailed theorem source used for the reduction. These are classical sources and a classical solution, not a new GCL result.

## What remains

The mathematical question itself is classically settled by Nesterenko. What remains is source- and formalization-boundary work, not a reason to call the original problem open. A responsible formal acceptance would compile the exact external proof commit with its declared Lean and mathlib versions, inspect all dependencies, and report whether any `sorry` or unreported axiom remains. It would also prove the semantic bridge from

$$
\mathrm{HasSum}(n\mapsto\sigma_1(n)/2^n,x)
$$

to the value $(1-P(1/2))/24$, including the $n=0$ convention, the meaning of `σ 1`, the real-versus-complex coercions, and the denominator/exponent convention. Uniqueness of the sum should be explicit rather than silently inferred from a theorem whose hypotheses have not been inspected.

The authenticated return proposes exactly this bounded successor check and expressly stops short of reproving Nesterenko. Until that check is replayed, “formalized in Lean” and “proved (Lean)” remain upstream reported statuses. They must not be rewritten as GCL-certified formal proof. Likewise, the finite computation above is a reproducibility check, not partial evidence for transcendence.
