## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1179](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1179) asks the following. The maintained status is **PROVED**.

Fix $0<\epsilon<1$, an abelian group $G$ of size $N$ and a uniformly random **exactly $k$‑element subset** $A$. Let $F_A(g)$ count all subsets $S\subseteq A$ (including empty) with $\sum_{x\in S}x=g$. Seek minimal random threshold $k=g_\epsilon(N)$ so that, with probability $\to1$ as $N\to\infty$, **all residues simultaneously** satisfy

$$
|F_A(g)-2^k/N|\le\epsilon\,2^k/N.
$$

The phrase “minimal $k$ such that probability tends to 1 as $N\to\infty$” requires a source-normalized asymptotic **function of $N$**, rather than pretending this probability is a fixed‑$N$ property. Also verify uniformity across finite abelian group isomorphism types.

## A small example

In $\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z$, $A=\{1,2,4\}$ has eight subsets. Their sum counts across residues $0,1,\dots,6$ are **$(2,1,1,1,1,1,1)$**. Ideal count is $8/7$; residue-zero relative deviation is $(2-8/7)/(8/7)=3/4$. This deterministic $A$ therefore fails the condition for $\epsilon<3/4$; it is neither a counterexample to a **random high-probability** theorem nor a proof of a sharp threshold.

## What is known

https://www.erdosproblems.com/1179 lists the target $g_\epsilon(N)=(1+o_\epsilon(1))\log_2N$ as **PROVED**. Erdős–Rényi [ErRe65] first obtained an upper $(2+o(1))\log_2N+O_\epsilon(1)$; Erdős–Hall [ErHa76] sharpened to $(1+O_\epsilon(\log\log\log N/\log\log N))\log_2N$, matching the elementary lower bound $\ge\log_2N$ in leading order. Published theorem proof not independently replayed.
