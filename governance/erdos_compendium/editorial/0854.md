## The question in plain language

The product of the first $k$ primes is called the *primorial* $N_k$. Consider all positive integers less than $N_k$ that have no common factor with it. Such integers are called *totatives*. In increasing order they are

$$
1=a_1<a_2<\cdots<a_{\varphi(N_k)}=N_k-1.
$$

Their successive differences $a_{i+1}-a_i$ are the gaps between neighboring totatives. For $k\ge2$, all these gaps are even because every totative is odd. [Erdős Problem #854](https://www.erdosproblems.com/854) asks for estimates of the **smallest positive even number that never occurs** as such a consecutive gap.

It also asks whether the number of *distinct* even gap lengths is at least a fixed positive multiple of the longest actual gap, for all sufficiently large $k$. In symbols, if

$$
G_k=\{a_{i+1}-a_i:1\le i<\varphi(N_k)\},
$$

is it true that $|G_k|\gg\max G_k$? The symbol $\gg$ means that an absolute positive constant works for every sufficiently large $k$. The question concerns only **consecutive** totatives, not arbitrary differences between members of the list.

## A small example

For $k=3$, the primorial is $N_3=2\cdot3\cdot5=30$. Its eight totatives are

$$
1,7,11,13,17,19,23,29.
$$

The consecutive gaps are $6,4,2,4,2,4,6$, so the occurring even lengths are exactly $2,4,6$ and the smallest missing even length is $8$.

A direct greatest-common-divisor enumeration gives the following additional finite examples.

| $k$ | $N_k$ | Largest gap | Smallest missing even gap | Distinct gap lengths |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 30 | 6 | 8 | 3 |
| 4 | 210 | 10 | 12 | 5 |
| 5 | 2310 | 14 | 16 | 7 |
| 6 | 30030 | 22 | 20 | 10 |
| 7 | 510510 | 26 | 28 | 13 |
| 8 | 9699690 | 34 | 32 | 16 |

The sixth and eighth rows are especially instructive: for $k=6$, a gap of $22$ occurs but a gap of $20$ does **not**; for $k=8$, a gap of $34$ occurs but $32$ does not. Thus it is false in general that every even number below the maximum gap must occur. Ten distinct gap sizes occur when $k=6$, and sixteen when $k=8$; neither value settles the asymptotic positive-fraction question.

## What is known

In his [1985 paper](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1985-17.pdf), printed page 80, Erdős explicitly asked for the smallest missing gap among totatives of a primorial. The present problem record also attributes the stronger question about the number of distinct gaps, and questions about where the largest gaps occur, to a separate Oberwolfach problem collection.

The maintained record reports historical calculations attributed to Lacampagne and Selfridge for $N_6=30030$, expressing doubt that all even lengths up to the maximum occur. The finite computation above independently reproduces the underlying missing-length phenomenon, but it does **not** independently verify the historical authorship, date, or wording of that report. The cited Oberwolfach original has not yet been read page by page.

The modern catalogue continues to mark the general questions open. The number of distinct differences is not the number of individual gaps: one gap size can recur at many positions. This distinction matters when comparing $|G_k|$ with $\max G_k$.

## GCL's current state

The [authenticated #854 source audit](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2125#issuecomment-6143246443) checked the exact first question against Erdős's original and enumerated the totatives for several small primorials. It also identified the unverified Oberwolfach citation as a distinct documentary obligation. No protected Lean statement exists for #854 in the frozen Formal Conjectures snapshot used by this cohort.

The enumeration uses only gaps between adjacent members of the finite displayed list. It **does not** insert a cyclic wraparound gap from the last totative back to the first. Neither the sample table nor the original citation supports a general asymptotic estimate.

## What remains

The mathematical problem is to control how many different gap lengths appear as the primorial grows, not merely to find individual missing lengths. A useful next experiment would compare direct enumeration of coprime integers with an independent incremental wheel-sieve algorithm over successive values of $k$. Agreement of the full gap multisets would strengthen the finite evidence, but finite checks alone cannot establish a lower bound for every large $k$.

The source-fidelity boundary is to obtain the exact Oberwolfach volume and page. This would determine which abundance and maximum-gap questions were originally asked there, rather than silently merging them with the narrower 1985 question.
