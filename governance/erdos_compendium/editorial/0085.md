## The question in plain language

A square in graph theory is a *cycle of length four*, not necessarily an induced square. Erdős #85 defines $f(n)$ as the least integer such that every graph on $n$ vertices whose minimum degree is at least $f(n)$ contains a $C_4$. Erdős asks whether $f(n)$ is eventually **nondecreasing**:

$$
f(n+1)\ge f(n)
$$

for every sufficiently large $n$. Knowing that $f(n)$ is approximately $\sqrt n$ does not settle this fine-scale monotonicity question; successive integer values can fluctuate.

## A small example

On four vertices, minimum degree two forces a $C_4$, while a triangle plus an isolated vertex is square-free. Thus $f(4)=2$. On five vertices, the five-cycle has minimum degree two but no square. Consequently $f(5)\ge3$. Since minimum degree three on five vertices forces a square, $f(5)=3$.

The minimum-degree parameter cannot be replaced by average degree, and $C_4$ need not be induced: any four distinct vertices joined in a four-cycle count, even when extra diagonals are present.

## What is known

The conjectured eventual monotonicity is **OPEN**. The maintained problem page notes the relation with the asymmetric Ramsey number $R(C_4,K_{1,t})$ and gives

$$
f(n)=(1+o(1))\sqrt n,\qquad
f(n)<\sqrt n+1.
$$

The asymptotic follows from classical extremal estimates for four-cycle-free graphs and incidence-geometry constructions. These control the global scale but allow possible small downward fluctuations between adjacent integers.

The exact original statement also exists in the protected [Formal Conjectures #85 file at revision 85f86371](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/85.lean). It defines $f(n)$ with an infimum over thresholds $k$ and asserts eventual monotonicity. GCL directly read that source. Its admitted theorem contains a proof hole and is **not** a certified result.

## GCL's current state

The original assignment is [#1354](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1354). The pinned catalogue lists **OPEN**, and the maintained page (6 December 2025) agrees. The direct comparison shows the Lean target has the right overall quantifier order, but there has been no kernel replay, full semantic-equivalence audit or protected-statement admission. A GCL brute-force enumeration of all labelled graphs for $n=4,5,6$ found respectively 54, 548 and 7984 graphs without a four-cycle, and verified $f(4)=2$, $f(5)=3$, $f(6)=3$. The exhaustive fixture is saved in the CHAD QA staging area.

An authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1354#issuecomment-6157120917) is posted on the original issue. It is unadjudicated source evidence, not independent proof certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded independent-replay task is to enumerate all simple graphs on four, five and six vertices, reproducing the GCL fixture. For each, compute minimum degree and test whether any ordered four-tuple of distinct vertices forms a four-cycle. Let $d_n$ be the greatest minimum degree among $C_4$ free graphs; then $f(n)=d_n+1$. Acceptance: a verified graph attaining $d_n$ and a complete exhaustive upper-bound certificate, with independent $f(4)=2$ and $f(5)=3$ checks. Stop at $n=6$ and make no inference about eventual monotonicity.
