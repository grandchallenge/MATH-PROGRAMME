## The question in plain language

Between two consecutive primes $p_j<p_{j+1}$, is there typically an integer that has **no prime factor smaller than the gap length**? [Erdős Problem #682](https://www.erdosproblems.com/682) asks whether for **almost all** prime gaps of length $g=p_{j+1}-p_j$ some integer $m$ strictly between them has $p(m)\geq g$, where $p(m)$ denotes the least prime factor. The answer is **yes**. Ayla Gafni and Terence Tao proved it in 2025–2026.

## A small example

The consecutive primes $7$ and $11$ have gap length $4$. Their interior points are $8,9,10$, whose least prime factors are $2,3,2$, all smaller than $4$. That gap is **exceptional**. The consecutive primes $11$ and $13$ have gap length $2$, with interior integer $12$ and least prime factor $2$. This gap **qualifies**, because the statement uses $\geq$, not strict $>$. A seemingly minor inequality sign changes the treatment of every twin-prime gap.

## What is known

The directly inspected primary paper [Gafni–Tao, *Rough numbers between consecutive primes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06463), Theorem 1.1, proves that if $N(X)$ counts exceptional prime gaps whose left endpoint lies in $[X,2X]$, then $N(X)\ll X/(\log X)^2$. The prime number theorem gives about $X/\log X$ primes in that interval, so the exceptional proportion is $O(1/\log X)$ and tends to zero. This proves Erdős's **almost-all** question. The paper also proves, assuming a precise form of the Dickson–Hardy–Littlewood prime tuples conjecture, an asymptotic $N(X)\sim cX/(\log X)^2$ for a positive constant $c$. That stronger formula is **conditional**. It remains unknown unconditionally whether infinitely many exceptional gaps exist. Erdős suggested such exceptions via arithmetic progressions with endpoints $2183+30030d$ and $2201+30030d$, conditional on the endpoints being primes; $d=0$ is not itself a pair of consecutive primes.
