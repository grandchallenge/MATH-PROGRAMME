## The question in plain language

Let $A$ be an **infinite** subset of the positive integers. Suppose every integer $n$ has **at most two representations** as a sum $n=a+b$ with $a,b\in A$ and $a\le b$. Such a set is usually called a $B_2[2]$ set, generalizing Sidon sets, for which each integer has at most one representation.

Erdős, Sárközy and Sós asked whether every such $A$ must have unusually sparse initial intervals along an unbounded sequence of cutoffs:

$$
\liminf_{N\to\infty}
\frac{|A\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}|}{\sqrt N}=0.
$$

This is a **liminf**, not a full limit: it only demands arbitrarily large sparse cutoffs. The constant two bounds unordered representations including diagonals.

## A small example

The infinite condition is essential: every finite set has the displayed liminf equal to zero because its numerator eventually stays constant. On a finite prefix, construct a representation histogram by enumerating all pairs $a\le b$ and counting their sums. A candidate passes the $B_2[2]$ test if no histogram count exceeds two.

The set $\{1,2,4\}$ is already a Sidon set and hence a $B_2[2]$ example. However, extending this finite set without ever creating a third representation is the actual challenge.

## What is known

The $B_2[2]$ problem remains **OPEN**. For strict Sidon sets ($B_2[1]$), Erdős proved the corresponding liminf-zero theorem. Replacing one allowed representation by two changes the problem substantially: a finite example or a statement about average density is insufficient.

Javier Cilleruelo, [*Sidon basis*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.5351), constructs an infinite $B_2[2]$ set that is also an asymptotic basis of order three. This does **not** settle the question here; an additive basis property alone gives no precise lower bound on $|A\cap[1,N]|/\sqrt N$ along every sufficiently large cutoff. The later genuine Sidon basis of order three constructed by Pilatte (#157) likewise does not answer the $B_2[2]$ liminf question.

## GCL's current state

The pinned and maintained [Erdős #158](https://www.erdosproblems.com/158) classify the question **OPEN**. Original GCL issue: [#1427](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1427). The older $B_2[1]$ theorem and Cilleruelo construction are source-attributed; neither is presented as resolving the $B_2[2]$ statement.

The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1427#issuecomment-6167537751). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded task enumerates exact representation histograms for finite subsets of $[1,18]$ and classifies $B_2[1]$ versus $B_2[2]$. Acceptance: include diagonal sums, verify at most two **unordered** representations for each integer, and exhibit an explicit finite set that is $B_2[2]$ but not Sidon. Record maximal prefix cardinalities only as finite evidence. Stop before claiming anything about all infinite $B_2[2]$ sets.
