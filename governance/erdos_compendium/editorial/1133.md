## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1133](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1133) asks for **robustly difficult** polynomial interpolation data. For any $C>0$, seek $\varepsilon>0$ such that for every sufficiently large $n$ and every configuration $x_1,\ldots,x_n\in[-1,1]$ there exist target values $y_i\in[-1,1]$ forcing any polynomial $P$ of degree

$$
\deg P<(1+\varepsilon)n
$$

which matches at least $(1-\varepsilon)n$ of the indexed data to satisfy $\max_{[-1,1]}|P|>C$.

The order of quantifiers is crucial: the data are chosen *after* the nodes, and the polynomial may ignore some of those values.

## A small example

For nodes $-1,0,1$, every three values $|y_i|\le1$ have a degree-at-most-two interpolant. By the exact three-node Lebesgue constant,

$$
|P(x)|\le\sum_{i=1}^{3}|\ell_i(x)|\le\frac54.
$$

For this finite $n=3$ situation, one cannot force norm greater than $C>5/4$ when exact interpolation with degree two is allowed. This **does not refute** a claim stated only for sufficiently large $n$.

## What is known

Erdős proved a weaker high-norm interpolation phenomenon involving a degree-$n$ polynomial bounded at about $(1+\varepsilon)n$ given nodes. The robust converse remains listed **open**.

An April 2026 comment reports a proposed solution via **Beurling interpolation in Bernstein spaces**, with an angular substitution $x=\cos\theta$. The online comment and favorable informal review are **not independent proof certification**. The frozen formal-conjectures file labels the main statement open, with an unproved theorem, and a separate weaker known result. Neither a claimed AI-generated proof nor a finite calculation justifies changing the open status.
