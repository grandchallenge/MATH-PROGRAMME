## The question in plain language

chi_S(n,e,H) is the MINIMUM number r of edge colors for which SOME n-vertex graph with e edges admits a coloring in which EVERY copy of H is RAINBOW (all its edges distinct colors). For e=floor(n²/4)+1 and H=C_(2k+1), Erdős asks chi_S~n²/8 for all k>=3.

The original k>=3 conjecture covers odd cycles of length at least seven. The 2026 theorem covers k>=4, namely cycles of length at least nine. The outstanding k=3 case is C7. The known anomalous answers for triangles (k=1) and C5 (k=2) fall outside the original quantifier range.

## A small example

A triangle C3 has three edges: to make any triangle rainbow takes at least three colors (if a triangle occurs). For n sufficiently large with e>floor(n²/4), a triangle is guaranteed by Mantel's theorem. Coloring with three colors so that all triangles are rainbow is achievable in suitable n-vertex/e-edge graphs according to cited extremum; verifying existence is distinct from counting triangle edges in isolation.

This finite fixture tests the conventions. It does not certify any of the asymptotic or all-graphs assertions.

## What is known

Maintained #809 OPEN overall: Bucić–Chen–Ma [BCM26] prove asymptotic for every k>=4 (cycle length >=9); k=3 is C7, still not covered. Separately k=1 (C3) chi_S=3, and k=2 (C5) chi_S=floor(n/2)+3 for sufficiently large n by Erdős–Simonovits as reported in [BEGS89]. These smaller k are OUTSIDE original k>=3 quantification. Original BCM26 and BEGS89 full papers not independently inspected. Do not call entire problem proved.

The distinctions between established partial theorems, original open parts, and disproved universal claims have been preserved. Historical publications are attributed, rather than presented as new GCL mathematics.

The stated target, for $k\geq3$, is

$$

\chi_S\left(n,\left\lfloor n^2/4\right\rfloor+1,
C_{2k+1}\right)\sim\frac{n^2}{8}.

$$

Bucić–Chen–Ma have proved this for $k\geq4$. The excluded case $k=3$ is precisely the seven-cycle. The triangle and five-cycle are indexed by $k=1,2$ and are outside the original quantifier.
