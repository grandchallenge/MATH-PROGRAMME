## The question in plain language

Colour each edge of the complete graph on the integers $\{2,\ldots,N\}$ red or blue. A subset $X$ is a *monochromatic clique* when **every edge joining two of its members** has one common colour.

Erdős asked whether, for **every prescribed constant $C>0$** and all sufficiently large $N$, every two-colouring contains a monochromatic clique $X$ with

$$
\sum_{x\in X}\frac1{\log x}\ge C.
$$

The logarithms are natural; the domain starts at two to avoid division by $\log1=0$. The weight is on **vertices**, although the colours are on edges.

## A small example

For $X=\{2,3,4\}$, the three edges must all be red or all blue. If just one edge differs in colour, $X$ is not monochromatic even though two of its three edges agree. Its vertex weight is $1/\log2+1/\log3+1/\log4$.

The statement is asymptotic in $N$. A monochromatic clique of logarithmic cardinality drawn entirely from very large labels may still have small reciprocal-logarithm weight.

## What is known

**PROVED.** Rödl (2003) first established that the maximum forced weight tends to infinity as $N\to\infty$, answering the qualitative question. Conlon, Fox and Sudakov, [*Two extensions of Ramsey's theorem*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.1548), *Duke Mathematical Journal* 162 (2013), 2903–2927, determined the sharp order:

$$
\min_{\text{red-blue colourings}}\ \max_{\text{monochromatic }X}
\sum_{x\in X}\frac1{\log x}
=\Theta(\log\log\log N).
$$

Their Theorem 1.1 supplies the explicit lower bound $2^{-8}\log\log\log N$ for sufficiently large $N$. Rödl's colouring gives the matching-order upper bound. The same source shows the corresponding universal claim fails for **three** edge colours: a 3-colouring can keep these weights uniformly bounded.

The maintained [Erdős #191](https://www.erdosproblems.com/191) classifies it **PROVED**. A two-colour result must not be transferred to three colours, and the upper construction must not be misreported as a lower bound.

## What remains

A bounded successor enumerates all two-colourings of the edges of $K_N$ for $N\le6$, calculates the maximum reciprocal-logarithm vertex weight over monochromatic cliques, and computes the minimum over colourings. Acceptance: check every pairwise edge of each clique and compute weights from labels rather than clique size alone. Stop at finite numerical fixtures; no proof of the iterated-logarithm asymptotic follows from enumeration.
