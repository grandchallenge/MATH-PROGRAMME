## The question in plain language

Count labelled ordered k×n Latin rectangles on n symbols: each row a permutation, and entries within a column all distinct. Erdős asks for a general asymptotic formula as n grows, particularly when k increases with n; the maintained page remains OPEN. A catalogue comment correctly warns that OEIS A001009 records a reduced/normalized count, not this fully labelled $L_{k,n}$.

## A small example

For k=1 there are n! rectangles. For k=2, after choosing the first row in n! ways, the second row must be a derangement relative to it, giving $L_{2,n}=n!\,D_n$. At n=3 the number is 6×2=12, whereas (3!)²=36 would ignore column collisions. This is an exact combinatorial count.

## What is known

Erdős–Kaplansky 1946 found $L_{k,n}\sim e^{-\binom{k}{2}}(n!)^k$ when k is much smaller than (log n)^(3/2). Yamamoto widened the range. PRIMARY publisher Godsil–McKay, 'Asymptotic enumeration of Latin rectangles', Journal of Combinatorial Theory B 48 (1990) 19–44, DOI 10.1016/0095-8956(90)90128-M, proved the asymptotic

$$
L_{k,n}\sim(n!)^k((n)_k/n^k)^n(1-k/n)^{-n/2}e^{-k/2}.
$$

The theorem applies in the range $k=o(n^{6/7})$. NEW external preprint by Eric Li, 'The Godsil–McKay Asymptotic for Latin Rectangles in the Sublinear Range of Erdős Problem 725', arXiv:2608.01671 (August 2026), claims an extension to the range

$$
k=o(n)
$$

with a corrected exponential factor

$$
\exp\{[n(H_n-H_{n-k})-k]/2\}
$$

and log-relative error $O(k^2/n^2)$, reported formalized in Lean. This later result is primary author abstract evidence, not a GCL-replayed kernel certificate or a proof for k comparable to n.
