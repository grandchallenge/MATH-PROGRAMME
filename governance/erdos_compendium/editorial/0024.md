## The question in plain language

How many different cycles of length five can a triangle-free graph contain? [Erdős Problem #24](https://www.erdosproblems.com/24) asks whether a triangle-free graph with exactly $5n$ vertices has at most $n^5$ different pentagons.

The answer is **yes**, proved independently in 2012 and 2013. A *different cycle* here means a different undirected five-cycle; rotating or reversing a listing of its vertices does **not** create another pentagon.

## A small example

Begin with a five-cycle. Replace each vertex with a class of $n$ independent vertices. Join all pairs of vertices between neighbouring classes, and none between non-neighbouring classes.

A five-cycle in this *blow-up* uses one vertex from each class, so there are exactly

$$
n\times n\times n\times n\times n=n^5
$$

different pentagons. The construction remains triangle-free because an edge must join neighbouring classes of the original pentagon, which contains no triangle. It shows the claimed upper bound would be best possible.

## What is known

Andrzej Grzesik, *On the maximum number of five-cycles in a triangle-free graph*, *Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B* **102** (2012), 1061–1066, proves

$$
\#C_5(G)\le\left(\frac{|V(G)|}{5}\right)^5
$$

for every triangle-free graph, using flag algebras. An independent proof by Hatami, Hladký, Král, Norine and Razborov also characterizes equality in terms of the balanced pentagon blow-up.

Substituting $|V(G)|=5n$ gives exactly Erdős's proposed $n^5$ bound. More general questions for longer odd cycles are separate problems.

The old admitted GCL formal-provider snapshot contains no Problem #24 statement; an external registry's reported Lean verification has not been replayed by GCL.

## What remains

Nothing remains to settle this five-cycle inequality in the mathematical literature. Further work would concern a reproducible formal or flag-algebra proof, the exact equality statement or genuinely separate longer-odd-cycle questions.
