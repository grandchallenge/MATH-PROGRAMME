## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1156](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1156). How tightly can the chromatic number of a binomial random graph concentrate, and how strongly must it spread out?

$$G\sim G(n,\tfrac12),\qquad\chi(G)=\text{chromatic number of }G.$$

G∼G(n,1/2), with independently chosen edges. Ask whether some UNIVERSAL constant C has chi(G) concentrated on at most C integer values asymptotically. A second formulation asks whether for every arbitrary centering f(n) and slowly divergent omega(n), probability |chi(G)-f(n)|<omega(n) is <1/2 for all sufficiently large n. The two questions have different quantifiers; avoid calling them logically identical.

## A small example

Exhaustive enumeration of all 8 graphs on 3 vertices gives chromatic distribution chi=1 with count1, chi=2 count6, chi=3 count1. All 64 labelled graphs on 4 vertices give histogram chi1:1, chi2:40, chi3:22, chi4:1. These exact finite distributions are not asymptotic concentration evidence.

## What is known

https://www.erdosproblems.com/1156 OPEN [AlSp92],[Va99,3.6]. Bollobás [Bo88] chi(G)∼n/(2log_2 n) whp; Shamir–Spencer [ShSp87] concentration within much larger widths. Heckel [He21] proves whp concentration width omega(n) must exceed n^c infinitely often for every c<1/4, improved by Heckel–Riordan [HeRi23] to every c<1/2. These polynomial lower bounds on WHP windows do not literally imply the separately quantified <1/2 anti-concentration assertion. Review the exact 2023 theorem conditions.
