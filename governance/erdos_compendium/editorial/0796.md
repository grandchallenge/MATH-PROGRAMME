## The question in plain language

For fixed k>=2 let g_k(n) be the maximal size A subset [1,n] such that each integer m has fewer than k unordered representations a1*a2=m with DISTINCT elements a1<a2 in A. Is there a second-order constant c for g_3(n) after the leading n loglog n/log n?

**Source-normalization hold:** the older log-squared denominator conflicts with the scale inferred from the 1964 arguments. Do not assert a second-order constant or silently select one convention until historical papers are inspected.

## A small example

A={2,3,5,7} has all 6 unordered distinct pair products 6,10,14,15,21,35, so each m occurs once, meeting k=2 and k=3. A={2,3,4,6} has 2*6=3*4=12, so it meets k=3 (at most two representations) but violates k=2. This precisely tests strict <k.

The calculation illustrates the exact definitions and hypotheses. No single finite example establishes the reported asymptotic assertion.

## What is known

Live /latex/796 records Erdős [Er64d] known first-order: if 2^(r-1)<k<=2^r then g_k(n)~n (loglog n)^(r-1)/((r-1)! log n). For k=3 r=2.
