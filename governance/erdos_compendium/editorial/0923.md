## The question in plain language

A graph is **triangle-free** if it has no three distinct vertices that are pairwise adjacent. Triangle-free graphs can nevertheless have arbitrarily large chromatic number, meaning they require arbitrarily many colors to color their vertices properly.

Erdős asked whether this phenomenon must occur **inside every sufficiently high-chromatic graph**. More precisely, for every positive integer $k$, is there a finite number $f(k)$ such that every graph $G$ with

$$
\chi(G)\ge f(k)
$$

contains a triangle-free **subgraph** $H$ with $\chi(H)\ge k$?

This is [Erdős Problem #923](https://www.erdosproblems.com/923). It was **proved** by Vojtěch Rödl in 1977. The subgraph need **not be induced**: edges may be discarded while retaining their endpoints. That seemingly small detail is central to the theorem.

## A small example

The five-cycle contains no triangle and has chromatic number three. The complete graph $K_5$, although filled with triangles, contains a copy of that five-cycle **as a subgraph**: keep just five edges forming a cycle and delete all other edges. Thus $K_5$ contains a triangle-free subgraph requiring three colors.

However, **no induced triangle-free subgraph of $K_5$** has chromatic number three. Every induced subgraph of a complete graph is another complete graph; an induced triangle-free one has at most two vertices and needs at most two colors. Hence the theorem cannot be changed silently from *subgraph* to *induced subgraph*.

The situation is qualitatively different from constructing one triangle-free graph with large chromatic number from scratch. Rödl proved that **every** sufficiently difficult-to-color ambient graph contains an appropriate triangle-free structure, despite arbitrary existing triangles.

## What is known

Vojtěch Rödl, [*On the chromatic number of subgraphs of a given graph*](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1977-0469806-4), *Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society* **64** (1977), no.2, pp.370–371, proves a stronger two-parameter theorem. His original [two-page article text](https://paperzz.com/doc/7151410/on-the-chromatic-number-of-subgraphs-of-a-given-graph) records the proof, including its induction and final edge partition; the [author's publication list](https://www.math.emory.edu/~rodl/publications.html) and the [journal bibliographic record](https://portal.mardi4nfdi.de/wiki/On_the_Chromatic_Number_of_Subgraphs_of_a_Given_Graph) independently confirm authorship, date and pages.

For all positive integers $m,n$, there is an integer $\Phi(m,n)$ such that every graph with chromatic number at least $\Phi(m,n)$ contains **either** a complete graph $K_m$ **or** a triangle-free subgraph of chromatic number at least $n$.

Rödl's theorem resolves #923 because arbitrarily high-chromatic finite triangle-free graphs were already known, for example from the Mycielski construction. Given a desired chromatic number $k$, choose a triangle-free graph $T$ with at least $k$ colors and let $m$ be its number of vertices. Every graph above Rödl's threshold $\Phi(m,k)$ contains either a triangle-free high-chromatic subgraph directly, or a complete graph on $m$ vertices, **which contains a copy of $T$ as a non-induced subgraph**. Both cases give the desired result.

The first paragraph of Rödl's original paper explicitly recognizes this derivation. Its edge-partition method gives a finite recursive upper bound on $\Phi$, though the author does not claim it is sharp.

A related but stronger Erdős–Hajnal problem asks for a high-chromatic subgraph with **arbitrarily large prescribed girth**, excluding all short cycles rather than just triangles. Rödl's 1977 theorem establishes the girth-at-least-four case, not a full answer for every girth. The [2021 Mohar–Wu paper](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/8E6011978AB76FCB7D4762508379D542/S0963548321000250a.pdf/triangle-free-subgraphs-with-large-fractional-chromatic-number.pdf) also treats an analogue involving fractional chromatic number, not a replacement proof for all possible girths.

The maintained [catalogue #923](https://www.erdosproblems.com/923) is marked **PROVED (LEAN)** and has April 2026 discussion comments announcing independent Lean developments, one with Aristotle assistance. GCL has not downloaded and compiled those exact developments against pinned Lean and Mathlib dependencies or inspected their axiom reports. They are external formalization evidence, not a GCL kernel certificate.

## GCL's current state

The elementary **edge-partition induction** in Rödl's paper is precise enough to separate into small mathematical lemmas. Fix $n\ge2$. Start with the threshold

$$
\Phi(2,n)=2,
$$

and for $m\ge3$ define the explicitly finite bound

$$
\Phi(m,n)=(n-1)^{\Phi(m-1,n)-1}+1.
$$

Assume by induction that the two-parameter theorem holds for $m-1$. Given a graph $G$ containing no $K_m$, order its vertices arbitrarily. For each vertex $v$, consider its **left neighborhood**: the earlier vertices adjacent to $v$, with the edges they inherit from $G$. It contains no $K_{m-1}$, or adjoining $v$ would produce a $K_m$.

If any such left neighborhood has chromatic number at least $\Phi(m-1,n)$, induction yields a triangle-free subgraph requiring at least $n$ colors. Otherwise, color each left neighborhood with at most

$$
P=\Phi(m-1,n)-1
$$

colors. Assign every edge $uv$, with $u$ earlier than $v$, to **the bucket indexed by the local color of $u$ in the left neighborhood of $v$**. Each bucket is triangle-free. Indeed, in a hypothetical triangle $u<v<w$, both edges $uw$ and $vw$ would belong to the same bucket only if adjacent vertices $u,v$ received the same color in the left neighborhood of $w$, contradicting proper coloring.

If every bucket graph required at most $n-1$ colors, then the **product coloring** of its $P$ color coordinates would color all of $G$ with at most $(n-1)^P$ colors. But our assumed chromatic number is at least $(n-1)^P+1$. Thus some bucket must contain the required triangle-free, high-chromatic subgraph. The induction is complete. This reconstruction matches the central logical mechanism of Rödl's printed pp.370–371; it does not claim a new theorem.

The executable fixture at governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_922_923.py checks the $m=4,n=3$ version of this split across **28,658 labelled graphs containing no $K_4$**, of orders at most six. Exactly **12** instances trigger the higher-chromatic left-neighborhood branch. In the remaining **28,646** cases the script actually colors every left neighborhood with two colors and builds the two edge buckets, verifying that both buckets are triangle-free and their combination contains every original edge exactly once. It also computes finite recursive thresholds and checks the coloring-product bound. This evidence is a bounded independent verification of the proof mechanism.

## What remains

A bounded formalization task is to prove the **left-neighborhood edge-partition lemma**. Inputs: a finite simple graph with a fixed linear vertex order and, for each vertex, a proper coloring of its earlier-neighbor graph using at most $P$ colors. Output: a partition of all original edges into exactly $P$ triangle-free edge sets. Acceptance requires a complete proof or pinned no-placeholder Lean code with a literal axiom report and reproduction of the 28,646 explicit two-bucket finite examples. A triangle surviving in any bucket, or an edge assigned twice or not assigned, falsifies the lemma.

The next elementary lemma is the **product-coloring inequality**: if all of the $P$ edge-disjoint layers on the same vertex set have chromatic number at most $q$, the union has chromatic number at most $q^P$. Use it and the left-neighborhood partition to formalize the recurrence for $\Phi(m,n)$, taking care of the difference between chromatic number **at least** $n$ and **exactly** $n$. If desired, a subgraph with chromatic number exactly $n$ may be obtained from a higher-chromatic graph by a vertex-minimal subgraph or suitable edge deletion, with its own proof obligation.

Finally, to infer #923 explicitly, give a source-locked construction of an arbitrarily high-chromatic finite triangle-free graph (for example, iterated Mycielski graphs), and embed it as a **non-induced** subgraph of a sufficiently large clique. Only after all these steps should the full result be declared independently reproduced in a formal system. The current historical mathematical status is already PROVED, independent of further GCL formalization.
