## The question in plain language

Imagine a large collection of entire functions. At each individual point $z$, the functions take only a limited number of **different values**. Does that force the collection of functions itself to be small?

[Erdős Problem #1119](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1119), a version of *Wetzel's problem*, asks this when the allowed number of values is an infinite cardinal $\mathfrak m$ with

$$
\aleph_0<\mathfrak m<\mathfrak c=2^{\aleph_0}.
$$

At every $z\in\mathbb C$, the set $\{f(z):f\in\mathcal F\}$ must have cardinality at most $\mathfrak m$. Must $\mathcal F$ itself have cardinality at most $\mathfrak m$?

**The answer is independent of ZFC** in a specified boundary case. This is a result of set theory, not merely an unsolved analytic estimate.

## A small example

Seven distinct entire functions

$$
f_j(z)=z+j,\qquad 0\le j<7
$$

take seven distinct values at every point, and there are seven functions. This illustrates the pointwise-counting condition in a finite setting.

It says nothing about independence: finite collections cannot reproduce the set-theoretic phenomena involving the cardinality of the continuum.

## What is known

Erdős first studied the countable-values version of Wetzel's question in 1964. The maintained catalogue reports an affirmative result for the more general case $\mathfrak m^+<\mathfrak c$.

In the boundary case $\mathfrak m^+=\mathfrak c$, the outcome depends on the set-theoretic universe. Published work cited by the catalogue establishes two different models of ZFC having

$$
\mathfrak c=\aleph_2,\qquad\mathfrak m=\aleph_1:
$$

**Kumar and Shelah (2017)** constructed a model where the answer is *yes*; **Schilhan and Weinert (2024)** constructed one where the answer is *no*.

Assuming the appropriate relative-consistency hypotheses, these opposing models establish that ZFC alone cannot decide the question in that regime. This does not mean both answers hold in the same mathematical universe.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source audit #2390](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2390#issuecomment-6169278413) identifies the base theory, the continuum cardinal boundary and the opposing results.

The [frozen Lean statement](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1119.lean) uses a placeholder for the headline truth value and includes unproved declarations. Its “solved” category denotes the external independence resolution, **not** a proof of either global answer in ZFC.

## What remains

Review the actual forcing constructions and relative-consistency hypotheses in the 2017 and 2024 papers. Check the exact meaning of the pointwise cardinality and the distinction between $\mathfrak m^+<\mathfrak c$ and $\mathfrak m^+=\mathfrak c$. A rigorous editorial account should present the logical dependency diagram rather than falsely select yes or no.
