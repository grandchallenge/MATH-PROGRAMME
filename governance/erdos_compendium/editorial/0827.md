## The question in plain language

Define n_k as least N such that EVERY N-point planar set in general position has a k-point subset whose binom(k,3) triples all have pairwise DISTINCT circumradii. All triples are noncollinear; exact general-position conditions (no three collinear vs no four cocircular) must be preserved for both source and problem. Estimate sharp n_k.

## A small example

Four points (0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,2) have no collinear triples and their squared circumradii for all four triples are exactly 1/2,5/2,5/4,1, all distinct. This is an existence EXAMPLE not a guarantee for every four points. Squared radii compare exactly as rational numbers.

The example can be checked exactly. A finite witness does not replace the universal, asymptotic, or infinitely-often quantifiers in the original question.

## What is known

Maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/827 OPEN for optimal size. Erdős 1978 finiteness argument and proposed bound k+2 binom(k-1,2)binom(k-1,3) was flawed. Martínez and Roldán-Pensado [MaRo15], *Points defining triangles with distinct circumradii*, Acta Math Hungar (2015), 136–141, correct and prove n_k=O(k^9) and n_4<=9,n_5<=37 under a weak general position assumption; an independent probabilistic argument in catalogue comments claims O(k^5), requiring proof readback. Sharp order remains OPEN. Formal n_k 'exists' is NOT open.

For three noncollinear points $P,Q,R$ with squared side lengths $u,v,w$ and twice the signed triangle area represented by determinant $\Delta$, the squared circumradius is the exact rational expression

$$

\rho^2=\frac{uvw}{4\Delta^2}.

$$

Comparing squared radii avoids square roots and numerical tolerances. A concrete four-point sample has four distinct squared radii; this demonstrates possibility, not the universal selection threshold. The corrected 2015 finiteness proof and the newer comment-based $O(k^5)$ claim have different evidence status.
