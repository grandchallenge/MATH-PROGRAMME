## The question in plain language

Instead of measuring the **largest** polynomial interpolation amplification, measure the total *squared* amplification across the whole interval.

[Erdős Problem #1131](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1131) asks which nodes $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ minimize

$$
I(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=
\int_{-1}^{1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\ell_k(x)|^2\,dx,
$$

with the usual Lagrange basis polynomials. The specific asymptotic conjecture asks whether

$$
\min I=2-\frac{1+o(1)}n.
$$

The integrand is $\sum|\ell_k|^2$, **not** $(\sum|\ell_k|)^2$. Squaring the sum would introduce cross terms and change the problem.

## A small example

For nodes $-1,1$, the basis functions are $(1-x)/2$ and $(1+x)/2$, giving

$$
\sum_{k=1}^2|\ell_k(x)|^2=\frac{x^2+1}{2}.
$$

Thus

$$
I(-1,1)=\int_{-1}^1\frac{x^2+1}{2}\,dx
=\frac43.
$$

This exact value is for one selected node pair; the finite calculation does not independently certify global minimality over all pairs or the conjectured first-order constant.

## What is known

Erdős initially proposed certain nodes associated with the integrated Legendre polynomial as exact minimizers, inspired by Fejér's work on a **maximal** squared norm. **Szabados (1966)** disproved that exact minimizer proposal for every $n>3$.

Erdős, Szabados, Varma and Vértesi (1994) established bounds of the form

$$
2-O\!\left(\frac{(\log n)^2}{n}\right)
\le\min I\le
2-\frac{2}{2n-1}.
$$

As of the maintained catalogue's formal classification, the sharp first-order asymptotic remains **open**.

Comments posted in April 2026 contain competing claims of a disproof, a revised preprint with conditional endpoint-universality arguments, and numerical evidence. These are **not independently adjudicated published theorem proofs**. They must not be used to overwrite the catalogue's OPEN classification without mathematical review.
