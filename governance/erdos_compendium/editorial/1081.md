## The question in plain language

A positive integer is **squarefull** when every prime factor occurs to exponent at least two. Thus $4$, $8$, $9$ and $36$ qualify, but $12=2^2\cdot3$ does not. The number $1$ qualifies vacuously.

[Erdős Problem #1081](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1081) asks how many **distinct integers** at most $x$ can be expressed as a sum of two positive squarefull integers. Writing this number as $A(x)$, Erdős guessed

$$
A(x)\sim c\frac{x}{\sqrt{\log x}}
$$

for some positive constant $c$. That guess is false.

## A small example

The first examples include $2=1+1$, $5=1+4$, $8=4+4$, $9=1+8$, and $10=1+9$.

Exact trial factorization finds 25 squarefull positive integers through $250$, including $1$, and 151 distinct sums of two such numbers at most $250$. Therefore $A(250)=151$ under this explicit convention. Counting multiple representations of the same integer would answer a different question.

## What is known

**Odoni (1981) disproved the proposed asymptotic.** Later work sharpened the counting estimates. A published article by Valentin Blomer, [*Binary Quadratic Forms with Large Discriminants and Sums of Two Squareful Numbers II*](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0024610704006040), *Journal of the London Mathematical Society* 71 (2005), 69–84, states in its author abstract that

$$
A(x)=x(\log x)^{-\alpha+o(1)},
\qquad
\alpha=1-2^{-1/3}\approx0.2063.
$$

The maintained catalogue attributes a more refined logarithmic-factor estimate to Blomer and Granville (2006). The correct exponent is not $1/2$.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source audit #2352](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2352#issuecomment-6160837068) checked the published author abstract, distinguished the 2005 Blomer result from the 2006 Blomer–Granville refinement, and independently enumerated $A(250)$.

GCL has not replayed the original analytic proof. No finite table proves a logarithmic asymptotic.

## What remains

Review Odoni's original theorem and both later papers, including the correct powers of $\log\log x$, the convention concerning $1$, and whether the source counts distinct integers or representations. Extend finite enumeration only as a reproducibility check.
