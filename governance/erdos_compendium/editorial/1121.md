## The question in plain language

Suppose several circles lie in the plane and cannot be divided into two nonempty groups by a straight line that avoids all of them.

[Erdős Problem #1121](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1121) asserts that these circles can all fit inside a larger disk whose radius is no greater than the **sum of their radii**:

$$
R\le\sum_{i=1}^n r_i.
$$

The condition is called *nonseparability*. It does **not** require every pair of circles to intersect; rather, no single line missing every circle may separate the whole collection into two nonempty subcollections.

The theorem was proved by Goodman and Goodman (1945).

## A small example

For just two filled disks with radii $r_1,r_2$ and center separation $d$, nonseparability means the disks overlap or touch:

$$
d\le r_1+r_2.
$$

Their minimal enclosing disk has radius

$$
R_{\min}=\max\left\{r_1,r_2,\frac{d+r_1+r_2}{2}\right\}.
$$

Under the nonseparability condition, each term is at most $r_1+r_2$, proving the required inequality **for two disks**.

For example, radii $2$ and $3$ at center distance $4$ have a smallest enclosing radius $9/2$, which is less than the sum $5$.

The exact two-disk formula is independently checked with rational arithmetic. It does not establish the general theorem for a nonseparable family of arbitrarily many disks.

## What is known

The maintained catalogue credits **Goodman and Goodman (1945)** with the original affirmative theorem, which also generalizes to higher dimensions.

Later work includes a convex-body generalization by **Hadwiger (1947)** and an alternative proof by **Bezdek and Litvak (2016)**.

In April 2026 the catalogue was updated to **PROVED (LEAN)** after a reported Aristotle formalization of the Goodman–Goodman result. This formal proof has **not** been independently downloaded and kernel-replayed by GCL; a label or external discussion is not itself a certificate.
