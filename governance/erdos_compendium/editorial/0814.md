## The question in plain language

For $k\ge2$ and n sufficiently large for the hypotheses to be feasible, an n-vertex graph with at least $(k-1)(n-k+2)+\binom{k-2}{2}+1$ edges must contain an INDUCED subgraph on <=(1-c_k)n vertices with minimum degree >=k, for some fixed c_k>0. This is a statement about induced k-cores and a linear vertex saving, not merely existence of any k-core.

## A small example

Take k=3,n=5. The edge threshold is 2(5-1)+1=9. The graph K5 with one missing edge has 9 edges. Removing one endpoint of the missing edge leaves an induced K4 with 4 vertices and minimum degree 3. This checks an instance, not uniform c_k over all n.

## What is known

Maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/814 and web source read 2026-10-14: EFRS [EFRS90] first obtained n-c_k sqrt(n) vertices; Mousset–Noever–Skorić [MNS17] obtained n-c_k n/log n; Lisa Sauermann [Sa19], *A proof of a conjecture of Erdős, Faudree, Rousseau and Schelp on subgraphs of minimum degree k*, JCTB 2019, 36–75, proves full statement with c_k>>1/k^3. The original full proof is not GCL-replayed.

The precise threshold is

$$
e(G)\geq (k-1)(n-k+2)+\binom{k-2}{2}+1.
$$

The conclusion asks for an INDUCED subgraph with minimum degree at least $k$ using no more than $(1-c_k)n$ vertices. Sauermann's result has a saving parameter of order at least $1/k^3$. Replacing the induced requirement with an arbitrary edge-deleted subgraph would not justify the theorem as stated.

For clarity, the exact edge condition and target vertex saving are

$$
e(G)\geq(k-1)(n-k+2)+\binom{k-2}{2}+1,
$$


$$
|V(H)|\leq(1-c_k)n,\qquad \delta(H)\geq k,
$$

where $H$ is an **induced** subgraph. Sauermann's established source reports a positive $c_k$ at least of order $1/k^3$. Earlier savings only grew like $\sqrt n$ or $n/\log n$.
