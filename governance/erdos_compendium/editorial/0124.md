## The question in plain language

For integers $d\ge3$ and $k\ge0$, let $P(d,k)$ consist of numbers formed by sums of **distinct powers** $d^j$ with $j\ge k$. Thus $P(d,0)$ is the set of nonnegative integers whose digits in base $d$ are only zero or one; $P(d,k)$ contains multiples of $d^k$ formed with the same digit restriction.

For a finite set of distinct bases $3\le d_1<\cdots<d_r$, consider the reciprocal condition

$$
\sum_{i=1}^r\frac1{d_i-1}\ge1.
$$

The first question asks whether every sufficiently large integer belongs to the sumset $P(d_1,0)+\cdots+P(d_r,0)$. The **second**, harder question adds $\gcd(d_1,\ldots,d_r)=1$ and asks for the same conclusion with $P(d_i,k)$ for every fixed $k\ge1$.

## A small example

The bases $3,4,7$ satisfy

$$
\frac1{3-1}+\frac1{4-1}+\frac1{7-1}
=\frac12+\frac13+\frac16=1.
$$

Burr, Erdős, Graham and Li proved the positive-shift $k\ge1$ result for exactly these bases. This is a special case, not a solution for all admissible finite lists.

If the bases have common divisor $g>1$, every element of $P(d_i,k)$ for $k\ge1$ is divisible by $g$, so the sumset cannot represent all sufficiently large integers. This proves why the gcd hypothesis in the second question matters.

## What is known

The **first** version with $k=0$ has a reported affirmative Lean proof by an Aristotle agent coordinated by Boris Alexeev; the **second**, with $k\ge1$ and coprime bases, remains **OPEN** in the maintained catalogue. The 1996 primary paper is Burr, Erdős, Graham and Li, [*Complete sequences of sets of integer powers*](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-77-2-133-138), *Acta Arithmetica* 77, 133–138. The source's stronger example $(3,4,7)$ should not be confused with the full conjecture.

**Source transcription defect:** the live [Erdős #124](https://www.erdosproblems.com/124), edited December 2025, prints $d_r$ in the denominator of every term of the introductory sum. Its own explanatory paragraph instead uses $d_i$, and the **protected [Formal Conjectures 124 declaration](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/124.lean)** independently records the indexed sum with $d_i$. These are different mathematical conditions: for $(3,4,7)$ the printed repeated-last-base sum is $3/(7-1)=1/2$, whereas the indexed sum is one. The indexed version is used here but the discrepancy must remain recorded.

The protected formal declaration contains separate branches for zero and nonzero digit cutoffs; its declarations carry proof holes. Reports of proofs or formalizations elsewhere are not equivalent to kernel verification under GCL's frozen source lock.

## What remains

A bounded task is to formalize both reciprocal conditions as separate statements and prove they differ on $(3,4,7)$; then implement exact finite base-digit membership and sumset enumeration for $P(3,k)+P(4,k)+P(7,k)$ with $k=0,1$. Acceptance: no base-digit coefficient exceeds one, the cutoff $k$ is enforced, and the gcd obstruction is checked for shared-divisor bases. Stop at a finite semantics and transcription audit, not an asymptotic representation theorem.
