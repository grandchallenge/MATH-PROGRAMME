## The question in plain language

Place $n$ points in the plane so that no pair is less than unit distance apart. Connect two points when they are **exactly** unit distance apart. We obtain a graph whose *independent sets* are collections of points with all mutual distances greater than $1$.

Let $g(n)$ be the largest number of vertices guaranteed to be independent, regardless of the permitted point arrangement. [Erdős Problem #1066](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1066) asks for the true growth of $g(n)$ and possibly the limiting ratio $g(n)/n$.

## A small example

An equilateral triangle of side length $1$ gives the complete graph $K_3$. Every pair of its three points is connected, so its largest independent set has size $1$. Put two such triangles far apart, making every cross-triangle distance greater than $1$. The resulting six-point graph consists of two disjoint triangles and has independence number $2$. Thus $g(6)\le2$.

Repeating this construction shows $g(3m)\le m$ for all positive $m$. This is an elementary finite construction, not a sharp asymptotic upper bound.

## What is known

Unit-distance graphs subject to minimum pairwise separation are planar, so the four-colour theorem guarantees an independent set of size at least $\lceil n/4\rceil$. The maintained catalogue reports stronger results: Swanepoel's bound of $8n/31$ below and the Pach–Tóth construction of $5n/16$ above, with suitable interpretation of integer rounding:

$$
\frac{8n}{31}\le g(n)\le\frac{5n}{16}.
$$

The published asymptotic coefficients and their hypotheses come from external research; their complete proofs have not yet been independently replayed by GCL. The optimal asymptotic fraction remains undetermined.

## What remains

The mathematical goal is to tighten the asymptotic bounds or determine the limiting fraction. The source-fidelity task is to hash-lock the relevant Erdős, Pach–Tóth and Swanepoel texts, check their exact constructions and hypotheses, and separate general planar-graph reasoning from special properties of minimum-separation unit-distance graphs.
