## The question in plain language

The product of two squarefree integers contains a squared prime precisely when they share a prime factor. This changes a question about nonsquarefree pairwise products into an extremal problem about intersecting prime-support families.

Among A subset [1,N] satisfying ab NOT squarefree for EVERY a,b in A, including a=b, is maximal size achieved by all even numbers plus all ODD non-squarefree numbers? YES. Since any non-squarefree number can be added without harming the property, the issue reduces to an intersecting family of squarefree integers sharing a prime factor in every pair.

## A small example

For N=10, squarefree values are 1,2,3,5,6,7,10; maximal pairwise-sharing-prime squarefree subfamily has size three, e.g. {2,6,10}. The full maximizing candidate adds all nonsquarefree numbers in [1,10], {4,8,9}; A={2,4,6,8,9,10} has cardinality six. Brute force confirms no larger subset at this N.

A finite instance tests the definition, not a claim quantified over every large graph, infinite set or arbitrary coloring.

## What is known

Maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/844 read 2026-10-14: Weisenberg positive proof invokes Chvátal [Ch74] hereditary intersecting family theorem (#701). Alexeev–Mixon–Sawin [AMS25], *The independence and clique cover numbers of the squarefree graph*, arXiv:2507.01928 (2025), independently prove. Full paper proof and exact hereditary-family mapping require critical source inspection.

If a and b are both squarefree, their product ab has a square factor if and only if gcd(a,b)>1. Any nonsquarefree integer can be added to A without breaking the condition; hence only the squarefree subfamily requires optimization. Chvátal's intersecting-family theorem gives the even squarefree family as a maximizer. Including ALL nonsquarefree numbers then recovers the even-plus-odd-nonsquarefree candidate. Repeated indices a=b are included, so 1 cannot appear.
