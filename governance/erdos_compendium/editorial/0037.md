## The question in plain language

A set $A$ is an *essential component* if adding it to **every** set $B$ with Schnirelmann density strictly between zero and one increases that density:

$$
0<d_s(B)<1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d_s(A+B)>d_s(B).
$$

[Erdős Problem #37](https://www.erdosproblems.com/37) asks whether a *lacunary* set, whose successive elements grow at least geometrically, could be an essential component. The answer is **no**, established by Ruzsa.

## A small example

Take the powers of two,

$$
A=\{1,2,4,8,16,\ldots\}.
$$

The number of its elements up to $N$ is at most $1+\log_2 N$. More generally, if successive elements grow by a factor of at least $q>1$, then the count up to $N$ is $O(\log N)$.

This extreme sparseness is the obstruction. Ruzsa proved that **every** essential component must have a counting function at least $(\log N)^{1+c}$ for some fixed $c>0$ and all sufficiently large $N$. A set with only $O(\log N)$ elements cannot satisfy that condition.

## What is known

Imre Ruzsa, *Essential Components*, *Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society* **54** (1987), 38–56, obtained the necessary superlogarithmic growth bound above. The maintained problem record also attributes nearly matching sparse constructions of essential components to Ruzsa. The central conclusion rules out lacunary examples but does **not** say that essential components must have positive natural density.

## GCL's current state

[Source audit #1306](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1306#issuecomment-6121345052) checks the definition and the easy geometric counting estimate. It identifies the Ruzsa bound as a deep external theorem; no internal kernel replay of that proof is asserted.

## What remains

Nothing remains to answer Erdős's lacunary-essential-component question negatively. Related examples, such as sets of numbers of the form $2^m3^n$, are separate questions and cannot be classified using this simple one-ratio lacunarity argument alone.
