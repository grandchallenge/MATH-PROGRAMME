## The question in plain language

Fix two coprime integers $p>q\ge2$. Consider numbers of the form $p^kq^\ell$, where both exponents are nonnegative integers.

[Erdős Problem #1110](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1110) asks which integers can be expressed as sums of **distinct** numbers of this form, under a further restriction: *none of the chosen summands may divide another*. Such a collection of summands is called a divisibility **antichain**.

For pairs other than $\{p,q\}=\{3,2\}$, the problem concerns the density of integers that cannot be represented, and whether infinitely many of those integers are also coprime to $pq$.

## A small example

For $\{p,q\}=\{3,2\}$,

$$
31=27+4=3^3+2^2.
$$

Neither summand divides the other. In fact, the original problem includes a constructive argument showing **every** positive integer has such a representation using powers of $2$ and $3$.

Here is the induction. Represent zero with no summands. For even $n=2m$, double every summand in an antichain representation of $m$. For odd $n$, let $3^k$ be the largest power of $3$ not exceeding $n$ and use

$$
n=3^k+2\left(\frac{n-3^k}{2}\right).
$$

Apply the induction to the integer inside parentheses and double its summands. Because each old summand is smaller than $3^k$, the doubled summands have a lower exponent of $3$ than $k$. Hence $3^k$ cannot divide any of them; being odd, it cannot be divisible by them. The entire representation remains an antichain.

The exact executable checker tests **all integers from 1 through 1,024**, verifying the sum, prime-power form, uniqueness and divisibility condition.

## What is known

Erdős and Lewin (1996) proved that the set of nonrepresentable integers is finite **if and only if** the base pair is $\{3,2\}$. That leaves finer questions about density and the occurrence of infinitely many *coprime* nonrepresentable integers.

Yu and Chen (2022), as summarized by the maintained catalogue, proved density-zero representability in broad regimes, including $q>3$, $q=3$ with $p>6$, and $q=2$ with $p>10$. They also proved the existence of infinitely many coprime nonrepresentable integers except for certain small pairs not covered by their stated result.

For the special $(3,2)$ case, a separate quantitative question asks how large the *smallest* summand can be required to be. Later published work provides bounds and a reported sharp asymptotic. It is not the same as the nonrepresentability question.
