## The question in plain language

For a graph $H$, $\mathrm{ex}(n;H)$ is the maximum number of edges in an $n$ vertex graph containing **no copy of $H$**. For a **finite** family of graphs $\mathcal F$, $\mathrm{ex}(n;\mathcal F)$ is the maximum number of edges in an $n$ vertex graph avoiding **every** member of $\mathcal F$.

Erdős and Simonovits asked whether every finite forbidden family has some member $G\in\mathcal F$ whose individual Turán number is bounded by a **constant multiple** of the joint extremal number:

$$
\mathrm{ex}(n;G)\ll_{\mathcal F}\mathrm{ex}(n;\mathcal F)
$$

uniformly as $n$ grows. The reverse inequality is automatic, so the conjecture asks whether one forbidden graph captures the order of the entire family's restriction.

## A small example

Take $\mathcal F=\{K_{1,2},2K_2\}$, consisting of a **two-edge star** and a **two-edge matching**. A graph that avoids the two-edge star has maximum degree at most one and is therefore a disjoint union of edges and isolated vertices. Avoiding the two-edge matching then limits the entire graph to **at most one edge**:

$$
\mathrm{ex}(n;\mathcal F)=1\quad(n\ge2).
$$

Separately, graphs avoiding $K_{1,2}$ have $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ edges at most (achieved by a matching); graphs avoiding $2K_2$ may have $n-1$ edges (a star). Thus each individual extremal number grows linearly while their **joint** maximum stays constant. No fixed multiplicative constant can compare them.

## What is known

**The literal universal conjecture is DISPROVED by this elementary finite-family counterexample.** The [maintained Erdős #180](https://www.erdosproblems.com/180), edited January 2026, records the star-and-matching construction credited to Zach Hunter but continues to label the **broader surviving problem OPEN**, asking implicitly whether there are nondegenerate families for which the conjecture remains true.

For families in which every forbidden graph has chromatic number at least three, the Erdős–Stone theorem supplies a common quadratic leading term determined by the smallest chromatic number in the family. That positive restricted case does **not** rescue the universal quantifier over all finite families.

The older pinned catalogue classifies #180 **DISPROVED**. This is consistent with the literal counterexample; the live **OPEN** headline appears to reflect an unformalized revised question. Both descriptions must be retained rather than silently deciding that one status supersedes the other.

## What remains

A bounded fixture enumerates graphs on up to eight vertices and computes the joint and individual extremal numbers for the two forbidden patterns. Acceptance: the joint extremal count equals one, the two-edge-star-free extremal count is $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$, and a star gives linear growth when only $2K_2$ is forbidden. A separate source-adjudication task must specify any *revised* restricted problem before treating it as OPEN.
