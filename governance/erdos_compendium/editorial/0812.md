## The question in plain language

Let R(n)=R(n,n) denote the 2-colour diagonal Ramsey number. Are there constants c>0 and n0 with R(n+1)/R(n)>=1+c for all n>=n0? Separately, is R(n+1)-R(n)>>n²? Both original questions remain OPEN; do not confuse them with bounds for a two-step increment.

## A small example

Known small values R(2)=2, R(3)=6, R(4)=18 give increments 4 and 12, both satisfying the linear 4n-8 lower bound. Finite values do not establish eventual multiplicative separation. Preserve n>=2 for displayed BEFS estimate.

## What is known

Maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/812, read 2026-10-14: Burr–Erdős–Faudree–Schelp [BEFS89] prove R(n+1)-R(n)>=4n-8 for every n>=2. A separate lower bound from Erdős problem #165 implies R(n+2)-R(n)>>n^(2-o(1)). This two-step estimate does NOT establish one-step n² or a uniform multiplicative gap. Full 1989 and #165 primary proof not independently replayed.

The quoted 1989 lower bound is $R(n+1)-R(n)\geq4n-8$ for $n\geq2$. The sharper two-step estimate is of the form $R(n+2)-R(n)\gg n^{2-o(1)}$. Since a two-step difference is the SUM of two consecutive increments, this does not give a quadratic bound for each individual increment. The multiplicative ratio conjecture is stronger still.

The established single-step result and reported two-step consequence are different assertions:

$$

R(n+1)-R(n)\geq 4n-8\quad(n\geq2),

$$

$$

R(n+2)-R(n)\gg n^{2-o(1)}.

$$

Writing the latter as the sum of two adjacent single-step gaps shows why it cannot settle both individual gaps, much less the proposed ratio separation.
