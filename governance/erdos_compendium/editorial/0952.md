## The question in plain language

A **Gaussian integer** is $a+bi$, where $a,b\in\mathbb Z$. A Gaussian prime is an irreducible nonunit element of $\mathbb Z[i]$. Can one find infinitely many **distinct** Gaussian primes $z_1,z_2,\ldots$ whose consecutive Euclidean distances are uniformly bounded:

$$
\exists C<\infty\quad\forall j\ge1,\qquad |z_{j+1}-z_j|\le C?
$$

This is the **Gaussian moat problem**, recorded as open in [Erdős Problem #952](https://www.erdosproblems.com/952).

## Historical attribution matters

Although listed in the Erdős catalogue, Erdős explicitly explained in [Er77c] that the conjecture had been conveyed to him by Motzkin in 1963 and apparently originated with **Basil Gordon and Theodore Motzkin**. It should not be described as an original conjecture of Erdős. In [Er80, p.114] Erdős considered the answer likely negative. An expectation is not a proof.

## A small example

The Gaussian integers $3$, $2+i$, and $1+2i$ are all Gaussian primes, since their respective norms are $9,5,5$, and $3$ is a rational prime congruent to $3\pmod4$. In fact $3$ is Gaussian prime, but $2+i$ and $1+2i$ have prime norm $5$. The successive distances are $\sqrt2$ and $\sqrt2$. This finite chain is valid, but cannot imply the existence of an infinite bounded-step path.

**Norm test:** the norm $N(a+bi)=a^2+b^2$ is multiplicative. If it is an ordinary rational prime, then $a+bi$ is Gaussian prime. The converse is false: $3$ has norm $9$ but is Gaussian prime. A finite verifier should implement the full Gaussian primality characterization and not rely only on prime norms.

## A precise graph formulation

Fix a step bound $C$. Form the graph whose vertices are Gaussian primes and connect distinct primes $z,w$ whenever $|z-w|\le C$. The question is whether there exists **some finite $C$** for which this graph has an infinite self-avoiding path. The requirement of distinctness cannot be dropped: otherwise repeatedly alternating between two nearby Gaussian primes would satisfy a bounded-step condition vacuously.

A finite search in a bounded square can determine connectivity there, but cannot prove there is no infinite path for *every* step bound $C$. Even finding a closed barrier at one numerical width does not rule out paths using a larger width.

## What is known

Tsuchimura's 2005 computational study, *Computational results for Gaussian moat problem*, reported extensive finite evidence, including a moat of width six separating the origin from infinity, as discussed in the problem forum. This is numerical information at a **specified** width, not a resolution of the existential quantifier over all possible finite widths. Jordan and Rabung's 1970 discussion provides lower restrictions on any hypothetical bound; later commenters caution that their work did not settle the conjecture. [Source discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/952).

The catalogue labels the **statement** formalized; this is not equivalent to a formal proof. No unconditional general theorem resolving the Gaussian moat problem is asserted here.

## Why the problem is difficult

Gaussian primes become sparse in the plane but display two-dimensional arithmetic structure. Their angular and radial distributions do not immediately determine whether they form an infinite cluster under a fixed distance threshold. Local density heuristics can predict isolated barriers, but turning such heuristics into infinitely many unavoidable barriers is the central difficulty.

## GCL's current state

The finite Gaussian-prime chain and graph formulation are elementary, exact observations. Published numerical evidence is cited, not independently reproduced here. No infinite path and no theorem precluding all bounded-step paths has been proved in this manuscript.
