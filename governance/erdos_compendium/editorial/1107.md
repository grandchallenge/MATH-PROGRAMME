## The question in plain language

A positive integer is *$r$-powerful* when each prime appearing in its factorization has exponent at least $r$. For example, $8=2^3$ is $3$-powerful, and $36=2^2 3^2$ is $2$-powerful. The number $1$ satisfies the condition vacuously.

[Erdős Problem #1107](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1107) asks whether, for each fixed integer $r\ge2$, **every sufficiently large positive integer** can be written as a sum of at most $r+1$ positive $r$-powerful numbers.

The assertion is eventual: small exceptions are permitted. The number of summands is at most $r+1$, not necessarily exactly that many.

## A small example

For $r=2$, the permitted summands include $1,4,8,9,16,25,27,\ldots$. Many integers can be written as sums of at most three such numbers:

$$
6=4+1+1,\qquad
12=8+4,\qquad
18=9+9.
$$

The integer $7$ cannot: using only squarefull numbers no greater than seven, the largest sum below seven of at most three positive terms is $4+1+1=6$.

Exact enumeration through $120$ finds the following small integers not representable by at most three positive squarefull numbers:

$$
7,\ 15,\ 23,\ 87,\ 111,\ 119.
$$

This is **not** a counterexample to the theorem that all *sufficiently large* integers have representations.

## What is known

**The case $r=2$ was proved** by Heath-Brown (1988), according to the maintained catalogue. The general problem, for all fixed $r\ge3$, is still listed as **open**.

The question arises from a powerful-number analogue of additive bases: one restricts summands to numbers with repeated prime factors but asks whether a fixed number of these sparse summands covers all sufficiently large integers.

The related problem of using only $r$ rather than $r+1$ summands is different and has its own history.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source audit #2378](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2378#issuecomment-6168368933) verifies the small exact representation search, distinguishing exceptional integers from genuine obstructions to eventual representation.

The [frozen formal source](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1107.lean) correctly separates its solved $r=2$ special case from its open general statement, but the theorem bodies have not been kernel-replayed.

## What remains

Inspect Heath-Brown's original proof and all positivity, unit, and eventual-exception conventions. Investigate the extension to $r\ge3$ without treating finite searches as evidence of a complete Waring-type theorem.
