## The question in plain language

Colour the edges of the complete graph $K_n$. Require **every** set of four vertices to use at least five distinct edge colours among its six edges. If $f(n)$ is the fewest colours that can be used globally, how does it grow as $n$ increases? The requirement is local to each four-vertex complete graph, but colours are shared throughout the entire host graph.

## A small example

For $n=4$, the entire graph has six edges and the rule requires five distinct colours; using five is possible, so $f(4)=5$. For $n=3$, there is no four-vertex subset, and the condition is vacuous. Small sizes must not be substituted into asymptotic inequalities stated for sufficiently large $n$.

## What is known

**SOLVED as an asymptotic problem.** Erdős and Gyárfás obtained bounds with leading terms $5n/6$ and $n$. Bennett, Cushman, Dudek and Prałat established $f(n)\sim 5n/6$ in 2022; Joos and Mubayi found a shorter proof. The exact value for every $n$ is not part of the asserted asymptotic result. The maintained [Erdős #136](https://www.erdosproblems.com/136) records these names and the older example $f(9)=8$. The full original proofs have not been independently replayed in this source audit.

## What remains

Build an exhaustive edge-colouring checker for $K_4,K_5$, canonicalizing colours by their order of first use. Acceptance: verify $f(4)=5$, count distinct colours globally, and reject any four-clique using four or fewer. The bounded fixture cannot prove the asymptotic result.
