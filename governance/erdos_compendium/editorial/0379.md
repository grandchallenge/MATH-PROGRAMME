## The question in plain language

Erdős Problem 379 asks whether the middle entries of some rows of Pascal's triangle can all have a large prime-power divisor, with the prime allowed to depend on the entry. More precisely, for a positive integer $n$, let $S(n)$ be the largest exponent $r$ for which every $C(n,k)$, with $1 ≤ k < n$, is divisible by $p^r$ for at least one prime $p$ that may depend on $k$. The question is whether

$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty} S(n) = \infty.
$$

This is much weaker than asking for one prime to divide an entire row. It also does not ask for the same exponent to be the exact prime-adic valuation of every entry: each entry only needs some prime whose power with exponent $r$ divides it. The original assignment and source packet are issue [#1649](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1649).

The original source is Erdős and Graham, *Old and New Problems and Results in Combinatorial Number Theory* (1980), printed p. 72 in the [scanned source](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~ronspubs/80_11_number_theory.pdf). There the problem is phrased with $S(n)$ as the largest integer for which, for every $k$ in the interior range through $n-1$, the binomial coefficient is divisible by the corresponding power of some prime. The maintained version's range $1 ≤ k < n$ is the same quantifier, written with a strict inequality.

## A small example

The construction used in the resolution has a small checkable instance. Take $p=3$, $r=2$, and $n=2^{φ(3^2)}=2^6=64$. An exact-integer enumeration of all $k=1,\ldots,63$ verifies

$$
4 ∣ C(64,k) ∨ 9 ∣ C(64,k).
$$

The recorded check found 62 values divisible by 4, 37 divisible by 9, 36 divisible by both, and no values divisible by neither. For concrete audit points, $C(64,1)=64$ has 2-adic valuation 6; $C(64,2)=2016$ is divisible by both 4 and 9; and $C(64,32)=1832624140942590534$ is not divisible by 4 but is divisible by 9. These are ordinary integer divisions, not probabilistic tests.

This establishes the finite lower bound $S(64) ≥ 2$, and illustrates why the prime may change with $k$. It does not establish the exact value of $S(64)$, and one finite row cannot establish an unbounded limsup. The value of the example is that it is a reproducible sanity check for the quantifiers and for the proposed family of rows.

## What is known

The public resolution is a first-hand proof comment by Terence Tao in the [Problem 379 discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/379?order=oldest), dated 28 August 2025. It identifies the key proposition

$$
S(2^{φ(p^r)}) ≥ r.
$$

This holds whenever $r ≥ 2$ and $p$ is prime with $p > 2^{r-1}$. Since there are arbitrarily large primes, this gives the required unbounded limsup.

The mechanism is short but has several quantifiers worth preserving. Put $m=φ(p^r)$ and $n=2^m$. From $C(n,k)k=C(n-1,k-1)n$, either $2^r$ already divides $C(n,k)$, or $k$ contains a large power of 2. In the latter case both $k$ and $n-k$ are small odd-prime multiples after removing that power of 2, so neither is divisible by $p$, because $p>2^{r-1}$. Euler's theorem gives $p^r ∣ n-1$. A second identity, $C(n,k)k(k-1)=C(n-2,k-2)n(n-1)$, then forces $p^r ∣ C(n,k)$. This handles every $1 ≤ k < n$, not merely typical or central entries.

The proof is available in full at Analysis/Misc/erdos_379.lean in the pinned [teorth/analysis commit 4f623b0f4cacdb967f1f8132db0becaee0f1fb3d](https://github.com/teorth/analysis/blob/4f623b0f4cacdb967f1f8132db0becaee0f1fb3d/Analysis/Misc/erdos_379.lean), file blob 764c2ecb0bc6f99147ed91a2fe37e94b0bcc9f84. No separate refereed resolution paper was located in this bounded inquiry; Tao's dated discussion proof is therefore cited as the primary public resolution source, not silently upgraded to a journal publication.

## What remains

The mathematical resolution is externally documented, but provenance still needs careful handling. A bounded successor task should compare the printed p. 72 statement, Tao's comment, the complete teorth proof, and the pinned FormalConjectures definition line by line. In particular, it should record the dependence of $p$ on $k$, the strict interior range, the use of Euler's theorem, the choice $n=2^{φ(p^r)}$, and the coercion from natural-valued $S$ to an extended-natural limsup. It should also explain that the FormalConjectures file is a statement carrier with by sorry, not an independently replayed proof.

No claim here is made about a newer paper, a prize, novelty, or independent formal certification. The live page's additional remark about constructions such as $3^{2^C}$ is an unneeded secondary observation for this audit and is not treated as the primary proof.
