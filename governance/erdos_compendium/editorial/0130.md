## The question in plain language

Choose an **infinite** set $A$ of distinct points in the Euclidean plane with no three collinear and no four lying on a common circle. Form a graph on $A$: two vertices are adjacent precisely when their Euclidean distance is an **integer**.

Andrásfai and Erdős asked how large this graph's clique number and chromatic number can be. In particular, could it require **infinitely many vertex colours**? Integer distance means $1,2,3,\ldots$ rather than an arbitrarily chosen real distance or a distance merely rational.

## A small example

Three vertices of an equilateral triangle of integer side length form a triangle in the integer-distance graph; their points are noncollinear and automatically satisfy the condition excluding four concyclic points. Thus chromatic number three is possible. Four generic points might satisfy the incidence restrictions yet have no integer pairwise distances at all, showing why the induced graph must be calculated rather than assumed dense.

The graph is not the classical unit-distance graph: allowing all positive integer distances changes its edges and may raise chromatic number.

## What is known

The main chromatic-number question remains **OPEN**. Anning and Erdős proved in [*Integral distances*](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1945-0013187-0) (1945) that an **infinite** planar set whose pairwise distances are all integers lies on a straight line. Consequently an infinite clique is impossible in this setting, because $A$ contains no three collinear points.

That conclusion does **not** establish a universal finite bound on the sizes of cliques, nor does it establish finite chromatic number. Graphs can have unbounded chromatic number while every clique is small. The question asks about this separation under a stringent geometric incidence hypothesis.

The maintained [Erdős #130](https://www.erdosproblems.com/130) cites original [Er97b] and distinguishes the disproved possibility of an infinite clique from the unresolved chromatic-number question. Any new proof claim reported in the forum must be audited separately; its mere presence does not establish a consensus result.

## What remains

A bounded semantics fixture is to construct finite rational-coordinate point sets with no collinear triples and no concyclic quadruples, compute integer squared distances exactly using perfect-square tests, and independently calculate clique and chromatic numbers for examples of at most eight vertices. Acceptance: all geometric preconditions are checked first, and the triangle example gives chromatic number three. Stop at finite examples: neither infinity nor a uniform bound follows from them.
