## The question in plain language

For every integer $k\ge3$, is there a finite collection of points in the plane with this property: no matter how the points are coloured red or blue, some straight line contains at least $k$ of the points, and **every point of the collection on that line is the same colour**?

[Erdős Problem #1090](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1090) is stronger than merely finding $k$ same-colour points on one line. Extra points of the other colour would invalidate the requested line.

The answer is **yes**, using the Hales–Jewett theorem and a carefully chosen planar projection.

## A small example

A combinatorial line in the discrete cube $\{0,1,2\}^3$ consists of three strings obtained by varying the same symbol in one or more chosen coordinates. For example,

$$
(0,1,0),\quad(1,1,1),\quad(2,1,2)
$$

form a line.

We independently checked a finite geometric illustration: map a string $(x,y,z)$ to

$$
L(x,y,z)=(x+7y+49z,\ x+13y+169z).
$$

All 27 grid points have distinct images. Among the $\binom{27}{3}=2925$ triples, exactly 49 are collinear in the original three-dimensional grid, and **no additional triple** becomes collinear under this projection.

This does not assert that dimension three is already large enough for the two-colour Hales–Jewett conclusion. It verifies the separate projection mechanism on a bounded example.

## What is known

The [Hales–Jewett theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hales%E2%80%93Jewett_theorem) guarantees that a sufficiently high-dimensional discrete grid, under every two-colouring, contains a monochromatic combinatorial line.

The final geometric step matters. Given the finite grid $B=\{0,\dots,k-1\}^N$, select a linear map $L:\mathbb R^N\to\mathbb R^2$ injective on $B$ and preserving every triple that is noncollinear in the original space. Such a map exists: each unwanted collision or new collinearity imposes a nonzero polynomial equation in the projection's matrix entries. A finite union of proper algebraic zero sets cannot cover all matrices.

A combinatorial line contains **exactly $k$ grid points** on its affine support. Under the chosen projection, no additional grid point can lie on its planar image line, since doing so would introduce new collinearity with two of the original points. Thus the Hales–Jewett monochromatic line satisfies the entire strengthened conclusion.

The maintained catalogue attributes the $k=3$ historical case to Graham and Selfridge, and Zach Hunter's general observation to generic projection. It also reports an external Lean proof of the strengthened statement.
