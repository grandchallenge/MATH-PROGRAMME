## The question in plain language

Arrange $n$ points in the plane so that distinct points stay at least one unit apart and some pair is exactly one unit apart. Among all such configurations, minimize the *diameter* (the largest interpoint distance). [Erdős #99](https://www.erdosproblems.com/99) asks whether, for all sufficiently large $n$, **every** minimizing configuration must contain three points at pairwise distance one: a unit equilateral triangle. The word *every* matters: one appealing minimizing picture is not enough to establish the conjecture.

## A small example

For $n=3$, the vertices of an equilateral triangle of side one have diameter one, the minimum possible because the required minimum separation is one. They exhibit the desired triangle. For $n=4$, the vertices of a unit square have diameter $\sqrt 2$ but no unit equilateral triangle; this small configuration cannot refute an assertion about *sufficiently large* $n$.

A useful check comes from the **unit-contact graph**: join points at distance exactly one. Two unit segments with disjoint endpoints cannot cross, because crossing would put some pair of endpoints closer than one. Thus the graph is planar. If the configuration has no unit triangle, it is triangle-free, giving at most $2n-4$ unit edges for $n\ge3$. This elementary necessary restriction does not prove that a diameter-minimizing set has a triangle.

## What is known

Bezdek and Fodor, *Minimal Diameter of Certain Sets in the Plane*, *Journal of Combinatorial Theory A* **85** (1999), 105–111, study exact small configurations, including $D(8)$; finite values do not determine eventual minimizers. The [dated GCL audit](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1368#issuecomment-6098661023) also records a September 2026 author manuscript proposing stress/rigidity reductions; these are author-reported reductions, not GCL-verified proof of #99.

## GCL's current state

The audit fixes the quantifiers and the pinned formal-provider statement, proves by elementary compactness that an optimal configuration exists for every $n\ge2$, and derives the planar contact graph and the degree-at-most-five condition when unit triangles are absent. This is analytical source work, not a Lean kernel replay or a global solution.

## What remains

The gap is between local constraints on unit contacts and a global conclusion about **all** diameter minimizers beyond some threshold. Contact sparsity alone cannot establish that conclusion. Any proposed proof must rule out every triangle-free optimizer for every sufficiently large size.
