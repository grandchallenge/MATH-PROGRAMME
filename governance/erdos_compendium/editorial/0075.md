## The question in plain language

Can a graph on $\aleph_1$ vertices require $\aleph_1$ different colors while all of its small finite pieces contain remarkably large **independent sets**? Erdős #75 asks for a graph with chromatic number $\aleph_1$ such that, for every $\epsilon>0$, all sufficiently large finite $n$-vertex subgraphs have independent sets of size greater than $n^{1-\epsilon}$.

A stronger variant asks whether each finite $n$-vertex subgraph can contain an independent set of size at least $c n$ for some fixed positive $c$. Both the size and chromatic-number requirements on the ambient graph matter. A countably chromatic example does not answer the question.

## A small example

An independent set consists of pairwise nonadjacent vertices. The five-cycle $C_5$ has independence number two; each vertex excludes its two neighbors from a maximum independent set. A single five-cycle cannot settle an asymptotic statement that quantifies over *all sufficiently large* finite subgraphs of one uncountable graph.

There is a useful link with bipartite subgraphs. If an induced $n$-vertex subgraph contains a bipartite induced subgraph on $b$ vertices, one side of its bipartition is independent and has at least $b/2$ vertices. A bound on almost-bipartite finite pieces can therefore imply a bound on independence numbers. The reverse implication need not hold.

## What is known

The source problem is marked **OPEN**. In their original 1982 paper, Erdős, Hajnal and Szemerédi proved that **for every fixed** $\epsilon>0$ and every cardinal $\kappa$ there exists a graph of chromatic number greater than $\kappa$ whose every finite $n$-vertex subset contains an induced bipartite graph on at least $(1-\epsilon)n$ vertices (Theorem 1, printed page 120). This gives an independent set of size at least $(1-\epsilon)n/2$. Crucially, it does **not** give a single graph with **exactly** $\aleph_1$ vertices and chromatic number $\aleph_1$ with the required *varying* exponent condition, and it cannot simply be restricted to an arbitrary $\aleph_1$-vertex subgraph without justifying the chromatic number.

On the same printed page, their Problem 2 explicitly asks whether a graph on $\omega_1$ vertices with chromatic number $\omega_1$ can have independence number at least $cn$ in every finite induced $n$-vertex subgraph. Their Theorem 2 studies a natural Specker graph that does *not* satisfy this stronger condition. These are historically precise distinctions between near-bipartiteness, independence number, and uncountable chromatic number.

## What remains

A bounded successor is to formalize the implication that a bipartite induced subgraph on at least $(1-\delta)n$ vertices gives an independent set of size at least $(1-\delta)n/2$, then audit whether any known $\omega_1$-chromatic construction attains such a bound **uniformly on all finite subsets**. A witness must preserve the same ambient graph and quantify over every eligible $n$ and every $n$-vertex subgraph. Exhibiting examples for each $n$ separately is insufficient.
