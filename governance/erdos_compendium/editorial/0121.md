## The question in plain language

For an integer $k\ge2$, let $F_k(N)$ be the largest size of a set $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ for which **no $k$ distinct elements** have product equal to a perfect square. Erdős, Sárközy and Sós asked whether, for odd $k\ge5$,

$$
F_k(N)=(1-o(1))N.
$$

This would mean one could retain almost every integer while avoiding square products of every specified odd number of *distinct* members. Reusing an element in a product is prohibited.

## A small example

For $A=\{2,3,6\}$, the product of all three elements is $2\cdot3\cdot6=36$, a square. This shows why excluding squares among individual elements does not suffice. The square status of a product is determined by parity of each prime exponent: exponent vectors can be added modulo two.

If $A$ is any four-element subset and $k=5$, there are no five distinct choices, so the condition is vacuously satisfied. Finite samples must therefore use $|A|\ge k$ before testing meaningful forbidden products.

## What is known

**DISPROVED.** Terence Tao, [*On product representations of squares*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10474-025-01505-7), *Acta Mathematica Hungarica* 175 (2025), 142–157, proved that for every fixed $k\ge4$, some constant $c_k>0$ exists with

$$
F_k(N)\le(1-c_k+o(1))N.
$$

The published primary abstract and [author's 20 May 2024 explanation](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2024/05/20/on-product-representations-of-squares/) both explicitly support this negative answer. Tao uses a probabilistic argument; the even-exponent asymptotics were established earlier by Erdős–Sárközy–Sós. The old $k=3$ estimate $F_3(N)=(1-o(1))N$ is **not** contradicted.

There is a distinct related problem: forbidding square products of **any odd number** of distinct members, with no prescribed $k$. It has a different extremal function and asymptotic constant, studied by Granville and Soundararajan. This cannot be interchanged with the fixed-parameter conjecture.

## What remains

A bounded finite-check task is to factor each positive integer up to 30, encode prime-exponent parity as a bit vector, and search all $k$ subsets of a proposed $A$ for XOR equal to zero. Acceptance: the triple $\{2,3,6\}$ is detected as square-product, duplicate-index tuples are rejected, and independent exact integer products agree with parity-vector tests. Stop at this semantic fixture; no asymptotic probabilistic theorem follows from finite enumeration.
