## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1192](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1192) is **OPEN FOR ALL r** in the source as of 15 October 2026.

Fix integer $r\ge2$ and $A\subseteq\mathbb N$. Let $f_r(n)$ count **ordered** solutions $n=a_1+\cdots+a_r$, each $a_i\in A$; if the source means unordered solutions, its moment changes and must be normalized. A basis of order $r$ requires $f_r(n)>0$ for **every sufficiently large integer $n$**, not just positive density of representable integers. The question is whether for **each** $r$ there exists such a basis with
$$
\sum_{n\le x}f_r(n)^2=O_r(x)
$$
for every large $x$. One bounded-moment set whose counting function is large enough is not necessarily a basis.

## A small example

For the finite set $A=\{1,2,4,8\}$, exactly 16 **ordered** pairs exist. Its complete sum histogram has $\sum_n f_2(n)^2=28$, reproduced by the phase23 fixture. But $A$ is finite and therefore is **not** a basis: its representations vanish for every sufficiently large $n$. This explicitly separates a small second moment from the essential eventual-positive-representation requirement.

## What is known

Erdős 1980 [Er80,p.99], https://www.erdosproblems.com/1192, marks the general problem **OPEN**. Erdős–Rényi probabilistically constructed $A$ with $\sum_{n\le x}f_r(n)^2\ll x$ and $|A\cap[1,x]|\gg x^{1/r}$, but this does not guarantee *every* large integer is represented. **Ruzsa [Ru90] solved the basis case $r=2$**, not all $r\ge3$. The site has a formalized-statement badge but no certification from the underlying Lean kernel or a claimed proof of all orders.
