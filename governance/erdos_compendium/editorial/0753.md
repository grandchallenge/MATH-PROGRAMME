## The question in plain language

Let χ_L(G) denote the list-chromatic number: the smallest t such that EVERY assignment of a t-element palette at each vertex permits a proper colour selection. Erdős, Rubin and Taylor asked whether a fixed c>0 ensures χ_L(G)+χ_L(complement G)>n^(1/2+c) for EVERY n-vertex graph. The answer is DISPROVED by Alon (1992), which constructs a graph for each n with χ_L(G)+χ_L(G^c)=O(sqrt(n log n)). The reported LEAN status is external, not a GCL kernel replay.

## A small example

For the complete graph K_n, χ_L(K_n)=n while χ_L(the empty n-vertex graph)=1: the sum is n+1. This example SATISFIES a super-square-root bound, so it cannot disprove the conjecture. Alon's counterexamples must balance the two list-colouring difficulties instead. For n=2, K2 has χ_L=2 and its complement 1, sum 3.

## What is known

Unlike ordinary chromatic number, χ_L quantifies over all lists of t colours, not a single shared palette, and χ_L(G)≥χ(G). Alon's uniform construction with both G and complement having sum at most C sqrt(n log n) is asymptotically smaller than n^(1/2+c) for ANY fixed c>0, since sqrt(log n)=o(n^c). Thus a single family for arbitrarily large n refutes the universal claim. Original Alon [Al92] full proof uninspected. Maintained site labels DISPROVED (LEAN), but neither a protected #753.lean in the GCL-pinned provider nor independently replayed Lean proof was established in this audit.
