## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1169](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1169) is classified **NOT DISPROVABLE** in the maintained catalogue.

For the **ordinal** $\omega_1^2$ (not the cardinal arithmetic product $\aleph_1^2=\aleph_1$), ask whether
$$
\omega_1^2\not\to(\omega_1^2,3)^2.
$$
The negative arrow asserts there exists a **two-coloring of unordered pairs** of the ordinal with no order-type-$\omega_1^2$ homogeneous first-color subset and no first-order triangle in the other color. The current source starts with 'for all finite $k<\omega$' but $k$ is **absent from its displayed formula**. This is a genuine binder/notation issue, not an extra parameter one may silently invent.

## A small example

Finite Ramsey theory supplies a useful sharp contrast: there is a two-edge-coloring of $K_5$ with no monochromatic triangle, while every two-coloring of $K_6$ has one. Exact exhaustive enumeration of $2^{15}$ colorings confirms $R(3,3)=6$. This does **not** test the color-1 exclusion of **order type** $\omega_1^2$ and cannot decide the CH-free question.

## What is known

Hajnal [Ha71] proved this negative partition relation under the **continuum hypothesis** (CH), according to Vardi [Va99,7.85], and the website therefore classifies it **NOT DISPROVABLE**, a relative-consistency notion. The site comment documents a January 2026 correction of a formerly missing negative arrow; that correction does **not** resolve the unused quantifier $k$. CH sufficiency does not imply a ZFC theorem, nor does it prove full independence. Related ordinal partition problem #592 has a different (countable) ambient ordinal.
