## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1149](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1149) asks whether, for every fixed real exponent $\alpha>0$ that is **not an integer**, the proportion of integers $n\le N$ satisfying $\gcd(n,\lfloor n^\alpha\rfloor)=1$ approaches $6/\pi^2$.

Let $C_\alpha(N)$ count those integers. The precise assertion is

$$
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{C_\alpha(N)}{N}=\frac6{\pi^2}.
$$

The restriction on $\alpha$ matters: for $\alpha=1$, $\gcd(n,n)=n$ and the desired density is zero. Distinguish natural density from logarithmic density and finite numerical proportions.

## A small example

For alpha=1/2, floor(n^alpha)=floor sqrt(n), exact gcd computation n<=10000 gives 6009 coprime values, ratio 6009/10000=0.6009, near 6/pi²≈0.6079. This is finite evidence not an asymptotic theorem. For alpha=1 the gcd(n,n)=n counterexample justifies the exclusion of positive integers alpha from domain.
