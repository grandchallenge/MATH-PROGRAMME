## The question in plain language

A *Sidon set* is a set of integers in which every sum of two elements, with repetitions allowed but with the pair unordered, has a unique representation. For a finite Sidon set $A$, order its distinct pair sums as

$$
A+A=\{s_1<s_2<\cdots<s_t\}.
$$

Erdős, Sárközy and Sós asked whether the **mean squared gap** between consecutive sums must grow without bound as the size of $A$ grows:

$$
\frac1t\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}(s_{i+1}-s_i)^2\longrightarrow\infty.
$$

The denominator is $t$, not the number $t-1$ of gaps. The question ranges over *all* finite Sidon sets with increasing cardinality, not only optimally dense ones.

## A small example

For $A=\{1,2,4\}$, the six distinct unordered sums, including $a+a$, are $2,3,4,5,6,8$. Their consecutive gaps are $1,1,1,1,2$, with squared sum $8$. The statistic is therefore $8/6=4/3$. One small example says nothing about divergence over every sequence of larger Sidon sets.

Cauchy–Schwarz yields a baseline bound: if the $t$ sums lie between $s_1$ and $s_t$, then

$$
\sum_{i<t}(s_{i+1}-s_i)^2\ge\frac{(s_t-s_1)^2}{t-1}.
$$

For dense Sidon sets this alone may give only a constant-order lower bound after dividing by $t$; the question needs additional nonuniformity.

## What is known

The general conjecture remains **OPEN**. A [corrected partial manuscript linked in the maintained discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/153) treats *asymptotically maximum* Sidon sets, for which the diameter is $(1+o(1))|A|^2$. It uses equidistribution of dense Sidon sets and the thinning of their pair-sum density near endpoints; localized Cauchy–Schwarz then yields divergent squared-gap averages in that restricted regime. Earlier versions were corrected. This is a source-reported partial result, not an independently refereed proof of the general statement.

The original Erdős–Sárközy–Sós 1994 paper [ESS94] is the source of the question. The maintained [Erdős #153](https://www.erdosproblems.com/153) continues to mark the unrestricted statement **OPEN**, appropriately separating the new restricted argument from the universal assertion.

## What remains

A bounded successor independently computes the distinct unordered pair sums and gap statistic for exact Sidon subsets of $[1,20]$ with three to six elements. Acceptance: every candidate is checked for uniqueness of unordered sums *including diagonals*, the fixture $\{1,2,4\}$ gives $4/3$, and Cauchy–Schwarz is verified numerically. Then isolate precisely where the asymptotically maximum hypothesis enters the corrected proof. Stop before inferring the universal limit from finite data or that restricted result.
