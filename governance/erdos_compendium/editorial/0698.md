## The question in plain language

Erdős and Szekeres asked whether the greatest common divisor of every two distinct binomial coefficients C(n,i) and C(n,j), with 2≤i<j≤n/2, is bounded below by a quantity h(n) tending to infinity uniformly in i,j. The maintained source lists PROVED, externally LEAN-reported. George M. Bergman's 2011 work supplies a much stronger explicit bound, not merely gcd>1.

## A small example

At n=10,i=2,j=3, C(10,2)=45 and C(10,3)=120, so gcd=15. At n=12,i=2,j=4, C(12,2)=66 and C(12,4)=495, so gcd=33. These are exact examples of the common-divisor phenomenon, not proofs that the minimum over all indices grows indefinitely.

## What is known

PRIMARY PUBLISHER SOURCE inspected: George M. Bergman, 'On common divisors of multinomial coefficients', Bulletin of the Australian Mathematical Society 83 (2011), 138–157, DOI 10.1017/S0004972710001723, theorem 2/section 2. The published bound is gcd(C(n,i),C(n,j)) ≥ n^(1/2)·2^(i−7/2)/(i·sqrt(i−1)) for 2≤i≤j≤n/2, thus at least a positive absolute multiple of sqrt(n), uniformly in i because 2^i/i^(3/2) has a positive minimum for i≥2. The maintained catalogue uses asymptotic notation gcd ≫ sqrt(n)2^i/i^(3/2).
