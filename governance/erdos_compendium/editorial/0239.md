## The question in plain language

Let $f:\mathbb N\to\{-1,1\}$ be **multiplicative**: whenever $\gcd(a,b)=1$, it satisfies $f(ab)=f(a)f(b)$. Erdős asked whether its Cesàro mean

$$
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N f(n)
$$

must always exist. Multiplicativity is weaker than *complete* multiplicativity, which would require the identity even for noncoprime pairs.

## A small example

The constant function $f(n)=1$ has mean one. The Liouville function $f(n)=(-1)^{\Omega(n)}$ is completely multiplicative and takes only the values $\pm1$. Its mean tends to zero as a consequence of the prime number theorem. These do not exhaust the possible behaviours of general multiplicative sign functions, especially those defined differently at higher prime powers.

The codomain restriction is crucial. For complex values on the unit circle, $f(n)=n^{it}$ with fixed nonzero real $t$ is completely multiplicative, but its normalized partial sums oscillate roughly as $N^{it}/(1+it)$ and generally do **not** converge.

## What is known

**PROVED.** Eduard Wirsing (1967) established existence of the mean for multiplicative functions with values in $\{-1,1\}$. Gábor Halász (1968) generalized the analysis through the mean-value theory of bounded multiplicative functions.

The theorem does not assert that the mean is necessarily **zero**. Nor does the complex counterexample refute it, since $n^{it}$ does not take values only in the permitted two-element sign set. The maintained [Erdős #239](https://www.erdosproblems.com/239) identifies both points.

## What remains

A bounded task constructs several completely and merely multiplicative sign functions, verifies multiplication on coprime inputs and computes finite normalized means. Acceptance: demonstrate the complex-valued $n^{it}$ counterexample is **outside** the source codomain, and that sample finite means do not establish convergence for every multiplicative sign function.
