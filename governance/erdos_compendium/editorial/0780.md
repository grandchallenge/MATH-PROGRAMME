## The question in plain language

Suppose all $r$-element subsets of an $n$-element set are assigned one of $t$ colours. How large must $n$ be to guarantee that one colour contains $k$ **pairwise disjoint** $r$-element subsets?

The exact answer is known. The threshold is

$$

ngeq kr+(t-1)(k-1).

$$

This is a generalized Kneser-colouring theorem, proved by Noga Alon, Peter Frankl and László Lovász in 1986. The classical Kneser **graph** corresponds to the special case $k=2$; the general problem permits larger monochromatic disjoint families.

## A small example

Take $k=r=t=2$. The statement says that when $ngeq5$, any red/blue colouring of the edges of $K_n$ contains two disjoint edges of the same colour. At $n=4$ this can fail. Put the vertices in groups $X_0={1,2,3}$ and $X_1={4}$. Colour every edge within $X_0$ red, and each edge incident to 4 blue. The red edges form a triangle, so no two are disjoint; the blue edges form a star, so no two are disjoint either.

This example proves that the 5-vertex threshold cannot be lowered. It does not prove the upper theorem for all parameters.

## What is known

The exact original statement is Theorem 1.1 on printed p. 359 of N. Alon, P. Frankl and L. Lovász, [*The chromatic number of Kneser hypergraphs*](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nalon/PDFS/Publications/The%20chromatic%20number%20of%20Kneser%20hypergraphs.pdf), *Transactions of the American Mathematical Society* **298** (1986), 359–370. They prove that for positive integers $r,t$ and $kgeq2$, if $ngeq kr+(t-1)(k-1)$, every partition of the $r$-subsets into $t$ families contains $k$ pairwise disjoint members in one family.

The source immediately demonstrates **sharpness**. At one vertex fewer,

$$

n=kr-1+(t-1)(k-1),

$$

split the ground set into $X_0$ of size $kr-1$ and $X_1,ldots,X_{t-1}$ each of size $k-1$. Colour an $r$-set 0 if it lies completely in $X_0$; otherwise give it the smallest positive colour $i$ for which it intersects $X_i$. In colour 0, $k$ disjoint sets would need at least $kr$ vertices inside $X_0$, one more than is available. In each positive colour $i$, $k$ disjoint sets would require $k$ distinct vertices from $X_i$, while only $k-1$ exist.

This verifies the lower-bound example **directly**. The opposite implication at the threshold is nontrivial and uses a topological generalization of Lovász's proof of the Kneser conjecture. The original paper explicitly credits the Bárány–Shlosman–Szűcs extension of the Borsuk–Ulam theorem.

There is a minor but important indexing issue in the maintained catalogue's sharpness description: the partition should start with $X_0$ and end with $X_{t-1}$, as the original printed paper does. Otherwise the first block label is accidentally repeated.

## What remains

Verify the $(k,r,t)=(2,2,2)$ obstruction by exhaustive edge-colouring, then check that all 2-colourings of the 5-vertex $r=2$ case contain a same-colour matching of size two. Preserve the primary theorem's parameter order and X-block indexing. Independent critical review and public-rendered MathJax inspection are prerequisites for admission.
