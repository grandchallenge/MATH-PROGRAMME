## The question in plain language

Must the order q of every finite projective plane be a prime power? Such a plane has q²+q+1 points and the same number of lines, each with q+1 points; every pair of points lies on exactly one line. This is FALSIFIABLE by a single non-prime-power plane but has no known counterexample. Existence for prime-power q is classical; it is unknown for q=12.

## A small example

For q=2 the seven Fano lines {j,j+1,j+3} modulo 7 form a projective plane; each line has 3 points and each unordered pair lies on exactly one line. By contrast, q=6 is impossible by the Bruck–Ryser condition: since 6≡2 (mod 4), a plane would force 6=x²+y² for integers x,y, which is false. This does NOT rule out q=12, where the Bruck–Ryser condition has no such consequence.

## What is known

Bruck–Ryser 1949: for q≡1 or 2 modulo 4, projective-plane existence requires q to be a sum of two integer squares; Bruck–Ryser–Chowla refinements are separate. Order q=10 was ruled out by Lam et al.'s exhaustive computer-assisted classification (1989/1990). All q≤11 have been resolved. The GCL-pinned Formal Conjectures #723 main and variants carry `answer(sorry)` and `by sorry` placeholders; no GCL kernel certification. A hypothetical non-prime-power plane would refute the prime-power order conjecture, but neither q=12 nor other orders have a certified plane in hand.
