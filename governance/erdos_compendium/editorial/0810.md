## The question in plain language

Does there exist epsilon>0 and for EVERY sufficiently large n a graph G on n vertices with >=epsilon*n² edges whose edges use at most n colors such that every copy of C4 has four different edge colors? The anti-Ramsey minimum chi_S(n,e,C4) is the related quantity.

A four-cycle being rainbow means that its four edges have four different colors. Requiring this in a graph with quadratically many edges while using at most n colors is substantially stronger than coloring a single isolated four-cycle. The link to forbidden configurations in three-uniform hypergraphs is an additional open obstacle, not a solution.

## A small example

The graph C4 alone on n=4 vertices has 4 edges colored with four DISTINCT colors: its one four-cycle is rainbow. This is a finite construction only, no uniform absolute density for arbitrarily large n. If a host graph has no C4 at all the coloring condition is vacuous, but it cannot be naively equated with dense existence.

This finite fixture tests the conventions. It does not certify any of the asymptotic or all-graphs assertions.

## What is known

Maintained #810 OPEN. Burr–Erdős–Graham–Sós [BEGS89] expect NO, and prove negative analogue when C4 is replaced by P4. Sárközy–Selkow [SaSe06] prove strong r/n->infinity for connected bipartite G other than a star EXCEPT complete bipartite graphs, leaving C4=K2,2 open. A weaker construction from 3-uniform (7,4)-free hypergraphs has e~g(n;7,4); g(n;7,4)=o(n²) itself unresolved [1178]. Original proofs not fully inspected.

The distinctions between established partial theorems, original open parts, and disproved universal claims have been preserved. Historical publications are attributed, rather than presented as new GCL mathematics.

Equivalently, in the anti-Ramsey notation, the question seeks a fixed $\varepsilon>0$ such that for all large $n$,

$$

\chi_S(n,\lceil\varepsilon n^2\rceil,C_4)\leq n.

$$

The ceiling simply enforces an integer edge count; care is needed about monotonicity in the exact original definition of $\chi_S$. The known general bipartite theorems leave $K_{2,2}=C_4$ as an exception.
