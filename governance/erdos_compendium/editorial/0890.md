## The question in plain language

Take a block of $k$ consecutive positive integers

$$
n,\ n+1,\ \ldots,\ n+k-1.
$$

Write $\omega(m)$ for the number of **distinct** prime factors of $m$, and $\omega_k(m)$ for the number of those prime factors **strictly greater than $k$**. Erdős and Selfridge asked two asymptotic questions, now recorded as [Erdős Problem #890](https://www.erdosproblems.com/890).

First, for every fixed positive $k$, is the least limiting size of the sum of *large-prime contributions* at most $k$?

$$
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\ \sum_{0\le j<k}\omega_k(n+j)\ \le k.
$$

Second, is the greatest limiting size of the sum of **all** distinct-prime counts, normalized by $\log n/\log\log n$, equal to one?

$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\left(\sum_{0\le j<k}\omega(n+j)\right)
\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}=1.
$$

The original 1967 formulation and the maintained first question have important differences. Their exact relationship is a **source-fidelity obligation**, not something to conceal in mathematical exposition.

## A small example

For $k=3$ and the block $10,11,12$, the prime supports are respectively $\{2,5\}$, $\{11\}$, and $\{2,3\}$. The full distinct-prime count is $2+1+2=5$. The contributions from primes larger than 3 are $1+1+0=2$, with the remaining three occurrences contributed by primes 2 and 3.

This difference is systematic. For **every** starting $n$, the small-prime contribution in a block of length $k$ is at least

$$
\sum_{p\le k}\left\lfloor\frac{k}{p}\right\rfloor.
$$

At $k=26$, the nine primes not exceeding 26 force at least **36** small-prime occurrences, before any contributions from larger primes. This already exceeds the historical printed candidate upper bound $k+\pi(k)=35$ for the **full** prime-count sum, explaining why that historical formulation cannot be correct literally.

The exact finite verifier computes both sums for $100\le n\le5100$ and $k=1,2,3,4,5,8,12,26$, and checks the small-prime decomposition at every block. These are not asymptotic certificates.

## What is known

Erdős and Selfridge, [*Some problems on the prime factors of consecutive integers*](https://combinatorica.hu/~p_erdos/1967-21.pdf), *Illinois Journal of Mathematics* **11** (1967), pp.428–430, discuss on printed page 430 the sum of numbers of distinct prime factors in short consecutive blocks. They use Pólya's theorem on unbounded gaps between integers composed exclusively of primes at most $k$ to obtain the lower bound $k-1$ for the large-prime contribution in the maintained normalization.

The printed original proposed an upper comparison of $k+\pi(k)$ for a sum written with $\omega$ rather than the restricted $\omega_k$. The April 2026 [maintained problem page](https://www.erdosproblems.com/890) flags an apparent error and replaces the first formulation with the large-prime sum. This is not a cosmetic rewrite: every block already contains at least $\lfloor k/p\rfloor$ multiples of each small prime $p\le k$.

Moreover, a public [April 2026 discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/890) reports a DeepMind Lean proof that **one** formulation of the first proposed inequality fails at $k=26$. Participants explicitly discuss whether a composite-block hypothesis was omitted, and note that this might conflate the 1967 paper with a separate 1971 Erdős–Selfridge paper about consecutive composite numbers. The full theorem statement, model of liminf, proof branch, and axiom output have **not** been independently inspected by GCL. The maintained multipart problem continues to carry an open status and the second asymptotic question remains separate.

A classical result gives, for a **single** integer,

$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\omega(n)\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}=1.
$$

It cannot simply be multiplied by the block length $k$: there are strong divisibility and congruence constraints between consecutive integers. The corresponding fixed $k$ block limsup is not independently established by that one-variable theorem alone.

## GCL's current state

We can make the source ambiguity precise with an elementary decomposition. For every positive $n,k$, let $S_{\mathrm{small}}(n,k)$ be the total number of occurrences of primes at most $k$ among the distinct-prime supports of $n,n+1,\ldots,n+k-1$. Then

$$
\sum_{0\le j<k}\omega(n+j)
=
\sum_{0\le j<k}\omega_k(n+j)+S_{\mathrm{small}}(n,k).
$$

Each prime $p\le k$ divides at least $\lfloor k/p\rfloor$ members of every such block. Thus

$$
S_{\mathrm{small}}(n,k)\ge
\sum_{p\le k}\left\lfloor\frac{k}{p}\right\rfloor.
$$

This elementary fact proves that a fixed $k$ upper bound formulated with full $\omega$ differs inherently from one using only $\omega_k$.

The reusable exact fixture at governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_889_890.py computes the sieve-based prime supports in every requested finite block. It checks exact equality of all-prime and large-plus-small-prime counts, the deterministic lower bound, and the observed finite minima. For $k=26$ the smallest large-prime sum **observed** in the test window is 11 and the smallest all-prime sum is 49. Neither number says anything about the true limiting infima at arbitrarily large $n$.

## What remains

The bounded highest-priority task is a **three-way source and formal-statement reconciliation**: compare the printed 1967 $\omega$ formulation, the maintained 2026 $\omega_k$ formulation, and the precise April 2026 externally claimed Lean counterexample, including any restriction to consecutive composite numbers. The mathematical target is an explicit table of hypotheses and a fully checked counterexample or proof for **one exact version**, not a global claim transferred across versions.

Acceptance requires the original page-430 definition, the actual pinned external Lean theorem statement and clean compiler/axiom report, and a proof that the statement adjudicated by Lean is semantically identical to the selected target. A missing composite condition, a switch between $\omega$ and $\omega_k$, or a false quantifier invalidates the certificate. The small-prime decomposition and $k=26$ lower bound serve as compulsory regressions.

Keep the second question separate. Its fixed block limsup demands a genuine large values of $n$ construction or obstruction with correctly normalized distinct-prime counts; a finite maximum is not admissible as proof.
