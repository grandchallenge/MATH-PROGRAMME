## The question in plain language

What is the smallest prime dividing the binomial coefficient $\binom nk$? [Erdős Problem #1094](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1094) conjectures that, whenever $n\ge2k$, the smallest such prime is at most

$$
\max\left(\frac nk,k\right)
$$

**apart from finitely many exceptional pairs** $(n,k)$.

This is a two-variable finiteness assertion. It does **not** claim that the only exceptional pairs are the fourteen numerical examples listed in the original 1988 paper. It also does not follow automatically from a bound that holds for sufficiently large $n$ *for each fixed $k$*.

## A small example

For $n=7$ and $k=3$,

$$
\binom73=35=5\cdot7.
$$

The smallest prime divisor is $5$, whereas $\max(7/3,3)=3$. Thus $(7,3)$ is genuinely exceptional.

We independently checked the **fourteen historical candidate exceptions** listed by Erdős, Lacampagne and Selfridge:

| $k$ | Exceptional $n$ in the 1988 list |
|---:|---|
| 3 | 7 |
| 4 | 13, 14 |
| 5 | 23 |
| 6 | 62 |
| 8 | 44 |
| 10 | 46, 47, 74, 94, 95 |
| 11 | 47 |
| 16 | 241 |
| 28 | 284 |

For each pair, exact integer binomial arithmetic confirms **no prime less than or equal to the conjectured threshold divides the coefficient**. The same program independently computes the related *deficiency* from #1093, including deficiency four at $(47,11)$ and nine at $(284,28)$.

A bounded sweep over $2\le k\le10$ and $2k\le n\le200$ found eleven exceptional pairs, all among the historical fourteen. This is not a proof that no further exceptions exist.

## What is known

The [maintained catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1094) attributes the conjecture to Erdős, Lacampagne and Selfridge (1988), with computational and analytic follow-up in 1993. It separately reports Selfridge's conjecture about the range $n\ge k^2-1$, and possible stronger bounds involving $\sqrt{k}$ or a constant such as $13$ in place of $k$.

Those are related but different assertions. The universal **finite-exceptions conjecture remains open**.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source audit #2365](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2365#issuecomment-6166439929) reproduces all fourteen historical candidate examples using exact integer division and verifies their positive deficiency. The frozen formal-conjectures statement labels the conjecture open and has no completed proof.

**#1094 is one of the twenty deterministic critical-review samples.** Reproducing examples is not an independent review of the general argument. The original 1988 and 1993 papers have not yet been proof-audited page by page.

## What remains

Check the original paper's exact exception list, every inequality endpoint, the quantifier over all pairs, and the literature since 1993. A separate implementation of binomial prime valuations should replay the finite data. Only after source adjudication and independent second-pass review can this critical sample be marked complete.
