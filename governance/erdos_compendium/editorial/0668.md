## The question in plain language

Among all sets of $n$ distinct points in the plane, some sets determine **as many unit-distance pairs as possible**. How many geometrically different maximizing configurations exist?

[Erdős Problem #668](https://www.erdosproblems.com/668) asks whether the number of pairwise **noncongruent** maximizers tends to infinity as $n$ grows. A subsidiary question asks whether there are always more than one for $n>3$. That second assertion is **false already for $n=4$**, while the asymptotic first question remains **OPEN**.

Two configurations are congruent if a Euclidean distance-preserving motion, possibly including reflection, carries one to the other. Counting only nonisomorphic **graphs** of unit-distance contacts is not equivalent to counting noncongruent point configurations.

## A small example

Two equilateral triangles of side length one, on opposite sides of a **shared side**, use four points. There are five unit-distance edges: the shared base and the four edges joining its endpoints to the two opposite apexes. The distance between the two apexes is $\sqrt3$, so the sixth pair is not at unit distance.

This example is uniquely optimal at $n=4$. All six pairs cannot be at distance one, because four mutually equidistant points cannot lie in the Euclidean plane. Thus the maximum is exactly five. Any four-point configuration with five unit pairs has unit-distance graph $K_4$ with one edge missing. The two vertices of that missing edge are each at distance one from **both** remaining vertices. The intersections of two unit circles around the remaining base endpoints are the two opposite equilateral apexes. Their positions are forced, up to a rigid motion or reflection.

Hence the number of noncongruent maximizers at $n=4$ is

$$

M(4)=1.

$$

This independently checked elementary argument refutes only the subsidiary every-$n>3$ assertion. One exceptional small $n$ cannot refute a claim that $M(n)\to\infty$.

## What is known

Let $U(n)$ be the largest number of unordered pairs at unit distance among $n$ distinct planar points. Let $M(n)$ be the number of congruence classes of configurations attaining $U(n)$. The principal question is $M(n)\to\infty$.

The maintained source refers to computational results by Engel, Hammond-Lee, Su, Varga and Zsámboki, and by Alexeev, Mixon and Parshall, suggesting uniqueness for several sizes between five and twenty-one. These computations largely addressed **unit-distance graph isomorphism**. Different Euclidean realizations can sometimes share a graph, so a graph count must not be silently presented as a classification of geometric congruence classes.

Problem [#90](https://www.erdosproblems.com/90) concerns the separate value of $U(n)$ itself, whereas #668 concerns the multiplicity of extremizers. Even perfect knowledge of the value $U(n)$ would not automatically enumerate all maximizers.

## GCL's current state

The [authenticated issue-bound source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1939#issuecomment-6156746123) for #668 records the geometric uniqueness proof at $n=4$ and explicitly keeps the asymptotic question OPEN. Its disposition is **PARTIAL** because the original Erdős [Er97f] source and the cited recent computational papers' exact certificate scopes were not independently inspected.

This is a substantive **unaccepted draft**, not a complete enumeration of all maximizers for larger $n$ or a formal mathematical certificate.

## What remains

A bounded useful successor would select one small $n$ and enumerate both: the graphs that can realize $U(n)$ edges, and the **geometric embeddings** of each graph, counted modulo Euclidean isometries. It must rule out flexible continuously many realizations when claiming finiteness or uniqueness, and must record whether reflected copies are identified.

Finite exhaustive calculations can settle individual sizes if genuinely complete, but cannot alone prove or disprove $M(n)\to\infty$. Historical source checking, critical editorial review, protected publication and public mathematics rendering checks also remain.
