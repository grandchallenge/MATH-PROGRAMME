**Editorial status:** UNACCEPTED_DRAFT. Erdős Problem #518 is **PROVED**, concerning covers of a two-edge-coloured complete graph by monochromatic paths **all of the same colour**. Pokrovskiy, Versteegen and Williams established the sharp square-root bound for all sufficiently large graphs in a peer-reviewed 2026 paper. A newer July 2026 preprint by Hangdi Chen and Yaojun Chen claims the sharp bound for **every** positive integer $n$. The latter proof and its dependencies were inspected in part but have not received an independent end-to-end GCL review.

## The question in plain language

Take the complete graph $K_n$ on $n$ vertices, and colour every edge **red** or **blue**. Can we cover all its vertices using at most $\sqrt n$ paths, requiring that **every path in the chosen collection has the same colour**?

A monochromatic path is a sequence of distinct vertices whose consecutive edges all have the specified colour. A **single vertex** is allowed as a length-zero path and can be treated as either colour. A collection is a **cover** when every vertex belongs to at least one path.

The exact question is whether

$$
\boxed{
p(n)\le\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor
}
$$

where $p(n)$ is the maximum, over all red–blue colourings of $K_n$, of the minimum number of red-only paths **or** blue-only paths needed to cover every vertex.

The floor is necessary because the number of paths is an integer. The paths are allowed to **intersect**. Requiring vertex-disjoint paths, or allowing some paths to be red and others blue in the same cover, produces different problems.

## A small example

Suppose $n=9$, and partition the vertices into sets $A$ and $B$ with

$$
|A|=7,\qquad |B|=2.
$$

Colour every edge **within $A$ blue**, and every other edge **red**.

A blue path cannot include any vertex of $B$ except as a singleton, because every edge touching $B$ is red. To cover all vertices with blue paths, we need one blue path through $A$ plus two singleton paths for $B$: at least **three** paths.

A red path cannot contain consecutive vertices of $A$, since their connecting edge is blue. Therefore each red path contains at most $|B|+1=3$ vertices of $A$. Covering seven vertices of $A$ requires at least

$$
\left\lceil\frac73\right\rceil=3
$$

red paths. Thus **no same-colour cover has fewer than three paths**, and $\sqrt9=3$ is sharp.

The same argument works with $n=s^2$ and

$$
|A|=s^2-s+1,\qquad |B|=s-1.
$$

Every blue cover needs $|B|+1=s$ paths, while every red path visits at most $s$ vertices of $A$, requiring at least

$$
\left\lceil\frac{s^2-s+1}{s}\right\rceil=s
$$

paths. Hence the conjectured coefficient one in front of $\sqrt n$ **cannot be reduced**. This example is an elementary lower-bound construction; it does not prove that every colouring also has a cover this efficient.

## What is known

### The original graph-colouring work

L. Gerencsér and A. Gyárfás, *On Ramsey-type problems*, *Annales Universitatis Scientiarum Budapestinensis* **10** (1967), 167–170, proved that every red–blue $K_n$ can be covered by **two** monochromatic paths, but the two paths need **not** have the same colour.

P. Erdős and A. Gyárfás, *Vertex covering with monochromatic paths*, *Mathematica Pannonica* **6** (1995), 7–10, studied the stronger **same-colour** question. They established a bound of $2\sqrt n$ paths and conjectured that the factor two could be removed. The full original 1995 article was not retrieved in this tranche; its exact bibliographic reference and theorem history are confirmed by the later primary papers.

### The peer-reviewed large-graph breakthrough

Alexey Pokrovskiy, Leo Versteegen and Ella Williams, *A proof of a conjecture of Erdős and Gyárfás on monochromatic path covers*, *Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B* **176** (January 2026), 551–560, [DOI 10.1016/j.jctb.2025.10.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jctb.2025.10.007), proved the sharp bound for all **sufficiently large** $n$.

The complete original [arXiv:2409.03623v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.03623) (October 2025) was retrieved. Theorem 1.3, visually checked on printed p.2, gives the explicit threshold

$$
\boxed{n>20^{40}\ \Longrightarrow\ p(n)\le\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor.}
$$

This enormous threshold is not believed to be optimal. The authors additionally observe that a bound of $\sqrt n+10$ can be established for all $n$ with further technical effort. **Neither statement by itself establishes the exact square-root bound for every smaller $n$.**

The argument uses long monochromatic paths and graph structure on the remaining vertices, including red–blue path alternatives in complete bipartite graphs, intersection control and efficient same-colour covers. The paper is peer-reviewed, but its complete proof has not been independently reconstructed in this editorial audit.

### A new July 2026 full-range claim

Hangdi Chen and Yaojun Chen, *On monochromatic path covers conjecture of Erdős–Gyárfás*, [arXiv:2607.21915v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21915), dated July 24, 2026, assert the sharp bound for **every positive integer**.

Their complete fourteen-page PDF was obtained and inspected directly on CHAD, including **Theorem 1.5 on p.2**, the setup of the minimal-counterexample proof on pp.3–4 and its closing argument on p.13. The theorem states

$$
\boxed{\forall\,n\ge1,\qquad p(n)\le\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor.}
$$

The Chen–Chen proof begins by assuming a minimal counterexample with $n=c^2+r$, where $c=\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor$ and $0\le r\le2c$. It combines earlier structural lemmas from Erdős–Gyárfás and Gerencsér–Gyárfás with three auxiliary lemmas from Pokrovskiy–Versteegen–Williams.

One concise reduction is worth isolating. In a minimal counterexample, any red path $R$ and any blue path $B$ must satisfy

$$
\boxed{|V(R)\cap V(B)|\le r.}
$$

If their intersection $S$ had at least $r+1$ vertices, then at most $c^2-1$ vertices would remain. By minimality, those remaining vertices could be covered by at most $c-1$ paths of one colour. Add either $R$ or $B$, matching that colour, to obtain a cover of all $n$ vertices with at most $c$ same-colour paths, contradicting the assumed counterexample.

This reduction is elementary once the minimal-counterexample assumption is made. It is **not the whole proof**. The subsequent detailed structural inequalities and imported auxiliary lemmas still require independent checking before GCL calls the fourteen-page proof certified.

The 2026 Chen–Chen paper is currently a **preprint** rather than a refereed journal article in the sources inspected. The maintained [Erdős #518 catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/518) labels the question PROVED but only cites the earlier Pokrovskiy–Versteegen–Williams result. That source is correct about the asymptotic theorem but does not yet reflect the later stronger all‑$n$ claim.

## What remains

The useful next source-proof obligation is to independently review every step of the Chen–Chen minimal-counterexample proof and its three imported Pokrovskiy–Versteegen–Williams lemmas. An error in a key structural inequality or an unlicensed change from a covering to a **disjoint partition** would invalidate the claimed extension.

The bounded computational task is **not** to enumerate impossibly many colourings near the published threshold. Instead, establish a reliable finite oracle on small graphs and the exact lower-bound family. This checks the definitions and elementary steps, without pretending to establish the all‑$n$ theorem.
