## The question in plain language

Let V_f={n+f(n):n>=1}, with f the Euler totient, sum of divisors, divisor count, or number of distinct prime factors. The question is whether V_phi has POSITIVE LOWER NATURAL DENSITY, not whether f itself has positive mean or the number of representations of a fixed target is large. The analogous sigma case must be separately tracked as printed.

## A small example

Exact first values of n+phi(n): n=1..6 yields 2,3,5,6,9,8. These include a collision-free sample but no quantitative asymptotic density. For n+sigma(n), n=1..6 yields 2,5,7,11,11,18, showing collisions may occur.

The example can be checked exactly. A finite witness does not replace the universal, asymptotic, or infinitely-often quantifiers in the original question.

## What is known

Live maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/822 read 2026-10-14: Gabdullin–Iudelevich–Luca [GIL24], *Numbers of the form k+f(k)*, J. Number Theory (2024) 58–85, credited PROVED positive lower density for phi, also tau and omega. Wording 'similar question' for sigma is present; inspect [GIL24] Theorem specifically before declaring sigma proven. Full printed proof not independently read.

The lower natural density of a set of positive integers $S$ is

$$

\underline{d}(S)=\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{|S\cap[1,x]|}{x}.

$$

Positive lower density is a statement about DISTINCT values of $n+f(n)$ rather than the number of input integers. Collisions between input images reduce the distinct-value count. The maintained catalogue credits Gabdullin, Iudelevich and Luca for the positive result, and the precise scope for the $\sigma$ analogue remains a primary-paper check.
