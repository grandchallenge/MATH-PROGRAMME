## The question in plain language

How efficiently can one express $n!$ as a product of **distinct integers all larger than $n$**? Define $f(n)$ to be the smallest possible largest factor $m$ among products

$$
n!=a_1a_2\cdots a_k,\qquad n<a_1<\cdots<a_k=m.
$$

[Erdős Problem #390](https://www.erdosproblems.com/390) asks whether there is an asymptotic constant $c$ such that

$$
f(n)-2n\sim c\,\frac{n}{\log n}.
$$

This is **open**. Knowing only that the difference has the same *order of magnitude* as $n/\log n$ is not enough to identify a limiting constant.

## A small example

For $n=5$,

$$
5!=120=10\times12,
$$

and both factors are strictly larger than 5. This gives $f(5)\le12$. In fact no factorization with maximum factor at most 11 works: a factorization with three or more distinct factors larger than 5 would already have product at least $6\cdot7\cdot8>120$, while none of the possible two-factor choices below 12 multiplies to 120. Hence $f(5)=12$.

For every $n\ge2$, the singleton factorization $n!=n!$ is formally allowed. It gives a very weak but valid existence bound $f(n)\le n!$.

## What is known

Erdős, Guy and Selfridge (1982) established two-sided order estimates,

$$
f(n)-2n\asymp \frac{n}{\log n}.
$$

These estimates allow the normalized difference to fluctuate between positive constants; they do **not** imply a single limit.

One useful exact identity comes from $(n+1)\cdots(2n)=n!\binom{2n}{n}$. Starting with the factors between $n$ and $2n$, deleting some factors and adding larger ones leads to a product relation involving the central binomial coefficient. This is a promising bookkeeping method but not a new asymptotic theorem.

## What remains

Determine whether the ratio $(f(n)-2n)\log n/n$ converges and, if it does, compute its limiting value. Finite factor searches and two-sided order estimates do not establish that convergence.
