## The question in plain language

Put $n$ distinct points in the plane subject to a minimum separation of one: every two points must be at distance at least one. Among all such configurations, minimize the **diameter**, the largest distance between any pair.

Let $h(n)$ count how many optimal configurations there are, up to **congruence** (translation, rotation and reflection). Erdős #103 asks whether

$$
h(n)\longrightarrow\infty.
$$

This is about the **number of geometrically different optimal packings**, not the growth of the optimal diameter itself. Uniform rescaling is not part of congruence because the minimum separation has been fixed at one.

## A small example

For two points, the minimum possible diameter is one, attained by a unit segment. Every optimal pair is congruent to every other: $h(2)=1$.

For three points, every pairwise distance is at least one. The smallest possible diameter is one, realized by an equilateral triangle of side one. If a three-point configuration attains diameter one, each of its three distances must equal one, so it too is congruent to that equilateral triangle. Therefore $h(3)=1$.

A unit square provides a valid four-point configuration with diameter $\sqrt2$, but exhibiting a feasible square does not establish that it minimizes diameter among all admissible four-point configurations. Even exact optimal diameter for a particular $n$ would not by itself count all inequivalent minimizers.

## What is known

This conjecture remains **OPEN**. The maintained [Erdős #103](https://www.erdosproblems.com/103) states that it is not even known whether $h(n)\ge2$ for **all sufficiently large** $n$. The related finite diameter-minimization problem is #99, previously accepted outside this cohort, but that numerical problem is not equivalent to the count of distinct equality cases.

Historical primary source: Erdős, *Some problems on elementary geometry*, [Er94b], listed in the maintained bibliography. ## What remains

A bounded successor is to formalize existence, optimality and congruence for the two- and three-point cases. Acceptance: prove that minimum separation at least one forces diameter at least one, that the unit segment and equilateral unit triangle attain the infimum, and that any diameter-one configuration with the corresponding number of points is congruent to the example. An extension to $n=4$ would require an exhaustive geometric optimality certificate, not merely numerical optimization. Stop before making any asymptotic inference.
