## The question in plain language

For which positive integers $n$ is

$$
n-k^2
$$

prime **whenever** $k^2<n$ and the integer $k$ is coprime to $n$?

[Erdős Problem #1141](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1141) asks whether infinitely many such $n$ exist. The maintained catalogue now reports a **negative solution with Lean verification**, although the external proof has not been independently replayed by GCL.

The coprimality condition makes this different from #1140. In particular, $n$ itself need not be prime.

## A small example

The historical suggestion that $968$ might be the largest qualifying number is incorrect:

$$
968-3^2=959=7\cdot137,
$$

and $\gcd(3,968)=1$. Therefore $968$ does not satisfy the condition.

Exact primality and gcd checks through $n=2500$ find **41 qualifying integers**, including $1722$. The latter is the **largest currently reported example**, not a proved global maximum.

## What is known

Earlier work by ChatGPT and Tang, cited on the maintained page, bounded the number of qualifying $n\le N$ by $N^{1/2+o(1)}$. Such a sparsity estimate does **not** show there are finitely many.

A later 2026 contribution, [APSSV26b], reportedly uses a theorem of **Pollack (2017)** to establish finiteness for a more general family:

$$
n-ak^2\text{ prime for all eligible }k,\qquad a\ge1.
$$

This stronger result is what disproves the infinitude conjecture. The maintained page also reports Lean verification, but a GCL kernel and assumption audit is still required.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source audit #2412](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2412#issuecomment-6181193514) independently checks the small examples and distinguishes a counting bound from a finiteness theorem.
