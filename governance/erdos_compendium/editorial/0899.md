## The question in plain language

Let $A$ be an **infinite set of positive integers** that occupies a vanishing proportion of the positive integers. In symbols, its counting function

$$
A(N)=|A\cap[1,N]|
$$

satisfies $A(N)/N\to0$ as $N\to\infty$. Such a set has **asymptotic density zero**.

Its positive difference set is

$$
D(A)=(A-A)\cap\mathbb N
=\{a-b:a,b\in A,\ a>b\}.
$$

[Erdős Problem #899](https://www.erdosproblems.com/899) asks whether, for every such infinite density-zero $A$, the number of distinct differences up to $N$ must be arbitrarily large **relative to the original number of elements up to $N$**:

$$
\limsup_{N\to\infty}
\frac{|D(A)\cap[1,N]|}{A(N)}=\infty.
$$

The answer is **yes**, attributed in the maintained catalogue to Imre Ruzsa. The limsup quantifier is crucial: it asserts arbitrarily large ratios along some scales, not necessarily convergence of the ratio to infinity at every scale.

## A small example

Take the powers of two,

$$
A=\{1,2,4,8,16,\ldots\}.
$$

For $N=2^m-1$, exactly $m$ elements of $A$ lie in $[1,N]$, namely $1,2,\ldots,2^{m-1}$. Yet **all** positive differences of powers of two up to $N$ have the form

$$
2^j-2^i=2^i(2^{j-i}-1),
\qquad 0\le i<j\le m.
$$

Different pairs $(i,j)$ give different differences. Indeed, the exact power of two dividing the difference is $2^i$, so its valuation at the prime 2 determines $i$; the remaining odd number $2^{j-i}-1$ then determines $j$. Every such difference is at most $2^m-1=N$. Thus

$$
|D(A)\cap[1,N]|=\frac{m(m+1)}2,
\qquad
\frac{|D(A)\cap[1,N]|}{A(N)}=\frac{m+1}2.
$$

The ratio diverges through these $N$ as $m\to\infty$. This is a complete proof for **one particular density-zero set**, not for all such sets.

For comparison, the positive-density set of multiples of three has $A(N)=\lfloor N/3\rfloor$ and exactly the same number of positive differences up to $N$. Its ratio is identically $1$ once the denominator is nonzero. The density-zero assumption cannot simply be omitted.

## What is known

The maintained [Erdős Problems #899](https://www.erdosproblems.com/899) classifies the statement as **PROVED**, citing **[Ru78]** for Ruzsa's affirmative answer. It also points to [problem #245](https://www.erdosproblems.com/245), the related question about *sum sets*. The proof for general density-zero sets is **not** supplied in full on the maintained page.

There is useful nearby primary research: [*On difference sets of sets of integers*](https://archive.numdam.org/item/SDPP_1977-1978__19_1_A4_0/), *Séminaire Delange–Pisot–Poitou* (1977–1978), records Ruzsa's related sparse-set difference result in its introduction. The published text distinguishes **ordinary difference sets**, **infinite-recurrence difference sets**, and **density-difference sets**, which are different objects; none may silently replace the literal $D(A)$ in this problem.

The exact bibliographic expansion of the catalogue's code **[Ru78]** was not independently confirmed in this execution. A related bibliography lists Ruzsa's [*On difference sequences*](https://archive.numdam.org/item/SDPP_1977-1978__19_1_A4_0/), *Acta Arithmetica* **25** (1974), pp.151–157, as well as a forthcoming work titled *On difference sets*. It would be misleading to assert that either one is definitely the precise 1978 source without a checked bibliography and theorem statement.

The maintained page says **“Formalised statement? Yes”**, but this indicates that an expression of the conjecture exists, not that GCL has built a completed Lean proof of Ruzsa's theorem. A separate formal statement and a kernel-verified solution have different provenance requirements.

## GCL's current state

The exact powers-of-two lemma exhibits two different forms of sparsity. The set itself has only logarithmically many elements below $N$; its difference set contains quadratically many distinct differences in that logarithm. The crucial difference-counting argument uses **both operands from the entire infinite set $A$**, not just operands from $A\cap[1,N]$.

This detail changes finite arithmetic. When $N=2^m-1$, the power $2^m$ lies **outside** the cutoff, but its differences $2^m-2^i$ for $i<m$ lie **inside** it. Omitting those pairs would undercount the true positive difference set. The formula $m(m+1)/2$ counts every pair with $0\le i<j\le m$, including those whose larger operand is $2^m$.

The executable fixture at governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_898_899.py directly enumerates these pairs through $m=16$, checks uniqueness by the independent valuation at the prime 2 and odd-part encoding, and verifies the resulting ratio exactly. It separately checks the positive-density multiples-of-three control over $N\le200$. The finite program is an independently reproducible proof regression, **not** Ruzsa's general analytic theorem.

## What remains

A bounded lemma suitable for formalization is the **powers-of-two positive-difference count**. For every $m\ge1$, show that the positive differences of all powers of two lying at most $2^m-1$ are in one-to-one correspondence with exponent pairs $0\le i<j\le m$, and that the resulting ratio equals $(m+1)/2$. Prove that $A(N)/N\to0$ for this infinite set, not just for the finite samples.

Acceptance is a complete elementary proof or pinned compiled Lean code without placeholders with an actual axiom readback, alongside exact replay of all 16 tested values. A missing difference contributed by an operand exceeding the cutoff, two distinct exponent pairs with equal difference, or an incorrect element count falsifies the lemma.

The **substantive next source task** is to identify the exact publication behind [Ru78], read its full theorem and hypotheses, and reproduce the argument for *every* infinite zero-density set using the exact positive-difference set in the maintained statement. A proof for the powers-of-two example alone cannot satisfy that obligation. Stop before claiming that the full Ruzsa theorem has been independently adjudicated or kernel-verified by GCL.
