## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1217](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1217) currently has source status **PROVED — 2026 IMPROVEMENT**.

For increasing $A=\{a_1<a_2<\cdots\}$ with positive **lower logarithmic density**, seek a subsequence forming an infinite **divisibility chain** $a_{n_1}\mid a_{n_2}\mid\cdots$, whose chain counting function, normalized by $\log\log x$, has upper limit at least
$$\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac1{\log\log x}\sum_{\substack{a\in A\\a<x}}\frac1{a\log a}.$$
The left counts chain elements, while the right weights **all** source elements reciprocally. The placement of the $\limsup$ on both sides is essential. Source says the logarithmic density assumption can be removed in the definitive theorem; do not silently substitute natural density.

## A small example

The list $(2,4,8,16,32)$ is a divisibility chain: each term divides the next. Its reciprocal-log weight $\sum1/(a\log a)$ is approximately **0.993355647945**, verified by the phase25 fixture. Finite chains cannot determine upper limits normalized by $\log\log x$ and do not independently establish the strengthened 2026 theorem.
