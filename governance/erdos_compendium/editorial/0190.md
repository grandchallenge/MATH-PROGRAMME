## The question in plain language

Colour the integers $1,\ldots,N$ using **any finite number of colours**. A $k$ term arithmetic progression is *monochromatic* if all its members share one colour and *rainbow* if every member has a different colour.

Define $H(k)$ as the smallest $N$ forcing **at least one** of these two patterns in every such colouring. Erdős and Graham asked for the growth of this canonical Ramsey threshold, and in particular whether

$$
\frac{H(k)^{1/k}}k\longrightarrow\infty.
$$

Unlike ordinary van der Waerden numbers, the number of colours here is not fixed in advance; requiring either a monochromatic or rainbow pattern is essential for a finite universal threshold.

## A small example

For $k=2$, a pair of different integers is always either monochromatic or rainbow, so $H(2)=2$. For $k=3$, a three-term progression with colours red, red, blue is **neither** monochromatic nor rainbow. One must check every nonconstant progression, not merely whether a colour occurs twice.

The quantifier “any finite colouring” permits arbitrarily many colours depending on $N$. A proof of a bound for just two colours cannot establish the canonical threshold.

## What is known

**SOLVED for the stated divergence.** Fox and Hunter, [*Three-color van der Waerden numbers grow super-exponentially*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02541) (2026), derived the strong lower estimate

$$
H(k)\ge k^{(1-o(1))k\log k}.
$$

It implies $H(k)^{1/k}/k\to\infty$ directly. Bae's independent [2026 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20588) gave the earlier lower estimate $H(k)\ge k^{(2-o(1))k}$, which would imply the same divergence. Hunter's preceding recurrence already implied that $H(k)^{1/k}\to\infty$, a **weaker** conclusion than division by an additional factor of $k$.

The maintained [Erdős #190](https://www.erdosproblems.com/190), updated June 2026, labels the original question SOLVED. The full exact asymptotic behaviour of $H(k)$ remains a separate problem; the stated limit does not give matching upper and lower bounds.

## What remains

A bounded successor enumerates all set partitions (colourings up to renaming) of $[1,N]$ for $N\le8$ and checks every $k=2,3$ progression for monochromatic or rainbow patterns. Acceptance: $H(2)=2$ and a second independent predicate checker agrees. Separately formalize the elementary implication from the 2026 lower bound to the divergence of $H(k)^{1/k}/k$; stop before asserting any new asymptotic constant.
