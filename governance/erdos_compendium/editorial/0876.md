## The question in plain language

An increasing sequence $A=\{a_1<a_2<\cdots\}$ of positive integers is *sum-free in the subset-sum sense* if no selected element is expressible as a sum of two or more **distinct smaller** selected elements. [Erdős Problem #876](https://www.erdosproblems.com/876) asks how small its consecutive gaps $a_{n+1}-a_n$ can be, especially whether one can arrange gaps less than $n$.

The literal inequality $a_{n+1}-a_n<n$ for **every** positive $n$ is impossible already at $n=1$. The interesting mathematical reading is therefore an eventual gap inequality, but its precise quantifier should be checked against the original Erdős 1998 chapter before formal admission.

This is not the same as ordinary sum-free sets that forbid $x+y=z$ with repetition allowed. Here sums may use any number of distinct earlier elements.

## A small example

The finite set

$$
A=\{4,5,6,7,8\}
$$

satisfies the #876 condition: the two smallest selected numbers already sum to $9>8$, so no member can be a sum of two or more smaller members. But $4+5+6=7+8=15$, so this set is **not admissible in the stronger sense of Problem #875**, which forbids equal subset sums having different numbers of summands.

For a finite $A\subseteq[1,N]$, let $H(N)$ denote the greatest size satisfying the #876 condition. There is an elementary exact theorem:

$$
\boxed{H(N)=\lfloor N/2\rfloor+1.}
$$

The lower construction is the interval $\{\lceil N/2\rceil,\ldots,N\}$: even its two smallest distinct members have sum exceeding $N$.

## What is known

The primary paper [Deshouillers, Erdős and Melfi, *On a Question About Sum-Free Sequences*](https://libra.unine.ch/bitstreams/142bfa87-1d9c-44b6-8269-2b2903cdc132/download), *Discrete Mathematics* **200** (1999), 49–54, defines this exact subset-sum-free predicate. Its Theorem 1 proves zero asymptotic density. Its Theorem 3 constructs an infinite sum-free sequence for which $a_{n+1}/a_n\to1$. Theorem 4 constructs, for every $\delta>0$, a sum-free sequence with

$$
a_n\sim n^{3+\delta}.
$$

These are published **sequence growth** or ratio results, not a theorem that every individual gap is eventually smaller than its index. The distinction matters because smoothness in the ratio and polynomial growth do not determine the largest isolated gaps.

Łuczak and Schoen (2000), cited by the [maintained #876 page](https://www.erdosproblems.com/876), give stronger counting-function results; these also do not by themselves settle the gap inequality.

A July 2026 [third-party working report](https://www.erdosproblemaday.com/report/876) records a proposed argument by Liam Price that every infinite sum-free sequence actually satisfies

$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n+1}-a_n}{n}=\infty.
$$

If valid, this would refute the eventual gap conjecture. The report explicitly calls the submission a *partial claimed proof* and says it was not independently verified. The underlying proof has not been obtained and checked by GCL. The maintained catalogue remains **open**; neither claimed theorem nor official status is silently promoted here.

## GCL's current state

The finite formula follows from a matching upper bound. If a nonempty sum-free subset $A\subseteq[1,N]$ has maximum element $m$, then for each pair $\{x,m-x\}$ of **distinct** positive integers below $m$, at most one member may belong to $A$. The pairs partition all integers below $m$ except the midpoint $m/2$ when $m$ is even. Counting one choice from each pair, the optional midpoint, and $m$ itself gives

$$
|A|\le \lfloor m/2\rfloor+1\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor+1.
$$

This matches the upper-half construction. Note that the midpoint must be treated correctly: $m/2+m/2=m$ is **not** a prohibited representation because the smaller elements would not be distinct.

GCL's exact program governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_875_876.py implements both the stronger #875 admissibility predicate and the weaker #876 subset-sum-free predicate. Its check reports 1,942 terminal-interval cases, 36 finite formula assertions, direct/DP agreement for $N=8,10,12,14$, and additional exact DP extrema at $N=16,18,20,24,30,36$. The finite witness $\{4,5,6,7,8\}$ passes #876 and fails #875.

These calculations support the exact finite theorem and the definition comparison, not an assertion about all infinite sequences.

## What remains

The bounded successor is an *independent audit of the July 2026 limsup divergence claim*. Required input is the actual submitted proof, not merely the working report, together with the distinct-summand definition and a precise eventual-index formulation. The target is a complete proof or explicit counterexample to

$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n+1}-a_n}{n}=\infty
$$

for every infinite subset-sum-free sequence. Acceptance requires an auditable source with all quantifiers and combinatorial lemmas exposed, independent review or formal compilation if available, and tests against the 1999 construction. Falsification could take the form of one infinite valid sequence with uniformly bounded normalized gaps, or a false intermediate lemma.

Stop before a solved-status change until the proof is actually accessible and independently adjudicated. The separate bounded formalization of $H(N)=\lfloor N/2\rfloor+1$ can proceed without any assumption about the 2026 claim.
