## The question in plain language

For a finite set of integers A whose doubling |A+A|<=K|A| for some fixed K, show the product set AA={ab:a,b in A} has size at least c_K |A|²/log|A| (for |A|>=2). K-dependence matters: the implied constant may depend on the doubling constant, not on |A|.

## A small example

A={1,2,3,4} has A+A={2,...,8} of size 7, <=2|A|. Its product set is {1,2,3,4,6,8,9,12,16} of size 9. This small calculation demonstrates distinct-products counting but does not establish the asymptotic inequality.

## What is known

Maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/818, read 2026-10-14, credits József Solymosi [So09d], *Bounding multiplicative energy by the sumset*, Advances in Mathematics (2009), 402–408. The stronger energy inequality |AA| >= const |A|^4/(|A+A|² log |A|) (with positivity and sufficiently large sets) implies the asserted K-dependent product bound. Full original 2009 proof not independently reconstructed; do not assert constant independent of K.

One formulation of the established energy result is

$$

|AA|\gg \frac{|A|^4}{|A+A|^2\log |A|}

$$

for finite positive sets with $|A|>1$. If $|A+A|\leq K|A|$, substitution gives $|AA|\gg_K |A|^2/\log |A|$. The dependence on fixed additive-doubling constant $K$ is important; neither a uniform constant over all $K$ nor a theorem for arbitrary signed sets follows from this citation.

**Extension to signed integer sets.** The original Solymosi energy inequality is applied here to positive integers, but the catalogue asks about arbitrary integers. For an arbitrary finite integer set $A$ of size at least four, one of its positive and negative subfamilies has at least $(|A|-1)/2$ elements. Call it $P$. If it is negative, replace its elements by their absolute values. Then the numbers of pair sums and pair products do not change under the sign flip, and

$$

|P+P|\leq |A+A|\leq K|A|\leq3K|P|,\qquad |PP|\leq|AA|.

$$

Apply the positive-set inequality to $P$ with doubling constant $3K$. This yields $|AA|\gg_K |A|^2/\log|A|$. Finitely many smaller cases can be absorbed by adjusting the constant depending on $K$. Thus the signed-integer formulation follows by a direct elementary reduction; it is not an extra claim of Solymosi's positive-set theorem.
