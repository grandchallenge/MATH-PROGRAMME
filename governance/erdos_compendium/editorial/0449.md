## The question in plain language

Suppose the positive divisors of an integer $n$ are arranged in increasing order. Two divisors $a<b$ are called *close* here if the larger is less than twice the smaller:

$$
a<b<2a.
$$

Let $\tau(n)$ be the total number of positive divisors, and let $r(n)$ be the number of **distinct pairs** $(a,b)$ of positive divisors satisfying that strict inequality. Erdős Problem #449 asks whether, for each fixed $\varepsilon>0$,

$$
r(n)<\varepsilon\tau(n)
$$

for *almost all* positive integers $n$. “Almost all” means natural density one.

The conjecture proposes that close pairs become negligible compared with the total number of divisors for typical large integers. That conjecture is false. Indeed, the reported stronger result is that **for every fixed $K>0$, a positive proportion of integers satisfy $r(n)>K\tau(n)$**. Thus the number of close pairs can overwhelm the number of divisors by an arbitrarily large prescribed factor, on a set with positive lower natural density.

## A small example

For $n=6$, the positive divisors are

$$
1,2,3,6.
$$

There is exactly one close pair, $(2,3)$: it satisfies $2<3<4$. The ratios for $(1,2)$ and $(3,6)$ are exactly $2$, and the strict upper inequality excludes them. Therefore

$$
\tau(6)=4,\qquad r(6)=1.
$$

Now divide the positive real axis into dyadic intervals $[2^j,2^{j+1})$. Denote by $\tau^+(n)$ the number of these intervals containing at least one divisor of $n$. For $n=6$, the occupied intervals are $[1,2)$, $[2,4)$, and $[4,8)$, so $\tau^+(6)=3$.

A commonly quoted explanation on the maintained Erdős Problems page asserts

$$
r(n)+\tau(n)\geq\frac{\tau(n)^2}{\tau^+(n)}.
$$

**This literal inequality is false**: at $n=6$ the left side is $5$ and the right side is $16/3>5$. That error concerns an auxiliary inequality, not the original conjecture. A universally correct and sufficient version is

$$
\boxed{\;2r(n)+\tau(n)\geq\frac{\tau(n)^2}{\tau^+(n)}.\;}
$$

The coefficient $2$ makes a difference. With $n=6$, the corrected inequality becomes $6\geq16/3$, which is true. The result does not rely solely on testing examples; we prove it next.

## What is known

The question is stated by Erdős and Graham on printed page 89 of *Old and New Problems and Results in Combinatorial Number Theory* (1980), where it is attributed to Erdős and Hall.

The maintained [Erdős Problems #449](https://www.erdosproblems.com/449) reports that the conjecture is **disproved** and cites a related argument in Hall and Tenenbaum's *Divisors* (1988), §4.6. The exact book passage was not directly accessible during this audit, so its detailed formulation is not reproduced as though checked.

Fortunately, an elementary argument recovers the stronger positive-density statement without needing the unavailable book passage.

First, let $a_j$ count divisors of $n$ in the dyadic interval $[2^j,2^{j+1})$. Any two **different** divisors within one such interval form a close pair, because the larger is less than twice the smaller. Consequently,

$$
r(n)\geq\sum_j\binom{a_j}{2}
=\frac12\left(\sum_j a_j^2-\tau(n)\right).
$$

There are $\tau^+(n)$ nonempty intervals, and $\sum_j a_j=\tau(n)$. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality now gives

$$
\sum_j a_j^2\geq\frac{\tau(n)^2}{\tau^+(n)},
$$

which proves the corrected bound.

Second, choose a positive integer $h$ and consider

$$
m=6^h=2^h3^h.
$$

Its divisors are precisely $2^i3^j$ with $0\leq i,j\leq h$. Hence

$$
\tau(m)=(h+1)^2.
$$

There are at most $3h+1$ possible dyadic intervals containing these divisors, because $\log_2 m=h\log_2 6<3h$. The bound already established therefore yields

$$
\frac{r(m)}{\tau(m)}
\geq\frac12\left(\frac{(h+1)^2}{3h+1}-1\right).
$$

The right side tends to infinity as $h\to\infty$. Thus, for any prescribed constant $K>0$, we can fix an $h$ so large that $r(6^h)>K\tau(6^h)$.

Third, multiply this fixed $m=6^h$ by any positive integer $t$ coprime to $6$. Since $m$ and $t$ are coprime, divisor counting is multiplicative:

$$
\tau(mt)=\tau(m)\tau(t).
$$

For each divisor $b$ of $t$, multiply every close pair of divisors of $m$ by $b$. The resulting close pairs of $mt$ are all distinct, by uniqueness of the coprime divisor factorization. Thus

$$
r(mt)\geq r(m)\tau(t)>K\tau(mt).
$$

Finally, the integers $t$ coprime to $6$ have natural density $1/3$. Therefore the integers

$$
n=6^h t,\qquad \gcd(t,6)=1,
$$

have natural density exactly $1/(3\cdot6^h)>0$, and **every one of them satisfies $r(n)>K\tau(n)$**. This proves the advertised positive-density conclusion.

The construction is not a proposed new theorem. It is an elementary reconstruction of a result already reported in the historical literature, with all steps exposed so they can be checked.
