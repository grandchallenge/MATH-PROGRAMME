## The question in plain language

Consider a collection of triples drawn from an $n$‑element vertex set. This is a $3$‑uniform hypergraph. For a fixed integer $k\ge5$, forbid every configuration with $k$ vertices carrying $k-2$ distinct triples.

How many triples can remain? The extremal function $\mathrm{ex}_3(n,\mathcal F_k)$ is the maximum possible number. [Erdős Problem #1076](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1076) asked whether, for every fixed $k$,

$$
\mathrm{ex}_3(n,\mathcal F_k)\sim\frac{n^2}{6}
\qquad(n\to\infty).
$$

The phrase **for fixed $k$** is important. The statement does not claim the same finite threshold for arbitrary $k$ growing with $n$.

## A small example

A simple triple system can be built from two disjoint triples on six vertices, such as $\{1,2,3\}$ and $\{4,5,6\}$. This has only two edges, so it cannot contain a forbidden configuration with three edges when $k=5$.

Another useful benchmark is a *linear* triple system: no pair of vertices occurs in more than one triple. Each triple contains three unordered vertex pairs, and there are only $\binom n2$ pairs altogether. Thus an elementary double count gives

$$
|E|\le\frac{1}{3}\binom n2=\frac{n(n-1)}6.
$$

This pair-counting result by itself does **not** show that a linear triple system avoids every $\mathcal F_k$. That avoidance property, while still reaching nearly this bound, is the difficult part.

## What is known

**The asymptotic conjecture was proved.** The [maintained catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1076) credits independent results by Bohman and Warnke (2019), and Glock, Kühn, Lo and Osthus (2020). Their work constructs sufficiently sparse approximate Steiner triple systems to achieve the required asymptotic edge count while avoiding the forbidden configurations.

The question originated with Brown, Erdős and Sós in 1973 and appears in Erdős's 1974 survey on page 81. Their earlier work established the correct *quadratic order* but not the sharp leading constant for every fixed $k$.

## What remains

Editorial review should check the original Brown–Erdős–Sós formulation, then inspect the two independent asymptotic proofs for fixed‑$k$ assumptions, design existence, and any auxiliary regularity or absorption hypotheses. Finite small systems can test the definitions but cannot verify the asymptotic construction.
