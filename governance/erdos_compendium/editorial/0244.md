## The question in plain language

Fix a real base $C>1$. Consider the set of positive integers represented as

$$
p+\lfloor C^k\rfloor,\qquad p\text{ prime},\quad k\ge0.
$$

Erdős and Kalmár asked whether this set has **positive natural density for every real base $C>1$**. The floor is essential for noninteger bases, and $C$ must be fixed as the number of integers counted tends to infinity.

## A small example

When $C=2$, this is the classical prime-plus-powers-of-two representation problem. When $C=3/2$, the first few floors are $1,1,2,3,5,\ldots$; duplicated values must be counted only once in a **set** of represented integers.

Numerically observing many represented numbers up to a large cutoff does not establish a positive limiting density. The definition concerns the number of **distinct** integers represented, not the number of their representations.

## What is known

The universal claim is **OPEN**. Romanoff (1934) proved positive-density results for every integer base $C\ge2$. Ding (2025) proved the required positive-density statement for **Lebesgue-almost every real $C>1$**.

Almost every does not mean every: a measure-zero exceptional collection of bases could still contain counterexamples. The maintained [Erdős #244](https://www.erdosproblems.com/244), updated October 2025, retains the open classification.

## What remains

A bounded task enumerates $p+\lfloor C^k\rfloor$ with $p$ prime and $k\ge0$ for several rational bases and finite cutoffs. Acceptance: deduplicate repeated floor values, report **distinct represented integers**, and distinguish empirical proportions from a proved natural density. A research successor must address the genuinely exceptional noninteger bases not covered by Ding's almost-everywhere theorem.
