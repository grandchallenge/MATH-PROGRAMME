However, the **piecewise formula currently printed in the maintained catalogue is mathematically inconsistent with explicitly proved small cases**. This article preserves the solved historical classification and records a separate source-correction obligation.

## The question in plain language

Take $N$ distinct points in the Euclidean plane. These points determine angles by choosing three distinct points and designating one as the vertex. Among all those angles, let $L(P)$ be the **largest angle determined by the configuration** $P$.

Now allow the point configuration to vary, and try to make its largest angle as small as possible. Blumenthal's minimax quantity is

$$
\alpha_N=\inf_{\substack{P\subset\mathbb R^2\\|P|=N}}L(P),
\qquad N\ge3.
$$

The order of operations matters: **first maximize within a chosen point set, then minimize over all point sets**. Equivalently, $\alpha_N$ is the largest angle threshold that every configuration of $N$ points is guaranteed to reach (up to whether the infimum is attained). A collinear triple may determine an angle of $\pi$; configurations are not silently assumed to be in general position.

Erdős asked for an exact determination of the sequence $\alpha_N$. By placing points carefully, one can sometimes keep every angle substantially below a straight angle, but with many points an increasingly large angle becomes unavoidable. The maintained [Erdős #504 entry](https://www.erdosproblems.com/504) classifies the historical problem **SOLVED**.

## A small example

For three noncollinear points, the angles of their triangle sum to $\pi$. Consequently, at least one angle is at least $\pi/3$. An equilateral triangle has all angles equal to $\pi/3$, so

$$
\boxed{\alpha_3=\frac{\pi}{3}.}
$$

This is a complete elementary theorem. It is not an approximation and does not require reading Sendov's later paper.

For a regular $N$‑gon, three vertices determine an inscribed triangle. If the circular gaps, in numbers of polygon steps, between the three cyclically ordered vertices are $a,b,c$, then

$$
a,b,c\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\qquad a+b+c=N.
$$

The three triangle angles are $\pi a/N$, $\pi b/N$ and $\pi c/N$. Thus the largest angle from all triangles on a regular $N$‑gon is exactly

$$
L(P_{\mathrm{regular},N})=\pi\frac{N-2}{N},
$$

attained by taking **three consecutive vertices** (one cyclic gap equals $N-2$). This gives valid, explicit *upper bounds* on the minimax value, not automatic lower bounds.

For example, the equilateral triangle gives $\pi/3$, the square gives $\pi/2$, and the regular pentagon gives $3\pi/5$. Published original work by Erdős and Szekeres establishes that all three are exact minimax values, not merely upper-bound examples.

### A demonstrable error in the maintained formula

The catalogue states that, for the indicated lower part of $2^{m-1}<N\le2^m$, the answer is

$$
\alpha_N=\pi\left(1-\frac{1}{2m-1}\right).
$$

For $m=3$, its stated range includes $N=5$. It therefore predicts

$$
\alpha_5=\frac{4\pi}{5}.
$$

But the regular pentagon already gives the rigorous inequality $\alpha_5\le3\pi/5$, and the original Erdős–Szekeres paper proves **equality** at $3\pi/5$. Therefore the catalogue's displayed formula is **false as written**. At $N=3$, the catalogue places the case in its **other branch** and predicts $\pi/2$, also contradicting the elementary value $\pi/3$.

This is not a counterexample to Sendov's theorem itself. It is an error in the cited *summary formula* until Sendov's exact printed theorem can be inspected.

## What is known

### Erdős and Szekeres's original verified results

P. Erdős, *Some unsolved problems* (1961), printed p.244, Section III.2, [original scanned page](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1961-22.pdf#page=24), defines the infimum of the largest angle and summarizes the earlier work of G. Szekeres and their joint results.

The full earlier paper P. Erdős and G. Szekeres, *On some extremum problems in elementary geometry*, *Annales Universitatis Scientiarum Budapestinensis*, Sectio Mathematica **3** (1960), 53–62, has been retrieved. Its [original printed p.54](https://renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1960-09.pdf#page=2) was **visually inspected**. The authors state the exact values

$$
\alpha_3=\frac{\pi}{3},\quad
\alpha_4=\frac{\pi}{2},\quad
\alpha_5=\frac{3\pi}{5},\quad
\alpha_6=\alpha_7=\alpha_8=\frac{2\pi}{3}.
$$

They also prove $\alpha_{2^m}=\pi(1-1/m)$ for $m\ge3$, combining a matching universal lower bound with a construction. The first-page historical statement $\alpha_{2^m-1}=\pi(1-1/m)$ needs the appropriate low-index qualification: substituting $m=2$ would give the false value $\alpha_3=\pi/2$, even though the paper's own p.54 explicitly gives $\pi/3$. The validated small cases therefore take precedence over an overextended quantifier in a compressed summary.

The sequence is **nondecreasing** in the number of points. If $Q$ is an $N$‑point subset of an $(N+1)$‑point set $P$, then $L(P)\ge L(Q)\ge\alpha_N$. Taking the infimum over $P$ proves

$$
\alpha_{N+1}\ge\alpha_N.
$$

In particular, the false catalogue prediction $\alpha_5=4\pi/5$ would also exceed the verified $\alpha_6=2\pi/3$, violating monotonicity. This is a second independent check of the problem in the catalogue formula.

### Sendov's subsequent resolution

The current catalogue cites Blagovest Sendov's *On a conjecture of P. Erdős and G. Szekeres*, *Comptes rendus de l'Académie bulgare des sciences* **45** (1992), 17–20, followed by *Angles in a plane configuration of points*, the same journal **46** (1993), no.5, 27–30. A more extensive article is *Minimax of the angles in a plane configuration of points*, *Acta Mathematica Hungarica* **69** (1995), 27–46, [DOI 10.1007/BF01874605](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01874605).

The [1995 original publisher record](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01874605) confirms publication details. Sendov's [author bibliography](https://www.math.bas.bg/serdica/bibl.pdf) independently lists the 1992, 1993 and 1995 papers. However, **the complete 1993 and 1995 theorem proofs are not freely accessible in this audit**. Their exact displayed formulas cannot yet be cross-checked against the catalogue's inconsistent summary.

A possible repair is that the erroneous first branch may have lost a numerator factor of two, namely $\pi(1-2/(2m-1))$. This gives the verified values $\alpha_3=\pi/3$ and $\alpha_5=3\pi/5$. **That agreement does not establish the formula for other $N$.** It is an editorial hypothesis, not an admitted replacement for the original Sendov theorem.
