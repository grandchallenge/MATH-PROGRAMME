## The question in plain language

Imagine adding distinct points to a circle, one at a time. After $n$ points have been placed, they divide the circumference into $n$ positive gaps. An *$r$‑span* is the combined length of $r$ successive gaps, counted cyclically. Let $M_n^{(r)}$ and $m_n^{(r)}$ be the longest and shortest such spans.

The 1949 problem of de Bruijn and Erdős asks whether *every infinite sequence of distinct insertions* must eventually exhibit increasingly pronounced imbalance when the number $r$ of adjacent gaps considered grows. Define

$$
\mu_r=\inf_{(x_n)\ \mathrm{distinct}}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{M_n^{(r)}}{m_n^{(r)}}.
$$

The original conjecture asks whether

$$
\sup_{r\ge 1}r(\mu_r-1)=\infty.
$$

The factor $r$ and the order of the infimum, limit superior, and supremum are essential. Other, related 1949 conjectures involve one-sided extremal quantities $\Lambda_r$ and $\lambda_r$.

## A small example

Place five points temporarily at equal intervals, $0,1/5,2/5,3/5,4/5$ around the unit circle. Every gap is $1/5$. For $r=2$, all five cyclic two-gap spans have length $2/5$, so $M_5^{(2)}/m_5^{(2)}=1$. This is a correct *finite snapshot*, not an infinite counterexample: one cannot infer anything about the limit superior from a single equally spaced stage.

A useful independent deduction needs no numerical simulation. If a theorem establishes, uniformly for **every** admissible infinite point sequence and all sufficiently large integers $r$,

$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{M_n^{(r)}}{m_n^{(r)}}
\ge 1+\frac{\log r}{100r},
$$

then taking the infimum over sequences gives $\mu_r\ge 1+(\log r)/(100r)$. Therefore $r(\mu_r-1)\ge (\log r)/100$, which diverges. This verifies the last logical step **conditionally on the theorem**, not its difficult proof.

## What is known

De Bruijn and Erdős introduced the problem in *Sequences of Points on a Circle* (1949). Korsky's May 2026 preprint reports an improved fixed‑$r$ bound $1+r/(r^2-1)$ for $r\ge2$; it alone does not establish divergence of $r(\mu_r-1)$.

Korsky's September 2026 preprint, *A Resolution of the de Bruijn--Erdős Consecutive-Gap Problem* (arXiv:2609.07196), reports the stronger logarithmic lower bound displayed above. An updated version also asserts two one-sided $\sqrt{\log r}$ discrepancy bounds corresponding to the other historical conjectures. The reported estimates would settle the ratio conjecture as stated, but this manuscript does not assert an independent proof-level verification of the preprint.
