## The question in plain language

Fix an integer $N\geq1$. How many distinct positive integers can be selected from $[1,N]$ if the least common multiple of **every pair** of selected integers is at most $N$? Erdős Problem 441 asks for the largest possible cardinality $g(N)$, and whether a particular construction of small integers and even integers is always extremal. This is different from Problem #440: there the least common multiple was tested only on **consecutive members** of an infinite sequence; here every pair in a finite set must pass the bound.

The condition is not simply that the product $ab$ be at most $N$, because $\mathrm{lcm}(a,b)=ab/\gcd(a,b)$. Numbers that share a large common divisor can coexist even when their ordinary product exceeds $N$. On the other hand, distinct large coprime numbers tend to violate the condition. The tension creates an extremal question that mixes divisibility and finite-set combinatorics.

## A small example

Take $N=8$ and select $A=\{1,2,4,8\}$. Every smaller member divides each larger member, so for any $a,b\in A$ the least common multiple is the larger of the two and is at most $8$. Thus $g(8)\geq4$. This is a complete exact verification of the pairwise condition, but it is not on its own an exhaustive proof that no larger set exists.

Erdős's proposed construction for large $N$ takes all integers up to approximately $\sqrt{N/2}$, together with the even integers from that threshold up to approximately $\sqrt{2N}$. If both numbers are in the small part, their product is at most $N/2$. If one lies in each part, their product is at most $N$. If both are even and at most $\sqrt{2N}$, their greatest common divisor is at least $2$, and consequently their least common multiple is at most $ab/2\leq N$. All pairs therefore satisfy the condition.

The number of selected integers in this construction is

$$
\sqrt{N/2}+\frac{\sqrt{2N}-\sqrt{N/2}}{2}+O(1)
=\sqrt{9N/8}+O(1).
$$

The calculation gives a rigorous lower bound for $g(N)$ with a leading constant $\sqrt{9/8}$, but does not prove exact finite optimality.

## What is known

Erdős posed the question in 1951 and revisited it in later work, including *Old and New Problems and Results in Combinatorial Number Theory* with Graham (1980), printed page 87. He proved an early bound $g(N)\leq2\sqrt N+O(1)$; Choi subsequently improved it. Chen (1998) established the sharp leading asymptotic

$$
g(N)\sim\sqrt{9N/8}.
$$

Chen and Dai (2006) strengthened the upper error estimate to $g(N)\leq\sqrt{9N/8}+O(\sqrt{N/\log N}\log\log N)$. These are published bounds for the extremal cardinality, not merely numerical experiments.

The proposed construction is nevertheless **not always exactly optimal**. Chen and Dai (2007) proved that infinitely many $N$ permit sets larger than Erdős's particular construction, with an advantage linked to iterated logarithms. Thus the originally suggested exact extremality is disproved while its leading asymptotic is correct. The maintained [Erdős Problem #441](https://www.erdosproblems.com/441) explains both outcomes. It would be incorrect to describe the entire question simply as “proved” or “disproved” without distinguishing the asymptotic formula from exact attainment.
