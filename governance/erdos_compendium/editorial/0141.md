## The question in plain language

For each fixed integer $k\ge3$, do **some $k$ successive primes**, with no other primes between them, form an arithmetic progression? The progression's common difference must be positive. The word *consecutive* is essential: an arithmetic progression made entirely of primes need not consist of consecutive members of the ordered prime sequence.

## A small example

The primes $3,5,7$ are three consecutive primes, and their common difference is two. Thus the existence question is settled for $k=3$, but this one example does not answer the question for every arbitrarily large $k$. Finite searches have found such progressions for $k\le10$. The related request for **infinitely many** such progressions is open even when $k=3$.

## What is known

**OPEN for arbitrary lengths.** Green and Tao proved that the primes contain arithmetic progressions of arbitrary finite length, but those primes are **not required to be consecutive** in the ordered list of all primes. Thus Green–Tao's theorem does not solve Erdős #141. The maintained [problem #141](https://www.erdosproblems.com/141) records the finite search boundary and the distinct infinitely-many question. Neither a finite table of longest examples nor the Green–Tao theorem proves that a consecutive-prime progression exists for every length.

## GCL's current state

Pinned status **OPEN** and original [issue #1410](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1410). The [frozen Formal Conjectures 141 file](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/141.lean) separately defines *successive primes* and *arithmetic progression*. Its finite three-prime test is present, but the general assertion remains a research statement with a proof hole.

The original source assignment has an authenticated [RESULT/1 report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1410#issuecomment-6166660596). This is unadjudicated source evidence, not a proof certificate or accepted article.

## What remains

Enumerate consecutive-prime windows of lengths three through six below one million. Acceptance: independently test primality, verify absence of intervening primes, check constant differences, and record the exact largest inspected bound. Avoid claiming general existence or infinitely many occurrences from finite examples.
