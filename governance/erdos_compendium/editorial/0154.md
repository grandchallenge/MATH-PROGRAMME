## The question in plain language

A Sidon subset $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ is *asymptotically maximum* when $|A|/\sqrt N\to1$ along a sequence with $N\to\infty$. Erdős, Sárközy and Sós asked whether the set of distinct sums $A+A$ becomes **equidistributed modulo each fixed integer $m\ge2$**. In particular, do approximately half the sums have even parity and half odd?

For every fixed residue $r\bmod m$, the precise requirement is that the fraction of distinct elements of $A+A$ congruent to $r$ tends to $1/m$. This is stronger than checking parity alone, and the condition that $m$ be fixed matters.

## A small example

Take $A=\{1,2,4\}$. Its six unordered sums are $2,3,4,5,6,8$: four are even and two odd. This small, not asymptotically maximum family does not contradict equidistribution, which is a limiting statement.

If an $n$ element Sidon set contains $a_r$ members in residue class $r\bmod m$, then unordered pair sums are unique. The number of sums congruent to $u$ is the corresponding ordered convolution

$$
\frac12\sum_{r+s\equiv u\bmod m}a_ra_s+O(n),
$$

where the $O(n)$ term accounts for diagonal pairs. If each $a_r=n/m+o(n)$, the convolution is $n^2/(2m)+o(n^2)$ and total distinct sums number $n(n+1)/2$, proving equidistribution of $A+A$.

## What is known

**PROVED.** Bernt Lindström, [*Well Distribution of Sidon Sets in Residue Classes*](https://doi.org/10.1006/jnth.1997.2217), *Journal of Number Theory* 69 (1998), 197–200, proved asymptotic equidistribution of dense Sidon sets $A$ themselves for fixed moduli. Mihail Kolountzakis, [*On the Uniform Distribution in Residue Classes of Dense Sets of Integers with Distinct Sums*](https://doi.org/10.1006/jnth.1998.2351), *Journal of Number Theory* 76 (1999), 147–153, provided quantitative refinements, allowing some growing moduli under stated restrictions. The elementary Sidon convolution above transfers fixed-modulus distribution from $A$ to $A+A$.

A [third-party Lean formalization](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/154) is reported for Lindström's result and the sumset consequence, but the complete Lean dependencies and build have not been independently replayed under GCL's protected provider lock. The original paper abstracts and precise fixed-modulus theorem were verified.

## What remains

A bounded successor formalizes the unordered Sidon-sum residue convolution and its $O(n)$ diagonal correction for fixed $m$. Acceptance: exact residue counts from arbitrary finite Sidon fixtures match the formula, and the limit from $a_r=n/m+o(n)$ to sumset equidistribution follows with correct normalization. Stop without claiming uniformity for unrestricted growing $m$ or an independently accepted Lean proof.
