## The question in plain language

For every fixed C>0, are there infinitely many positive integers a,b,n with a+b>n+C log n, where the denominator of n!/(a!b!) in reduced form contains only prime factors bounded by a constant K depending on C but NOT on a,b,n? The maintained source says PROVED (externally LEAN-reported). This is not the same as n!/(a!b!) being integral, and K cannot depend on n.

## A small example

Take a=4,b=4,n=6: n!/(a!b!)=720/576=5/4, so the denominator has only prime factor 2. This is a finite control illustrating the *reduced* denominator, but is not a witness to every C or infinitude. For a=b=n≥3, n!/(a!b!)=1/n!, whose denominator includes arbitrarily large primes, showing the trivial #728 witness does NOT carry to #729.

## What is known

The maintained catalogue attributes a 2026 affirmative proof to Kevin Barreto and Leeham using ChatGPT-5.2 and Aristotle adapting #728. Pinned FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/729.lean encodes ∀C>0 ∃K≥3 with an INFINITE set of triples and no denominator prime p>K; the statement has a sorry body and links an external formal proof at plby/lean-proofs.
