## The question in plain language

A polynomial can have a complicated *lemniscate*: the region where its absolute value is at most one.

[Erdős Problem #1120](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1120) considers a **monic polynomial of degree $n$**, all of whose zeros are inside the closed unit disk, and the set

$$
E=\{z\in\mathbb C:|f(z)|\le1\}.
$$

How long must the shortest path inside $E$ be when it starts at the origin and finishes anywhere on the unit circle $|z|=1$?

The main question is about the **worst-case shortest length as a function of degree**, not the length for an arbitrarily chosen example.

## A small example

For $f(z)=z^n$,

$$
E=\{z:|z|\le1\}.
$$

The straight path from $0$ to $1$ lies entirely in $E$ and has length exactly one. Conversely, **any** curve from the origin to a point of modulus one has length at least one by the triangle inequality. Thus the shortest allowable length for this polynomial is exactly

$$
L(z^n)=1
$$

for every $n\ge1$.

This gives a sharp elementary example of the trivial lower bound, but it cannot bound the worst case over all monic degree‑$n$ polynomials.

## What is known

The catalogue attributes the original question to Erdős via **Hayman (1974), Problem 4.22**. It reports a result of **Clunie and Netanyahu**, communicated privately, that some path of the required kind always exists.

Erdős expected the maximum of these shortest lengths to increase with degree, although slowly. The exact growth remains **open**.

The difficulty comes from the geometry of the sublevel region: even though the zeros lie near the origin, the set $E$ need not resemble a disk. A connected route from the origin to the boundary may have to wind around complicated narrow regions.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source audit #2391](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2391#issuecomment-6169286077) proves the monomial case with exact metric reasoning and distinguishes the existence of a path from minimizing its length.

The source statement was recovered from the indexed [problem](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1120) and [LaTeX source](https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/1120). The reported original path-existence proof has not been obtained in a verifiable published form.

## What remains

Obtain the original Hayman statement and a citable proof of the path-existence lemma. Investigate certified shortest paths for low-degree polynomials through exact semialgebraic geometry, then bound the maximum over all allowed polynomials. A single monomial case is not an asymptotic result.
