## The question in plain language

For positive integers $a\leq B$, put the consecutive unit-fraction sum into lowest terms:

$$
\sum_{n=a}^{B}\frac{1}{n}=\frac{u_{a,B}}{v_{a,B}},
$$

where $u_{a,B}$ and $v_{a,B}$ are coprime integers and $v_{a,B}>0$. Erdős Problem #290 asks whether every starting point $a\geq1$ is eventually followed by an endpoint at which the reduced denominator gets smaller when the last term is added:

$$
\forall a\geq1\ \exists B>a,\qquad v_{a,B}<v_{a,B-1}.
$$

The quantifiers matter. This is not merely the claim that some denominator drop occurs, nor that drops occur for infinitely many starting points. It asks for at least one later endpoint for each fixed $a$. Van Doorn proves more: for every fixed $a$, there are infinitely many such endpoints, and the first one is at most linear in $a$.

There are two endpoint conventions in the evidence. The maintained [Erdős Problems entry](https://www.erdosproblems.com/290) writes the two adjacent sums with endpoints $b$ and $b+1$. The pinned [Formal Conjectures file](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/290.lean), blob `55bc70e2663d52304004b50d0a40da6bc1411dcd`, uses the pre-addition endpoint $b$:

$$
\forall a\in\mathbb N,\quad 1\leq a\to\exists b\in\mathbb N,\quad a<b\ \land\ v_{a,b+1}<v_{a,b}.
$$

Thus its $b$ corresponds to the question's $B-1$. This is an index shift, not a different mathematical assertion. The original Erdős–Graham citation is *Old and New Problems and Results in Combinatorial Number Theory* (1980), p. 34; the authenticated source audit did not obtain a direct scan for page-level quotation, so the book wording is retained here as a bibliographic and source-reported boundary rather than silently reconstructed.

## A small example

Take $a=3$. Exact fraction reduction gives

$$
\sum_{n=3}^{4}\frac1n=\frac13+\frac14=\frac7{12},
$$

$$
\sum_{n=3}^{5}\frac1n=\frac{47}{60},\qquad
\sum_{n=3}^{6}\frac1n=\frac{19}{20}.
$$

Consequently,

$$
v_{3,6}=20<60=v_{3,5}.
$$

So $B=6$ is a witness for $a=3$, while the formal pre-addition witness is $b=5$. This is an exact integer-arithmetic calculation, not a decimal approximation. A reproducible Python 3 check is:

```python
from fractions import Fraction

def h(a, b):
    return sum((Fraction(1, n) for n in range(a, b + 1)), Fraction(0))

for b in (4, 5, 6):
    q = h(3, b)
    print(b, q, q.denominator)
assert h(3, 6).denominator < h(3, 5).denominator
```

It prints `4 7/12 12`, `5 47/60 60`, and `6 19/20 20`. The authenticated [RESULT/1 audit](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1560#issuecomment-6137973521) records the same exact check and reports that the explicit witness construction was checked for $1\leq a\leq20$. A finite computation illustrates the statement; it cannot establish the universal quantifier over all positive $a$.

## What is known

The primary resolving source inspected for this article is Wouter van Doorn, “On the non-monotonicity of the denominator of generalized harmonic sums,” arXiv version 2, revised 23 July 2025. In its introduction, the paper defines $u_{a,b}/v_{a,b}$ and states that in the original all-coefficients-one case there are infinitely many $b>a$ with $v_{a,b}<v_{a,b-1}$. Its [Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03073v2#Thmiflargepthennotmonotone2) gives the explicit bound

$$
b(a)\leq4.374(a-1)\qquad(a\geq6),
$$

where $b(a)$ is the first successful endpoint. The same paper gives the simpler bound $b(a)\leq6(a-1)$ for $a>1$ and, in [Theorem 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03073v2#Thmiflargepthennotmonotone8),

$$
0.54<\liminf_{a\to\infty}\frac{b(a)-a}{\log a}<0.61.
$$

The theorem is stronger than the original existence question: it supplies infinitely many successful endpoints for every fixed $a$, an effective upper bound, and asymptotic information about the first one. The source archive identified by the audit has SHA-256 `3a838d971ea669b049f880e9c38c64dfd1f70482b63477d0c3d1bac063ee3904`.

Accordingly, the mathematical status of the registered question is **proved**, in the sense that this inspected primary preprint gives an affirmative theorem. The live maintained entry and the pinned catalogue record agree with that status. This wording does not turn a catalogue label, a web exposition, or a formal source reference into GCL certification.
