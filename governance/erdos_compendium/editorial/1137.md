## The question in plain language

Prime numbers have irregular gaps. Sometimes two comparatively large gaps occur consecutively. [Erdős Problem #1137](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1137) asks whether the product of two consecutive gaps is always small relative to the **square of the largest single gap**, in the limit.

Let $p_n$ be the $n$th prime and let

$$
d_n=p_{n+1}-p_n.
$$

The conjecture is

$$
\frac{\max_{2\le n<x}d_{n-1}d_n}
     {\bigl(\max_{1\le n<x}d_n\bigr)^2}
\longrightarrow0.
$$

The original formula uses a cutoff on the **index $n$**, not on the numerical size of the prime.

## A small example

Among the 2,262 primes up to 20,000, the largest gap between consecutive primes in that list is $52$. The largest product of neighboring gaps is $1,040$. Their finite ratio is

$$
\frac{1040}{52^2}=\frac5{13}.
$$

This computation is a bounded prime-**value** diagnostic rather than the original index cutoff. It illustrates the relative scale but cannot prove or refute a limiting ratio.

The numerator uses **neighboring** gaps, whereas the denominator can be determined by a completely different record gap.

## What is known

The problem remains **open** according to the maintained catalogue, citing Vardi's 1999 prime census.

Probabilistic heuristics, such as treating suitably normalized consecutive gaps as approximately independent exponential random variables, motivate conjectures about record gaps. They are **heuristics**, not rigorous bounds on the occurrence or adjacency of large prime gaps.

The asymptotic behavior of maximal gaps is itself a difficult problem, so the extra condition about *two neighboring* large gaps requires additional information.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated issue audit #2408](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2408#issuecomment-6181184965) records the difference between index and prime-value cutoffs. An exact sieve-based diagnostic verifies the finite ratio above.

## What remains

Source-review Vardi's original index convention, independently examine the behavior near record prime gaps over larger provable ranges, and separate numerical evidence and probabilistic models from unconditional asymptotic theorems.
