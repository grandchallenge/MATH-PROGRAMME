## The question in plain language

For each positive integer $n$, remove from $n(n+1)$ all prime factors except $2$ and $3$. More precisely write

$$
n(n+1)=2^{k(n)}3^{\ell(n)}m(n),\qquad \gcd(m(n),6)=1.
$$

Thus $k(n)=v_2(n(n+1))$ and $\ell(n)=v_3(n(n+1))$. Erdős asked whether

$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{2^{k(n)}3^{\ell(n)}}{n\log n}=\infty.
$$

The answer is not known. [Original-source catalogue, #933](https://www.erdosproblems.com/933).

## A small example

*A concrete family, and what it really proves*

Take $n=2^{3^r}$ for any integer $r\ge1$. Because $n$ is a power of two, $k(n)=3^r$. The lifting-the-exponent identity gives

$$
v_3(2^{3^r}+1)=v_3(2+1)+v_3(3^r)=r+1.
$$

Therefore

$$
2^{k(n)}3^{\ell(n)}=2^{3^r}3^{r+1}=3^{r+1}n,
$$

while $\log n=3^r\log 2$. Consequently the normalized expression is **exactly**

$$
\frac{2^{k(n)}3^{\ell(n)}}{n\log n}=\frac{3}{\log2},
$$

along this infinite sequence.

This proves a positive lower bound on the limsup, and in particular gives $2^{k(n)}3^{\ell(n)}>n\log n$ infinitely often. It does **not** make the normalized ratio unbounded: the ratio is constant on this family. Confusing the two claims would falsely resolve the open question.

## What is known

The maintained catalogue attributes to Mahler a more general result implying

$$
2^{k(n)}3^{\ell(n)}<n^{1+o(1)}
$$

as $n\to\infty$. This upper bound still permits the quotient by $n\log n$ to grow without bound, since the $o(1)$ exponent does not furnish a uniform logarithmic bound. The catalogue credits Steinerberger with the simple $n=2^{3^r}$ observation above, explaining Erdős's claim that the unnormalized inequality $2^k3^\ell>n\log n$ occurs infinitely often.

## Why the problem is difficult

The two neighbouring integers are coprime, so no prime can divide both. The $2$‐ and $3$‐adic valuations of their product must arise from either side separately. Producing a large power of two can force a controlled power of three in the neighbouring integer, but the elementary construction above generates only a fixed multiple of $n\log n$. The conjectured infinite limsup demands arbitrarily large multiples, substantially more than one successful family with a constant ratio.

## GCL's current state

The $2$‐ and $3$‐adic calculations and exact constant are derived here from standard divisibility identities. The Mahler upper bound is a literature attribution, not an independently proved result. The unbounded-limsup assertion remains open, and no machine formalization or numerical asymptotic certification is claimed.
