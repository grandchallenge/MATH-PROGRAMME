## The question in plain language

Divide the first $2n$ positive integers into two disjoint sets $A$ and $B$, each with $n$ elements. For any integer shift $k$, count the ordered pairs $(a,b)\in A\times B$ satisfying $a-b=k$. The *maximum overlap* is the largest such count over all shifts; let $M(n)$ be the smallest possible maximum overlap among all balanced partitions. [Erdős Problem #36](https://www.erdosproblems.com/36) asks for the precise limiting constant of $M(n)/n$, with related refinements.

This minimizes a **maximum** over shifts, so the average alone cannot determine the optimum.

## A small example

Every cross-pair contributes to exactly one difference $k=a-b$, hence

$$
\sum_k\#\{(a,b)\in A\times B:a-b=k\}=n^2.
$$

Because $A$ and $B$ are disjoint, zero never occurs as a difference, and all differences lie among the $4n-2$ nonzero integers between $-(2n-1)$ and $2n-1$. Pigeonhole therefore implies

$$
M(n)\ge\left\lceil\frac{n^2}{4n-2}\right\rceil.
$$

For $n=2$, the lower bound is one. The GCL audit's finite search reports $M(2)=1$. Such small cases and this weak bound do not settle the limiting constant.

## What is known

The maintained registry lists several stronger literature bounds but still marks the exact asymptotic value as OPEN. The audited source packet did not verify all historical paper hypotheses, so those constants should not be treated as newly certified here.

## GCL's current state

[Source audit #1305](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1305#issuecomment-6104193038) checks the balanced-partition normalization, ordered-pair convention and frozen formal definitions. It reports an exact enumeration for $n=1,\ldots,6$, obtaining $M(n)=1,1,2,2,3,3$, and derives the elementary cross-pair mass identity. The formal target is not proved by this arithmetic.

## What remains

Sharper structural control on the overlap profiles of balanced partitions is needed to identify the exact asymptotic optimum. Counting the total number of pairs alone cannot fix the best distribution of their differences.
