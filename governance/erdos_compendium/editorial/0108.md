## The question in plain language

The **chromatic number** $\chi(G)$ of a graph is the minimum number of vertex colours avoiding monochromatic edges. Its **girth** is the length of its shortest cycle, with forests assigned infinite girth. Erdős and Hajnal asked whether very large chromatic number forces a subgraph that is **both** highly chromatic and free of short cycles.

More precisely, for every integer $r\ge4$ and $k\ge2$, does a finite threshold $f(k,r)$ exist such that every graph of chromatic number at least $f(k,r)$ contains a subgraph $H$ satisfying

$$
\chi(H)\ge k,\qquad \mathrm{girth}(H)\ge r?
$$

The desired subgraph is not necessarily induced. The quantifier allows $f(k,r)$ to depend on **both** parameters but not on the host graph's number of vertices.

## A small example

A triangle has chromatic number three and girth three. Removing one edge leaves a bipartite path of girth infinity but chromatic number two. Thus simply deleting a shortest cycle can destroy the desired chromatic complexity.

There are triangle-free finite graphs of arbitrarily large chromatic number, so large chromatic number is not always caused by triangles. The problem asks to extract such high-girth complexity *inside an arbitrary graph* with sufficiently high chromatic number.

## What is known

The general statement remains **OPEN**. Rödl proved the case $r=4$ in [*On the chromatic number of subgraphs of a given graph*](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~erdosproblems/erdos/newproblems/SubgraphsOfLargeGirth.html), *Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society* 64 (1977), 370–371. Since a simple graph's cycles have length at least three, the constraint girth at least four means **triangle-free**. This proof does not cover $r=5$, which also forbids four-cycles.

The maintained catalogue separately asks if the thresholds necessarily grow very rapidly with $r$, specifically whether

$$
\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{f(k,r+1)}{f(k,r)}=\infty.
$$

That asymptotic refinement cannot be taken as a consequence of existence, and depends on precise conventions for the minimal threshold.

A related problem about preserving **infinite** chromatic number while increasing girth is another, stronger question; using an ordinary finite Erdős high-girth/high-chromatic construction is not enough to answer the extraction property.

## GCL's current state

The pinned catalogue and [maintained #108](https://www.erdosproblems.com/108), edited January 2026, mark it **OPEN**. Source issue [#1377](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1377) is preserved. Rödl's precise $r=4$ published bibliographic information is checked through the historical problem record, but his full proof has not been independently reproduced.

The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1377#issuecomment-6162330695). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded test is to enumerate graphs on up to six vertices, calculate girth and chromatic number exactly, and verify that every reported triangle-free subgraph has girth at least four. Acceptance: every tested subgraph uses only edges of its host, girth is computed from simple cycles, and forests are assigned infinite girth. This is a source-semantics fixture, not a proof of uniform $f(k,r)$ for unbounded graphs.
