## The question in plain language

An **ordering of the real numbers** is an arbitrary linear order $\prec$ on $\mathbb R$, not necessarily the usual numerical order. A *monotone three-term arithmetic progression* is a triple of distinct reals $a,a+d,a+2d$, with nonzero real $d$, that appears in the same or reverse order under $\prec$ as it does numerically.

Erdős and Graham asked whether **every** linear ordering of $\mathbb R$ must contain such a progression of some length $k\ge3$. A single linear order avoiding all monotone **three**-term progressions would refute every longer forced-length assertion.

## A small example

The usual order of the reals has progressions everywhere. In contrast, for the three numbers $1,2,3$, an order placing $2$ first and then $1,3$ prevents those particular points from appearing monotonically. Reordering just one triple does not solve the whole problem: the ordering must cover uncountably many reals and block **every** arithmetic triple.

A sequence or permutation of $\mathbb Z$ is not the same object as a linear order on $\mathbb R$. Extensions from rational to real orders require explicit construction rather than assuming countable and uncountable cases coincide.

## What is known

**DISPROVED.** Hayri Ardal, Tom Brown and Veselin Jungić, [*Chaotic Orderings of the Rationals and Reals*](https://doi.org/10.4169/amer.math.monthly.118.10.921), *American Mathematical Monthly* 118 (2011), 921–925, construct a linear ordering of **all real numbers** containing no monotone three-term arithmetic progression. Their primary published abstract explicitly identifies the Erdős–Graham conjecture and its negative answer.

The argument builds on orderings of the rationals and their extension to $\mathbb R$; it is not simply a permutation of a finite interval. If there is no monotone three-term progression, then there can be no monotone progression of any longer length, since consecutive three members of a longer progression form a forbidden triple.

## What remains

A bounded task enumerates all orderings of $\{1,\ldots,8\}$, checks exact nonzero-difference arithmetic triples, and finds orderings avoiding **monotone** triples. Acceptance: the checker treats increasing and decreasing numerical progressions symmetrically, and distinguishes the constructed total order from the usual order. Separately formalize the extension mechanism in the published paper with its choice assumptions; stop before presenting finite avoidance as a proof for $\mathbb R$.
