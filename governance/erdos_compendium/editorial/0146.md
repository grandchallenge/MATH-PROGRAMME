## The question in plain language

For a fixed graph $H$, $\mathrm{ex}(n,H)$ is the maximum number of edges in any $n$ vertex graph with no copy of $H$. A graph $H$ is *$r$ degenerate* when every nonempty subgraph has some vertex of degree at most $r$. Erdős and Simonovits conjectured that **every bipartite $r$ degenerate graph** satisfies

$$
\mathrm{ex}(n,H)=O_H(n^{2-1/r})
$$

for each positive integer $r$. A condition on degeneracy is weaker than a maximum-degree bound on an entire bipartition class.

## A small example

Any tree is 1-degenerate, since every nontrivial subtree has a leaf. A cycle is 2-degenerate, since it has no subgraph of minimum degree above two. The $r=2$ conjecture proposes an $O(n^{3/2})$ extremal bound for **every** bipartite 2-degenerate $H$; showing this bound for selected cycles or complete bipartite graphs does not establish the universal statement.

If a graph has minimum degree three in one of its subgraphs, it cannot be 2-degenerate. Therefore a counterexample must be verified against **all subgraphs**, not merely its average degree.

## What is known

**DISPROVED.** The maintained [Erdős #146](https://www.erdosproblems.com/146), updated 31 August 2026, credits an OpenAI model with a *connected bipartite 2-degenerate* counterexample $H$ for which

$$
\mathrm{ex}(n,H)\gg n^{3/2+c}
$$

with some fixed $c>0$. This contradicts the proposed $O(n^{3/2})$ bound for $r=2$. The recent upstream [Formal Conjectures file](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/main/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/146.lean) cites the publicly inspectable [OpenAI *ten-proofs* proof snapshot](https://github.com/openai/ten-proofs/blob/94bc0feb6a9ff12c7d31d6de640a725c9d43d2b6/CompactnessAndDegeneracy.lean). Its formal theorem describes a connected bipartite 2-degenerate $H$ and constants $c,\epsilon>0$ with an eventual lower bound of order $n^{3/2+\epsilon}$.

Alon, Krivelevich and Sudakov earlier proved the weaker $O(n^{2-1/(4r)})$ bound, and the conjectured exponent in the special case where one bipartition class has maximum degree $r$. Those **positive** theorems remain valid after a counterexample to the broader degeneracy claim.

## GCL's current state

The pinned issue [#1415](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1415) classifies #146 as disproved. The claim was checked against the currently published counterexample statement and exact named proof artifact. GCL has **not** independently built the external Lean project, audited its axioms or reconstructed all extremal construction estimates. Provenance of the external model result must remain explicit.

The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1415#issuecomment-6167237527). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded successor independently audits the counterexample graph's finite bipartiteness, connectedness and 2-degeneracy, then verifies how its protected extremal lower bound exceeds $n^{3/2}$. Acceptance requires an exact source commit, a toolchain and axiom receipt for the formal theorem, and a separate graph-level checker. Stop before calling an external proof an admitted GCL kernel certification.
