## The question in plain language

A graph $H$ is *$d$ degenerate* when every nonempty subgraph of $H$ has a vertex of degree at most $d$. Its ordinary Ramsey number $R(H)$ is the least $N$ such that every red-blue edge colouring of $K_N$ contains a monochromatic copy of $H$.

Burr and Erdős conjectured that for every fixed integer $d\ge1$, there is a constant $C_d$ such that **every $d$ degenerate graph on $n$ vertices** satisfies

$$
R(H)\le C_d n.
$$

The factor $C_d$ must depend on $d$ but **not** on the number of vertices. Merely having bounded average degree in $H$ itself is weaker than requiring all subgraphs to have bounded average degree.

## A small example

Every forest is 1-degenerate, because each nonempty subforest has a vertex of degree zero or one. The conjecture asserts that forests have Ramsey numbers growing only linearly in their number of vertices. A star can have very high maximum degree and still be 1-degenerate, so replacing degeneracy by a maximum-degree restriction discards important cases.

A cycle is 2-degenerate, and bipartite graphs can be highly degenerate; the statement covers both bipartite and nonbipartite graphs with the same fixed degeneracy parameter.

## What is known

**PROVED.** Choongbum Lee's [*Ramsey numbers of degenerate graphs*](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2017.185.3.2), *Annals of Mathematics* 185 (2017), 791–829, proves a quantitative form of the Burr–Erdős conjecture. For graphs $H$ of degeneracy $d$ and chromatic number $r$, the source theorem gives bounds of the form

$$
R(H)\le 2^{\,d2^{O(r)}}|V(H)|
$$

after a size threshold depending on $d$ and $r$. Since $r\le d+1$ for a $d$ degenerate graph, and finitely many smaller graph orders can be absorbed into a constant, the uniform linear in the number of vertices conclusion follows. A weaker explicit form recorded by the [maintained Erdős #163](https://www.erdosproblems.com/163) is $C_d\le2^{2^{O(d)}}$.

A sharper bound $C_d=2^{O(d)}$ remains conjectural. Thus resolution of the **linear in the number of vertices** question does not establish optimal dependence on $d$.

## What remains

A bounded successor formalizes the elementary implication from $d$ degeneracy to $(d+1)$ colourability via repeatedly removing a vertex of degree at most $d$, and derives the uniform constant $C_d$ from the published Ramsey theorem. Acceptance: an induction with exact quantifiers, explicit finite-size handling, and no assertion of the conjecturally sharper $2^{O(d)}$ dependence.
