## The question in plain language

A subset $A$ of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ is *non-averaging* if no member is the arithmetic mean of **two or more other distinct members**. Let $F(N)$ be the largest possible size of such a set. Originally asked by Straus and reported by Erdős, the problem is to determine the growth of $F(N)$. It is more restrictive than avoiding three-term arithmetic progressions: averages of three or more elements must also be excluded.

## A small example

The set $\{1,2,4\}$ is non-averaging. The set $\{1,2,3\}$ fails because $2=(1+3)/2$. To check a candidate, choose $a\in A$ and a subset $B\subseteq A\setminus\{a\}$ with $|B|\ge2$, and reject precisely when $\sum_{b\in B}b=|B|a$. The members of $B$ must be **distinct**; repetition would be a different condition.

## What is known

**SOLVED at the level of the growth exponent.** Bosznay's 1989 construction gives $F(N)\gg N^{1/4}$. Pham and Zakharov's [*Sharp bound for the Erdős–Straus non-averaging set problem*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14624) proves $F(N)\le N^{1/4+o(1)}$, building on work of Conlon, Fox and Pham. Therefore $\log F(N)/\log N\to1/4$. This does **not** supply a leading constant or an exact formula. Erdős and Sárközy's earlier upper bound was $O((N\log N)^{1/2})$.

The related #131 *non-dividing* condition is strictly stronger. It forbids a sum divisible by a selected member; non-averaging only forbids a sum equal to the **number of summands** times that member. The exact extremal functions should not be conflated.

## What remains

A bounded successor enumerates every subset of $\{1,\ldots,12\}$, checks all averages of two or more distinct other members using integer arithmetic, and computes the maximum size $F(N)$ for small $N$. Acceptance: $\{1,2,3\}$ fails, $\{1,2,4\}$ passes, and every rejection includes the witness $a,B$. Stop at finite checks; do not claim a new asymptotic constant.
