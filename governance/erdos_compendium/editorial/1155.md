## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1155](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1155). Delete a uniformly random triangle, including its three edges, over and over until no triangle remains. How many edges typically survive?

$$\mathbb Ef(n)\asymp n^{3/2}\quad\text{and}\quad f(n)=O(n^{3/2})\text{ with high probability?}$$

Begin K_n. At each step choose uniformly among triangles PRESENT in the current graph, DELETE their three edges and stop when no triangles remain. f(n)=number surviving edges is a random variable. Ask whether E f(n) is Theta(n^{3/2}) AND terminal edge count is O(n^{3/2}) with high probability/asymptotic almost surely. The n^{3/2+o(1)} whp statement is strictly weaker; expectation cannot automatically be derived from a typical exponent without tail control.

## A small example

Exact rational-state dynamic programming enumerating all triangle deletions yields expected terminal edges K3=0, K4=3, K5=4. In K4 deletion of any triangle leaves star K1,3. These finite expectations do not imply an asymptotic for growing n.

## What is known

https://www.erdosproblems.com/1155 OPEN, Bollobás–Erdős 1990 reported [Bo98,p.231], Vardi [Va99,3.61]. Grable 1997 gave P(f(n)>n^{7/4+epsilon})→0. Bohman–Frieze–Lubetzky [BFL15] proved f(n)=n^{3/2+o(1)} with high probability. This proves exponent but not exact O(n^{3/2}) nor E f(n)∼constant n^{3/2}; no stronger claim certified.
