## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1218](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1218) currently has source status **OPEN GCH PARTITION OBSTRUCTION**.

Assume the **generalized continuum hypothesis (GCH)**. Let $\beta=\omega_{\omega+1}$ be an **ordinal**, not the cardinal $\aleph_{\omega+1}$. The Erdős–Hajnal–Rado problem asks whether the following **negative triple partition relation** holds:

$$
\aleph_{\beta+1}\not\to(\aleph_\beta,4,4,\ldots)^3,
$$

with **countably infinitely many** colors other than the distinguished $\aleph_\beta$‑homogeneous target. To prove the negative arrow one must construct a coloring of **unordered triples** on $\aleph_{\beta+1}$ avoiding an $\aleph_\beta$‑sized homogeneous set in its first color and **any monochromatic four-set** in each other color. A finite 3-hypergraph with a coloring cannot prove this cardinal relation.

## A small example

On a five-vertex finite set there are $\binom53=10$ unordered triples. This is only a counting and **arity** check, accompanied by a receipt explicitly recording $\beta$ as an ordinal, GCH as a hypothesis, countably many additional color classes, the ambient cardinal $\aleph_{\beta+1}$ and first target $\aleph_\beta$. It supplies no coloring at the necessary cardinality or independence/consistency argument.
