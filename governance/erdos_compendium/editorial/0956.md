## The question in plain language

Suppose \(C\subset\mathbb R^2\) is **one compact convex set** and \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\) are translation vectors such that the translated copies \(C+x_i\) are pairwise disjoint. For two sets, define their Euclidean separation by
\[
\delta(C+x_i,C+x_j)=\inf\{\|u-v\|:u\in C+x_i,\ v\in C+x_j\}.
\]
Let \(h(n)\) be the maximum number of **unordered pairs of distinct copies** whose separation is **exactly one**. Erdős and Pach asked for the growth of \(h(n)\), particularly whether there exists \(c>0\) such that
\[
h(n)>n^{1+c}
\]
for **all sufficiently large \(n\)**. The catalogue continues to classify the problem **OPEN**, but a 2026 claim of the sharp exponent is pending review. [Original-source problem #956](https://www.erdosproblems.com/956).

The word *translates* is essential: all copies must have the same shape and orientation. Allowing unrelated convex sets is a different extremal problem.

## Concrete geometric example

Take \(C\) to be the closed Euclidean disk of radius \(1/4\) centered at the origin. Its translates with centers \((0,0)\) and \((3/2,0)\) are disjoint, and their minimum separation is
\[
\delta(C,C+(3/2,0))=\frac32-2\cdot\frac14=1.
\]
Thus \(h(2)\ge1\), and equality follows because two copies have only one unordered pair. This example tests the *set-to-set distance*: mistakenly counting the center distance \(3/2\) as the prescribed distance would give the wrong predicate.

There is also an exact reduction to ordinary point sets. A singleton \(C=\{0\}\) is compact and convex. For this choice, the translated sets are points and \(\delta(C+x_i,C+x_j)=\|x_i-x_j\|\). Consequently
\[
h(n)\ge u(n),
\]
where \(u(n)\) is the maximum number of unit-distance pairs among \(n\) distinct planar points. This is a correct lower bound, but does not immediately match the known upper exponent.

## Established bound and a genuinely pending claim

Erdős and Pach proved
\[
h(n)=O(n^{4/3})
\]
in *Variations on the theme of repeated distances*, Combinatorica **10** (1990), pp.261–269. Their separate bound for \(n\) arbitrary disjoint convex sets, not necessarily translates, is \(O(n^{7/5})\). These upper bounds must not be interchanged. [Maintained source statement](https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/956).

In April 2026, Przemek Chojecki posted *Unit distances between disjoint convex translates*, claiming an explicit construction with
\[
h(n)\ge c_0 n^{4/3}
\]
for an absolute \(c_0>0\) and all sufficiently large \(n\). Combined with the original upper bound, this would establish
\[
h(n)=\Theta(n^{4/3}),
\]
and would answer the requested superlinear lower bound. The note is [publicly available](https://www.ulam.ai/research/erdos956.pdf). The author traces its construction to a parabolic-grid idea in earlier work of Pavel Valtr, plus an additional **disjointness and Euclidean unit-separation conversion**.

This conversion is indispensable. Valtr's older norm-based unit-distance construction allows translates that overlap in their Euclidean interiors; it therefore is not, without modification, a configuration admitted by #956. An April 27 discussion reports a favorable informal check of the mathematical note, while separately warning that an Aristotle Lean file formalized substantially less than the note. A successful Lean build, without theorem equivalence, would not establish the full claim. [Problem discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/956).

An October 2026 independent research index records the 2026 construction, Valtr's historical announcement and the Lean files as **pending claims**, with no independently accepted proof of the full bound. The standing therefore remains **open with a claimed resolution**, not *proved by GCL*. [Research assessment](https://erdosproblems.ai/wiki/problems/distance_problems/E0956).

## Why the final step matters

A family of many unit distances in a specially chosen norm is not automatically a family of **set-to-set Euclidean distances one** between disjoint translates. Three predicates must hold simultaneously: a common compact convex shape, disjointness of every distinct pair of translates, and Euclidean separation exactly one for the claimed pairs. Losing any one predicate breaks source equivalence.

The quantifier on the lower bound matters as well: a construction working only for infinitely many selected \(n\) must include a valid padding/density-of-indices argument before it supplies the required **all sufficiently large \(n\)** bound.

## GCL's current state

The original upper theorem and the singleton lower reduction are established mathematical claims. The elementary two-disk example follows directly from disk geometry. The 2026 sharp construction and its incomplete or unreviewed formalization are **not admitted as a GCL theorem**, and no novelty or formal certification is asserted.
