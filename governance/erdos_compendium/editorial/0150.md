## The question in plain language

A *minimal vertex separator* of a graph is a set of vertices whose removal disconnects some pair of previously connected vertices, but for which no proper subset does the same. Let $c(n)$ be the maximum number of distinct minimal vertex separators in any graph on exactly $n$ vertices.

Erdős and Nešetřil asked whether the exponential growth rate

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}c(n)^{1/n}=\alpha
$$

exists and satisfies $\alpha<2$. They separately asked whether a particular construction attains $c(3m+2)=3^m$. The main rate-of-growth question and this proposed **exact finite formula** must be distinguished.

## A small example

Join two vertices $s,t$ by $m$ internally vertex-disjoint paths of length four. Each path has three internal vertices, so the graph has $3m+2$ vertices. To disconnect $s$ from $t$, one must remove at least one internal vertex of each path. Choosing **exactly one** of the three internal vertices on every path gives $3^m$ different minimal $s$–$t$ separators.

This construction gives $c(3m+2)\ge3^m$. It does not show that no other graph with the same number of vertices has **more** separators. The stronger equality is now known to be false.

## What is known

**PROVED:** the exponential rate exists and is strictly below two. Fomin, Kratsch, Todinca and Villanger (2008) obtained an upper constant $1.7087$. Fomin and Villanger (2012) and later simplified work by Gaspers and Mackenzie improve it to the golden ratio

$$
\alpha\le\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}\approx1.618.
$$

Gaspers and Mackenzie also give the currently reported lower estimate $\alpha\ge1.4457$, which exceeds $3^{1/3}\approx1.4422$ and **disproves** the proposed equality $c(3m+2)=3^m$ for all $m$. The maintained [Erdős #150](https://www.erdosproblems.com/150), edited June 2026, attributes the limit-existence argument to Bradač (2024). The primary full algorithms and separator enumeration proofs have not been independently recreated.

The distinction between existence of $\alpha$, the bound $\alpha<2$, and the rejected exact $3^m$ formula is retained. A source report or Lean statement alone does not certify those external combinatorial proofs under GCL governance.

The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1419#issuecomment-6167239633). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded task generates the $3m+2$ path construction for $m=1,2,3$ and enumerates its minimal $s$–$t$ vertex separators. Acceptance: exactly $3^m$ separator choices in that family, and an explicit demonstration that this supplies a **lower** bound rather than universal equality. Separately verify the logical passage from published exponential upper and lower estimates to the claimed rate under the source's existence theorem.
