## The question in plain language

Take an arbitrary configuration of $n$ distinct points in the plane, with **no convexity requirement**. For each positive distance $u$, let $f(u)$ be the number of unordered point pairs separated by $u$. Erdős #95 asks whether, for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$,

$$
\sum_u f(u)^2=O_\varepsilon(n^{3+\varepsilon}).
$$

This is a distance-multiplicity bound, not merely the distinct-distance count. Unlike #94, collinear points are permitted. The constant may depend on $\varepsilon$.

## A small example

The four vertices of a unit square have four unit-distance pairs and two diagonal pairs. Therefore $\sum f(u)^2=20$ and $\sum f(u)=6$. These formulas are exact. If $n$ points lie on an arithmetic progression on a straight line, the distance $j$ between positions $j$ apart occurs $n-j$ times, so

$$
E(A)=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(n-j)^2
=\frac{n(n-1)(2n-1)}6.
$$

Thus cubic growth cannot be replaced by a smaller **power of $n$** in the unrestricted problem.

## What is known

**PROVED.** Guth and Katz's [*On the Erdős distinct distances problem in the plane*](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2015/181-1/p02), *Annals of Mathematics* 181 (2015), 155–190, yields the stronger bound

$$
E(A)=O(n^3\log n).
$$

For every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, $\log n=O_\varepsilon(n^\varepsilon)$; hence the proven estimate implies Erdős's formulation. The original paper uses the Elekes–Sharir reduction to incidences of lines in three-dimensional space, polynomial partitioning and the geometry of ruled surfaces.

The same paper gives the related distinct-distance lower bound $\gg n/\log n$. That latter estimate **alone** does not prove the cubic-logarithmic energy upper bound: the energy estimate is part of the finer incidence counting. The maintained catalogue previously attributed the convex-polygon case to Fishburn, but corrected the citation to Altman in February 2026.

## What remains

A bounded successor is to prove symbolically the exact collinear example above and verify the normalization $f(j)=n-j$ for equally spaced points. Acceptance: compare a direct $O(n^2)$ pair enumeration with the closed energy formula for $2\le n\le20$; then prove $\log n=o(n^\varepsilon)$ for every $\varepsilon>0$. The technical Guth–Katz incidence bound remains an explicit imported theorem rather than a new proof.
