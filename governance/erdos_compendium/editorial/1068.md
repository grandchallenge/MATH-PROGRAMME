## The question in plain language

An infinite graph can require uncountably many colours to colour its vertices properly. [Erdős Problem #1068](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1068) asks:

Does **every** graph with chromatic number $\aleph_1$ contain a **countable** subgraph that is infinitely vertex-connected?

Here, infinite vertex connectivity means any two vertices can be joined by infinitely many paths with pairwise disjoint interiors. Crucially, the countable subgraph does **not** need uncountable chromatic number. This makes #1068 different from [Problem #1067](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1067), whose stronger conclusion was disproved.

## A small example

Take the complete graph on an uncountable set of $\aleph_1$ vertices. Its chromatic number is $\aleph_1$, because each pair of vertices is adjacent.

Choose any countably infinite subset of vertices. The resulting induced subgraph is the complete countably infinite graph $K_{\aleph_0}$. For any two distinct vertices $u,v$, and for each other vertex $w$, there is a path $u-w-v$. Paths for distinct $w$ share no interior vertices. Since there are infinitely many choices of $w$, this countable subgraph is infinitely vertex-connected.

That is a **positive example**, not a proof about all graphs requiring $\aleph_1$ colours.

## What is known

The maintained catalogue records #1068 as **open**, specifically under the vertex-connectivity interpretation. Its historical references include a problem in Väänänen (1999), and work by Soukup, Bowler and Pitz on related uncountably chromatic graphs.

Soukup and Bowler–Pitz show that uncountably chromatic graphs need not contain uncountable infinitely vertex-connected subgraphs. Their negative result does not itself rule out the existence of a **countable** infinitely vertex-connected subgraph.

Infinite **edge** connectivity is also different: separating two vertices by deleting edges is not the same as separating them by deleting vertices. The site's January 2026 clarification explicitly makes the vertex interpretation the intended one.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source audit #2339](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2339#issuecomment-6156701961) records the four combinations of countable versus uncountable subgraph and edge versus vertex connectivity, and verifies the elementary positive complete-graph example.

The [frozen formal-conjectures file](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1068.lean) expresses the existence of a countable induced subgraph with infinite vertex connectivity. Its general theorem is unproved. No independent GCL formal certification is claimed.

## What remains

The open problem requires either a proof for **every** uncountably chromatic graph or a counterexample. A useful source-bound next step is to examine Bowler–Pitz's actual construction and ask whether it has any countable infinitely vertex-connected induced subgraphs, while preserving the exact historical question and graph-theoretic definitions.
