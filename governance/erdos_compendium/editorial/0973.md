## The question in plain language

For an integer \(n\ge2\), choose complex numbers
\[
z_1,\ldots,z_n\in\mathbb C,\qquad
z_1=1,\quad |z_j|\ge1\ \text{for all }j.
\]
Define their *power sums*
\[
S_k=\sum_{j=1}^n z_j^k.
\]
Erdős asked whether there is **one absolute constant** \(C>1\) such that, **for every \(n\ge2\)**, one can choose a configuration with
\[
\boxed{\max_{2\le k\le n+1}|S_k|<C^{-n}.}
\]
The window of exponents is **\(2,\ldots,n+1\)**, not \(1,\ldots,n\), and the points lie **on or outside** the complex unit circle. This is the *exterior* version of a Turán power-sum problem.

The maintained [Erdős Problem #973](https://www.erdosproblems.com/973), last edited 23 January 2026, still says **OPEN**. This catalogue status is materially stale relative to new research. In July 2026, Yanping Luo, Ruiyi Yang and Keheng Zhu posted a **negative-answer proof**. An independent August 2026 preprint by Xiaojun Tan, Qihang Wang, Wei Huang and Kun Chen gives a second negative proof. Both claim the *same exact quantified disproof*, stronger than merely restricting the possible \(C\). These are **public mathematical preprints**, not an independently accepted, kernel-replayed or GCL-certified theorem in this tranche. The appropriate status is **CATALOGUE OPEN / TWO EXTERNAL NEGATIVE PROOFS PENDING GCL REVIEW**, with the strong source evidence plainly stated.

## Why the exponent window and modulus direction matter

For \(n=2\), let \(z_1=1\), \(z_2=z\). The two sums are
\[
S_2=1+z^2,\qquad S_3=1+z^3.
\]
They **cannot both vanish**: the equations \(z^2=z^3=-1\) imply \(z^2(z-1)=0\), hence \(z=1\), contradicting \(z^2=-1\). This is an exact finite algebraic check. It does not imply a *uniform* lower bound independent of \(n\), since the conjecture permits a different \(n\)-tuple for each \(n\).

Consider the \(n\)-th roots of unity
\[
z_j=\exp(2\pi i(j-1)/n),\qquad 1\le j\le n.
\]
All \(|z_j|=1\) and \(z_1=1\). Their power sums obey
\[
S_k=
\begin{cases}
n,&n\mid k,\\
0,&n\nmid k.
\end{cases}
\]
Therefore, over the required window, \(\max_{2\le k\le n+1}|S_k|=n\). This simple admissible configuration **does not** meet Erdős's exponentially small target. It nevertheless gives a useful polynomial upper bound on the optimal maximum; combined with the 2026 claimed lower theorem, it pins down the asymptotic \(n\)-th-root scale exactly.

If one changes the problem to \(\max_j|z_j|\le1\), very small **exponentially decaying** maxima are known. An exterior lower bound cannot be contradicted by an interior construction; reversing the inequality in the source hypotheses changes the question.

## The sharp asymptotic statement claimed in July 2026

Define the infimum over the **original, normalized** admissible class by
\[
M_n=
\inf_{\substack{z_1=1\\ |z_j|\ge1\ (1\le j\le n)}}
\max_{2\le k\le n+1}\left|\sum_{j=1}^n z_j^k\right|.
\]
Theorem 1.1 in Luo–Yang–Zhu, [*Exterior power sums*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22017), preprint of 24 July 2026, asserts:

> For **every** \(\lambda>0\), there is \(N(\lambda)\) such that for all \(n\ge N(\lambda)\) and **every** \(n\)-tuple with \(|z_j|\ge1\), even *without* \(z_1=1\),
> \[
> \max_{2\le k\le n+1}|S_k|>e^{-\lambda n}.
> \]

The lack of \(z_1=1\) makes the theorem stronger than the precise Erdős conjecture's negation. If an affirmative constant \(C>1\) existed, choose \(\lambda=\log C>0\). For all \(n\ge N(\lambda)\), the theorem would demand \(\max |S_k|>e^{-\lambda n}=C^{-n}\) for **every** admissible tuple, contradicting the required tuple with \(\max |S_k|<C^{-n}\). Thus the theorem **directly disproves** the original existential-\(C\) statement, if its proof is correct.

The same theorem and the root-of-unity example give
\[
e^{-\lambda n}\le M_n\le n\qquad
(n\ge N(\lambda)),
\]
for every fixed \(\lambda>0\). Taking \(n\)-th roots and then allowing \(\lambda\downarrow0\) yields
\[
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}M_n^{1/n}=1.}
\]
This limit is a straightforward deduction from the external theorem, **not an independent new proof** of that theorem. It still allows subexponentially small minima, such as \(e^{-o(n)}\), and does not assert \(M_n\ge c>0\) for all \(n\).

## Why the proof is substantially stronger than older bounds

The earlier Turán First Main Theorem (Theorem 6.1 of [Tu84b]) gives, under \(\min_j|z_j|=1\),
\[
\max_{2\le k\le n+1}|S_k|
\ \ge\
n\left(\frac{n}{2e(n+1)}\right)^{n}.
\]
Here \(\min_j|z_j|=1\) is automatic for the original problem because \(z_1=1\) and every \(|z_j|\ge1\). The lower bound has exponential rate approximately \((2e)^{-n}\). It prevents Erdős's requested \(C\) from being **too large**, but still allows \(1<C<2e\); it is therefore **not** the desired disproof.

A separate 2026 analysis by Turturean sharpened this obstruction, reporting \(C\le1.0615716674\ldots\) as necessary for an affirmative answer. Again, excluding large \(C\) does **not** exclude **all** constants \(C>1\).

In the **interior** variant, with \(\max_j|z_j|=1\) and no requirement that every \(|z_j|\ge1\), Erdős's earlier construction gives an exponentially small maximum with \(C\) about \(1.32\). L. Erdős later proved an optimal exponential window described on the catalogue as
\[
(1.746)^{-n}<M_n^{\rm interior}<(1.745)^{-n}
\]
in the large-\(n\) regime. There is no contradiction: the domains of minimization are opposite. The interior optimization cannot be silently substituted for the exterior one.

## The two recent proof approaches and their boundaries

**Luo–Yang–Zhu, 24 July 2026.** Their seven-page manuscript begins with the polynomial
\[
F_n(t)=\prod_{j=1}^n(1-z_jt)
\]
and logarithmic identities expressing its coefficients in terms of the power sums \(S_k\). Under an assumed exponentially small block \(S_2,\ldots,S_{n+1}\), this polynomial is approximated by a **truncated exponential** with a controlled coefficient-small remainder. A coefficient comparison at degree \(n+1\) bounds the scale of the uncontrolled first sum \(S_1\). The key further step is *overconvergence*: the approximation extends onto an open disk lying **outside** the closed unit disk. Since \(\alpha_j=1/z_j\) lie in the closed unit disk, normalized logarithmic derivatives of \(F_n\) are **Cauchy transforms of probability measures supported there**. Normal-family compactness and the identity theorem rule out the nonzero constant limit forced by the exponential approximation. This contradiction establishes the stated uniform subexponential lower limit. Each of these analytic steps is an independent mathematical verification obligation; checking a formal-power-series identity alone would not establish the result.

**Tan–Wang–Huang–Chen, 3 August 2026.** Their [alternative proof](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02043) uses a *stable-residual principle*. It studies polynomials \(P\) of degree \(n\) with \(P(0)=1\) and all roots in the closed unit disk, proving a subexponential residual lower bound of the form
\[
\inf_P
\frac{\|P'-P'(0)P\|_{H^2}}{\|P\|_{H^2}}
\ge
\exp\!\left[-D n^{2/3}(\log(en))^{2/3}\right]
\]
for sufficiently large \(n\), with some absolute constant \(D\). A projected Newton identity links this to exterior power sums, again implying for every fixed \(\lambda>0\) that the maximum of \(S_2,\ldots,S_{n+1}\) exceeds \(e^{-\lambda n}\) for all sufficiently large \(n\). **This is a second research proof claim**, not a separately GCL-reproduced theorem, and the inequality displayed is for the residual rather than automatically the identical bound for the power-sum maximum.

The two methods offer valuable independent routes to the same theorem. They are **not equivalent to two verified independent audits** until the analytic proofs, theorem hypotheses and any computational or formal dependencies have been checked.
