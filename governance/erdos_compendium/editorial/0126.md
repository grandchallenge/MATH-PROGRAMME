## The question in plain language

For a finite set $A$ of $n$ **distinct positive integers**, collect the prime divisors of all sums $a+b$ for distinct $a,b\in A$. Denote the number of distinct primes in this collection by $s(A)$ and define

$$
f(n)=\min_{|A|=n}s(A).
$$

Erdős and Turán asked whether $f(n)/\log n\to\infty$: no matter which $n$ distinct integers are chosen, their pairwise sums must collectively involve substantially more than $\log n$ different primes. Counting a prime once for **each** pair would be a different and much easier condition.

## A small example

For $A=\{1,2,3\}$, the three pair sums are $3,4,5$. Their distinct prime divisors are $\{2,3,5\}$, so $s(A)=3$. Multiplying the pair sums over **ordered** pairs would square their product, but its distinct prime support would be unchanged. This explains why the formal definition may use an ordered off-diagonal product.

The conjecture is universal over all sets $A$ with $n$ distinct elements. A single family that exhibits many prime divisors cannot establish the lower bound for the minimizing value $f(n)$.

## What is known

**PROVED by a recent machine-checked 2026 development, with independent GCL kernel replay still outstanding.** The primary published artifact is [Tom Adamczewski's Erdős #126 Lean repository](https://github.com/tadamcz/erdos126), documenting GPT-6 Astra proofs found during the FrontierMath Erdős benchmark. The maintained [Erdős #126](https://www.erdosproblems.com/126), updated 3 September 2026, records a stronger estimate $f(n)\gg\sqrt n$.

The source repository describes **four distinct machine-generated proof developments**. Its module documentation gives bounds with exponents $1/8$, $1/3$, $1/2$, and $1/5$. One square-root route proves, for the number $r$ of primes supporting all off-diagonal pair sums,

$$
|A|\le3r^2+2.
$$

Thus for sufficiently large $n$ it implies $f(n)\ge c\sqrt n$, far stronger than $f(n)/\log n\to\infty$. The repository reports a Comparator check against the trusted statement and a NanoDa/kernel/axiom audit using Lean 4.27.0. These are testable **source claims**; GCL has not independently executed that complete toolchain for this tranche.

The protected [Formal Conjectures #126 file](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/126.lean) was directly inspected. Its statement defines the extremal maximal guaranteed prime-support count via all finite subsets of $\mathbb N$, and asserts divergence after division by $\log n$. Because $\mathbb N$ may include zero, the source repository explicitly explains why that convention does not invalidate the positive-integer extremal problem. The protected declaration contains a proof hole and is not a GCL proof receipt.

## What remains

A bounded successor first enumerates finite positive sets $A\subseteq[1,12]$ and counts prime divisors of all distinct-element sums, verifying equivalence with prime support of the ordered off-diagonal product. Acceptance: $\{1,2,3\}$ yields $\{2,3,5\}$ and repeated prime factors are counted once. A separate formal gate should reproduce the external repository's clean Lean build, theorem comparator and allowed-axiom audit at an exact source SHA. Stop before asserting that reproducing these finite checks proves the unbounded theorem.
