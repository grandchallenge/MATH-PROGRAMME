## The question in plain language

A set $B$ is an *essential component* if adding it to any set of intermediate Schnirelmann density increases that density. [Erdős Problem #38](https://www.erdosproblems.com/38) asks for a **stronger property**: can $B$ fail to be an additive basis, yet for every set $A$ of Schnirelmann density $\alpha$ strictly between zero and one and every cutoff $N$, some single shift $b\in B$ increase the number of covered integers in $[1,N]$ by at least a fixed positive fraction $f(\alpha)$?

The maintained problem registry presently reports **PROVED (LEAN)**, attributing a 2026 positive solution to GPT 5.5 Pro prompted by a contributor. That is an external provenance claim, not evidence of an independently reproduced GCL certification.

## A small example

Take $A=\{1,3,5,\ldots\}$, the positive odd integers. Its Schnirelmann density equals $1/2$. Choosing the shift $b=1$ makes

$$
A\cup(A+1)=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}.
$$

The combined set has density one, a gain of $1/2$ in this example. This does **not** exhibit the required set $B$, since a valid $B$ must simultaneously handle **every** admissible $A$ and **every** cutoff while itself not being an additive basis.

A simple bound valid for any such universal improvement is $f(\alpha)\le1-\alpha$, because coverage cannot exceed all integers.

## What is known

The registry's May 2026 update reports a sparse random construction of $B$ with improvement on the scale $\alpha(1-\alpha)^2$. Its provenance is disclosed as a model-assisted solution discussed in the public forum, with externally reported Lean verification. Earlier work by Linnik provided an essential component that was not an additive basis, but that older, weaker fact does not establish the strong one-shift requirement.
