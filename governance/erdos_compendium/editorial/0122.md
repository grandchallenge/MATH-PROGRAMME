## The question in plain language

For a number-theoretic function $f$, consider the shifted map $n\mapsto n+f(n)$. Erdős #122 asks which functions force this map to send **many more inputs than the length of a short target interval** into that interval. If $F$ is small compared with the normal order of $f$, the intended quantity is

$$
\frac{\#\{n\in\mathbb N:n+f(n)\in(x,x+F(x))\}}{F(x)}
$$

and the question asks whether this ratio is unbounded along large $x$. The precise admissibility conditions on $F$, and the source's use of “almost all $n$,” must be specified to make the quantifiers mathematically rigorous.

## A small example

The number-of-divisors function satisfies $\tau(12)=6$ and $\tau(13)=2$, so $12+\tau(12)=18$ but $13+\tau(13)=15$. The shifted map need not be monotone, and a short output interval can receive many inputs. Counting preimages means counting each input separately, even when shifted outputs coincide.

The function $\omega(n)$ counts **distinct prime divisors**, not all prime factors with multiplicity. For example $\omega(12)=2$ while $\tau(12)=6$.

## What is known

The general classification remains **OPEN**. The maintained [Erdős #122](https://www.erdosproblems.com/122), edited 1 April 2026, cites Erdős, Pomerance and Sárközy [EPS97]. They established, in particular, that for all large $x$ there are intervals $I,J\subset[1,x]$ with

$$
|I|\asymp\sqrt{\frac{\log x}{\log\log x}},
\qquad |J|\asymp\sqrt{\log\log x},
$$

and $n+\omega(n)\in J$ for every $n\in I$. Their ratio $|I|/|J|$ tends to infinity. Erdős also reported positive results for the divisor function $\tau$, while expecting failure for Euler's $\varphi$ and the divisor-sum $\sigma$.

There is a **statement-capture defect**: the maintained page says “infinitely many $x$ such that [the ratio] tends to infinity,” although a single value of $x$ cannot be the variable in a limit. One must consult [Er97] and [EPS97] to fix the precise limsup or sequence quantifiers rather than silently formalizing ambiguous prose. The original paper's exact pages were not independently inspected in this audit.

## What remains

A bounded two-stage successor first transcribes the 1997 original statement and explicitly defines allowed positive functions $F$, their smallness condition, and a correct asymptotic $x$ quantifier. It then enumerates $\tau(n)$ and $\omega(n)$ exactly for $1\le n\le1000$ and counts shifted preimages of integer intervals of lengths 2 to 8. Acceptance: independently factored counts agree, collisions are **not** deduplicated, and no asymptotic claim is inferred from finite data. Stop before investigating new families of functions.
