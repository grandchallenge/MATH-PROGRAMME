## The question in plain language

For each sufficiently large integer $n$, can we choose a polynomial $P(z)$ of **degree exactly $n$** whose coefficients are **all $+1$ or $-1$**, such that its absolute value remains comparable to $\sqrt n$ at **every** point of the complex unit circle?

$$
c\sqrt n\le |P(z)|\le C\sqrt n
\qquad(|z|=1),
$$

where the positive constants $c,C$ are **independent of $n$ and $z$**. These are called *flat Littlewood polynomials*. The pointwise **lower** bound is as important as the upper bound.

## A small example

The polynomial $1+z+\cdots+z^n$ has the required coefficient alphabet, but at $z=1$ its modulus equals $n+1$, much larger than $\sqrt n$. Other simple sign choices may vanish on the unit circle and violate the lower bound.

Parseval's identity gives

$$
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
|P(e^{i\theta})|^2\,d\theta=n+1.
$$

Therefore the natural overall scale of the modulus is indeed $\sqrt{n+1}$, but an **average-square identity** cannot guarantee uniform bounds away from zero and infinity.

## What is known

**PROVED for every integer degree at least two.** Paul Balister, Béla Bollobás, Robert Morris, Julian Sahasrabudhe and Marius Tiba, [*Flat Littlewood polynomials exist*](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2020.192.3.6), *Annals of Mathematics* 192 (2020), 977–1004, established the existence of absolute constants $0<\delta<\Delta$ and, for each degree $n$, a polynomial of that degree with coefficients in $\{-1,1\}$ satisfying

$$
\delta\sqrt n\le|P(z)|\le\Delta\sqrt n
$$

at **all** points of the unit circle. This resolved Littlewood's 1966 conjecture, as recorded in [Erdős #228](https://www.erdosproblems.com/228).

The argument is substantially deeper than constructing low-peak polynomial sequences: both sides must hold at every angle. Contrast #230, where unimodular **complex** coefficients, rather than signs, permit much flatter asymptotics.

## What remains

A bounded task enumerates coefficients in $\{-1,1\}$ for degrees from 2 through 8 inclusive, evaluates the modulus on a fine angular mesh and then certifies any proposed full-circle lower bound using derivative Lipschitz estimates. Acceptance: report $\min_{|z|=1}|P(z)|$ and $\max_{|z|=1}|P(z)|$ with numerical error bounds, without extrapolating finite small-degree results into the uniform all-degree theorem.
