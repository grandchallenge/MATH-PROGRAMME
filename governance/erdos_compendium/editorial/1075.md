## The question in plain language

A *hypergraph* generalizes an ordinary graph: an edge can connect more than two vertices. An $r$‑uniform hypergraph has exactly $r$ vertices in every edge.

[Erdős Problem #1075](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1075) asks whether a hypergraph slightly denser than a balanced complete $r$‑partite construction must contain a **growing** subhypergraph whose edge density is uniformly larger than that construction's density.

More precisely, for every fixed $r\ge3$, it asks for a constant $c_r>r^{-r}$ such that every sufficiently large $r$‑uniform hypergraph with at least

$$
(1+\varepsilon)(n/r)^r
$$

edges contains a subhypergraph with $m=m(n)\to\infty$ vertices and at least $c_rm^r$ edges. Critically, $c_r$ must work for *every* $\varepsilon>0$; it must not be quietly chosen separately for each $\varepsilon$.

## A small example

Divide $n=rt$ vertices into $r$ equally sized parts, each with $t$ vertices. Make a hyperedge by choosing exactly one vertex from each part. The resulting complete $r$‑partite hypergraph has

$$
t^r=(n/r)^r
$$

edges. If we similarly select $m=rs$ vertices with $s$ from each part, the selected hypergraph has $s^r=r^{-r}m^r$ edges.

For $r=3$, take three parts of two vertices each. There are $n=6$ vertices and exactly $2^3=8$ triples. This is the *boundary* example: it does **not** meet the problem's strict $(1+\varepsilon)$‑times-boundary edge requirement. Adding a ninth triple can give a finite example above the threshold, but the conjecture is about larger and larger subhypergraphs as $n\to\infty$.

## What is known

The maintained [catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1075) records this question as **open** and locates its original formulation on page 80 of Erdős's 1974 paper *Extremal problems on graphs and hypergraphs*.

It also reports an earlier Erdős theorem from 1964 with the boundary value $c_r=r^{-r}$ under a different positive-density hypothesis. Raising that value to a strict $c_r>r^{-r}$ is the substantive new demand. A stronger hypothesis on the number of edges cannot simply be substituted without checking the exact dependence on $\varepsilon$.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source audit #2346](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2346#issuecomment-6158389532) fixes the quantifiers and independently proves the complete-partite edge-count identities. It confirms that the protected formal-conjectures snapshot has no available Lean statement for #1075.

The full 1964 and 1974 published proofs have not yet been examined page by page. The elementary construction explains the numerical boundary but is neither a proof nor a disproof of the desired uniform density increment.

## What remains

The immediate obligation is to lock the original 1974 statement, distinguish induced and arbitrary subhypergraphs, and reconstruct the earlier 1964 density argument. The ensuing research challenge is to find a density-increment or amplification theorem uniform in $\varepsilon$ and valid on a vertex set whose size diverges with $n$.
