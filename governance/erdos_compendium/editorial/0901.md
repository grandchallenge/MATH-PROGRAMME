## The question in plain language

How few edges can an n-uniform hypergraph have while being impossible to colour red and blue without a monochromatic edge? This minimum is denoted m(n), and the open problem is to determine its asymptotic growth as n increases. Every hyperedge contains exactly n vertices; a proper two-colouring must give each edge at least one vertex of each colour. This is Property B.

## A small example

For n=2, a triangle of three graph edges cannot be properly two-coloured: alternating colours around an odd cycle is impossible. For n=3, the seven lines of the Fano plane form a seven-edge 3-uniform hypergraph that is not two-colourable. Exhaustive checks on CHAD confirmed both examples, and confirmed that deleting any one edge from either witness restores two-colourability. These witnesses establish upper bounds m(2) <= 3 and m(3) <= 7; the equalities m(2)=3 and m(3)=7 require their separate minimality arguments.

## What is known

The asymptotic question remains open. Classical results of Erdős and Lovász and later Radhakrishnan and Srinivasan give the published range

$$
  m(n)=\Omega\!\left(2^n\sqrt{\frac{n}{\log n}}\right),
  \qquad m(n)=O(n^2 2^n).
$$

The elementary random-colouring argument supplies a weaker lower bound. Any fixed n-edge is monochromatic with probability 2^(1-n) under uniformly independent vertex colours. A union bound implies that every n-uniform hypergraph with strictly fewer than 2^(n-1) edges has a proper colouring; hence m(n) >= 2^(n-1). The strict inequality matters at the boundary. The conjectural order n 2^n is not established by these estimates.

## What remains

The bounded successor is to encode the finite hypergraphs as edge bitsets and construct independently checkable certificates of non-two-colourability and edge-criticality, then reproduce one exact finite lower-bound lemma from Grill–Linzmayer's 2024 work. Acceptance requires explicit hypotheses, reproducible witnesses, and counterexample searches for weakened statements. This does not authorize launching an unselected Solve campaign. Accountable source review, protected batch admission and public math-rendering checks remain separate gates.
