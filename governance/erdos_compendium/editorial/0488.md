**Editorial status:** UNACCEPTED_DRAFT. Erdős Problem #488 has special status **FALSIFIABLE**, meaning the corrected conjecture remains open but a counterexample, if one exists, would be finite and independently checkable. The 1961 and 1980 primary sources use a different, disproved *complement* formulation; the 1966 source explicitly states the corrected *multiples* version.

## The question in plain language

Choose a finite nonempty set $A$ of positive integers. Color an integer $t\ge1$ as selected when it is a **multiple of at least one member of $A$**. Let

$$
B_A=\{t\ge1:\ \exists a\in A,\ a\mid t\},
\qquad
F_A(x)=|B_A\cap[1,x]|.
$$

Erdős asks whether for **every** such $A$ and every pair of integers $m>n\ge\max A$,

$$
\boxed{\frac{F_A(m)}m<2\frac{F_A(n)}n.}
$$

The statement asserts that the fraction of selected integers cannot later jump to at least *twice* its value measured at a threshold not smaller than any generator. The strict inequality and the exact threshold are essential.

What does **FALSIFIABLE** mean here? The original corrected proposition is a universally quantified arithmetic claim. To *disprove* it, one needs only exhibit a **finite** list $A$ and integers $m>n\ge\max A$ with

$$
nF_A(m)\ge2mF_A(n).
$$

Every quantity in this certificate is an integer calculated by a finite divisibility test. Thus a counterexample would be mechanically checkable. This does **not** establish that one exists. In particular, FALSIFIABLE does not mean *disproved*, *decidable*, *unprovable*, or *independent of arithmetic*. The corrected problem is listed as [FALSIFIABLE (open)](https://www.erdosproblems.com/488).

## A small example

For a singleton $A=\{a\}$ with $a\ge2$,

$$
F_A(x)=\left\lfloor\frac xa\right\rfloor.
$$

Write $k=\lfloor n/a\rfloor\ge1$. Because $n<(k+1)a\le2ka$, we have

$$
\frac{F_A(n)}n=\frac kn>\frac1{2a},
\qquad
\frac{F_A(m)}m\le\frac1a.
$$

Therefore the proposed strict inequality **holds for every singleton generator**. This is a complete elementary special case, not the universal assertion.

The factor $2$ is asymptotically best possible. Take $n=2a-1$ and $m=2a$. Then

$$
\frac{F_A(m)/m}{F_A(n)/n}
=\frac{2a-1}{a}=2-\frac1a.
$$

These ratios approach $2$ from below, so no smaller *universal* factor can replace $2$.

Now consider the **different** set of integers avoiding all listed divisors:

$$
C_A=\{t\ge1:\ \forall a\in A,\ a\nmid t\}.
$$

For $A=\{2,3,5,7\}$, $n=10$ and $m=20$, the only such integers at most $10$ are $\{1\}$, while those at most $20$ are $\{1,11,13,17,19\}$. Thus

$$
\frac{|C_A\cap[1,20]|}{20}=\frac5{20}=\frac14
>\frac15
=2\frac{|C_A\cap[1,10]|}{10}.
$$

This is an exact finite counterexample to the **complement** version, not to the maintained multiples conjecture. On the same inputs the multiples counts are $F_A(10)=9$ and $F_A(20)=15$, which satisfy the corrected inequality.

## What is known

### Historical wording and a genuine status conflict

In *Some unsolved problems* (1961), printed p. 236, Problem I.27, Erdős defines $B$ using numbers **none of which** is a multiple of an $a\in A$. This is the *complement* set $C_A$, and the explicit example above disproves that literal version.

In *Remarks on number theory V: Extremal problems in number theory II* (1966), *Matematikai Lapok* **17**, printed p. 150, item 6, Erdős writes in Hungarian about those integers **which are multiples of at least one** member of $A$; this is the corrected set $B_A$. This primary page was inspected directly. The [maintained catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/488) cites that 1966 restatement as evidence that the 1961 opposite wording was likely a typo.

There is an additional historical complication: Erdős's *A survey of problems in combinatorial number theory* (1980), printed p. 112, again describes the integers “no one of which is the multiple of any of the a's.” Its printed page was also inspected directly. Thus the **1980 text repeats the complement wording** and is not independent primary support for the corrected version. In all three sources the intended sharpness example takes $A=\{a\}$, $n=2a-1$, and $m=2a$, which makes mathematical sense for the *multiples* definition. This reinforces the editorial reason to prefer the 1966 definition without silently pretending that the documents say the same thing.

The maintained problem, last edited 8 April 2026, calls the corrected **multiples** inequality FALSIFIABLE (open) and discusses counterexamples to the alternate complement question. No example disproving the corrected inequality has been established in this audit.

### Exact finite arithmetic

There is a useful closed formula for counting the selected integers. The inclusion–exclusion principle gives

$$
F_A(x)=
\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq A}
(-1)^{|S|+1}
\left\lfloor\frac{x}{\operatorname{lcm}(S)}\right\rfloor.
$$

The formula is exact for every positive integer $x$ and finite $A$. It furnishes an **independent check** on direct enumeration by divisibility and supports finite counterexample certificates using only integer arithmetic.

One may also remove a generator $b$ when another generator $a\in A$ divides $b$, since every multiple of $b$ is already a multiple of $a$. The resulting *primitive divisor-minimal core* generates the same $B_A$. It may have a smaller maximum; the original requirement $n\ge\max A$ must be retained when translating results for the reduced core back to the original $A$.

If $1\in A$, then $B_A$ is every positive integer and the strict inequality is simply $1<2$, so this case is trivial. Requiring $1\notin A$ in a nontrivial formalization does not itself change the core question, provided that reduction is made explicit.

## GCL's current state

The authenticated [source-return record](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1759#issuecomment-6181186135) identifies the literal 1961, 1966 and 1980 primary wordings, confirms the maintained FALSIFIABLE classification, and records the exact counterexample to the complement formulation.

The pinned Formal Conjectures file [488.lean](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/488.lean) (blob d32dda9c6eee99ed0e2e5602deb4a32d61cc7d6d) states the **multiples** variant with a nonempty finite set of natural-number generators, explicitly excludes $0$ and $1$, and uses exact rational density comparisons with $m>n\ge\max A$. The theorem body contains a Lean placeholder, not a proof. Restricting to positive, non-unit generators is a legitimate reduced scope only once the omitted cases are explained. The base theory for a concrete counterexample is elementary arithmetic with computable finite sets, not a claim about proof-theoretic independence.

GCL reproduced the complement counterexample and performed an exploratory exact finite search over divisor-minimal cores with maximum generator at most $12$, cutoffs $\max A\le n\le40$, and $n<m\le80$: **427,167 comparisons, no corrected-version counterexample**. This bounded search is not evidence that the universal theorem holds beyond its tested domain, and it is not a decision procedure for the unbounded question.

## What remains

The bounded successor is to verify the enumerated membership counts against inclusion–exclusion at each tested input, reproduce the correct/incorrect-source split as a permanent regression, and independently prove the singleton inequality and asymptotic sharpness. If a corrected-version counterexample is found later, publish the finite list and exact cross-multiplied inequality rather than a floating-point estimate.

An independent proof or counterexample for *arbitrary finite* $A$ remains the substantive mathematical problem. The special FALSIFIABLE classification must be preserved without converting it to an ordinary PROVED/DISPROVED flag. For editorial admission, reconcile the three original source wordings with the locked modern statement, test exact theorem quantifiers, and verify deployed mathematics rendering.
