## The question in plain language

Let $\mathrm{ex}(n,C_4)$ denote the maximum number of edges in a simple $n$‑vertex $C_4$‑free graph. Erdős and Simonovits asked whether every graph with $\mathrm{ex}(n,C_4)+1$ edges must contain $\Omega(\sqrt n)$ copies of $C_4$; their stronger formulation predicts $(1+o(1))\sqrt n$ copies.

The distinction between the two formulations matters. Erdős’ 1990 formulation uses an absolute constant $c>0$, while the 1984 formulation asks for the asymptotic coefficient $1$. The general problem remains open.

He, Ma, and Yang proved the conjectured scale for infinitely many orders. For $q=2^k$, $k\ge40$, they establish

$$
h(q^2+q+1)=q-1,
$$

where $h(n)$ is the minimum number of four-cycles in an $n$‑vertex graph with $\mathrm{ex}(n,C_4)+1$ edges. Their proof uses stability of near-extremal $C_4$‑free graphs and orthogonal polarity graphs.

The remaining issue is uniformity over arbitrary $n$. A natural reduction shows that any counterexample with $o(\sqrt n)$ cycles would contain a $C_4$‑free graph only $o(\sqrt n)$ edges below extremal, together with a small set of added edges whose individual saturation multiplicities sum to $o(\sqrt n)$. Controlling those selected edges, rather than only average codegrees, is the principal unresolved step.

### Six-section mathematical explanation

For a simple graph $G$, define

$$
\mathrm{ex}(n,C_4)
=
\max\{|E(H)|: |V(H)|=n,\ C_4\nsubseteq H\}.
$$

The threshold for forcing one $C_4$ is $\mathrm{ex}(n,C_4)+1$. Therefore the live-page condition

$$
|E(G)|>\mathrm{ex}(n,C_4)
$$

is exactly the integer condition

$$
|E(G)|\ge\mathrm{ex}(n,C_4)+1.
$$

The catalogue problem asks for constants $c>0$ and $n_0$ such that

$$
\forall n\ge n_0\ \forall G
\left(
|V(G)|=n,\ |E(G)|\ge\mathrm{ex}(n,C_4)+1
\Rightarrow
\#C_4(G)\ge c\sqrt n
\right).
$$

The stronger historical form replaces $c\sqrt n$ by $(1+o(1))\sqrt n$.

## A small example

For vertices $x,y$ let

$$
c_G(x,y):=|N_G(x)\cap N_G(y)|.
$$

Then

$$
\#C_4(G)
=
\frac12
\sum_{\{x,y\}\subseteq V(G)}
\binom{c_G(x,y)}2.
$$

Proof: choose an unordered pair $\{x,y\}$ of opposite vertices of a four-cycle. Two distinct common neighbors of $x$ and $y$ determine one four-cycle. Every four-cycle has exactly two opposite vertex-pairs, hence the factor $1/2$.

This identity is also equivalent to

$$
2\#C_4(G)=\sum_{\{x,y\}}\binom{c_G(x,y)}2.
$$

Take $G=K_{2,3}$. The unique unordered pair in the part of size $2$ has codegree $3$, contributing

$$
\binom32=3.
$$

The three unordered pairs in the part of size $3$ each have codegree $2$, contributing

$$
3\binom22=3.
$$

Thus

$$
\#C_4(K_{2,3})
=
\frac12(3+3)=3.
$$

Directly, a four-cycle is obtained by choosing both vertices of the part of size $2$ and any two of the three vertices in the other part:

$$
\binom22\binom32=3.
$$

The two calculations agree.

## What is known

For $N=q^2+q+1$, orthogonal polarity graphs give $C_4$‑free graphs with

$$
\frac12q(q+1)^2
$$

edges when $q$ is a suitable prime power. Füredi’s theorem gives equality for the relevant large even parameters. He–Ma–Yang prove that, for large even $q$, every graph with one additional edge either has many four-cycles or is a polarity graph plus one edge.

For $q=2^k$, $k\ge40$, the exact minimum is

$$
h(N)=q-1.
$$

Since

$$
\frac{q-1}{\sqrt{q^2+q+1}}\longrightarrow1,
$$

the strongest asymptotic conjecture holds along this infinite sequence.

Suppose $G$ has $\mathrm{ex}(n,C_4)+1$ edges and only $T$ four-cycles. Choose a maximum $C_4$‑free spanning subgraph $H\subseteq G$, and write

$$
R=E(G)\setminus E(H),\qquad r=|R|.
$$

Then

$$
|E(H)|=\mathrm{ex}(n,C_4)+1-r.
$$

For every $e\in R$, adding $e$ to $H$ creates at least one four-cycle. Let $p_H(e)$ be the number created by $e$. Distinct edges of $R$ yield distinct cycles containing their respective added edge, so

$$
T\ge\sum_{e\in R}p_H(e)\ge r.
$$

Thus $r\ge c\sqrt n$ already proves the desired bound. A counterexample must therefore have $r=o(\sqrt n)$ and hence an almost-extremal $C_4$‑free core $H$.

The unresolved step is to show that such a core cannot have a selected set $R$ of $r$ nonedges with

$$
\sum_{e\in R}p_H(e)=o(\sqrt n).
$$

Global codegree inequalities do not automatically control these particular selected nonedges.

## What remains

For the fixed order $n=7$ (only $2^{21}$ labeled graphs):

1. Determine $\mathrm{ex}(7,C_4)$ by canonical generation of all $C_4$‑free graphs.
2. Enumerate all non-isomorphic simple graphs with $\mathrm{ex}(7,C_4)+1$ edges.
3. Compute $\#C_4(G)$ in two independent ways:
   - direct enumeration of 4-vertex subsets;
   - the codegree identity proved below.
4. Output the exact value of $h(7)$ and a graph6 witness attaining it.

Acceptance: every canonical graph is covered, both counting methods agree for every graph, and the reported minimum has a machine-checkable witness.

Falsification: a missed canonical graph, disagreement between the two counting methods, or a witness with a smaller verified cycle count.

Stopping rule: stop after the complete finite order‑$7$ enumeration and independent certificate; do not infer the asymptotic conjecture from the finite result.
