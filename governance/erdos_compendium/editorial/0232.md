## The question in plain language

Let $A\subseteq\mathbb R^2$ be a **Lebesgue-measurable** set with no two points at Euclidean distance **exactly one**. Its *upper planar density*, measured in growing disks, is

$$
\overline\delta(A)=\limsup_{R\to\infty}
\frac{\lambda(A\cap B_R)}{\lambda(B_R)}.
$$

Write $m_1$ for the supremum of this density over all such sets. Moser and Erdős asked whether

$$
m_1\le\frac14.
$$

The exact distance one and the **measurability** assumption both matter. A bounded, finite set has upper density zero even if its local packing is dense.

## A small example

For any unit vector $u$, the sets $A$ and $A+u$ are disjoint: if a point lay in both, $A$ would contain two points separated by exactly one. Comparing their densities in large disks yields the elementary upper bound $m_1\le1/2$, which is weaker than the proposed quarter.

For a lower bound, take open disks of radius $1/2$ centered at the triangular lattice with nearest-center separation two. No two points in these disks lie exactly one apart. Each disk contributes area $\pi/4$, and a fundamental lattice cell has area $2\sqrt3$, giving

$$
m_1\ge\frac{\pi}{8\sqrt3}\approx0.2267.
$$

The disks must be **open**: including boundary points of two neighboring disks could produce a pair at distance exactly one.

## What is known

**PROVED.** Gergely Ambrus, Adrián Csiszárik, Máté Matolcsi, Dániel Varga and Pál Zsámboki, [*The density of planar sets avoiding unit distances*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.14179), published in *Mathematical Programming* (2023/24), established the stronger upper bound

$$
m_1\le0.2470<\frac14.
$$

This resolves Erdős's specific quarter-density conjecture, but **does not determine the exact value of $m_1$**. Croft's earlier construction increased the known lower bound to approximately $0.22936$; a nontrivial gap between current upper and lower bounds remains.

The maintained [Erdős #232](https://www.erdosproblems.com/232) reports this theorem and distinguishes the solved inequality from the open optimization problem.

A bounded task computes the elementary triangular-lattice packing density from the cell determinant and disk area, checks the disjointness of $A$ and its unit translate, and verifies that $0.2470<0.25$. Determining the exact extremal density remains distinct.
