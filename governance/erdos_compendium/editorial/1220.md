## The question in plain language

For any two-coloring of the edges between members of a set of size $\lambda$, a *partition relation* such as

$$
\lambda\longrightarrow(\lambda,\aleph_1)^2
$$

asserts that there must be either a color-0 homogeneous set of size $\lambda$, or a color-1 homogeneous set of size $\aleph_1$. The two alternatives have different sizes.

[Erdős Problem #1220](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1220) asks about this assertion when $\lambda$ is a **singular cardinal**: an infinite cardinal approached by an increasing sequence of fewer than $\lambda$ smaller cardinals. Its cofinality $\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)$ is the least length of such a cofinal sequence.

There is a strong arithmetic hypothesis: both $\lambda$ and $\operatorname{cf}(\lambda)$ are $\aleph_0$-inaccessible, meaning, for each relevant cardinal $\kappa$,

$$
\mu^{\aleph_0}<\kappa\quad\text{for every cardinal }\mu<\kappa.
$$

The question is whether these assumptions force the stated two-color relation in ZFC.

## A small example

Even in finite graph coloring, monochromatic cliques need not be large. Place five vertices on a pentagon. Color its five perimeter edges red and its five diagonals blue. Both the red graph and blue graph are five-cycles, which have no triangle. Thus $5\nrightarrow(3,3)^2$: this coloring has no homogeneous triple of either color.

This checks the meaning of partition notation, not the singular-cardinal hypothesis. Finite examples cannot settle a forcing or independence question about ZFC.

## What is known

The pinned Erdős catalogue records the problem as **open**. Shelah and Stanley, *A theorem and some consistency results in partition calculus* (1987), present relevant relative-consistency arguments involving failures of partition relations. The exact bridge from their model construction to **both** $\aleph_0$-inaccessibility assumptions of Problem #1220 requires care.

A September 2026 Palomar entry describes an external Lean development claiming that the affirmative assertion is not provable in ZFC. This is a research lead, not a result that GCL has independently replayed or certified. Furthermore, “ZFC cannot prove the universal assertion” does **not** entail “ZFC proves its negation.”
