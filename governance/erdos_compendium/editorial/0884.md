## The question in plain language

Let $1=d_1<d_2<\cdots<d_t=n$ be the positive divisors of an integer $n$, in increasing order. Define two measures of their clustering: the **all-pairs reciprocal-gap energy** $T(n)$ and the **consecutive reciprocal-gap energy** $S(n)$,

$$
T(n)=\sum_{1\le i<j\le t}\frac1{d_j-d_i},\qquad
S(n)=\sum_{1\le i<t}\frac1{d_{i+1}-d_i}.
$$

[Erdős Problem #884](https://www.erdosproblems.com/884) asks whether an absolute constant $C$ exists with $T(n)\le C(1+S(n))$ for **all** positive integers $n$. The additive $1$ is essential: showing unboundedness of $T(n)/S(n)$ alone does not necessarily settle the registered conjecture.

## A small example

For $n=12$, the ordered divisors are $1,2,3,4,6,12$. Both energies are rational numbers and can be computed exactly.

An independent exact-rational computation for every $2\le n\le1200$ finds the largest ratio $T(n)/(1+S(n))$ in that **finite range** at $n=1080$, approximately $2.496309$. This is not an asymptotic counterexample. No finite search by itself can show that the ratio is unbounded over every positive integer.

The accompanying fixture records exact rational sums, each tested divisor count, and the complete ordered divisor list for the observed maximizer.

## What is known

Terence Tao, [*On the Sum of Reciprocals of Gaps Between Divisors*](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/erdos-884.pdf), dated September 9, 2025, Theorem 1.1, proves that the displayed ratio is unbounded **conditionally** on the qualitative Hardy–Littlewood prime-tuples conjecture. His construction uses a cluster of nearly equally spaced prime divisors. The prime-tuples assumption is essential to this first result.

Daniel Larsen, [*A Question of Erdős on Reciprocals of Gaps Between Divisors*](https://github.com/Larsen-Daniel/Erdos-884/blob/main/884.pdf), publicly posted March 29, 2026 and revised March 30, provides an **unconditional** disproof. Larsen combines many separated scales of clustered divisors so that all-pairs contributions accumulate while adjacent-gap and cross-scale terms remain controlled. The maintained [Erdős catalogue #884](https://www.erdosproblems.com/884) correctly records the conjecture as disproved. GCL has not independently replayed every sieve step in Larsen's full manuscript.

The public [Honicky formalization repository](https://github.com/honicky/erdos884) reports a Lean 4 disproof using the same big-O definitions as the Formal Conjectures statement. Its July 2026 documentation reports a successful Lake build at Mathlib v4.31.0, no placeholders, and exactly the three standard axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. This is significant external verification evidence, but **not** an independently executed GCL build or semantic review.

Tao also notes that the historical 1998 text appears to use a different upper endpoint in the all-pairs sum. This is a source-fidelity matter, not permission to silently change the theorem's quantifiers.

## GCL's current state

A useful universal bound follows from Cauchy–Schwarz. Let the successive positive divisor gaps be $g_i=d_{i+1}-d_i$, and put $m=j-i$. Then

$$
\frac1{d_j-d_i}=\frac1{g_i+\cdots+g_{j-1}}
\le\frac1{m^2}\sum_{r=i}^{j-1}\frac1{g_r}.
$$

For a fixed $m$, each gap participates in at most $m$ of these windows. Sum over all windows and lengths $m$ to obtain

$$
T(n)\le H_{t-1}S(n),\qquad
H_{t-1}=\sum_{m=1}^{t-1}\frac1m.
$$

This is a universal **harmonic-factor** comparison, with a loss of order $\log t$, rather than the false uniform constant proposed by Erdős. Tao's 2025 paper observes the same logarithmic phenomenon.

The exact fixture governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_883_884.py evaluates $T$ and $S$ for $2\le n\le1200$ using exact fractions and tests the harmonic inequality in each case. The finite exercise does not establish Larsen's unconditional unboundedness or reproduce the claimed external Lean axiom output.
