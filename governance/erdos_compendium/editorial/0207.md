## The question in plain language

A *Steiner triple system* on $n$ vertices is a collection of three-element blocks such that **every unordered pair of distinct vertices lies in exactly one block**. These systems can exist only when $n\equiv1$ or $3\pmod6$.

Erdős asked whether, for every fixed integer $g\ge2$ and **all sufficiently large admissible** $n$, there is such a system with *high girth*: every collection of $j$ distinct blocks, for $2\le j\le g$, must contain at least $j+3$ distinct vertices. This forbids configurations in which a small number of triples are unusually concentrated.

## A small example

Two distinct blocks cannot share two vertices, because that would repeat a pair, so their union contains at least five vertices, as the requirement $j=2$ demands. A *Pasch configuration* has four triples on six vertices and violates the required inequality when $j=4$ (it would demand at least seven vertices). The small-configuration condition is stricter than the basic Steiner pair-uniqueness condition.

For $n=7$, the Fano plane is a Steiner triple system: it has seven triples and each pair lies in exactly one. Its existence illustrates the divisibility requirements but does **not** demonstrate arbitrarily high girth at fixed order.

## What is known

**PROVED.** Matthew Kwan, Ashwin Sah, Mehtaab Sawhney and Michael Simkin, [*High-Girth Steiner Triple Systems*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04554), prove Erdős's 1973 conjecture: for every fixed girth threshold $g$, every sufficiently large $n\equiv1,3\pmod6$ supports a Steiner triple system avoiding all forbidden small dense configurations.

Their argument uses randomized constructions and absorbing methods. The statement is an asymptotic **existence** theorem; it does not say that a random Steiner triple system automatically satisfies the high-girth condition with high probability without further construction.

The maintained [Erdős #207](https://www.erdosproblems.com/207) classifies the question **PROVED** and cites the authors' paper. The exact $j+3$ vertex count is crucial: changing it to $j+2$ produces a weaker property, while demanding the property for all $j$ without fixing $g$ is a substantially stronger claim.

## What remains

A bounded task constructs the Fano-plane blocks and checks exact pair multiplicities. Enumerate unions of $j$ distinct blocks for $2\le j\le4$, record violations of $|\cup E_i|\ge j+3$, and distinguish a small finite design from the general theorem for large $n$. Acceptance: verify every pair occurs once, demonstrate a Pasch-type obstruction when present, and never conflate fixed girth with unrestricted growing girth.
