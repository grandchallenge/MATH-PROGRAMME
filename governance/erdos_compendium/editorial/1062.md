## The question in plain language

Select some numbers from $\{1,\ldots,n\}$. A selection is **fork-free** if no member divides two *different other members* of that same selection. For example, $\{2,4,6\}$ is forbidden because $2$ divides both $4$ and $6$.

Let $f(n)$ be the largest possible size of a fork-free selection. [Erdős Problem #1062](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1062) asks for its asymptotic size and whether the limiting ratio $f(n)/n$ is irrational. An approximate numerical range alone does not settle a question about irrationality.

## A small example

The interval

$$
A=\{\lfloor n/3\rfloor+1,\ldots,n\}
$$

is always fork-free. Every $a\in A$ exceeds $n/3$, so $3a>n$. Thus, among elements of $A$, at most the single distinct multiple $2a$ can follow $a$. It follows immediately that

$$
f(n)\ge n-\lfloor n/3\rfloor
=\lceil 2n/3\rceil.
$$

For $n=15$, the set $\{6,7,\ldots,15\}$ has ten members and satisfies the rule. Exhaustive inspection of **all** $2^{15}$ subsets of $\{1,\ldots,15\}$ confirms $f(15)=10$.

## What is known

Kenneth Lebensold's paper *A Divisibility Problem* (1976/77), pp. 291–294, established stronger asymptotic bounds. The maintained catalogue gives, for sufficiently large $n$,

$$
0.6725n\le f(n)\le0.6736n.
$$

This narrow interval lies above the elementary $2n/3$ lower construction, but does not determine whether any limiting ratio is irrational. An April 2026 discussion proposes effective computability of the limit via divisor-graph methods; this newer research claim requires independent source and proof review before changing GCL's status record.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source audit #2333](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2333#issuecomment-6156559149) checks the exact three-distinct-elements condition and compares it to the [frozen Lean definition](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1062.lean). The pinned file proves the elementary interval lower bound, but its Lebensold and irrationality claims include unproved placeholders.

Independent exhaustive search gives the values of $f(n)$ for $n=1,\ldots,15$ as

$$
1,2,2,3,4,4,5,6,6,7,8,8,9,10,10.
$$

This is finite verification only, not mathematical certification of a global extremal theorem.

## What remains

The original mathematical question seeks an exact asymptotic density and its arithmetic nature. GCL's next bounded task is to inspect Lebensold's paper page by page, examine the recent proposed effective-computability theorem, and test its exact assumptions against the fork-free graph formulation. Finite extremal searches can test examples but cannot decide irrationality.
