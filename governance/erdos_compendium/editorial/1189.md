## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1189](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1189) is **OPEN — SEVERAL SUBCLAIMS** in the maintained catalogue.

An **irreducible covering set** is a finite set of distinct moduli $1<n_1<\cdots<n_k$ admitting *some* residues that cover all integers, but such that after removing **any modulus** no choice of *new* residues for remaining moduli can cover. Ask for the count $I(k)$ of irreducible sets of size $k$, bounds on smallest/largest possible largest modulus $n_k$, the maximal reciprocal sum $\sum_i1/n_i$, and whether infinitely many integers have their positive divisors $>1$ form irreducible covering sets. The last is **already resolved**; the rest remains incompletely understood.

## A small example

The divisors of 12 larger than one are $\{2,3,4,6,12\}$. An exact search gives a covering assignment $(0\bmod2),(0\bmod3),(1\bmod4),(1\bmod6),(11\bmod12)$; exhaustive search over *all* new residue choices after removing **each** modulus shows that no proper subset of these moduli covers. This verifies irreducibility of this one modulus set. Its reciprocal sum is exactly $4/3$, compatible with the necessary covering-density bound.

## What is known

https://www.erdosproblems.com/1189 cites [Er80,p.95] and marks the omnibus problem **OPEN**. Sun [Su07] proves that for every odd prime $p$, the divisors $>1$ of $2^{p-1}p$ form an irreducible covering set, settling the 'infinitely many integers' subquestion **YES**. Simpson [Si85] established a bound $n_k\le2^{k-1}$ in the relevant context, and BBMST24 derived $I(k)\le\exp((c+o(1))k^{3/2}/\sqrt{\log k})$ for an explicit-source constant. Count of irreducible **sets of moduli** differs from count of minimal **residue assignments** in #1188.
