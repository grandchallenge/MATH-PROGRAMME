## The question in plain language

Let

$$
Q_k=2\cdot3\cdot5\cdots p_k
$$

be the product of the first $k$ primes. List the reduced residues $1=a_1<a_2<\cdots<a_{\varphi(Q_k)}<Q_k$, and divide each **internal consecutive gap** $a_i-a_{i-1}$ by its average spacing $Q_k/\varphi(Q_k)$.

Erdős asked whether, for every $c\ge0$, the proportion of normalized gaps **at most $c$** converges to a continuous function as $k\to\infty$. These are gaps between integers **coprime to a primorial**, not gaps between actual consecutive primes.

## A small example

For $Q_2=6$, the reduced residues are $1,5$ and the only internal gap is four. Its normalized length is $4/(6/2)=4/3$. A finite proportion with threshold $c=1$ is therefore zero.

The denominator in the catalogue is $\varphi(Q_k)$, although only $\varphi(Q_k)-1$ internal gaps are counted. This distinction disappears in the limit, but must not be silently replaced with a cyclic wrap-around gap.

## What is known

**PROVED.** Hooley (1965) showed the gap distribution is **exponential**, with limiting cumulative distribution

$$
F(c)=1-e^{-c}\qquad(c\ge0).
$$

The maintained [Erdős #235](https://www.erdosproblems.com/235) attributes the theorem to [Ho65]. The displayed formula on that page includes an extraneous-looking factor $(1+o(1))$ applied to what is called the **limit function**; the sound interpretation is that the finite empirical distributions at index $k$ distribution approaches the fixed function $1-e^{-c}$. The limit is continuous, with $F(0)=0$.

This result is **not** a theorem that gaps between genuine consecutive primes follow an exponential distribution. That stronger distributional question is #234 and remains open.

## What remains

A bounded task enumerates reduced residues for the first several primorials and computes empirical cumulative distribution values at selected thresholds. Acceptance: calculate the $Q_2=6$ example exactly; exclude the wrap-around gap; use $Q_k/\varphi(Q_k)$; and state clearly that convergence for primorial reduced residues is not a proof for prime gaps.
