## The question in plain language

A graph is *free of four-cliques* if no four vertices are all mutually connected. Its *independence number* is the greatest number of vertices with no edges between them. [Erdős Problem #22](https://www.erdosproblems.com/22) asked whether, for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, all sufficiently large numbers of vertices $n$ admit a four-clique-free graph having **at least** $n^2/8$ edges and independence number at most $\varepsilon n$.

The exact edge threshold matters. Having $(1/8-o(1))n^2$ edges is close asymptotically, but does not by itself guarantee at least $n^2/8$. The original question is **proved**.

## A small example

A four-cycle has four vertices and four edges, so it contains no four-clique and has more than $4^2/8=2$ edges. But its independence number is 2, half its vertices. Hence for $\varepsilon<1/2$ this finite graph fails the required small-independence condition despite having plenty of edges.

It illustrates why controlling edge count alone is insufficient; both requirements must hold in the **same graph** at arbitrarily large sizes.

## What is known

Fox, Loh and Zhao, *The critical window for the classical Ramsey–Turán problem*, *Combinatorica* **35** (2015), 435–476, established exact-threshold constructions with independence number bounded by

$$
C n\frac{(\log\log n)^{3/2}}{(\log n)^{1/2}}
$$

for some absolute constant $C$ and all sufficiently large $n$. Because the factor involving logarithms tends to zero, this yields the requested $\varepsilon n$ upper bound for each fixed $\varepsilon>0$ while retaining **at least** $n^2/8$ edges.

Earlier Bollobás–Erdős constructions established the limiting density near one eighth; these alone did not supply the exact-threshold theorem.
