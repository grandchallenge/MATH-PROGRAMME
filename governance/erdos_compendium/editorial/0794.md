## The question in plain language

Claim: every 3-uniform hypergraph with 3n vertices and at least n^3+1 edges contains a four-vertex subhypergraph with at least three triples OR a five-vertex subhypergraph with at least seven triples. This is DISPROVED for n=3 by a 9-vertex construction.

**Separate claims:** the explicit nine-vertex hypergraph refutes the literal conjecture. It does not resolve the still-different sharp extremal density question related to the Frankl–Füredi construction.

## A small example

Partition vertices 1..9 into X={1,2,3},Y={4,5,6},Z={7,8,9}. All XxYxZ triples (27) and X as an additional edge (one). For n=3, n^3+1=28. Exhaustively check no 4-vertex subset contains 3 triples and no 5-vertex subset contains 7; exact finite certificate is the bounded successor.

The calculation illustrates the exact definitions and hypotheses. No single finite example establishes the reported asymptotic assertion.
