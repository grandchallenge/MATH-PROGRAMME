**Editorial status:** UNACCEPTED_DRAFT. Erdős Problem #538 asks for the best upper bound on the sum of reciprocals of a set of integers with **at most $r$ representations of any integer as a prime times a set element**, where $r\ge2$ is fixed. Erdős's 1973 bound is $O_r(\log N/\log\log N)$. A July 2026 AI-assisted claim says this **order** can be matched by a construction, but its external Lean proof has not been independently replayed or accepted by the maintained catalogue. The register remains **OPEN** and this article is a designated critical-review sample.

## The question in plain language

Let $A\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,N\}$ and fix an integer $r\ge2$. For each positive integer $m$, count the number

$$
R_A(m)=\#\{(p,a): p\text{ prime},\ a\in A,\ pa=m\}.
$$

A set is *admissible* if $R_A(m)\le r$ for **every** $m$, not merely for the integers $m\le N$. Distinct representations mean distinct pairs $(p,a)$. Since $m$ is fixed, different primes yield different set elements.

Define the largest possible reciprocal mass

$$
M_r(N)=\max_{\substack{A\subseteq[1,N]\\R_A(m)\le r\ \forall m}}
\sum_{a\in A}\frac1a.
$$

The task is to estimate $M_r(N)$ as $N$ grows. The original Erdős question asks whether his known upper estimate can be improved. One can ask separately for the **order of magnitude**, the sharp leading constant, and its dependence on $r$.

The stronger prime-multiple question, Erdős #537, is related. Ruzsa constructed relatively dense sets in a *single upper half interval* with at most two representations of each $m$, but that alone does not determine the maximal reciprocal mass collected over all scales.

## A small example

For $N=6$, consider $A=\{2,3,5\}$. The products with primes at most $N$ include

$$
2\cdot3=3\cdot2=6,
\qquad
2\cdot5=5\cdot2=10,
\qquad
3\cdot5=5\cdot3=15.
$$

Each of these has two distinct representations, not three. The reciprocal sum is

$$
\sum_{a\in A}\frac1a
=\frac12+\frac13+\frac15=\frac{31}{30}.
$$

For any finite admissible set the prime bound in computational tests needs care: although the original hypothesis permits primes larger than $N$, **any repeated product involving at least two different prime representations must use primes at most $N$**.

Indeed, if $pa=qb$ with distinct primes $p\ne q$ and $a,b\le N$, primality implies $p\mid b$ and $q\mid a$. Consequently $p,q\le N$. Every violation of a cap $r\ge1$ involves at least two representations, so the violation is detected using only primes $p\le N$.

This makes the finite representation condition exactly decidable without guessing an arbitrary cutoff on the prime factor.

## What is known

### Erdős's original weighted count

In *Problems and Results on Combinatorial Number Theory*, *A Survey of Combinatorial Theory* (1973), Chapter 12, [printed p.124](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1973-21.pdf#page=8), Erdős derives his crucial weighted double count (4.5). The original page was directly inspected.

Restrict the prime variable temporarily to $p\le N$. Since $a\le N$, every product $pa$ lies in $[1,N^2]$. Rearranging the nonnegative terms **exactly** gives

$$
\left(\sum_{a\in A}\frac1a\right)
\left(\sum_{\substack{p\le N\\p\text{ prime}}}\frac1p\right)
=\sum_{m\le N^2}\frac{R_{A,N}(m)}m,
$$

where $R_{A,N}(m)$ counts representations with $p\le N$. The hypothesis $R_A(m)\le r$ implies $R_{A,N}(m)\le r$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{
\left(\sum_{a\in A}\frac1a\right)
\left(\sum_{p\le N}\frac1p\right)\le rH_{N^2},
}
$$

with $H_{N^2}=\sum_{m=1}^{N^2}1/m$.

Since $H_{N^2}=2\log N+O(1)$ and the prime reciprocal sum satisfies $\sum_{p\le N}1/p=\log\log N+O(1)$, this yields

$$
\boxed{M_r(N)=O\left(r\frac{\log N}{\log\log N}\right).}
$$

This was already known in 1973. The finite identity is the mathematical core, while the asymptotic bound also uses estimates for prime reciprocals.

### A pending July 2026 sharp-order claim

On July 15, 2026, Colin Snyder submitted an [AI-assisted matching-order proof claim](https://erdosproblems.ai/wiki/problems/integer_sequences/E0538/claims/2026_07_15_snyder) asserting the lower construction

$$
M_r(N)\gg_r\frac{\log N}{\log\log N}
$$

for fixed $r\ge2$, which, together with Erdős's bound, would give

$$
M_r(N)=\Theta_r\left(\frac{\log N}{\log\log N}\right).
$$

The proposed construction uses squarefree numbers with a prescribed number of prime factors. Within a layer of $(k+1)$ distinct primes, deleting one prime gives $k+1$ possible representations. The condition of at most $r$ prime representations becomes a **cap on selected facets** of this prime-set hypergraph.

The reported innovation is a finite-field construction selecting a fraction on the scale $1/k$ of the $k$-subsets while allowing at most two selected facets in each $(k+1)$-subset, replacing a weaker $1/k^2$ construction. A multiplicative weighting argument is then claimed to transfer this cap to reciprocal mass.

This is **not an independently accepted result** in GCL. The [maintained catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/538) still labels #538 OPEN; the claim remains pending. The finite-field proof and full external Lean dependency tree have not been independently checked. Even if the order were certified, the leading asymptotic constant and optimal dependence on $r$ would remain to be determined.
