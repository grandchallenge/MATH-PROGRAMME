## The question in plain language

Let f(n) count sum-free subsets A of {1,...,n}: there are no x,y,z∈A with x+y=z, including cases x=y. Cameron–Erdős asked whether f(n)=2^{(1+o(1))n/2}. The answer is PROVED; independent work of Sapozhenko and Green gives the stronger f(n)∼c_{n mod 2} 2^{n/2}, with two parity-dependent positive constants. One must distinguish number of subsets from maximum size of one sum-free set.

## A small example

Any subset of the upper half {floor(n/2)+1,...,n} is sum-free: for x,y in that half, x+y>n. Therefore f(n)≥2^{ceil(n/2)}. At n=4 the upper-half set {3,4} yields four sum-free subsets, though other sum-free subsets also exist. The lower bound alone does not establish the asymptotic count.

## What is known

Erdős and Cameron conjectured the asymptotic exponent. Green [Gr04] and Sapozhenko [Sa03] independently proved an upper f(n)=O(2^{n/2}), with parity-refined leading constants c_even,c_odd for f(n)/2^{n/2}. The maintained page warns that counting maximal sum-free sets is a SEPARATE problem (#877); applying these counts to maximal families would be invalid. Original journal proofs not reconstructed here.
