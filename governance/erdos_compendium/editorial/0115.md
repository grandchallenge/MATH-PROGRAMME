## The question in plain language

Let $p(z)$ be a complex polynomial of degree $n$. The filled unit lemniscate is

$$
E_p=\{z\in\mathbb C:|p(z)|\le1\}.
$$

Erdős asked whether, assuming **$E_p$ is connected**, the largest modulus of the derivative there is at most $(1/2+o(1))n^2$. The connectedness condition is indispensable: far-apart roots can produce separated components and much larger derivatives at the boundary.

## A small example

Take $p(z)=z^n$. Its filled lemniscate is the closed unit disk, and $|p'(z)|=n|z|^{n-1}\le n$. Thus the maximum is exactly $n$, smaller than the conjectured quadratic worst-case scale. The monic Chebyshev-polynomial family supplies the large-derivative extremizers; an example with small derivative is not evidence of a sharper universal bound.

The statement is **asymptotic**. It permits a small positive error coefficient tending to zero, rather than asserting $\max|p'|\le n^2/2$ for all degrees.

## What is known

**PROVED.** Alexander Eremenko and László Lempert, [*An extremal problem for polynomials*](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~eremenko/dvi/lempert.pdf), *Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society* 122 (1994), 191–193, establish a stronger sharp comparison and identify scaled Chebyshev polynomials as extremizers.

In their theorem, after appropriate monic normalization and translation, the maximal derivative on the connected filled lemniscate is bounded by

$$
2^{1/n-1}n^2.
$$

The coefficient $2^{1/n-1}$ tends to $1/2$, giving the problem's asymptotic assertion. This exact coefficient matters: replacing it prematurely with $1/2$ loses the finite-degree correction. The Eremenko–Lempert primary theorem and its characterization of equality are explicitly available in the author-hosted article; its full complex-analytic argument has not been independently replayed by GCL.

A community-linked Lean implementation is reported on the maintained problem page, but GCL has not run its exact toolchain and audited its axioms.

## What remains

A bounded proof task is to show that $2^{1/n-1}\to1/2$ and hence the exact theorem implies the asserted $(1/2+o(1))n^2$ statement. Acceptance: explicitly quantify the $o(1)$, preserve the connectedness of $E_p$, and confirm the direct $z^n$ derivative example. Stop short of claiming an independent proof of the extremal Eremenko–Lempert theorem itself.
