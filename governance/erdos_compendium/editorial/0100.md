## The question in plain language

Place $n$ distinct points in the Euclidean plane. Assume every distance between two different points is at least one. Additionally, whenever two pairwise distances are **different**, their numerical values must differ by at least one.

Erdős #100 asks whether the diameter $D$, the largest pairwise distance, must satisfy

$$
D\ge c n
$$

for some universal constant $c>0$ and all sufficiently large $n$. A stronger conjecture proposes $D\ge n-1$. Equal distances are permitted without restriction; requiring all distances to be different would be a different problem.

## A small example

For points $(0,0),(1,0),\ldots,(n-1,0)$, the distinct distances are $1,2,\ldots,n-1$. Their pairwise gaps are at least one, and the diameter equals $n-1$. Thus the stronger conjectured value would be sharp if it held universally.

In contrast, a square of side length one has distance values $1$ and $\sqrt2$, which differ by **less** than one. Despite its unit minimum separation, it fails the second hypothesis. This example shows why that hypothesis cannot be discarded.

## What is known

The conjecture is **OPEN**. The maintained [Erdős #100](https://www.erdosproblems.com/100) records Kanold's historical lower bound $D\ge n^{3/4}$ and a nine-point construction of Piepmeyer with diameter less than five. The latter does not refute a claim asserted only for sufficiently large $n$, and the original construction has not been independently audited here.

Guth and Katz's primary [2015 Annals theorem](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2015/181-1/p02) proves that any $n$ distinct planar points determine at least $c n/\log n$ distinct distances. In this problem those distinct lengths $u_1<\cdots<u_t$ obey $u_1\ge1$ and $u_{j+1}\ge u_j+1$. Therefore the largest one, $D=u_t$, satisfies $D\ge t$, giving the unconditional consequence

$$
D\gg\frac{n}{\log n}.
$$

This is a rigorous reduction from a primary theorem, but still weaker than the desired linear lower bound.

## GCL's current state

The pinned catalogue and maintained #100 classify it **OPEN**. The original assignment is [#1369](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1369). The Guth–Katz abstract was verified, and the transfer from distinct-length count to diameter is elementary and self-contained. Kanold's original proof and Piepmeyer's coordinates remain bibliographic leads rather than primary-page-checked evidence.

The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1369#issuecomment-6161023011). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded proof task is to formalize the separation lemma: $t$ distinct real numbers, all at least one and separated by gaps at least one, have maximum at least $t$. Apply it to a finite metric distance set and replay the Guth–Katz consequence with correct universal quantifiers. Acceptance: the one-dimensional arithmetic progression gives equality $D=n-1$; the unit square is rejected as an invalid separated-distance input. Stop without asserting $D\gg n$.
