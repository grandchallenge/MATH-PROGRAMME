## The question in plain language

Let F denote ALL configurations of three distinct 3-uniform hyperedges on at most six vertices. The Brown–Erdős–Sós (6,3) question asks whether any n-vertex 3-uniform hypergraph avoiding F has $o(n^2)$ hyperedges, i.e. $\mathrm{ex}_3(n,F)=o(n^2)$. The answer is YES by the classical Ruzsa–Szemerédi theorem, while later generalizations are distinct. The source describes 'on six vertices', which may include isolated vertices; the intended property is that no three edges span at most six vertices.

## A small example

Three triples {1,2,3}, {3,4,5}, {1,4,6} use exactly six vertices, so a 3-uniform hypergraph containing these edges violates the (6,3)-free condition. Two disjoint triples on six vertices are allowed (the condition forbids configurations of THREE edges), illustrating the number-of-edges quantifier.

## What is known

Brown, Erdős and Sós [BES73] conjectured the (6,3) density bound and Ruzsa–Szemerédi [RuSz78] proved it, classically linked to the triangle removal lemma and induced matchings.
