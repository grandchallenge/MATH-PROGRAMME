## The question in plain language

The question concerns two forms of additive structure inside the perfect squares: nearly constant successive gaps and high-dimensional cubes made from subset sums. Finite examples do not establish an absolute gap tolerance for every length.

The square numbers are asked to contain (i) arbitrarily long quasi-arithmetic progressions with successive gaps in one interval [d,d+C] for an ABSOLUTE C independent of length, and (ii) arbitrarily high-dimensional nondegenerate Hilbert cubes a+sum epsilon_i b_i consisting entirely of distinct squares. The two questions are related; do not equate a four-term exact arithmetic progression with a bounded-error quasi-progression.

## A small example

A genuine two-dimensional square cube is given by base 1 with increments 48 and 120: the four corners 1,49,121,169 are the squares of 1,7,11,13. This confirms dimension two, not arbitrary dimensions.

Finite exact example: 1,4,9 have gaps 3,5 and satisfy d=3,C=2; 1,4,9,16 have 3,5,7 and satisfy d=3,C=4. This is not a universal C valid for every k. An exact 2-dimensional cube example is 1,25,49,169: base a=1, increments 24,48 give 1,25,49,73, so NOT a square; do not mislabel it as a cube. Exhaustively search a,b1,b2 positive and four square corners within a finite cutoff, returning a valid witness or an exact bounded negative certificate.
