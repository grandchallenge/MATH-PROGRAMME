## The question in plain language

Can we construct an infinite set of integers whose pairwise sums are always *squarefree*, while keeping its elements relatively small? A number is squarefree if no square of a prime divides it.

[Erdős Problem #1103](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1103) asks how rapidly an infinite increasing sequence

$$
A=\{a_1<a_2<\cdots\}
$$

must grow if every number in $A+A=\{a+b:a,b\in A\}$ is squarefree.

The definition includes **self-sums** $2a$, not just sums of different elements. Erdős suspected that an infinite sequence with merely polynomial growth might not exist.

## A small example

An exact search finds the four-element set

$$
A=\{1,5,21,37\}
$$

whose distinct pair sums, including the self-sums, are

$$
A+A=\{2,6,10,22,26,38,42,58,74\}.
$$

Every one is squarefree. The verification tests divisibility by all prime squares up to the square root of each sum. This is only a **finite** example; the problem asks about an *infinite* extendible sequence.

## What is known

Erdős originally noted that an exponentially growing sequence with squarefree pair sums exists.

The [maintained problem page](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1103) cites Konyagin (2004) and [van Doorn and Tao (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01087), reporting bounds including

$$
a_j\gg j^{15/11-o(1)}
$$

and the existence of some sequence with

$$
a_j<\exp(5j/\log j)
$$

for sufficiently large $j$. The stronger form of the lower estimate is derived from a finite-set extremal argument. These leave a substantial gap between known polynomial lower bounds and subexponential upper constructions.

An important source distinction concerns a much stronger condition Erdős also proposed: restricting *each individual* $a\bmod p^2$ to the strictly positive lower half for **every** prime $p$. The maintained source explains why that stricter requirement cannot hold for infinitely many $a$. That is **not** a disproof of the weaker squarefree-$A+A$ problem.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source audit #2374](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2374#issuecomment-6168365272) separately records the two conditions, the external 2025 results and the exact four-element example. The published 2025 authors' abstract confirms treatment of squarefree-sum sequence growth, but GCL has not reconstructed its full proof.

## What remains

Review the original 1981, Konyagin 2004 and van Doorn–Tao 2025 proofs and determine the precise hypotheses and exponent losses. Separate the impossible stronger residue condition from the still-open minimum growth of a squarefree-sum infinite sequence.
