## The question in plain language

Take $n$ distinct points in the plane, with **no three collinear**. How many different distances must occur?

[Erdős Problem #1082](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1082) actually contains two questions. The first asks whether the entire point set determines at least $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ distinct distances. The stronger version asks whether **one particular point** must have that many different distances to the other points.

The distinction is decisive. The stronger version is false, while the general, global version remains open.

## A small example

Build a square of side length $1$, and erect an outward equilateral triangle on **each** side. Retain the square's four corners and the four outer triangle tips.

Write $r=\sqrt3$. Exact coordinates for the eight points are

$$
(0,0),\ (1,0),\ (1,1),\ (0,1),
$$

and

$$
(1/2,-r/2),\ (1+r/2,1/2),\
(1/2,1+r/2),\ (-r/2,1/2).
$$

No three points are collinear. Among all $28$ unordered point pairs, the complete set of **squared distances** is

$$
\{1,\ 2,\ 2+\sqrt3,\ 4+2\sqrt3\}.
$$

Thus the configuration has **four** distinct global distances, meeting $\lfloor8/2\rfloor=4$.

But each square corner sees only the three squared distances $\{1,2,2+\sqrt3\}$. Each triangle tip sees only $\{1,2+\sqrt3,4+2\sqrt3\}$. No point sees four different distances. The stronger, single-point conjecture therefore fails.

These identities, and the noncollinearity of all $\binom83=56$ triples, were independently checked with exact symbolic algebra. No floating-point approximation is required.

## What is known

The [maintained catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1082) credits Szemerédi with a weaker bound involving roughly $n/3$ distances and associates the eight-point counterexample with **Heiko Harborth**, as described by Erdős and Fishburn (1997) and Fishburn (2002).

The historical result already disproved the stronger local conjecture before the recent formal work. The [2026 discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/1082) reports a Lean-verifiable eight-point construction found by a DeepMind prover, while explicitly acknowledging the older construction.

The original **global** lower-bound conjecture, however, has not been disproved by this example. It holds with equality for these eight points.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated GCL source audit #2353](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2353#issuecomment-6158539696) separately records the two mathematical claims and gives the complete algebraic coordinates. It checks all $28$ pairwise squared distances and the orientation determinants of all $56$ triples.

The [frozen formal-conjectures statement](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1082.lean) likewise separates the open global theorem from the disproved single-point theorem and links an external Lean proof. GCL has **not** independently kernel-replayed that development, and the original journal sources remain to be page-audited.

## What remains

For the single-point claim, the next obligation is provenance and formal-verification review, not rediscovery: check the 1997 and 2002 constructions, then independently replay the external Lean proof with its exact axioms.

For the global claim, the substantial problem remains whether every noncollinear $n$-point set determines at least $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ different distances. A finite eight-point configuration with exactly four distances cannot settle the general question.
