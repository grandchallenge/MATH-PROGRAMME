## The question in plain language

Let $A$ consist of nonnegative integers whose base-three expansion uses **only digits zero and one**, and let $B$ consist of nonnegative integers whose base-four expansion also uses only zero and one. Form their sumset

$$
C=A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,\ b\in B\}.
$$

Erdős #125 asks whether $C$ has **positive lower asymptotic density**: does there exist a constant $c>0$ such that, for all sufficiently large $X$,

$$
|C\cap[1,X]|\ge cX?
$$

This is stronger than requiring positive upper density along a subsequence. If lower density is zero, the original conjecture is false even if the sumset remains sizeable on some scales.

## A small example

The first few members of $A$ are $0,1,3,4,9,10,12,13,\ldots$; the first few of $B$ are $0,1,4,5,16,17,20,21,\ldots$. Therefore $2=1+1$, $3=3+0$ and $5=1+4$ belong to $C$. However, $62$ does not belong to $C$: enumerating the allowed base-three and base-four digit patterns up to $62$ yields no pair with sum $62$. Finite omissions or local density measurements do not settle asymptotic lower density.

## What is known

**DISPROVED in the lower-density interpretation.** A DeepMind-associated Lean proof, discussed on [Erdős #125](https://www.erdosproblems.com/125) and in the [original March–April 2026 discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/125), establishes that the lower asymptotic density of $C$ is zero. The maintained source links the [formal proof revision](https://github.com/mo271/formal-conjectures/blob/c27415379b5dbe34105d1fdd707994540c4c6fc7/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/125.lean#L468).

The proof explanation iterates a scale-dependent density reduction. Near pairs of powers $3^k$ and $4^\ell$, certain gaps constrain the “top digits” of members of $C$. A map separating top and bottom digits yields, at selected scales, a density at most a fixed fraction (described as 99%) of the density at an earlier scale. Iteration forces the **liminf** to vanish. This does not, by itself, show the **limsup** vanishes. Thus the question of positive upper density remains separate.

Earlier Melfi and Hasler–Melfi obtained power-law lower counts, including $|C\cap[1,X]|\gg X^{0.9777}$, which are fully compatible with zero lower density. In the March 2026 discussion, the proof authors also flag a bibliographic uncertainty about purported Pach–Pintz references. That is a source-fidelity issue; one should not repeat those citations without checking them.

## GCL's current state

The pinned and maintained catalogue status is **DISPROVED** for the positive-lower-density statement. The original issue is [#1394](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1394). GCL read the protected [Formal Conjectures 125 file](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/125.lean): it separates positive lower density, positive upper density, and zero density into distinct statements and contains proof holes. The external completed formalization has not been independently built and axiom-audited here.

The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1394#issuecomment-6163031838). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded successor is to enumerate the binary-digit-restricted sets up to $X=10^5$ and compute $|C\cap[1,X]|$ exactly at each power-of-three and power-of-four cutoff. Acceptance: independent digit-parser and constructive-set implementations agree, zero belongs to both $A,B$, and no finite density observation is misrepresented as proving a liminf result. An optional formal audit should replay the external lower-density theorem at its pinned commit and independently verify its dependencies.
