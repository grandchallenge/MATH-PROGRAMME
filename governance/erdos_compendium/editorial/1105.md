## The question in plain language

Colour every edge of a complete graph with as many distinct colours as possible, but avoid a *rainbow* copy of a particular graph. A copy is rainbow when **all its edges have different colours**.

The maximum number of edge colours avoiding a rainbow copy of $H$ is the *anti-Ramsey number* $\mathrm{AR}(n,H)$. [Erdős Problem #1105](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1105) asks for formulas when $H$ is a cycle $C_k$ or a path $P_k$.

## A small example

Label the six vertices of $K_6$ by $0,1,\ldots,5$. Give each edge $\{i,j\}$, with $i<j$, the colour $i$.

This uses **five distinct colours**. In any triangle with ordered vertices $i<j<k$, the two edges $\{i,j\}$ and $\{i,k\}$ have the same colour $i$. Thus **no triangle is rainbow**.

The construction proves $\mathrm{AR}(6,C_3)\ge5$. Erdős, Simonovits and Sós established the matching general formula $\mathrm{AR}(n,C_3)=n-1$; the simple colouring is only the lower-bound part of that theorem.

## What is known

The maintained catalogue credits Montellano-Ballesteros and Neumann-Lara (2005) with an exact cycle formula implying, for fixed $k$,

$$
\mathrm{AR}(n,C_k)
=\left(\frac{k-2}{2}+\frac1{k-1}\right)n+O_k(1).
$$

For paths $P_k$, a more intricate exact formula is proposed when $n\ge k\ge5$. It uses $\ell=\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor$, a maximum of two expressions involving binomial coefficients, and a parity-dependent correction $\varepsilon$.

The provenance of that path formula needs care. Earlier **published** results cover specific parameter ranges, including work of Simonovits and Sós (1984); the maintained page describes the all‑$n\ge k$ formula as having been **announced** by Yuan (2021). An announcement should not be silently represented as an independently verified published proof.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source audit #2376](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2376#issuecomment-6168367080) distinguishes cycles from paths and published results from the announced full path theorem. The exact six-vertex edge colouring has been computationally checked for every triangle.

The frozen formal-conjectures file labels both cases solved but its theorem bodies are not independently kernel-replayed by GCL.
