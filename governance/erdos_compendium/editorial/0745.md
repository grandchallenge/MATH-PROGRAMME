## The question in plain language

The maintained source literally asks about the second-largest component of G(n,p) with p=1/n and claims it has O(log n) vertices almost surely. This is MATHEMATICALLY INCOMPATIBLE with the critical random-graph theory: at p=1/n, the largest components (including the second) have size scale n^(2/3), not logarithmic. The cited 1980 Komlós–Sulyok–Szemerédi theorem concerns the SUPERCRITICAL p=λ/n with fixed λ>1 and is not a proof of the critical statement. Source must be corrected before admission.

## A small example

At p=1/n the expected degree is approximately one, the critical phase-transition location. In Aldous's 1997 critical scaling, the joint ranked component sizes multiplied by n^(−2/3) converge to nondegenerate random excursion lengths, including a positive second excursion in the limit. By contrast, for fixed λ>1, a linear-size giant component emerges while all components outside it are O(log n) w.h.p. These are different parameter regimes, not two estimates for the same graph.

## What is known

PRIMARY AUTHORS' KSS80 paper 'Second largest component in a random graph' (Studia Sci. Math. Hungar. 15 (1980), 391–395) abstract explicitly assumes p=λ/n with λ>1 and states that the second-largest component has logarithmic size. David Aldous, 'Brownian excursions, critical random graphs and the multiplicative coalescent', Ann. Probability 25 (1997), 812–854, establishes joint n^(2/3) critical component scaling; the author research page confirms all leading components have that order. Thus the maintained #745 text's p=1/n and its O(log n) proved attribution cannot simultaneously hold. This is a source-parameter error, NOT a novel disproof of random graph theory.
