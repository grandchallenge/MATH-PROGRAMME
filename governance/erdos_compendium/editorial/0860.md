## The question in plain language

Take every prime up to a cutoff $n$. We want to assign each prime a *different* multiple of that prime, with all assigned numbers lying in one short interval. How long must the interval be to guarantee that this can be done no matter where it begins? This is [Erdős Problem #860](https://www.erdosproblems.com/860), originating with Erdős and Pomerance.

A number divisible by two primes may serve either prime, but cannot be assigned twice. The difficulty is therefore a competition for a limited supply of integer positions, not merely whether each prime has *some* multiple in the interval.

## A small example

For primes $2,3,5$, consider six consecutive integers following $m=12$: $13,14,15,16,17,18$. The assignment $2\mapsto14$, $3\mapsto18$, $5\mapsto15$ succeeds. Smaller intervals may fail for some starting points. Exact enumeration over every residue modulo $2\cdot3\cdot5=30$ gives the least **right-closed** interval length $H=6$ that works for every $m$.

| Largest prime $n$ | Least right-closed length $H$ | Literal open-interval parameter $h$ |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 2 | 3 |
| 3 | 4 | 5 |
| 5 | 6 | 7 |
| 7 | 10 | 11 |
| 11 | 14 | 15 |

The last column differs by one because the integers in $(m,m+h)$ are $m+1,\ldots,m+h-1$ when $m,h$ are integers, whereas $(m,m+H]$ includes $m+H$. We must not confuse these conventions when comparing papers.

## What is known

The 1980 Erdős–Pomerance paper proved an upper bound of order $n^{3/2}/\sqrt{\log n}$. Subsequent work of Erdős–Selfridge and Ruzsa gave lower bounds, including $H(n)/n\to\infty$. The maintained catalogue still displays the 1980 upper bound and marks the asymptotic estimation problem open.

A significant later source is [Kaizhe Chen and Samuel Korsky, *Improved Bounds for Distinct Multiples in Intervals*, arXiv:2607.26450v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26450v2). Their Theorems 1.2 and 1.3 state, for the prime-moduli right-closed convention,

$$
H(n)\ll\frac{n^{4/3}}{(\log n)^{1/3}}
$$

and

$$
H(n)\ge n\exp\!\left[\left(\frac{\log2}{2}-o(1)\right)\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\right].
$$

The paper defines a different, stronger function $F(n)$ for **all** moduli $1,\ldots,n$, and proves $F(n)\ge H(n)$; its upper bound for $F(n)$ is not the same as the prime-moduli bound. The cited results are published claims from the authors' preprint, not independently certified GCL proofs. They improve the recorded historical bounds but do not determine the asymptotic order.

## GCL's current state

A local finite verification program (`governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_860.py`) exhaustively examines the $\prod_{p\le n}p$ possible starting residues for $n=2,3,5,7,11$. It constructs the bipartite graph with primes on one side and eligible integer positions on the other. A maximum-matching search is independently checked against Hall's criterion: for every subset $S$ of primes, the union of their eligible positions must contain at least $|S|$ elements.

The two methods agree on every residue and give the five exact values in the table. This verifies the finite matching implementation, not either asymptotic bound. A primary-source and version comparison has identified the Chen–Korsky update and the endpoint difference. A complete line-by-line audit of the new analytic proofs is still pending.

## What remains

The unresolved question is the true growth rate of the worst-case interval length. A bounded next task is to extract the exact hypotheses and parameter normalization from Chen–Korsky Theorems 1.2–1.3, compare them with the 1980 proof, and rerun the finite matching/Hall checks with a documented reproducibility hash. Any claim that the new paper *solves* #860 would be premature.
