## The question in plain language

A graph is *free of four-vertex cliques* if it contains no complete subgraph on four vertices. [Erdős #595](https://www.erdosproblems.com/595) asks whether some infinite free of four-vertex cliques graph **cannot** have its edges covered by countably many triangle-free subgraphs. Equivalently, can one find such a graph for which **every** countable edge-colouring produces a monochromatic triangle? The question is existential: not every free of four-vertex cliques graph needs to have this property.

## A small example

Every graph on a countable vertex set admits a countable covering by triangle-free graphs: list its vertices and put all edges at a vertex into its *star*. Stars contain no triangles.

In fact the same covering assertion holds whenever the vertex set has cardinality at most the continuum (the cardinality of all infinite binary sequences). Label each vertex by a distinct infinite binary sequence. Put an edge in class $i$ where the endpoint sequences first differ at coordinate $i$. Every class is bipartite, hence triangle-free, and the countably many classes cover all edges. The construction uses no free of four-vertex cliques assumption at all.

## What is known

The continuum threshold is a classical edge-decomposition observation in the setting of Erdős–Hajnal's 1967 work. It means a hypothetical witness to #595 must have **strictly more than continuum-many vertices**. A disjoint union of finite graphs needing increasingly many colours can have countably infinite cover number and is not, by itself, a counterexample.


## GCL's current state

The [authenticated source audit](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1866#issuecomment-6098889544) reconciles the frozen statement, primary literature and bounded elementary checks. These observations are not a new GCL mathematical certification.

## What remains

Produce a free of four-vertex cliques graph of cardinality larger than the continuum for which no countable triangle-free decomposition exists, or prove such graphs cannot occur. Finite Folkman-type obstructions do not automatically settle this much stronger infinite-cardinal question.
