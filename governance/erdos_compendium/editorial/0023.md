## The question in plain language

A graph is *triangle-free* when it contains no three mutually connected vertices. It is *bipartite* if its vertices can be divided into two classes with no edge inside either class.

[Erdős Problem #23](https://www.erdosproblems.com/23) asks whether **every** triangle-free graph on $5n$ vertices can be made bipartite by removing at most $n^2$ edges. The status is **FALSIFIABLE**: the conjecture remains open, but one explicitly verified finite counterexample would disprove it.

## A small example

A cycle on five vertices has no triangle, but it is not bipartite because its cycle length is odd. Removing just one edge leaves a path and makes it bipartite. With $n=1$ the proposed bound is exactly one edge, and the example attains it.

The *balanced blow-up* of the pentagon replaces each of its five vertices by an independent set of $n$ vertices, and joins every pair of vertices in classes corresponding to adjacent positions on the cycle. There are $5n$ vertices and $5n^2$ edges. Its maximum cut has $4n^2$ edges, so precisely $n^2$ edges must be removed to make it bipartite. The proposed universal bound, if true, would therefore be sharp.

## What is known

Balogh, Clemen and Lidický, *Max Cuts in Triangle-free Graphs* (2021), establish that any triangle-free graph with $V$ vertices can be made bipartite after removing at most $V^2/23.5$ edges. With $V=5n$ this is

$$
\frac{25}{23.5}n^2\approx1.064n^2.
$$

That approaches but does not reach the proposed coefficient one. The published methods also establish the target in specific edge-density regimes, without completing the universal conjecture.

## GCL's current state

[Source audit #1292](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1292#issuecomment-6121180103) compares the original claim and frozen formal statement, including its quantifier over all triangle-free graphs on exactly $5n$ vertices. It records the elementary pentagon blow-up extremal construction and warns that auxiliary theorem declarations with unfilled proofs do not constitute GCL kernel acceptance.

## What remains

Prove the global sharp bound for every triangle-free graph, or exhibit and independently verify a finite graph violating it. The blow-up example demonstrates optimality of the conjectured coefficient, not correctness of the universal claim.

- [Balogh–Clemen–Lidický (2021), author paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14179): finite constant improvement, not the open sharp bound.
