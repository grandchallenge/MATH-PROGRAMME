## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1180](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1180) asks the following. The maintained status is **PROVED**.

For every $\epsilon>0$ ask whether some constant $C_\epsilon$ independent of prime $p$ suffices to represent **every residue** $a\bmod p$ as a sum of **at most** $C_\epsilon$ elements of
$$
A_{p,\epsilon}=\{n^{-1}\bmod p:1\le n\le p^\epsilon\}.
$$
Repetitions of summands are allowed unless the original source specifies otherwise; this is an additive **sumset diameter** question, not multiplicative inverses of arbitrary residues or a covering by distinct denominators. For very small $p$ where the denominator range contains zero mod $p$, restrict to invertible $n$ as needed; the main statement concerns all primes under fixed $\epsilon$.

## A small example

For $p=11$ and $\epsilon=1/2$, the denominators are $1,2,3$ and their inverses modulo 11 are $\{1,6,4\}$. Exact breadth-first modular sumset computation shows reachable-residue counts with at most 0,1,2,3 summands are respectively **1,4,9,11**. Thus every residue modulo 11 is covered by **at most 3** summands, and two do not suffice. This says nothing about one $C_{\epsilon}$ uniformly for *all* primes.

## What is known

The maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/1180 marks **PROVED**. Croot obtained $(\log p)^{3+o(1)}$ summands, insufficient for constant $C_\epsilon$. Shparlinski [Sh02] established a uniform $C_\epsilon\ll\epsilon^{-3}$, improved by Glibichuk [Gl06] to $C_\epsilon\ll\epsilon^{-2}$. Counting possible ordered sums forces a lower bound of order $\epsilon^{-1}$ in the relevant asymptotic regime. The precise integer rounding for $p^\epsilon$ and zero-residue conventions need primary-source verification.
