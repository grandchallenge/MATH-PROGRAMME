## The question in plain language

A *maximal clique* is a set of pairwise adjacent vertices that cannot be enlarged by adding another vertex. A *clique transversal* is a set of vertices meeting every maximal clique that has at least two vertices. Let $\tau(G)$ be the smallest such transversal size.

For each $n$, let $H(n)$ be the largest integer such that every triangle-free graph on $n$ vertices has an independent set of at least $H(n)$ vertices. Erdős and Gallai ask whether, for **every** graph $G$ on $n$ vertices,

$$
\tau(G)\le n-H(n).
$$

The cliques to hit are **maximal**, not all cliques, and isolated vertices do not require coverage.

## A small example

In a triangle-free graph, every edge forms a maximal clique of size two. A clique transversal is then a vertex cover, whose minimum size equals $n-\alpha(G)$, where $\alpha(G)$ is the independence number. By definition $\alpha(G)\ge H(n)$, so the conjecture is automatically true when $G$ is triangle-free.

For the complete graph $K_n$, only the whole vertex set is maximal, and one vertex hits it; thus $\tau(K_n)=1$. Neither this trivial dense case nor the triangle-free case settles the question for general graphs with overlapping large maximal cliques.

## What is known

The universal inequality remains **OPEN**. The maintained [Erdős #151](https://www.erdosproblems.com/151), edited December 2025, credits Erdős and Gallai with the original question and notes that they could not resolve it even for $K_4$ free graphs. A general but weaker estimate $\tau(G)\le n-\sqrt n$ is recorded. The extremal function $H(n)$ is governed by independence bounds for triangle-free graphs; replacing it with a guessed asymptotic would introduce an unjustified step.

The maintained page contains a **proof claim** in discussion, but does not classify the conjecture as proved. Without a verified primary argument or an admitted formal proof, the claim is not a mathematical status change. A nonmaximal triangle nested inside a four-clique is not a separate clique that the transversal must hit: overlooking maximality changes the set-cover problem.

## What remains

A bounded task enumerates graphs on up to six vertices, lists inclusion-maximal cliques of size at least two, computes their minimum transversal size, and separately computes $H(n)$ by exhaustive enumeration of triangle-free graphs. Acceptance: isolated vertices create no clique constraints, the triangle-free identity $\tau(G)=n-\alpha(G)$ holds, and the conjectured inequality is checked only over enumerated finite cases. Failing to find a small counterexample would not establish the theorem.
