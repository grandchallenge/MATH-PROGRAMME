## The question in plain language

For a positive integer $N$, consider every subset $A$ of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ and form the reciprocal sum $\sum_{n\in A}1/n$. Different subsets can produce the same rational number: for example, $1/2=1/3+1/6$. Let $S(N)$ be the number of distinct rational sums that actually occur, including the empty sum $0$. Erdős Problem 320 asks for the growth of $S(N)$ as $N$ becomes large.

This is not a question about how many subsets there are: there are exactly $2^N$ subsets. It asks how much arithmetic collision occurs among those subsets. The natural scale is expressed through iterated logarithms. Write $log_1 x=log x$ and $log_{j+1}x=log(log_jx)$ when defined. The relevant stopping index is the last depth at which all iterated logarithms down to that depth remain at least $1$; the final value lies in the bounded interval $[1,e)$.

The exact catalogue statement is deliberately short: “Let $S(N)$ count the number of distinct sums of the form $\sum_{n\in A}1/n$ for $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$. Estimate $S(N)$.” It does not itself prescribe a particular asymptotic normalization.

## A small example

An exact enumeration with rational arithmetic gives the following initial values:

$$
S(1)=2,\quad S(2)=4,\quad S(3)=8,\quad S(4)=16,\quad S(5)=32,\quad S(6)=52.
$$

The first five values show no collision, while at $N=6$ collisions appear. One is

$$
\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{6}=\frac{1}{2}.
$$

The calculation was checked by constructing all $2^N$ subsets for $N\leq6$, summing as exact reduced fractions, and counting the resulting set; the assertions $S(6)=52$ and the displayed equality both passed. This verifies only a finite toy case. It neither estimates the eventual order of growth nor supports any asymptotic theorem. The value $S(6)=52$ is consistent with the exact small table discussed by Bettin, Grenié, Molteni, and Sanna, but the finite computation here is independent of a Lean replay.

## What is known

The primary paper by M. N. Bleicher and P. Erdős, *The number of distinct subsums of $\sum_{i=1}^N1/i$*, Mathematics of Computation 29 (1975), 29–42, proves a lower bound. Its Corollary 3, on printed page 39, gives for $k>3$ and $\log_{k+1}N>k+1$ a lower bound of the form

$$
\log S(N)\geq \frac{N}{\log N}\left(\log 2\prod_{i=3}^{k+1}\log_iN\right).
$$

The older Bleicher–Erdős work on Egyptian-fraction denominators supplies the complementary upper-bound framework recorded on the maintained problem page. Bettin, Grenié, Molteni, and Sanna later improved the lower bound and published exact small values; the page cites their 2025 arXiv preprint. These results establish the scale up to the relevant iterated-logarithmic factors, but they should not be silently rewritten as the full asymptotic requested by the catalogue question.

The maintained [Erdős Problems entry](https://www.erdosproblems.com/320), accessed during this audit, was last edited 16 July 2026. It labels the problem solved and displays two later proof claims. Claim 55, by Ray Young, Keheng Zhu, and Yanping Luo, is marked accepted by that site and states an order-of-magnitude result. Claim 115, by Scott Duke Kominers and Joachim Neu, states a stronger full asymptotic with a positive continuous nonconstant phase function. These are important dated reports, but a website label or proof-claim marker is not GCL mathematical certification.
