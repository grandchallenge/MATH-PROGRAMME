## The question in plain language

Choose pairwise **distinct** integers $1<n_1<\cdots<n_r\le N$, and a residue class $a_i\bmod n_i$ for each modulus. Require these classes to be **pairwise disjoint**: no integer satisfies two of the chosen congruences. Write $f(N)$ for the largest achievable number $r$ of classes.

Erdős and Stein asked how large $f(N)$ can be. It is a problem about **disjoint** classes, not a covering system in which every integer must belong to at least one class.

## A small example

The classes $0\bmod2$ and $1\bmod2$ are disjoint, but their moduli repeat, violating the distinct-modulus requirement. The classes $0\bmod2$ and $1\bmod4$ are disjoint and use distinct moduli; however, adding $1\bmod3$ intersects both. To check compatibility, the generalized Chinese remainder theorem says two classes have a common solution exactly when $a_i\equiv a_j\pmod{\gcd(n_i,n_j)}$. A valid family must therefore have **unequal residues modulo every pairwise gcd**.

## What is known

The principal asymptotic is reported **SOLVED** by a May 2026 AI-assisted proof. Define

$$
L(N)=\exp\bigl(\sqrt{\log N\,\log\log N}\bigr).
$$

The maintained [Erdős #202](https://www.erdosproblems.com/202) gives

$$
f(N)=N L(N)^{-1+o(1)}.
$$

Erdős–Szemerédi (1968) first proved $f(N)=o(N)$. Croot and later de la Bretéche, Ford and Vandehey improved bounds; the latter obtained upper exponent $\sqrt3/2$ and lower exponent one on the $L(N)$ scale. The 2026 argument, attributed explicitly to **GPT-5.4 Pro prompted by Ho Boon Suan**, combines the de la Bretéche–Ford–Vandehey argument with Park and Pham's resolution of the **Kahn–Kalai expectation-threshold conjecture**.
