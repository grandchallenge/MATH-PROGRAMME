## The question in plain language

A *chordal* graph is one that has no induced cycle of length four or more. Erdős #81 asks whether the edges of every chordal graph on $n$ vertices can be **partitioned**, without overlap, into at most

$$
\frac{n^2}{6}+O(n)
$$

complete subgraphs (cliques). Every edge must belong to exactly one selected clique. A clique covering that permits the same edge to appear more than once is not an answer.

## A small example

In a star $K_{1,4}$, no triangle exists. Each edge must therefore be its own two-vertex clique, and exactly four cliques are needed. In a complete graph $K_n$, the entire edge set forms one clique. Thus chordality alone does not make the clique-partition number a fixed function of the number of edges; it depends on how triangles overlap.

For the sharp asymptotic example, join every vertex of a clique on about $n/3$ vertices to an independent set of about $2n/3$ vertices. This *split graph* is chordal. Its dense pattern of shared clique edges makes edge-disjoint clique partitioning unexpectedly expensive, producing the conjectured $n^2/6+O(n)$ scale.

## What is known

The exact coefficient $1/6$ remains **OPEN**. The primary 1993 paper by Erdős, Ordman and Zalcstein, [*Clique partitions of chordal graphs*](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0963548300000808), *Combinatorics, Probability and Computing* 2 (1993), 409–415, explicitly states both the extremal example and a universal upper bound of $(1-c)n^2/4$ for some absolute $c>0$. This improves the elementary bound of about $n^2/4$, but does not reach $n^2/6$.

Chen, Erdős and Ordman later obtained $(3/16)n^2+O(n)$ for split graphs, still above $n^2/6$. Split graphs form a proper subclass of chordal graphs, so an argument valid only for split graphs does not automatically cover every chordal graph.

## GCL's current state

The original protected assignment is [#1350](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1350). The 1993 publisher primary abstract was checked. Its universal constant $c$ is not independently reconstructed from the full paper, and recent community-proposed refinements have not been independently adjudicated. Both pinned and maintained statuses are **OPEN**.

An authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1350#issuecomment-6157118986) is posted on the original issue. It is unadjudicated source evidence, not independent proof certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded successor is to implement an exact edge-partition integer program for chordal graphs with at most seven vertices. Each variable represents a clique of size at least two; impose equality one over the cliques containing every edge and minimize the number selected. Enumerate chordal graph isomorphism classes and compare split-graph examples. Acceptance: a partition certificate with each edge appearing exactly once; falsification: a nonchordal test graph entering the sample, an uncovered edge, or a repeated edge. These finite fixtures test semantics, not the asymptotic conjecture.
