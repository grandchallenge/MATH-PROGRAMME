## The question in plain language

In Ramsey theory, the number $R(T,G)$ is the least number of vertices such that every red-blue colouring of a complete graph contains either a red copy of the tree $T$ or a blue copy of $G$.

[Erdős Problem #550](https://www.erdosproblems.com/550) concerns a tree $T$ with $n$ vertices and a *complete multipartite graph* $G$ with parts of sizes $m_1\le\cdots\le m_k$. For sufficiently large $n$, it asks whether

$$
R(T,G)\le (\chi(G)-1)
\bigl(R(T,K_{m_1,m_2})-1\bigr)+m_1.
$$

Here $K_{m_1,m_2}$ is the complete bipartite graph with the two smallest part sizes. The proposed bound remains OPEN.

## A small example

When $k=2$, the multipartite graph $G$ is precisely $K_{m_1,m_2}$ and its chromatic number is two. The desired inequality becomes

$$
R(T,G)\le R(T,G)-1+m_1,
$$

which follows immediately because $m_1\ge1$. Thus the two-part case is trivial; the substantive difficulty starts with at least three nonempty parts.

## What is known

Chvátal proved the exact tree-versus-clique formula

$$
R(T,K_m)=(m-1)(n-1)+1.
$$

The open multipartite estimate is more delicate because the graph on the right is complete **bipartite**, and the graph on the left has several independently sized parts. An estimate involving only a complete clique cannot simply be substituted for the desired bipartite Ramsey number.

## GCL's current state

[Source audit #1821](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1821#issuecomment-6121459116) checks the parameter order and the trivial two-part specialization. It records the maintained problem's historical Erdős–Faudree–Rousseau–Schelp source lead, but does not claim to have located the exact original paper pages or proved the general asymptotic inequality.

## What remains

Prove the stated Ramsey inequality with the correct dependence on the multipartite part sizes and a justified sufficiently-large-tree threshold, or produce a counterexample. The $k=2$ identity and Chvátal's separate complete-clique result do not settle the genuinely multipartite case.
