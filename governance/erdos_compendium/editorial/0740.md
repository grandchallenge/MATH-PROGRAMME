## The question in plain language

For an infinite cardinal m and graph G with chromatic number exactly m, and integer r≥1, can one remove edges or pass to a subgraph H without reducing the chromatic cardinal while eliminating EVERY odd cycle of length at most r? Erdős–Hajnal #740 remains OPEN in the maintained source. The form asking for arbitrary finite GIRTH, rather than just absence of short odd cycles, is a stronger, separate question. The source reports a positive case for m=aleph_0 and r=3 by Rödl, but not an all-cardinals theorem.

## A small example

For r=3, 'no odd cycle of length at most three' says exactly triangle-free, because simple graphs have no odd cycles of length 1 or 2. A 5-cycle C_5 has chromatic number 3 and no triangles, and can serve as a finite illustration of this condition; it does NOT prove existence of a subgraph retaining an infinite chromatic cardinal. For r=5, the same C_5 fails because its five-cycle is prohibited.
