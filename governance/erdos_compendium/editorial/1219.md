## The question in plain language

An infinite version of Ramsey's theorem asks how large a completely uniform set must appear when every pair of points has been colored red or blue. The notation

$$
\kappa\longrightarrow(\alpha)^2_2
$$

means that every red/blue coloring of the two-element subsets of a set of cardinality $\kappa$ has a monochromatic subset of cardinality $\alpha$.

[Erdős Problem #1219](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1219) considers a strictly increasing sequence of indices $(n_k)_{k<\omega}$ for which the cardinal powers $2^{\aleph_{n_k}}$ strictly increase and $2^{\aleph_{n_0}}>\aleph_\omega$. It asks whether

$$
\sum_{k<\omega}2^{\aleph_{n_k}}
\longrightarrow(\aleph_\omega)^2_2.
$$

These are infinite cardinalities, not real-valued powers or ordinary numerical sums. The delicate point is the first limit cardinal $\aleph_\omega$.

## A small example

In a finite analogy, take six vertices labeled $1,\ldots,6$ and color the edge $\{i,j\}$ red if $i+j$ is even, blue otherwise. Both $\{1,3,5\}$ and $\{2,4,6\}$ form red monochromatic triples: a sum of two odd numbers, or two even numbers, is even. This directly illustrates how an edge coloring can contain large homogeneous sets.

The example is **not** a verification of an infinite-cardinal partition relation. The latter must hold for *every* coloring at the stated cardinality.

## What is known

The frozen catalogue lists the problem as **proved**. Shelah's 1975 paper *Notes on Partition Calculus* (Sh:40), particularly its early canonization and partition results, is the primary literature cited for the affirmative resolution in ZFC. Roughly, its methods first regularize edges among carefully chosen large blocks and then apply Ramsey's theorem to the countably many block indices. The precise hypotheses of these reductions matter, and that paraphrase is not a replacement for the published proof.

A public Lean project by Ji Ho Bae has also been reported as formalizing this problem in 2026. Its existence, source code, and reported axiom checks are **external evidence**, not an independently replayed GCL certification.
