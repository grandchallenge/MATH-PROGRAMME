## The question in plain language

A set of 2,3-smooth integers has prime factors only two and three. Even requiring all summands to lie within a fixed multiplicative interval leaves enough flexibility to represent every positive integer, contradicting the old density-zero conjecture.

Given fixed C>0, consider integers representable as sum n=b1+...+bt of DISTINCT strictly increasing 2,3-smooth positive integers bi=2^a3^b (nonnegative exponents) with bt <= C b1. Erdős proposed these integers have natural density zero for every fixed C. DISPROVED already at C=6: EVERY positive integer has such a representation with bt<6 b1 by van Doorn and Everts. Do not confuse this bounded-ratio sum result with the older pairwise-nondividing smooth summand conjecture.

## A small example

For n=1,2,3,4,5,6 each is 2,3-smooth itself (single-term ratio 1), except 5 is not smooth and 5=2+3 has ratio 3/2. n=7=3+4 gives ratio 4/3. These small checks do not prove the all-integers theorem.

A finite instance tests the definition, not a claim quantified over every large graph, infinite set or arbitrary coloring.

## What is known

Maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/845 read 2026-10-14: van Doorn–Everts [vDEv25], *Smooth sums with small spacings*, arXiv:2511.04585 (2025), prove all positive integers can be written with max/min<6. Erdős–Lewin [ErLe96] give optimal universal C>2, van Doorn–Everts C>=3 and comment-based conjecture perhaps ~3.604. The exact least C remains a DIFFERENT OPEN optimization. Original full preprint proof unreviewed.

The allowed generators are positive numbers of the form 2^u 3^v with nonnegative exponents. For a representation by distinct increasing generators, the spread is maximum divided by minimum; it equals one for a singleton. The reported van Doorn–Everts theorem gives strict spread less than six for every positive integer, so the density is actually ONE at C=6. Finding the sharp universal spread remains a separate optimization.
