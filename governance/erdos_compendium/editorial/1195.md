## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1195](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1195) is **EXTERNAL SOLVED — PROOF REVIEW PENDING** in the source as of 15 October 2026.

Seek Lebesgue-measurable $S\subset(0,\infty)$ with **infinite measure** and no two distinct $x,y\in S$ such that $x/y$ is a positive integer (equivalently, multiplicative translates of $S$ by integers $\ge2$ are disjoint from $S$). The symbol $|S\cap(0,x)|$ here means **Lebesgue measure**, not cardinality; every nonempty interval contains uncountably many points. Determine how rapidly this measure can diverge. For nondecreasing unbounded target $F:[1,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$ the maintained report claims existence of such $S$ satisfying $|S\cap(0,x)|\ge F(x)$ iff

$$
\int_1^\infty F(x)x^{-2}\,dx<\infty.
$$

This equivalence must be source-checked for regularity and equality/all-large‑$x$ conventions.

## A small example

The interval $S=(1,7/5)$ has measure $2/5$, and the ratio of two distinct elements has absolute range between $5/7$ and $7/5$, excluding all integers $\ge2$. The fixture also evaluates the integrals for $F(x)=\sqrt{x}$ (finite) and for $F(x)=x/\log x$ (divergent when integrated from $e$). This local example does **not** construct an infinite-measure $S$ or certify the reported iff theorem.
