## The question in plain language

[Chvátal's conjecture, Erdős #701](https://www.erdosproblems.com/701) concerns a family F of subsets of a finite nonempty set X that contains every subset of each of its members. Does some element x occur in at least as many members of F as the size of ANY intersecting subfamily of F? The conjecture remains OPEN for arbitrary downsets.

## A small example

Take all eight subsets of X={1,2,3}. The four sets containing element 1 form an intersecting star. The eight subsets can be partitioned into four complementary pairs A and X\A; an intersecting family cannot contain both members of a complementary pair. Thus no intersecting subfamily can exceed four, and the star reaches the maximum. This does not settle general downsets.

## What is known

Chvátal proved a related theorem under a stronger shifted family closure condition, not just downward closure. Sterboul proved another structured class, and Frankl–Kupavskii established cases with covering number two. The individual hypotheses matter; they cannot be dropped to announce a general proof. The [pinned 701.lean](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/701.lean) encodes the general conjecture via lower sets and intersecting subfamilies, but its theorem body still contains a proof hole.
