## The question in plain language

For a fixed graph $H$, let $f_H(n)$ be the number of labeled graphs on $[n]$ containing no copy of $H$, and let $\operatorname{ex}(n,H)$ be the largest number of edges in an $H$-free graph on $n$ vertices. Erdős asked whether

$$
f_H(n)\le 2^{(1+o(1))\operatorname{ex}(n,H)}
$$

for every fixed $H$.

The lower bound $f_H(n)\ge 2^{\operatorname{ex}(n,H)}$ is immediate: all subgraphs of an extremal $H$-free graph remain $H$-free. Erdős–Frankl–Rödl proved the matching asymptotic upper bound for every non-bipartite $H$, using the regularity method. Thus the conjecture is correct whenever $\chi(H)\ge 3$.

It is false in general. Morris and Saxton proved that for $H=C_6$, there are infinitely many $n$ for which

$$
f_{C_6}(n)\ge 2^{(1+c)\operatorname{ex}(n,C_6)}
$$

for some constant $c>0$. The weaker estimate $f_H(n)\le 2^{O_H(\operatorname{ex}(n,H))}$ is known for several bipartite families, including all even cycles, but remains open for general cyclic bipartite graphs. See Erdős’s [1990 source](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511983917.040), the [Erdős–Frankl–Rödl paper](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1986-17.pdf), and the [Morris–Saxton paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.2927).

===SIX_SECTION_MATHEMATICAL_EXPLANATION===

The correct interpretation is:

$$
\forall H\ \text{fixed},\quad
\log_2 f_H(n)\le (1+o_H(1))\operatorname{ex}(n,H).
$$

The graph $H$ is fixed before $n\to\infty$. The forbidden copy is non-induced. The natural lower bound is

$$
\log_2 f_H(n)\ge \operatorname{ex}(n,H).
$$

Therefore the question asks whether the number of $H$-free graphs is determined to first order by the largest possible edge count.

## A small example

Let $X$ be an extremal $H$-free graph with $e(X)=\operatorname{ex}(n,H)$. Every subset of $E(X)$ defines an $H$-free graph, so

$$
f_H(n)\ge 2^{e(X)}=2^{\operatorname{ex}(n,H)}.
$$

This is the basic reusable interface for any enumeration proof: only the upper bound requires substantial structure.

For $H=C_6$ and $n=6$, the graph $K_5$ with one pendant vertex has $11$ edges and is $C_6$-free. Thus

$$
f_{C_6}(6)\ge 2^{11}=2048.
$$

To verify $\operatorname{ex}(6,C_6)=11$, suppose a six-vertex graph has $12$ edges. Its complement has three edges.

If the complement has maximum degree at most $2$, the original graph has minimum degree at least $3=6/2$, so Dirac’s theorem gives a Hamilton cycle.

If the complement has a vertex of degree $3$, all three complement edges form a star. The other five vertices induce $K_5$, while the center has exactly two allowed neighbors. Ordering those two neighbors as $a,b$ and the remaining three vertices as $x,y,z$ gives

$$
v-a-x-y-z-b-v,
$$

a copy of $C_6$. Hence every $12$-edge graph contains $C_6$, while the $11$-edge pendant construction does not.

## What is known

If $\chi(H)=r\ge 3$, Erdős–Frankl–Rödl prove

$$
f_H(n)=2^{(1+o(1))\operatorname{ex}(n,K_r)}.
$$

The Erdős–Stone–Simonovits theorem gives

$$
\operatorname{ex}(n,H)
=(1+o(1))\operatorname{ex}(n,K_r).
$$

Combining the two relations yields

$$
f_H(n)=2^{(1+o(1))\operatorname{ex}(n,H)}.
$$

This proves the original estimate for every non-bipartite forbidden graph.

Morris–Saxton prove that there is $c>0$ and infinitely many $n$ such that

$$
f_{C_6}(n)\ge
2^{(1+c)\operatorname{ex}(n,C_6)}.
$$

Because $c$ is fixed and positive, the exponent is not asymptotic to $\operatorname{ex}(n,C_6)$. Thus the original universal claim is false.

Their same paper proves, for every $\ell\ge 2$,

$$
f_{C_{2\ell}}(n)\le 2^{O(n^{1+1/\ell})}.
$$

For $C_6$, this is an $O(n^{4/3})$ exponent, consistent with the known order of $\operatorname{ex}(n,C_6)$ and hence gives the weaker $2^{O(\operatorname{ex}(n,C_6))}$ estimate.

The sharp universal statement is disproved.

- Erdős–Frankl–Rödl, *Graphs and Combinatorics* 2 (1986), 113–121, define $F_n(H)$ and prove in Theorem 1.6, p. 114, that for $\chi(H)\ge 3$,

  $$
  f_H(n)=2^{(1+o(1))\operatorname{ex}(n,H)}.
  $$

  Their proof uses the regularity reduction on pp. 116–117. Accessible primary paper: [PDF](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1986-17.pdf); publisher record: [Springer DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01788085).

- Morris–Saxton, “The number of $C_{2\ell}$-free graphs,” arXiv:1309.2927v3 (2015), Proposition 1.4, prove that for some $c>0$ and infinitely many $n$,

  $$
  f_{C_6}(n)\ge 2^{(1+c)\operatorname{ex}(n,C_6)}.
  $$

  This directly disproves the sharp $(1+o(1))$ claim. See the [primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.2927), especially pp. 3–4 and 8–9.

- The weaker conjecture

  $$
  f_H(n)\le 2^{O_H(\operatorname{ex}(n,H))}
  $$

  for every fixed cyclic bipartite $H$ remains open in the audited literature. Morris–Saxton prove it for every even cycle, including $C_6$, via $f_{C_{2\ell}}(n)\le 2^{O(n^{1+1/\ell})}$. Jiang–Longbrake (2024) state that the general bipartite problem remains open and prove it under a broad balanced-supersaturation hypothesis; see the [2024 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.10572v4) and [journal version](https://www.advancesincombinatorics.com/article/120235-balanced-supersaturation-and-turan-numbers-in-random-graphs).
