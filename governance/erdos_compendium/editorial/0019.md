## The question in plain language

Suppose a graph is formed from exactly $n$ copies of the complete graph on $n$ vertices, with **no edge shared** by two copies. Such a graph already needs at least $n$ colours, because it contains a clique containing $n$ vertices. [Erdős Problem #19](https://www.erdosproblems.com/19), the Erdős–Faber–Lovász conjecture, asks whether $n$ colours always suffice.

Its status is unusual: **resolved up to a finite check**. A major published theorem proves the answer for every sufficiently large $n$, but the original assertion for every value of $n$ is not thereby established for its remaining finite range.

## A small example

For $n=2$, the graph is the union of two different single edges. They may share one endpoint, giving a three-vertex path, or be disjoint. Either arrangement is bipartite, has an edge, and therefore has chromatic number exactly two. For $n=1$, the single vertex of the one clique requires just one colour.

These cases illustrate the problem; they cannot substitute for the finite cases with small $n$ not covered by the general theorem.

## What is known

Kang, Kelly, Kühn, Methuku and Osthus, *A proof of the Erdős–Faber–Lovász conjecture*, *Annals of Mathematics* **198** (2023), 537–618, prove the conjecture for every sufficiently large size. Their central theorem is formulated using **linear hypergraphs**: hypergraphs in which two edges intersect in at most one vertex. Properly edge-colouring the hypergraph corresponds to vertex-colouring the graph built from its overlapping cliques.

This is a deep asymptotic theorem, not an exhaustive verification of all finite exceptions. The registry consequently labels the full question **DECIDABLE**, rather than simply PROVED.

## GCL's current state

[Source audit #1288](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1288#issuecomment-6121164915) checks the exact remaining finite-parameter boundary and proves the $n=1,2$ illustrations. No corresponding statement is present in the admitted frozen GCL formal-provider snapshot. The published theorem's full proof and an explicit exhaustive small-case cutoff were not replayed.

## What remains

Reconcile an effective threshold for large $n$ with rigorous verification of all excluded small values before changing the status of the original universal statement. Formal proof work may also require a precise incidence duality relating edge-disjoint cliques and linear hypergraphs.
