## The question in plain language

A finite additive two-basis generates every integer from zero through n using the sum of two members, where both members may be equal. The goal is the smallest possible basis and its growth rate.

For every n, let g(n) be smallest |A| among A subset {0,...,n} such that every integer 0,...,n belongs to A+A (repetitions allowed). This is a finite additive 2-basis. General asymptotic estimation remains OPEN, but the specific conjecture g(n)~2sqrt(n) is already disproved by better constructions.

## A small example

For n=5, the set A={0,1,2,3} supplies every integer up to 5 as a sum of two selected elements. In particular 2+2=4 is legal, so the distinct-summand convention would change the problem.

Finite example n=5 and A={0,1,2,3}: its pair sums include 0,1,2,3,4,5, so g(5)<=4. Distinctness between summands is NOT required: 2+2=4. Simple counting gives at most |A|(|A|+1)/2 unordered sums so g(n)(g(n)+1)/2 >= n+1; asymptotically g(n)>=sqrt(2n)(1-o(1)), weaker than best published lower constant.

## What is known

Maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/791 OPEN, attributes (2.181...+o(1))n <=g(n)^2 <=(3.458...+o(1))n to Yu [Yu15] and Kohonen [Ko17]. Mrose [Mr79] previously gave g(n)^2<=3.5n, ruling out g(n)~2sqrt(n) since this would require g(n)^2/n->4. The older Rohrbach bound has larger upper constant 4. Source statements are catalogue-attributed; original Mrose, Yu, Kohonen full publications have not been read.
