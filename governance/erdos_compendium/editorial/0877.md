## The question in plain language

A set of positive integers is *sum-free* if it does not contain a solution of $a+b=c$ with **all three terms in the set**. The two summands may be equal. For example, a set containing both 2 and 4 is not sum-free because $2+2=4$.

A sum-free subset $A$ of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ is *inclusion-maximal* if no further integer from that interval can be added without destroying the property. It need not have maximum cardinality. Erdős and Cameron asked how many such maximal subsets there are, particularly whether their number $f_m(n)$ is $o(2^{n/2})$. They also asked whether $f_m(n)=o(f(n))$, where $f(n)$ counts all sum-free subsets of the interval.

## A small example

The set $\{3,4\}$ is sum-free in $[4]$, because neither integer can be obtained as the sum of two members. It is also maximal: adding 1 creates $1+3=4$, and adding 2 creates $2+2=4$. By contrast, the empty set is sum-free but not maximal.

An exact program enumerates every subset of $[n]$ for $1\le n\le16$, checks sum-freeness including doubled terms, and counts the inclusion-maximal subsets. Some outputs are:

| $n$ | All sum-free subsets $f(n)$ | Maximal sum-free subsets $f_m(n)$ |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 9 | 4 |
| 8 | 61 | 13 |
| 12 | 369 | 37 |
| 16 | 1954 | 118 |

A separately implemented obstruction criterion checks the maximality result against the direct test of adjoining each omitted element for $n\le11$. These are exact finite examples, not proof of asymptotic growth.

## What is known

Cameron and Erdős constructed at least $2^{\lfloor n/4\rfloor}$ different maximal sum-free subsets. Łuczak and Schoen, *On the number of maximal sum-free sets*, *Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society* **129** (2001), 2205–2207, proved an exponential upper bound with coefficient strictly below $1/2$. This already answered both original smallness questions.

Balogh, Liu, Sharifzadeh and Treglown subsequently proved the sharper asymptotic formula. In [*Sharp bound on the number of maximal sum-free subsets of integers*, Theorem 1.1](https://web.mat.bham.ac.uk/A.Treglown/exactsumfree-ACCEPTEDFINAL.pdf), accepted manuscript dated May 8, 2018, the authors establish that there exist explicitly computable constants $C_0,C_1,C_2,C_3$ such that, along each residue class,

$$
f_m(n)=(C_{n\bmod4}+o(1))\,2^{n/4}.
$$

This is a much stronger statement than $f_m(n)=o(2^{n/2})$. Its leading exponent $1/4$ matches Cameron and Erdős's construction. The paper appeared in *Journal of the European Mathematical Society* **20** (2018), 1885–1911, DOI 10.4171/JEMS/802.

The same authors' [earlier paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.5661) had already proved the logarithmic-scale estimate $f_m(n)=2^{(1/4+o(1))n}$. The 2018 result supplies the residue-dependent leading constants. Meanwhile $f(n)\ge2^{\lceil n/2\rceil}$ by taking arbitrary subsets of odd numbers. Thus the ratio $f_m(n)/f(n)$ tends to zero exponentially.

## GCL's current state

GCL checked the 2018 primary manuscript's Theorem 1.1 directly and verified the exact sum-free definition printed in its introduction: Schur triples may have coinciding terms. The proof in that source uses Green's container and removal lemmas, the Deshouillers–Freiman–Sós–Temkin structure theorem, and a counting theorem of Green and Morris. These dependencies were identified, **not** independently re-proved or Lean-compiled.

The finite verifier at governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_877.py uses one algorithm to enumerate sum-free sets and an algebraic obstruction criterion for maximality. For each omitted integer $x$, it tests whether inserting $x$ creates a forbidden equation of one of three types: $2x$ already in the set, $x+a$ already in the set for another member $a$, or two existing members summing to $x$. The two-member sum permits equal summands. A separate direct extension test agrees for $n\le11$, so a possible definition mismatch is caught in small instances.

The established asymptotic theorem is substantially stronger than the frozen 2025 catalogue status. Nevertheless, finite enumeration and a primary published proof are not a GCL mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded formalization target is the **maximality obstruction lemma**. For every finite $A\subseteq[n]$, prove that $A$ is inclusion-maximal sum-free if and only if it is sum-free and, for each omitted $x$, at least one of the three explicit Schur-triple obstructions arises.

Inputs are finite sets and the inclusive-diagonal sum-free predicate. Acceptance requires a complete paper proof or a compiled Lean theorem without placeholders, accompanied by the independent finite comparison through $n=11$ and a saved axiom report. A counterexample to either implication or a discrepancy in the finite counts falsifies the lemma. Stop at the exact finite statement unless the external container, removal, structural and counting theorems supporting the 2018 asymptotic are separately sourced and checked.
