## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1164](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1164). How far from the origin does a long two-dimensional random walk completely cover the lattice?

$$
\frac{(\log R_n)^2}{\log n}\quad\text{and its limiting distribution require source verification.}
$$

For simple symmetric random walk S_0=0,S_1,... on Z², let R_n be the radius of the largest Euclidean lattice disc around the origin fully visited by time n (a random variable depending on the path), not a deterministic radius guaranteed on almost every finite walk. Original Erdős–Taylor question asks log R_n scale sqrt(log n); R_n can be zero at small n, so log requires a large-n convention. The live site displays "P(((log R_n)^2/log n)≤x)=e^{-4x}" for all x>0. This is IMPOSSIBLE as a CDF: a cumulative distribution function is nondecreasing, while e^{-4x} is strictly decreasing. Record a source typography conflict; do not invent an inverse-x or survival correction.

## A small example

Among 4^7=16384 length-7 planar walks, exactly 112 cover all four axial neighbors (and origin), finite probability 112/16384=7/1024. Covering unit disc cannot happen in fewer than 7 steps. Diagonal-path detours between axial neighbors explain why 24 is incorrect. This is finite evidence only. For any 0<x<y, e^{-4x}>e^{-4y}, contradicting CDF monotonicity.
