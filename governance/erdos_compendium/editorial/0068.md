## The question in plain language

Factorials grow rapidly: $2!=2$, $3!=6$, $4!=24$, and so on. Erdős asked whether

$$
S=\frac1{2!-1}+\frac1{3!-1}+\frac1{4!-1}+\cdots
$$

is irrational. Every finite partial sum is a fraction of integers, but the infinite limit might or might not be. The maintained catalogue still records this as an **open problem**.

## A small example

The first four terms give the exact rational number

$$
S_5=1+\frac15+\frac1{23}+\frac1{119}=\frac{17132}{13685}.
$$

The remaining terms are small. Since $1/(n!-1)\le2/n!$ for $n\ge2$, for every $N\ge2$ the tail obeys

$$
0<S-S_N\le\frac{2(N+2)}{(N+1)(N+1)!}.
$$

The last inequality follows by bounding successive factorial denominators below by a geometric progression. This gives certified numerical intervals for $S$. It does not establish irrationality.

## What is known

The ordinary geometric-series identity gives

$$
\frac1{n!-1}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac1{(n!)^k},\qquad n\ge2.
$$

All terms are nonnegative, so one may exchange the sums and obtain

$$
S=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac1{(n!)^k}.
$$

The maintained catalogue attributes this rewriting to Desmond Weisenberg. It is useful for understanding the number, but is not an irrationality proof.

Erdős considered this and related irrational series in work from 1968/1969 and a 1988 survey. Results about other series, including the Erdős–Borwein constant, do not automatically apply when the denominator is $n!-1$.

## GCL's current state

The pinned catalogue and maintained Erdős Problems page classify #68 as **OPEN**. The GCL analysis checks an exact partial sum, a geometric expansion and a universal tail estimate. These are elementary calculations, not a solution.

The original GCL assignment is [ERDOS-CATALOGUE-0068-S01](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1337). This is an unaccepted local draft. **Primary-source statement check completed on 2026-10-13:** Erdős explicitly says on printed p. 223 of his 1969 article that he cannot show this factorial series is irrational. The displayed lower index in the historical printing is $n=1$, although the denominator $1!-1=0$ makes that index undefined; the registered mathematically valid normalization starts at $n=2$. His later 1988 account prints $n=2$ and repeats the same open question. The full proofs of the paper's unrelated theorems were not audited.

The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1337#issuecomment-6156907297). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded task is to formalize the tail inequality, the geometric expansion and the fraction $17132/13685$. The unresolved question is different: proving that no rational number equals the infinite sum. Very accurate decimal approximations, or even rigorously tiny error intervals, cannot by themselves decide that.
