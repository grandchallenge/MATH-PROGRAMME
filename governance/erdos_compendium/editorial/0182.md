## The question in plain language

A graph is **$k$ regular** when every vertex in it has degree exactly $k$. A graph *contains a $k$ regular subgraph* if some nonempty subgraph, not necessarily induced or connected, is $k$ regular.

Erdős and Sauer asked how many edges an $n$ vertex graph may have while containing **no $k$ regular subgraph**, for any fixed integer $k\ge3$. In particular, is this extremal number at most $n^{1+o(1)}$? The question permits the $k$ regular subgraph to use only a subset of the vertices and edges of the host graph.

## A small example

A complete graph $K_{k+1}$ is $k$ regular. Thus any host graph containing $K_{k+1}$ as a subgraph contains a $k$ regular subgraph, but a general $k$ regular graph need not be a clique. Checking for $K_{k+1}$ alone would miss many witnesses.

A star $K_{1,n-1}$ has many edges yet contains no 3-regular subgraph, since all edges are incident with its centre. It shows why merely exceeding a constant number of edges does not force a regular subgraph.

## What is known

**PROVED.** Janzer and Sudakov, [*Resolution of the Erdős–Sauer problem on regular subgraphs*](https://doi.org/10.1017/fmp.2023.19) (2023) showed that for each fixed $k\ge3$ there is $C(k)>0$ such that any $n$ vertex graph with at least

$$
C(k)\,n\log\log n
$$

edges contains a $k$ regular subgraph. Consequently the extremal number for graphs avoiding $k$ regular subgraphs is $O_k(n\log\log n)$, which is indeed $n^{1+o(1)}$.

Chakraborti, Janzer, Methuku and Montgomery (2024) improved the parameter dependence to $C(k)\ll k^2$, sharp up to an absolute constant in the cited setting. A construction by Pyber, Rödl and Szemerédi explains why a logarithmic iterated factor cannot simply be removed across all cases by the existing argument.

A distinct Szemerédi question requires the resulting $k$ regular subgraph to be **connected**. A disconnected union of two regular components answers the original question but not this stronger one. The [maintained Erdős #182](https://www.erdosproblems.com/182), updated March 2026, keeps this distinction explicit.

## What remains

A bounded successor enumerates graphs on up to seven vertices, searches subgraphs for exactly $k$ regular vertex-degree patterns with $k=3$, and separately tests **induced**, **not necessarily induced**, and **connected** conventions. Acceptance: $K_4$ is recognized as a 3-regular witness, a star has none, and no claim of a general $n\log\log n$ theorem is inferred from finite enumeration.
