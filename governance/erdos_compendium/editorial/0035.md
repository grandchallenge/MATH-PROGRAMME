## The question in plain language

The *Schnirelmann density* of a set $A$ is the smallest density seen in any finite initial interval:

$$
d_s(A)=\inf_{N\ge1}\frac{|A\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}|}{N}.
$$

Suppose $B$ is an additive basis of fixed order $k$ containing zero and $A$ has Schnirelmann density $\alpha$. [Erdős Problem #35](https://www.erdosproblems.com/35) asks whether

$$
d_s(A+B)\ge\alpha+\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)}{k}.
$$

The problem is **proved**. It concerns Schnirelmann density, not ordinary asymptotic density.

## A small example

Take $A=\mathbb N$, interpreted with nonnegative integers, so $\alpha=1$. Because $0\in B$, the sumset $A+B$ contains all of $A$ and has Schnirelmann density one. Both sides of the proposed inequality equal one.

There is a more instructive elementary numerical inequality. For every $0<\alpha\le1$ and $k\ge1$, convexity of $x^{-1/k}$ gives

$$
\alpha^{-1/k}\ge 1+\frac{1-\alpha}{k}.
$$

Multiplying by $\alpha$ yields

$$
\alpha^{1-1/k}\ge\alpha+\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)}{k}.
$$

The case $\alpha=0$ follows separately by equality of zeros.

## What is known

Plünnecke (1970) established a stronger bound,

$$
d_s(A+B)\ge\alpha^{1-1/k}.
$$

Ruzsa observed that the elementary inequality above transfers Plünnecke's theorem to the original Erdős estimate. Proving the real-number inequality is not a re-proof of the nontrivial additive-density theorem; the latter is the essential published input.

## GCL's current state

[Source audit #1304](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1304#issuecomment-6121344262) records the theorem's hypotheses, the subtle difference between Schnirelmann and natural density, and a bounded convexity-based transfer. The complete primary Plünnecke argument has not been independently replayed and there is no GCL mathematical certification of it in this article.

## What remains

The historical inequality is resolved. Further useful work would supply a fully pinned Plünnecke theorem with precise additive-basis assumptions and formalize its elementary consequence. Separate strengthened questions remain outside this article.
