## The question in plain language

Suppose someone chooses an infinite set of real numbers $A$. Can we build a measurable set $E$ of **positive Lebesgue measure** which contains no nontrivial affine image of the entire set $A$?

Formally, Erdős #120 asks whether for every infinite $A\subseteq\mathbb R$ there is a measurable set $E\subseteq\mathbb R$, $m(E)>0$, such that

$$
aA+b\not\subseteq E
\quad\text{for every }a\ne0,\ b\in\mathbb R.
$$

The scale $a$ may be negative. Crucially, $E$ may depend on $A$. This is the **Erdős similarity conjecture**. For a finite $A$, Steinhaus's theorem supplies scaled and translated copies inside any positive-measure set, so the word *infinite* cannot be omitted.

## A small example

Take $A=\mathbb Z$. The interval $E=[0,1]$ has positive measure but cannot contain a translate and nonzero rescaling of the entire unbounded set $\mathbb Z$. For a bounded example, take a decreasing sequence $A=\{1,1/2,1/4,\ldots\}$ tending to zero. A bounded interval **can** contain an affine copy of this sequence; excluding every possible translated, rescaled copy is substantially harder.

If $A$ is dense in some interval, an affine copy is dense in a nondegenerate interval. A positive-measure set with no complete interval, chosen appropriately, can avoid such a copy. That observation alone does not handle rapidly convergent sequences.

## What is known

The conjecture is **OPEN**, including the geometric sequence $\{2^{-j}:j\ge0\}$. The primary survey by Yeonwook Jung, Chun-Kit Lai and Yuveshen Mooroogen, [*Fifty years of the Erdős similarity conjecture*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11062), published in a January 2025 revised edition, reviews major partial results. Eigen and Falconer show that a decreasing null sequence with successive ratios tending to one is not measure-universal. Bourgain proves the result for sumsets of three infinite subsets of $\mathbb R$. Kolountzakis supplies other gap-structure criteria and almost-everywhere variants.

The maintained [Erdős #120](https://www.erdosproblems.com/120), last edited 23 January 2026, identifies unbounded sets and sets dense in an interval as solved classes. Those reductions leave the hard lacunary-sequence case. A theorem that almost every scale fails to embed does **not** prove that *no* scale embeds, and replacing positive measure with residual Baire category gives another distinct problem.

## What remains

A bounded successor is to formalize the unbounded-set $A$ case: construct $E=[0,1]$, prove that for every $a\ne0$ the image $aA+b$ is unbounded, and therefore not contained in $E$. Acceptance requires both positive measure and the correct order of quantifiers. Stop at this special case. It does not address bounded lacunary sets such as $\{2^{-j}\}$, and must not be used to claim a proof of the full conjecture.
