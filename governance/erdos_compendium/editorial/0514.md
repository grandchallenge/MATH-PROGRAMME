**Editorial status:** UNACCEPTED_DRAFT. Erdős Problem #514 is **OPEN as a combined three-part question** about how entire functions grow along paths to infinity. The first part was reportedly proved by R. P. Boas (unpublished). The third part has a negative answer under a particular **universal comparison-function** interpretation, using a theorem attributed to Hayman (1960) and revisited in a 2026 note and formalization. The second question, about path length, is a separate obligation and must not be declared solved by either result.

## The question in plain language

Let $f:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C$ be **transcendental entire**, meaning it is complex differentiable everywhere and is not a polynomial.

Erdős asked three related but different questions.

First, can we find a continuous curve escaping to infinity along which the function grows **faster than every fixed power of the distance from the origin**? More precisely, is there a path $\gamma(t)$ with $|\gamma(t)|\to\infty$ such that

$$
\frac{|f(\gamma(t))|}{|\gamma(t)|^n}\longrightarrow\infty
\qquad\text{for every fixed }n\in\mathbb N?
$$

Second, can one bound or estimate the **length of such a path** in terms of the maximum modulus of $f$,

$$
M_f(r)=\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|?
$$

Third, is there a path on which the function grows faster than some *fixed function* of $M_f(r)$, for instance $M_f(r)^\varepsilon$?

This last question contains an important quantifier ambiguity. Is the comparison function selected **once for all entire functions**, or is a different comparison function allowed for each $f$? The answers can differ. A result excluding one universal function does not automatically exclude a function-dependent comparison.

## A small example

Consider $f(z)=e^z$, and take the positive real ray $\gamma(r)=r$ as $r\to\infty$. Since

$$
\frac{e^r}{r^n}\longrightarrow\infty
$$

for every fixed $n$, this path satisfies the first requested property. Its length from the origin out to radius $R$ is just $R$.

The maximum modulus of $e^z$ on $|z|=r$ is $M_f(r)=e^r$. Along a ray $\gamma(r)=re^{i\theta}$,

$$
|e^{re^{i\theta}}|=e^{r\cos\theta}.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{
\frac{|f(re^{i\theta})|}{M_f(r)^\varepsilon}
=\exp\bigl((\cos\theta-\varepsilon)r\bigr).
}
$$

On the positive real ray, every exponent $0<\varepsilon<1$ produces a quotient tending to infinity. On the imaginary ray, the quotient tends to zero. The **path matters**.

More generally, for $f(z)=e^{z^m}$,

$$
\frac{|f(re^{i\theta})|}{M_f(r)^\varepsilon}
=\exp\bigl((\cos(m\theta)-\varepsilon)r^m\bigr).
$$

This gives exact examples in which radial rays have simple lengths and computable growth. It does **not** prove any assertion quantified over all transcendental entire functions.

The transcendence hypothesis is essential: if $f$ is a polynomial of degree $d$, then along any escaping path $|f(z)|/|z|^n$ tends to zero for $n>d$, not infinity.

## What is known

### Erdős's original three questions

Erdős, *Some unsolved problems* (1961), [printed p.249](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1961-22.pdf#page=29), Section IV.6, states the superpolynomial-path question and explicitly credits **R. P. Boas** with an unpublished affirmative proof. The original page was visually inspected. Erdős then separately asks about the path length and about growth compared with a function of $M(r)$, including a positive power.

The neighbouring problem about integrability of $|f|^{-\lambda}$ along a common curve is **a different question**, numbered Erdős #515. Its later solution cannot silently be imported to decide the geometric path-length question in #514.

### Earlier work of Hayman

W. K. Hayman, *On the growth of integral functions on asymptotic paths*, *Journal of the Indian Mathematical Society* (New Series) **24** (1960), 251–264, is identified as an important source for the comparison-function question. Its Theorem 2 is reported to provide a negative answer for a *universally fixed power* of $M_f$, including cases where the maximum modulus grows extremely rapidly.

The original Hayman article's publication record was located, but its complete theorem and proof pages were **not independently recovered** in this audit. Consequently, the stronger universal-comparison statement below is source-attributed rather than independently proved from the 1960 primary paper.

### A revised 2026 note and the role of quantifiers

Yuta Oriike, *A Negative Answer to the Universal-Function Version of Erdős's Third Question in Problem 514* (2026), provides a public [original TeX note](https://github.com/yuta0x89/ErdosProblems/blob/main/notes/514/erdos_514_third_question_forum_note.tex) and reports an accompanying Lean 4 formalization. The source was directly fetched through the GitHub connection. Its revised discussion explicitly credits Hayman's 1960 theorem and presents its own constructive proof as an alternative, not as the discovery of the older power-case result.

The principal claim uses the following order of quantifiers. **For every** nondecreasing function $\Phi(T)$ tending to infinity, there is **some transcendental entire** $f$ such that **every** path $\gamma$ escaping to infinity satisfies

$$
\boxed{
\liminf_{t\to\infty}
\frac{|f(\gamma(t))|}
{\Phi(M_f(|\gamma(t)|))}
=0.
}
$$

In particular, no one comparison function $\Psi(T)\to\infty$, selected **before** $f$ and shared by all $f$, can force a path on which the quotient grows to infinity for every transcendental entire function.

The note also explains why restricting attention to nondecreasing comparison functions loses no candidate *universal positive theorem*. For a proposed arbitrary positive function $\Psi(T)\to\infty$, consider its tail lower envelope

$$
\Phi(T)=\inf_{S\ge T}\Psi(S).
$$

Eventually $\Phi$ is positive, nondecreasing and tends to infinity, with $\Phi\le\Psi$. A counterexample for $\Phi$ defeats a universal claim for $\Psi$.

The revised note's proof points to Hayman's asymptotic-path theorem and uses a suitable choice of an auxiliary function; it additionally describes a direct explicit construction. Both pathways require more detailed proof review than the present elementary tests. The author reports Lean 4, but **GCL has not compiled the exact source and imported library or audited the proof axioms**. The public [Erdős #514 thread](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/514) records both the claim and its priority correction.

The theorem is about a *universal* $\Phi$. It does not prove that for some individually fixed $f$ no suitable **function-dependent** growth comparison can ever work, nor does it estimate the length of a superpolynomial-growth path. The aggregate [maintained #514 page](https://www.erdosproblems.com/514) therefore still labels the combined question OPEN.

### An elementary restriction on power exponents

For any entire function and any point $z$,

$$
|f(z)|\le M_f(|z|).
$$

Since $M_f(r)\to\infty$ for a nonconstant entire function, no path can have $|f(z)|/M_f(|z|)^\varepsilon\to\infty$ for $\varepsilon\ge1$. The meaningful positive-power question concerns $0<\varepsilon<1$, and the easy inequality cannot decide it.

The exponential examples show that growth faster than $M_f^\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon<1$ **can** occur for particular functions and paths. Hayman's source-reported negative result concerns the difficulty of guaranteeing such a choice **for all transcendental entire functions**.
