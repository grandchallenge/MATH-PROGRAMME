## The question in plain language

When we interpolate values at $n$ chosen points with a polynomial, errors in the data can be amplified. How should we choose the interpolation points to keep the worst possible amplification as small as possible?

[Erdős Problem #1129](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1129) asks for the points minimizing the **Lebesgue constant**. With distinct ordered nodes $x_1<\cdots<x_n$ in $[-1,1]$, define the Lagrange basis

$$
\ell_k(x)=\prod_{i\ne k}\frac{x-x_i}{x_k-x_i}
$$

and then

$$
L(x)=\sum_{k=1}^n|\ell_k(x)|,\qquad
\Lambda=\max_{x\in[-1,1]}L(x).
$$

This is an extremal optimization problem, not merely the choice of some convenient interpolation nodes.

## A small example

For two nodes $-1,1$, the Lagrange polynomials are $(1-x)/2$ and $(1+x)/2$. Both are nonnegative on the interval, so $L(x)=1$ and

$$
\Lambda(-1,1)=1.
$$

For the canonical three-node choice $-1,0,1$,

$$
\ell_{-1}(x)=\frac{x(x-1)}2,\quad
\ell_0(x)=1-x^2,\quad
\ell_1(x)=\frac{x(x+1)}2.
$$

The maximum of the sum of their absolute values is exactly $5/4$, attained at $x=\pm1/2$:

$$
\Lambda(-1,0,1)=\frac54.
$$

These examples are exact, but they do not independently establish optimality or uniqueness over **all** possible three-node configurations.

## What is known

Faber, Bernstein and Erdős established increasingly sharp lower bounds, culminating in the optimal leading coefficient

$$
\Lambda\ge\frac2\pi\log n-O(1).
$$

Chebyshev-root interpolation nodes achieve the same coefficient up to an additive constant.

Erdős suggested that optimal nodes should have the same peak $L(x)$ value in each interval between successive nodes. **Kilgore and Cheney (1976)** established existence of a configuration with this property, **Kilgore (1977)** established its necessity for optimality, and **de Boor and Pinkus (1978)** established a uniqueness result. This resolved the main structural characterization.

The **canonical** problem additionally fixes the endpoints $x_1=-1$ and $x_n=1$. Known explicit canonical optimizers include $-1,1$ for $n=2$, $-1,0,1$ for $n=3$, and an algebraic symmetric four-node configuration described by Rack and Vajda (2015).

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source audit #2400](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2400#issuecomment-6180920112) verifies the $n=2$ and $n=3$ Lebesgue constants and separates canonical and unrestricted formulations.

The older variational existence, necessity and uniqueness proofs have not been independently reconstructed.

## What remains

Review Kilgore's and de Boor–Pinkus's original theorem statements, the precise equioscillation definition, and endpoint conventions, and independently verify the known algebraic $n=4$ canonical optimizer.
