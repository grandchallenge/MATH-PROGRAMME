## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1150](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1150) concerns the following mathematical question and its exact quantifiers:

$$P(z)=\sum_{j=0}^n\varepsilon_jz^j,\quad \varepsilon_j\in\{-1,1\},\quad \max_{|z|=1}|P(z)|>(1+c)\sqrt n.$$

For every polynomial P(z)=sum_{j=0}^n epsilon_j z^j of DEGREE n with ALL n+1 coefficients epsilon_j in {-1,1}, ask if there exists absolute c>0 such that for all sufficiently large n EVERY such polynomial satisfies max_{|z|=1}|P(z)|>(1+c)sqrt(n). The website phrases 'ultraflat' question; distinguish strict >, degree n versus n coefficients, and unrestricted complex unimodular coefficients, where ultraflat examples are known.

## A small example

For P(z)=1+z+z²+z³, triangle inequality upper bound 4 and equality at z=1 give exact sup=4, while unit-circle mean square=4, hence Parseval lower bound2. A two-polynomial Rudin–Shapiro step P1=1+z,Q1=1-z has |P1|²+|Q1|²=4 everywhere on unit circle, providing explicit flatness control but NOT sharp sqrt(n) asymptotics or the desired universal gap.
