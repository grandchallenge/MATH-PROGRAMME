## The question in plain language

For integers $k>r>2$, let $\operatorname{ex}_r(n,K_k^r)$ be the maximum number of r-element hyperedges on n labelled vertices with no complete r-uniform hypergraph on k vertices. Erdős asks to determine the limiting Turán density $\pi(K_k^r)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{ex}_r(n,K_k^r)/\binom{n}{r}$; the limit exists, but its value is OPEN in general. A literal source normalization is inconsistent: the maintained page gives the graph case $r=2$ limit as $\tfrac12(1-\tfrac1{k-1})$, but with denominator $\binom{n}{2}$ the correct limit is $1-1/(k-1)$. The extra factor $\tfrac12$ corresponds instead to $\operatorname{ex}(n,K_k)/n^2$.

## A small example

For $k=4$, $r=3$, and $n=4$ there are exactly four possible triples on four vertices. Any three avoid $K_4^3$, while all four contain it, so $\operatorname{ex}_3(4,K_4^3)=3$ and its normalized density is $3/4$. For $r=2$, $k=4$ Turán's theorem gives $\operatorname{ex}(n,K_4)/\binom{n}{2}\to 2/3$, not $1/3$. These are finite/source-normalization checks, not a solution of $\pi(K_4^3)$.

## What is known

The normalized sequence $a_n=\operatorname{ex}_r(n,K_k^r)/\binom{n}{r}$ is nonincreasing: restrict an extremal $(n+1)$-vertex hypergraph to a uniformly chosen $n$-vertex subset, and note its expected edge count is $\operatorname{ex}_r(n+1,K_k^r)\binom{n}{r}/\binom{n+1}{r}$; this cannot exceed $\operatorname{ex}_r(n,K_k^r)$. Hence the nonnegative sequence has a limit. Turán's graph theorem gives the graph-case normalized density $1-1/(k-1)$, but for $r\ge3$, even the $r=3$, $k=4$ case is a famous open hypergraph Turán-density question.
