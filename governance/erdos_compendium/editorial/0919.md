## The question in plain language

In infinite graph theory, **cardinality** and **order type** are different measures of the size of a vertex set. A cardinal records how many vertices there are. An **ordinal order type** additionally remembers their fixed well-ordering, so two vertex subsets may have the same cardinality but different order types.

Erdős and Hajnal asked, in [Erdős Problem #919](https://www.erdosproblems.com/919), whether there is a graph whose vertex set is **well-ordered with order type**

$$
\omega_2^2=\omega_2\cdot\omega_2,
$$

such that the whole graph has chromatic number $\aleph_2$, yet **every** subgraph on a vertex subset of strictly smaller order type is countably colorable. They also ask whether such a graph is possible with total chromatic number only $\aleph_1$.

Here $\omega_2$ denotes the initial ordinal of cardinality $\aleph_2$ and the square is **ordinal multiplication**, not Cartesian exponentiation, nor merely a statement about $\aleph_2$ vertices. Both versions remain OPEN. The condition applies to **all** smaller-order-type vertex subsets, not just bounded initial segments of the well-order.

## A small example

Take a finite rectangular grid of pairs $(i,j)$, with $0\le i<a$, $0\le j<b$, and join two pairs exactly when **both coordinates strictly increase** in one direction. The graph is simple and undirected: $(i,j)$ and $(i',j')$ are adjacent if

$$
i<i'\text{ and }j<j',
$$

or with both inequalities reversed.

Its chromatic number is exactly $\min(a,b)$. Coloring by the first coordinate uses $a$ colors, and coloring by the second uses $b$ colors, so at most $\min(a,b)$ suffice. The diagonal vertices $(0,0),(1,1),\ldots$ form a clique of order $\min(a,b)$ and force at least that many colors. For an $a$ by $b$ grid there are exactly

$$
\binom a2\binom b2
$$

edges. The exact finite verifier independently checks this for all 49 grids with $1\le a,b\le7$.

This elementary construction is a **finite analogy** to Erdős and Hajnal's ordered-pair construction on $\omega_1^2$, but it cannot establish the chromatic number of an uncountable graph. Finite maximal chains and finite proper colorings do not capture the difficult ordinal-reflection property.

## What is known

In [*Problems and results in chromatic graph theory*](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1969-13.pdf), *Proof Techniques in Graph Theory* (1969), pp.27–35, printed p.29, Erdős explicitly records the question jointly with Hajnal. He refers to a theorem of László Babai: a graph on a well-ordered vertex set with infinite chromatic number has a subgraph whose vertex set has order type $\omega$ and whose chromatic number is $\aleph_0$. Erdős and Hajnal showed that this **does not automatically extend** to larger initial ordinals.

Their illustrative graph has vertex set ordered with order type $\omega_1^2$. Each vertex is a pair $(\alpha,\beta)$ of countable ordinals, the well-order is lexicographic, and edges join precisely those pairs whose two coordinates increase. The resulting graph has chromatic number $\aleph_1$, although every subgraph with vertex order type **strictly smaller than** $\omega_1^2$ is at most countably chromatic. This is a nontrivial infinite-graph result, not proved by the finite grid example above.

The 1969 source also remarks that the analogous graph on $\omega_2^2$ has total chromatic number $\aleph_2$, while **every smaller-order-type subgraph has chromatic number at most $\aleph_1$**. This is a weaker reflection barrier than the desired countable bound $\aleph_0$. The open problem asks whether one can improve that local bound by a whole uncountable cardinal without losing the large global chromatic number.

A later [source-focused examination of Erdős's 1969 article](https://erdosproblems.ai/wiki/library/graph_coloring/erdos_1969_problems_results_chromatic_graph_theory) records the exact questions on the original page images, including the two target chromatic numbers and the difference between the known $\aleph_1$ local bound and the asked $\aleph_0$ bound. The full 1969 proof underlying its ordered-pair example has not been independently reconstructed in this GCL task.

The [maintained catalogue #919](https://www.erdosproblems.com/919) lists both versions as OPEN and indicates that no formalized statement is admitted. Neither existence follows from the weaker $\omega_2^2$ construction, and no ZFC independence classification is established by these sources.

## GCL's current state

There is a simple but important ordinal obstruction to an overly easy reduction. A subset of smaller order type need not be bounded in the whole vertex order. In fact, with $\kappa=\omega_2$, the set

$$
S=\{\kappa\cdot\xi:\xi<\kappa\}
\subseteq\kappa\cdot\kappa
$$

has order type exactly $\kappa$, which is **strictly smaller** than $\kappa^2$, but it is **cofinal** in $\kappa^2$. Thus a proof that every bounded initial segment is countably colorable would **not** establish the full #919 requirement. Nor does a condition only on vertex subsets of cardinality less than $\aleph_2$ suffice, since this cofinal subset itself has cardinality $\aleph_2$.

For a vertex subset $W$ of an ordered graph, the induced graph $G[W]$ is the largest subgraph on that same set of vertices. Because deleting edges cannot raise chromatic number, requiring every **induced** subgraph on a subset of smaller order type to have $\chi\le\aleph_0$ is equivalent to requiring the same of **all** subgraphs on such subsets. This handles the subgraph convention but does not remove the difficult ordinal quantifier.

The executable finite fixture at governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_918_919.py independently builds all **49** positive rectangular grids with side lengths between one and seven, computes their edge sets from the exact strict-two-coordinate rule, and verifies both an explicit proper coloring and a matching diagonal clique of size $\min(a,b)$. It obtains exactly $\binom a2\binom b2$ edges in every case.

These checks verify the finite encoding used to explain the infinite construction. They cannot compute the chromatic number of a graph with $\aleph_2$ vertices, nor can they justify an uncountable order-type reflection principle.

## What remains

A bounded mathematical obligation is the **cofinal smaller-order-type lemma**. In ordinary ordinal arithmetic prove that $S=\{\omega_2\cdot\xi:\xi<\omega_2\}$ has type $\omega_2$, is cofinal in $\omega_2^2$, and nevertheless has cardinality $\aleph_2$. Explain why an initial-segment-only condition is weaker than #919's universal restriction. Acceptance is a complete source-independent ordinal proof or a pinned no-placeholder Lean theorem with literal axiom report; a bounded purported counterexample or incorrect ordinal multiplication invalidates it.

A companion finite graph obligation is to prove that the strict-two-coordinate rectangular graph has chromatic number $\min(a,b)$ by explicit coloring and diagonal-clique lower bound. Acceptance requires a complete elementary proof plus the 49 exact finite instances, not an extrapolation to uncountable cardinals.

The **substantive open question** remains whether one can arrange countable chromatic number for every vertex subset of type less than $\omega_2^2$, while the whole graph requires either $\aleph_2$ or $\aleph_1$ colors. Source-lock the original 1969 ordered-pair construction and its precise smaller-order-type theorem, then identify the missing ordinal-reflection or coloring obstruction. A graph known only to have its smaller-order-type subgraphs of chromatic number at most $\aleph_1$ is insufficient. Stop before asserting a ZFC solution or independence result without a complete published proof.
