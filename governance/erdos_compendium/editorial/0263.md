## The question in plain language

Take a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers

$$
a_1<a_2<a_3<\cdots.
$$

Call it a Type-2 irrationality sequence when every positive-integer sequence $(b_n)$ satisfying $b_n/a_n\to1$ has an irrational reciprocal sum. In other words, the requirement is universal: every multiplicative perturbation that becomes asymptotically negligible must still leave

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{b_n}
$$

irrational. The problem asks two questions. Is $a_n=2^{2^n}$ such a sequence? Must every Type-2 sequence satisfy $a_n^{1/n}\to\infty$?

The increasing hypothesis is essential. The live problem page corrected an earlier version on 2 April 2026 after a counterexample was reported for the non-monotone formulation. Thus the corrected question is not interchangeable with the older formal statement or with a counterexample to that older statement. The maintained catalogue still labels the problem open.

## A small example

For a reproducible finite check, use zero-based indexing for the same double-exponential formula. The first five terms are $2,4,16,256,65536$. They satisfy the exact recurrence $a_{n+1}=a_n^2$, so the boundary ratio is $a_{n+1}/a_n^2=1$. The five-term reciprocal sum is

$$
\frac12+\frac14+\frac1{16}+\frac1{256}+\frac1{65536}=\frac{53505}{65536}.
$$

The following Python calculation checks both facts with exact integers and rational arithmetic:

```python
from fractions import Fraction

a = [2 ** (2 ** n) for n in range(5)]
partial = sum((Fraction(1, x) for x in a), Fraction())
ratios = [Fraction(a[n + 1], a[n] ** 2) for n in range(4)]
assert a == [2, 4, 16, 256, 65536]
assert partial == Fraction(53505, 65536)
assert ratios == [Fraction(1, 1)] * 4
print(a, partial, ratios)
```

This verifies only a finite partial sum and the recurrence. A finite rational partial sum says nothing about whether the infinite sum is rational, and the recurrence says nothing by itself about all possible perturbations $(b_n)$.

## What is known

The classical source is Erdős and Graham, *Old and New Problems and Results in Combinatorial Number Theory* (1980), p. 63. Erdős restated the issue in “On the irrationality of certain series: problems and results,” *New advances in transcendence theory* (1988), p. 105. Those sources give the Type-2 definition and present the double-exponential sequence as an unresolved, nontrivial case. Erdős’s 1975 paper, “Some problems and results on the irrationality of the sum of infinite series,” *Journal of Mathematical Sciences* 10 (1975), 1–7, supplies related classical context, but is not a proof of this specific Type-2 question.

The sharpest directly relevant proved interface in the audited literature is Kovač and Tao, *On several irrationality problems for Ahmes series*, arXiv:2406.17593v4, Theorem 2.4, p. 6. If $(a_n)$ is strictly increasing, $sum 1/a_n$ converges, and

$$
\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n^2}\longrightarrow0,
$$

then $(a_n)$ is not Type-2. Its conclusion is a genuine obstruction: under those hypotheses there is a positive-integer perturbation with ratio tending to $1$ whose reciprocal sum is rational. It is not a classification theorem, and it does not cover the boundary case where the displayed ratio tends to $1$.

The same paper records the positive folklore criterion that sufficiently rapid root growth, such as $a_n^{1/2^n}\to\infty$, forces the relevant reciprocal sums to be irrational; stronger growth assumptions, for example $\liminf a_{n+1}/a_n^{2+\varepsilon}>0$ for some $\varepsilon>0$, imply that criterion. For $a_n=2^{2^n}$, however, $a_n^{1/2^n}=2$ (with either consistent index convention), not infinity. Both broad interfaces therefore stop exactly where Problem #263 is interesting.

Koizumi’s *Irrationality of the reciprocal sum of doubly exponential sequences*, arXiv:2504.05933v1, Theorem 4, p. 3, proves that $\lfloor\alpha^{2^n}\rfloor$ is Type-2 for all but countably many $\alpha>1$, using a nondecreasing convention. This is a powerful almost-everywhere result, but it does not decide the exceptional-looking specific value $\alpha=2$. It is an external mathematical result, not GCL certification.

## What remains

Question 1, whether $2^{2^n}$ is Type-2, remains open in the checked primary evidence. Kovač–Tao do not apply because the ratio is exactly $1$, and the folklore sufficient condition does not apply because the relevant root is constantly $2$. Koizumi’s all-but-countably-many statement also leaves the particular base $2$ undecided. Question 2 remains open after the monotonicity correction; the pre-correction negative result is not evidence against it.

The smallest responsible next step is a source/formalization reconciliation, not a claim of completion. It should use positive-natural indexing, prove the reciprocal-ratio equivalence rather than assume it, and state explicitly whether the reciprocal series is required to converge. Any later mathematical work would need a new theorem or a counterexample satisfying strict increase and the corrected quantifiers. Finite computation cannot settle either infinite assertion.
