## The question in plain language

Choose $n$ distinct points in the Euclidean plane and list the distances between distinct unordered pairs of points. Some pairs may have equal lengths, so the number of **different distances** can be much smaller than $\binom n2$.

Erdős #89 asks whether every such point set determines at least

$$
c\,\frac{n}{\sqrt{\log n}}
$$

distinct distances for some absolute $c>0$ and all sufficiently large $n$. The crucial word is **every**: an example achieving this scale cannot prove the lower bound for arbitrary configurations.

## A small example

Take the $3\times3$ integer grid. The possible nonzero squared distances are $1,2,4,5,8$. It determines five distinct distances among its 36 unordered point pairs. Squared lengths are a convenient exact computational representation because taking square roots is injective on nonnegative reals.

Larger approximately square integer grids yield few distinct distances because the squared lengths are integers represented as sums of two squares. Classical number theory gives the upper scale $O(n/\sqrt{\log n})$ for such grid examples, explaining why the conjectured lower bound would be asymptotically sharp.

## What is known

The conjecture remains **OPEN**. Guth and Katz, [*On the Erdős distinct distances problem in the plane*](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2015/181-1/p02), *Annals of Mathematics* 181 (2015), 155–190, prove a lower bound of order

$$
\frac{n}{\log n}.
$$

Their primary publisher abstract explicitly states that result. Its proof transforms a distinct-distances problem into incidence geometry for rigid motions and uses polynomial partitioning and ruled surfaces. It establishes the sharp exponent of $n$ but leaves a factor $\sqrt{\log n}$ between the proved lower bound and Erdős's conjectured estimate.

Requiring one vertex to have many distinct distances to the others is a stronger *pinned-distance* condition. It cannot be silently substituted for the original total-distance question.

## GCL's current state

The pinned catalogue and maintained [Erdős #89](https://www.erdosproblems.com/89), edited January 2026, report **OPEN**. GCL independently enumerated the exact $3\times3$ grid squared-distance set $\{1,2,4,5,8\}$; the finite receipt is stored on CHAD. The original GCL issue [#1358](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1358) classifies further native research as deferred until a new mechanism is available. The 2015 Annals primary theorem was checked at the abstract level, but its deep incidence proof was not independently reproduced.

The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1358#issuecomment-6158438765). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded fixture is to enumerate exact squared distances for rectangular integer grids through $5\times5$. Acceptance: for $3\times3$ the distinct squared-length set equals $\{1,2,4,5,8\}$ and unordered pairs are counted once. Test integer representations of $a^2+b^2$ separately. Stop at finite enumeration; it neither proves a new universal lower bound nor selects a Solve campaign.
