## The question in plain language

For a set $A$ of positive integers not exceeding $N$, form all **nonempty subset sums**, using each element at most once. Write the resulting set of positive integers as $\Sigma(A)$. It is **primitive** if no two distinct values in $\Sigma(A)$ divide one another.

[Erdős Problem #882](https://www.erdosproblems.com/882) asks how large such an $A$ can be. The condition is much stronger than requiring the elements of $A$ themselves to be primitive or pairwise coprime. Every restricted subset sum, including those made from many summands, enters the divisibility test.

## A small example

The set $A=\{4,6,7\}$ is contained in $[1,7]$. Its seven positive nonempty subset sums are

$$
\Sigma(A)=\{4,6,7,10,11,13,17\}.
$$

No two different entries divide each other, so this is a valid example of size 3. An exhaustive finite verifier calculates the greatest permitted size for every $N\le18$, including maxima 2, 3 and 4 for $N=3,7,15$, respectively.

A systematic lower construction is

$$
A_m=\{2^m-2^j:0\le j<m\}\subseteq[1,2^m-1].
$$

It contains exactly $m$ numbers. The exact verifier checks all $2^m-1$ nonempty subset sums for each $1\le m\le10$ and finds that the sums are distinct and primitive under divisibility. Hence sets of size approximately $\log_2N$ exist.

## What is known

The source is Erdős, Lev, Rauzy, Sándor and Sárközy, [*Greedy algorithm, arithmetic progressions, subset sums and divisibility*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0012-365X(98)00385-9), *Discrete Mathematics* **200** (1999), pp.119–135. Their binary family gives the matching logarithmic lower order. The maintained [Erdős Problems #882](https://www.erdosproblems.com/882) reports the upper estimate

$$
|A|\le\log_2N+\frac12\log_2\log N+O(1).
$$

Thus the leading coefficient of the logarithmic maximum is established. The finer question of an error bounded by an absolute constant remains distinct from this reported upper estimate. The complete 1999 source proof and the precise provenance of its upper bound have **not** been independently replayed by GCL. The earlier [Erdős 1998 Eger chapter](https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110809794.169) mentions another shifted binary construction of Sándor without full proof details.

## GCL's current state

A simple injectivity lemma follows directly from primitivity. Suppose two **different nonempty subsets** of $A$ have equal positive sum. Remove their common elements to obtain two disjoint nonempty subsets with the same positive sum $u$. The union of these subsets has sum $2u$. Both $u$ and $2u$ are nonempty subset sums and are **different positive values**, with $u$ dividing $2u$. This contradicts primitivity.

Therefore all $2^{|A|}-1$ nonempty subset sums are distinct. Each is at most $N|A|$, so

$$
2^{|A|}-1\le N|A|.
$$

In particular, this independently proves $|A|\le\log_2N+O(\log\log N)$. It is weaker than the reported upper coefficient of one-half on the iterated logarithm. There is no claim that GCL has reproved that stronger estimate.

The exact finite program computes subset-sum values and checks every pair of different sums for divisibility. A pruning-based exhaustive search through $N=18$ is independently checked by fixed-size combinations enumeration through $N=12$. These finite checks are not an asymptotic or a Lean proof.

## What remains

A bounded successor is the **subset-sum injectivity and binary-family lemma**. Given positive finite $A$ with primitive $\Sigma(A)$, prove that the map from nonempty subsets to sums is injective, derive the elementary counting bound, and prove the primitive-subset-sum property of $A_m$.

Acceptance is a source-locked complete proof or a compiled no-placeholder Lean theorem with actual axiom output, the exact binary-family check through $m=10$, and independent finite maxima through $N=12$. A collision of nonempty subset sums in a primitive example, divisible binary-family sums, or an incorrect finite optimum falsifies the corresponding statement. Stop without claiming a sharper additive asymptotic than the primary source supports.
