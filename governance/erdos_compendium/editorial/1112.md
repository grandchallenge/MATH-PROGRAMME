## The question in plain language

An increasing sequence is *lacunary* when its successive terms grow by a factor bounded away from one. [Erdős Problem #1112](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1112) asks when one can construct a sequence $A=\{a_1<a_2<\cdots\}$ whose successive gaps all lie in a fixed interval

$$
d_1\le a_{j+1}-a_j\le d_2
$$

yet whose sumset formed from $k$ summands, denoted $kA$,  completely avoids a prescribed sufficiently lacunary sequence $B$.

A central requirement is the correct order of quantifiers: fix $d_1,d_2$ and $k$, then ask whether some gap ratio $r$ works for **every** sufficiently separated $B$ by permitting a suitable $A$ to depend on $B$.

## A small example

If every gap of $A$ lies between $d_1$ and $d_2$, then telescoping gives

$$
a_1+(j-1)d_1\le a_j\le a_1+(j-1)d_2.
$$

The set $A$ is therefore relatively numerous, in marked contrast to a geometric sequence such as $\{2^j\}$. Such bounded-gap sets can nevertheless have structured sumsets whose interaction with widely spaced target values is difficult to control.

For sums of **two** elements, Erdős and Graham described a positive avoidance construction with gaps 2 or 3 and target ratios at least two. For sums of **three** elements, Bollobás, Hegyvári and Jin established an obstruction for these same gap constraints, even for targets whose successive ratios can grow arbitrarily rapidly.

## What is known

The maintained registry reports that the threshold for two-fold sums with gap interval $[2,3]$ equals two, while **no universal ratio exists** for three-fold sums with that interval. Other higher-fold and general-gap parameters remain incompletely classified. It is therefore wrong to describe the entire family as either universally true or universally false.

The registry also notes that this precise multi-parameter question is an interpretation of an ambiguous remark in the original Erdős–Graham discussion; its historical provenance is less exact than many numbered Erdős statements.

## GCL's current state

[Source audit #2383](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2383#issuecomment-6121460106) identifies the already-negative parameter triple, the elementary bounded-gap counting estimates and the original-source ambiguity. It does not claim an independent replay of the 1997 proof or a result across every remaining parameter regime.

## What remains

Develop an accurately sourced classification of those $(d_1,d_2,k)$ for which a uniform avoidance ratio exists, while separating proven positive and negative cases. Resolving the original Erdős–Graham wording would also improve historical reliability.
