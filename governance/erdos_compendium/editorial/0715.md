## The question in plain language

Berge (or Berge–Sauer) asked whether every finite 4-regular graph contains a 3-regular subgraph; Erdős also asked whether some fixed regular degree r suffices. The maintained source marks the 4-regular question PROVED, attributing the affirmative result to Tashkinov 1982. The subgraph is not assumed induced or spanning; adding either requirement changes the statement.

## A small example

The complete graph K_5 is 4-regular. On any chosen four vertices its K_4 subgraph is 3-regular, giving a positive finite example. The bipartite graph K_{4,4} is 4-regular; deleting one perfect matching yields a spanning 3-regular subgraph. Neither verifies the universal theorem.

## What is known

Tashkinov [Ta82] answered the degree-4 case affirmatively. Alon–Friedland–Kalai [AFK84] proved that any 4-regular graph augmented by a single edge contains a cubic (3-regular) subgraph; the source derives every r-regular graph with r≥5 contains a cubic subgraph. The stronger family of results must retain simple-graph versus multigraph assumptions. Published theorem attributions come from maintained #715 (edited 2025-10-06); original Tashkinov Russian-language proof and AFK publication were not directly read.
