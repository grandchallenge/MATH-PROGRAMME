## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1198](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1198) is **EXTERNAL DISPROVED** in the source as of 15 October 2026.

Erdős asked whether every two-coloring of positive integers admits an infinite increasing sequence $A=\{a_1<a_2<\cdots\}$ such that **all nontrivial** expressions

$$
\prod_{i\in S_1}a_i+\cdots+\prod_{i\in S_k}a_i
$$

with **pairwise disjoint finite blocks** $S_1,\dots,S_k$ have the same color. The empty-product convention, whether individual blocks must be nonempty and the exclusion of trivial singleton expressions $a_i$ require exact source normalization. This is stronger than Hindman's finite **sums** theorem, because blocks can have more than one index and hence introduce products.

## A small example

For $A=\{2,3,5\}$, choosing singleton blocks for 2 and 3 gives $2+3=5$, using the block $\{2,3\}$ alone gives $2\cdot3=6$, and taking disjoint blocks $\{2,3\}$ and $\{5\}$ gives $2\cdot3+5=11$. This exact finite calculation shows the product terms omitted from Hindman's standard finite-sums theorem; it is **not** a universal two-color counterexample and does not re-prove Smith 1995.
