## The question in plain language

An exact additive complement pair covers every sufficiently large integer while its two counting functions multiply to approximately x. The question asks for a strictly stronger divergence of the counting surplus.

Let infinite A,B subsets positive integers satisfy A+B contains all sufficiently large integers and counting functions A(x)B(x)~x. Ask whether A(x)B(x)-x tends to positive infinity. Both coverage and asymptotic exactness are premises; do not infer the conclusion from either alone.

## A small example

For a finite pair of sets A={1,2,3,4,5} and B={1,3,5}, every integer 2 through 10 is in A+B. This checks elementary coverage but does not imply the infinite asymptotic result.

Finite combinatorial mechanism: for any integer t covered by sums a+b with a,b positive, counting pairs with both a,b <=t yields A(t)B(t) possible ordered representations, while t in [T,x] contributes at least one. This simple bound only proves A(x)B(x)>=x-O(1); it does NOT prove divergence of surplus and must not be presented as the Sárközy–Szemerédi argument. Check toy A={1,...,5},B={1,3,5} and count coverage and pair counts, avoiding asymptotic conclusions.
