## The question in plain language

The sum-of-divisors function $\sigma(n)$ adds all positive divisors of $n$. Ordinarily there is no simple relationship between $\sigma(a)+\sigma(b)$ and $\sigma(a+b)$. Yet sometimes they are equal:

$$
\sigma(a)+\sigma(b)=\sigma(a+b).
$$

[Erdős Problem #1061](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1061) asks how often this happens for positive integers $a,b$ with $a+b\le x$, and whether the number of solutions behaves like $cx$ for some constant $c>0$ as $x$ grows.

There is a counting convention to fix first: **ordered pairs** count $(a,b)$ and $(b,a)$ separately. The frozen Lean formulation uses ordered pairs. Ignoring that distinction would change the proposed leading constant.

## A small example

For $a=4$ and $b=5$, we obtain

$$
\sigma(4)+\sigma(5)=(1+2+4)+(1+5)=7+6=13=\sigma(9).
$$

For $a=2$, $b=6$, the equality is $3+12=15=\sigma(8)$. Thus $(4,5)$ and $(2,6)$ are genuine solutions, as are their reversals.

Independent integer enumeration gives exactly 8 ordered solutions with $a+b\le10$, 12 with $a+b\le20$, and 26 with $a+b\le40$. These are finite facts; the ratios to $x$ do not determine an asymptotic constant.

## What is known

The [canonical catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1061) records the question as open and attributes it to Guy, *Unsolved Problems in Number Theory*, problem B15. An external 2026 claim of resolution has been mentioned in the discussion, but its manuscript, proof, and relationship to the exact pair-counting convention have not been independently checked by GCL.

A plausible heuristic is not a theorem. Even a convincing number of finite experiments would not prove existence of the limiting positive constant $c$.

## GCL's current state

[Source audit #2332](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2332#issuecomment-6156558618) verifies the exact ordered-pair convention of the [frozen formal statement](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1061.lean), whose asymptotic theorem is unproved. Two integer divisor-sum methods agree in the bounded examples. The audit does not claim a source-locked original Erdős publication or a completed external proof review.

## What remains

One needs a rigorous asymptotic count or a disproof of the proposed linear law, with ordered and unordered conventions stated explicitly. A bounded GCL successor can extend the finite enumeration with two independent divisor-sum implementations, then investigate the external 2026 manuscript for primary evidence, an exact leading constant, and its mathematical assumptions.
