## The question in plain language

Can Euclidean space $\mathbb R^n$ be divided into **countably many sets** such that no two distinct unordered pairs of points within any one set have the same distance?

[Erdős Problem #1127](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1127) requires each class $S$ to satisfy, whenever $\{a,b\}\ne\{c,d\}$,

$$
\|a-b\|\ne\|c-d\|.
$$

The maintained catalogue labels the statement **independent of ZFC**, but the claimed negative direction needs a specific source-fidelity check.

## A small example

The points $\{0,1,3\}\subset\mathbb R$ have pair distances $1$, $2$ and $3$, all distinct, while $\{0,1,2\}$ repeats distance $1$.

More generally, any set of real numbers linearly independent over $\mathbb Q$ has pairwise distinct distances: an equality $|a-b|=|c-d|$ yields a rational linear relation $a-b=\pm(c-d)$, impossible for distinct unordered pairs in a linearly independent set.

This proves a useful implication, **not** that all real numbers can be partitioned into countably many such sets.

## What is known

Assuming the **continuum hypothesis**, Erdős–Kakutani (1943) established a countable decomposition of the line into rational-linearly-independent sets. Davies (1972) extended the positive distance-partition conclusion to the plane, and Kunen (1987) to every fixed finite dimension.

The maintained catalogue reports an obstruction when CH fails, but its explanatory sentence explicitly describes **finite-colouring** rather than **countable-colouring** failure. A statement that finitely many classes do not suffice is logically weaker than saying countably many classes do not suffice. That gap must be closed with an appropriate theorem before independently certifying the claimed ZFC independence.

[Authenticated source return #2398](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2398#issuecomment-6180698900) records the distinction as a **status-evidence conflict** without claiming the catalogue is necessarily wrong.

The [publisher record for Kunen (1987)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100067426), *Partitioning Euclidean space*, *Mathematical Proceedings of the Cambridge Philosophical Society* **102**, pp. 379–383, is verified. The full original argument and the non-CH obstruction have not been independently replayed.
