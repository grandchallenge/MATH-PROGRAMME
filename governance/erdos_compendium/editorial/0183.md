## The question in plain language

Colour every edge of the complete graph $K_N$ with one of $k$ colours. Define $R(3;k)$ as the least $N$ such that **every** such colouring contains three vertices whose three connecting edges have one colour: a monochromatic triangle.

Erdős asked whether the exponential growth rate has a limit and what that limit is:

$$
\lim_{k\to\infty} R(3;k)^{1/k}.
$$

Unlike the *off-diagonal two-colour* Ramsey number $R(3,k)$, the semicolon in $R(3;k)$ denotes a **$k$ colour diagonal triangle problem**. Confusing these two functions would change the asymptotic problem entirely.

## A small example

For one colour, $R(3;1)=3$. For two colours, $R(3;2)=6$: every two-colouring of $K_6$ has a monochromatic triangle, whereas $K_5$ admits a red five-cycle and its blue complementary five-cycle with no monochromatic triangle.

For higher $k$, deleting one vertex from a colouring and using its incident-edge colour classes gives the standard recurrence

$$
R(3;k)\le 2+k\bigl(R(3;k-1)-1\bigr).
$$

Iterating yields a factorial upper bound, not a constant-base exponential bound.

## What is known

The existence and finite value of the proposed limit remain **OPEN** in the [maintained #183](https://www.erdosproblems.com/183), updated April 2026. Xu, Xie and Chen's refinement gives

$$
R(3;k)\le (e-1/6)\,k!+1.
$$

The strongest lower estimate listed in that source, from Ageron, Casteras, Pellerin, Portella, Rimmel and Tomasik (2021) via Schur numbers, is

$$
R(3;k)\ge 380^{k/5}-O(1),
$$

giving a limiting *lower* exponential base at least $380^{1/5}\approx3.28$. A factorial upper bound has an unbounded $k$ th root and cannot establish finiteness of the limit; the exponential lower bound cannot establish that the limit exists.
