## The question in plain language

Let $\phi(n)$ be Euler's totient function: the number of integers from $1$ through $n$ that are coprime to $n$. Erdős asks whether the real number

$$
S=\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{\phi(n)}{2^n}
$$

is irrational, meaning that there are no integers $a$ and $b\ne0$ with $S=a/b$. The series converges absolutely because $0\leq\phi(n)\leq n$ and

$$
\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{n}{2^n}=2.
$$

The quantifier is over every positive integer $n$, with the fixed denominator base $2$. It is not a question about whether many partial sums are nonintegral, nor about the decimal expansion merely looking irregular. A rational number has an eventually periodic expansion in every integer base; the difficulty is proving enough global nonperiodicity for this particular coefficient sequence.

The formal-provider version writes the sum over $n:\mathbb N$. Under the usual convention $\phi(0)=0$, this is the same real number, but that reindexing is a normalization fact that should be proved in a formal development, not silently treated as the original wording. The formal declaration also leaves the proposition under `answer(sorry)`.

## A small example

The first ten totients are

$$
\bigl(\phi(1),\ldots,\phi(10)\bigr)=(1,1,2,2,4,2,6,4,6,4).
$$

Exact rational arithmetic therefore gives

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{10}\frac{\phi(n)}{2^n}=\frac{87}{64}=1.359375.
$$

This is a finite certificate, not an answer to the infinite question. If $S_N$ denotes the sum through $N$, then the elementary bound $\phi(n)\le n$ gives

$$
0<S-S_N\leq\sum_{n>N}\frac{n}{2^n}=\frac{N+2}{2^N}.
$$

For a second, exact check, keep only the terms whose index is a power of two. Since $\phi(2^m)=2^{m-1}$ for $m\ge1$, the subseries is

$$
T=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\phi(2^m)}{2^{2^m}}=\sum_{m\ge1}2^{-(2^m-m+1)}.
$$

Its first terms are $1/4,1/8,1/64,1/8192$, at binary positions $e_m=2^m-m+1$. These positions are strictly increasing because

$$
e_{m+1}-e_m=2^m-1>0.
$$

There is exactly one binary $1$ at each of these positions and no carrying between positions. The gaps tend to infinity. If $T$ were rational, its binary expansion would be eventually periodic. An eventually periodic binary sequence with infinitely many occurrences of the digit $1$ has bounded gaps between successive $1$ digits, contradicting $e_{m+1}-e_m\to\infty$. Thus this separate subseries is irrational. This elementary lemma is independently checked here; it is not a proof about $S$, because the omitted positive terms can change binary digits through carries.

## What is known

The original sources do not give a solution. P. Erdős and R. L. Graham, *Old and new problems and results in combinatorial number theory* (1980), identify the problem on printed p. 61 and describe the irrationality as probably hopeless to prove at that time. P. Erdős, “On the irrationality of certain series: problems and results” (1988), pp. 102–109, repeats the question on p. 102 and says that its irrationality was probably unattackable by his methods. These are historical problem/status remarks, not theorem statements proving rationality or irrationality.

The maintained page currently still presents the problem as open, has no proof exposition and zero proof claims, and links the same two references. Its page history displays a 2025-10-20 version while the page metadata says it was last edited 28 September 2025; this is editorial-date drift, not a changed mathematical statement. The page also has later user comments that are explicitly unverified by the site. One records the valid-looking convolution identity

$$
S=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\mu(n)}{(2^n-1)^2}+\frac12,
$$

which is a reformulation, not a denominator exclusion or irrationality proof. Another gives the powers-of-two lemma above. A September 2026 comment reports bounded-coefficient and tail-separation results, while explicitly leaving the original universal condition open and noting that its notes have not had independent mathematical review. These are useful leads and partial deductions, not classical solutions.

## What remains

The original task remains to decide whether $S\notin\mathbb Q$. A finite computation, a tail bound, the convolution identity, an irrational subseries, or a large finite denominator exclusion does not supply that decision. In particular, irrationality of $T$ does not pass to $S=T+(S-T)$: an arbitrary real remainder can cancel the relevant digit pattern, even though all terms here are positive.

A useful bounded successor task is to formalize the subseries lemma with explicit hypotheses: prove $\phi(2^m)=2^{m-1}$, prove the formula and strict growth of $e_m$, and invoke the eventual-periodicity characterization for rational binary expansions. Its acceptance condition must explicitly say that it proves only $T\notin\mathbb Q$. A stronger task would need a quantified tail or denominator argument that controls all indices, not just the sparse powers of two. Any such argument should be checked against carries, the $n=0$ convention, and the exact ambient type of the formal sum.

The live comments and linked external notes may contain useful source-limited deductions, but their reported formal proofs, code, or Lean references require replay and scope comparison before they can support a claim about the original problem. No current source inspected here supplies that missing closure.
