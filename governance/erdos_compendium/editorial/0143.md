## The question in plain language

Let $A$ be a countably infinite subset of the real interval $(1,\infty)$. Assume for any **distinct** $x,y\in A$ and every positive integer $k$ that

$$
|kx-y|\ge1.
$$

Does this multiplicative separation force the elements of $A$ to be sparse? Erdős asked especially whether $\sum_{x\in A}1/(x\log x)$ converges, and whether the restricted harmonic sum is $o(\log N)$. These are **distinct questions**; sublogarithmic partial harmonic sums alone do not imply convergence of the extra logarithmically weighted series.

## A small example

If $A$ consists only of positive integers, the inequality forbids $y=kx$ for any distinct $x,y\in A$, making $A$ a *primitive set*. This links the problem to classic work of Erdős and Behrend. The hypothesis permits noninteger real elements, which cannot simply be replaced by integers without proof. Since $k=1$ is allowed, all distinct points of $A$ are also separated by at least one in ordinary distance.

## What is known

**PARTIALLY SOLVED; the stronger convergence question remains OPEN.** The maintained [Erdős #143](https://www.erdosproblems.com/143), updated April 2026, credits Koukoulopoulos, Lamzouri and Lichtman (2025) with the harmonic-sum conclusion

$$
\sum_{\substack{x<N\\x\in A}}\frac1x=o(\log N).
$$

Its main question whether $\sum_{x\in A}1/(x\log x)<\infty$ remains open. For integer primitive sets, Erdős established the stronger convergence statement, while other authors obtained counting estimates. The real-valued extension is material and not automatic.

## What remains

Prove the elementary consequence that the $k=1$ hypothesis implies separation by at least one, then build exact finite predicates on rational sets and test the stronger $k\ge1$ conditions for bounded multipliers. Acceptance: each test reports a bound on checked $k$, does not conflate finite checking with the full quantified property, and distinguishes the open weighted-series convergence from the proven harmonic estimate.
