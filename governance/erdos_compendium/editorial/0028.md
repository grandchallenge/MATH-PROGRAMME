## The question in plain language

A set $A$ of positive integers is an *additive basis of order two* when **every sufficiently large integer** can be expressed as $a+b$ with $a,b\in A$. Let $r_A(n)$ count the **ordered pairs** $(a,b)$ that represent $n$, so that $a+b$ and $b+a$ are counted separately when $a\ne b$.

[Erdős Problem #28](https://www.erdosproblems.com/28), the Erdős–Turán conjecture, asks whether the numbers $r_A(n)$ must be **unbounded** for every such additive basis. It remains open.

## A small example

With $A=\{1,2,4\}$, the ordered representation counts at sums 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 8 are respectively

$$
1,\ 2,\ 1,\ 2,\ 2,\ 1.
$$

The total is nine, the number of ordered pairs from the three-element set. More generally, for $F=A\cap[1,N]$ with $k$ elements,

$$
\sum_{t=2}^{2N}r_F(t)=k^2.
$$

Consequently some sum has at least $\lceil k^2/(2N-1)\rceil$ representations. This finite average bound can be only a constant when $k$ is around $\sqrt N$, so it does not settle the conjecture.

## What is known

Erdős constructed additive bases with representation counts growing no faster than a constant multiple of $\log n$, showing that representations can remain very sparse. Whether their counts can be uniformly bounded is the outstanding issue. An *explicit* sparse construction satisfying a related subpolynomial upper bound is a **different**, now-solved Erdős Problem #29.

Even finding a basis for which every integer has at most 100 representations would disprove #28, but no such global construction has been established in the reviewed record.

## GCL's current state

[Source audit #1297](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1297#issuecomment-6121317459) fixes the ordered-count convention, records the finite double-counting identity and the eventual-coverage predicate, and separates the related #40 conditional formal statement. The audit neither constructs a bounded-representation additive basis nor proves that no such basis exists.

## What remains

Prove that **every** order-two additive basis has arbitrarily large representation counts, or exhibit one with a fixed finite upper bound for every sum. Finite convolution identities, positive representation counts and average-case estimates do not supply the required global extremal conclusion.
