## The question in plain language

A *permutation of $\mathbb Z$* is a sequence listing every integer **exactly once**. It may be indexed one-sidedly by the positive integers; the ordering of the listed values, not their ordinary numerical order, determines whether a progression is *monotone*.

A **monotone $k$ term arithmetic progression** is a subsequence appearing from left to right whose values form either an increasing or decreasing arithmetic progression of nonzero common difference. Erdős and Graham asked for the **largest $k$** for which every such permutation must contain a monotone $k$ term progression.

## A small example

The list $3,1,2,4$ contains the numerical arithmetic progression $1,2,3$ as a *set*, but those values occur in the list in the order $3,1,2$, which is not monotone. Counting a progression without checking the relative positions of its values would give false positives.

For a finite permutation, test each set of $k$ distinct values in arithmetic progression and check whether its **positions** are strictly increasing or strictly decreasing as the values run in numerical order. Larger monotone progressions automatically contain shorter ones.

## What is known

The exact largest forced $k$ remains **OPEN**, with the current bound **$3\le k\le4$**. Davis, Entringer, Graham and Simmons (1977) proved that no permutation of the **positive integers** can avoid monotone three-term progressions. This also forces a three-term progression in a permutation of **all** integers: restricting its order to the positive entries gives a positive-integer permutation.

Geneson (2019) constructed a permutation of $\mathbb Z$ avoiding **six**-term monotone progressions, showing the largest forced length is at most five. Sarosh Adenwalla, [*Avoiding monotone arithmetic progressions in permutations of integers*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2024.114183), *Discrete Mathematics* 347 (2024), 114183, constructed a permutation avoiding **five**-term progressions. Thus the upper bound is now four.

The existence of a one-sided permutation of all integers avoiding *four*-term progressions remains a separate question from #196 about positive integers. Ho's September 2026 solution to #196 constructs a permutation **only of positive integers** and does not automatically supply a permutation of all integers.

## GCL's current state

The pinned and [maintained #195](https://www.erdosproblems.com/195) classify the exact threshold **OPEN**. Original task: [#1464](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1464). The 2024 published construction and its distinction between ambient integer sets were checked; its complete proof was not reproduced.

The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1464#issuecomment-6179221062). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded successor constructs and independently validates finite permutations of signed intervals avoiding monotone three- and four-term progressions. Acceptance: check both orientations in the **position** order, reject repeated elements, and show that a positive-integer permutation witness cannot be silently extended to all integers. Stop at finite semantics; the remaining infinite-four-term question is not settled by enumeration.
