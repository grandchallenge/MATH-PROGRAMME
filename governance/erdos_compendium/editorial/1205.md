## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1205](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1205) has maintained status **SOLVED — ASYMPTOTIC REPORT** at this audit.

For each integer modulus $1\le n\le x$, choose **exactly one** residue class $a_n\bmod n$. Let $F(x)$ be the largest number such that **every positive integer $m\le x$** lies in **at least $F(x)$** of the chosen residue classes. This is a **multiplicity** covering problem, not the one-hit covering in #1200. Modulus $1$ contributes one unavoidable hit for every $m$; allowing one class for every composite modulus is central. The asymptotic question is whether the optimum is $\sim\log x$.

## A small example

Exhaustively choose one residue for each $n=1,\dots,x$ for $2\le x\le8$; there are **$x!$** assignments. For $x=2,3,4$, the exact maximum minimum multiplicities are **1,2,2**, respectively; the receipt stores the maximizing residue vectors for all $x\le8$. This is a finite verification of the definition, not a proof of the $\log x$ asymptotic or its concentration error.

## What is known

The maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/1205 records a complete **elementary/probabilistic resolution**: $F(x)\sim\log x$, with refined bounds $\log x-O(\sqrt{\log x\log\log x})\le F(x)\le\log x+O(1)$. The upper follows from averaging covered multiplicity and $\sum_{n\le x}1/n$; the lower combines independent random residue choices among $n\le x/2$, concentration on the covered counts, and assignment of larger moduli to cover exceptional points. The exact concentration/greedy allocation step needs primary proof verification before GCL certifies the stated $O$‑term; a heuristic expectation by itself is not a proof of simultaneous coverage for every $m$.
