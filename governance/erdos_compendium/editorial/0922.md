## The question in plain language

An **independent set** in a graph is a set of vertices with no edges between them. Write $\alpha(H)$ for the largest independent set in a graph $H$, and $\chi(G)$ for the number of colors needed to color the vertices of $G$ properly.

Erdős and Hajnal asked the following question. Fix an integer $k\ge0$. Suppose **every subgraph** $H$ of a finite graph $G$, with $m=|V(H)|$ vertices, satisfies

$$
\alpha(H)\ge\frac{m-k}{2}.
$$

Must the graph $G$ be colorable with at most $k+2$ colors?

This is [Erdős Problem #922](https://www.erdosproblems.com/922), **proved affirmatively by Jon Folkman**. It is far stronger than observing that a *single* large graph has one large independent set: it requires the independence property in **every smaller vertex subset**. The statement is valid for arbitrary finite simple graphs, with no maximum-degree, planarity or triangle-freeness assumption.

## A small example

The five-cycle has five vertices and an independence number of two. Its whole-graph deficit is

$$
|V(C_5)|-2\alpha(C_5)=5-4=1.
$$

Every proper induced subgraph is a disjoint union of paths, hence bipartite and has an independent set of size at least half its vertices. Thus the hypothesis holds for $k=1$. The five-cycle needs three colors, achieving the conclusion $\chi(C_5)=k+2$ **with equality**. It shows that the constant $k+2$ cannot be replaced universally by $k+1$.

For each $k\ge0$, the complete graph on $k+2$ vertices provides another exact sharpness example. Every induced subgraph of order $r\ge1$ has independence number one, and $2\ge r-k$ since $r\le k+2$. Its chromatic number is exactly $k+2$.

For $k=0$, the hypothesis says that every induced subgraph has an independent set containing at least half its vertices. In particular no induced odd cycle can exist, since an odd cycle with $2s+1$ vertices has independence number $s$. A graph with no odd cycles is bipartite, so the $k=0$ conclusion is elementary. Folkman's theorem extends this to any fixed nonnegative deficit $k$.

## What is known

The original question is recorded in Erdős and Hajnal, [*Problem 3*](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1968-04.pdf), *Theory of Graphs*, Tihany conference proceedings (1968), p.362, and in Erdős, [*Problems and results in chromatic graph theory*](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1969-13.pdf), pp.27–35. Erdős specifically noted that even the case $k=1$ had not been resolved.

Jon H. Folkman settled it in [*An upper bound on the chromatic number of a graph*](https://www.erdosproblems.com/922), *Combinatorial Theory and Its Applications*, volume II (1970), pp.437–457. This original paper is the bibliography key [Fo70b] in the catalogue. It must **not** be confused with Folkman's different 1970 SIAM paper about monochromatic cliques under edge coloring, which instead concerns Erdős #924.

An authoritative, much shorter proof was published by Marthe Bonamy, Pierre Charbit, Oscar Defrain, Gwenaël Joret, Aurélie Lagoutte, Vincent Limouzy, Lucas Pastor and Jean-Sébastien Sereni, [*Revisiting a Theorem by Folkman on Graph Colouring*](https://doi.org/10.37236/8899), *Electronic Journal of Combinatorics* **27** (2020), article #P1.56. The authors' complete [paper PDF](https://perso.limos.fr/~vilimouz/papers/Folkman.pdf), printed pp.3–7, provides a new self-contained proof and explicitly identifies the original Folkman source. GCL inspected its Theorem 1.3, its potential-function reformulation and the beginning of the proof directly on the page image.

Their key equivalent formulation is

$$
\chi(G)\le
\max_{H\subseteq_{\mathrm{ind}}G}
\left(|V(H)|-2\alpha(H)+2\right).
$$

Here the maximum includes the empty induced subgraph, for which the expression is two. The authors call the expression inside the maximum the **potential** of $H$. This exact inequality immediately yields Erdős #922, since the hypothesis makes every term in the maximum at most $k+2$.

The 2020 Theorem 1.3 also has an equivalent *half-stable deletion* wording. If each induced $H$ admits deletion of at most $k$ vertices such that the remaining induced graph has an independent set on at least half its vertices, then $\chi(G)\le k+2$. To see equivalence, when $\alpha(H)\ge(m-k)/2$, remove up to $k$ vertices **outside** a maximum independent set to make the remaining order at most $2\alpha(H)$. Conversely, if at most $k$ vertices can be removed to make $H$ half-stable, then $\alpha(H)\ge(m-k)/2$. Thus the apparently different formulations encode the **same** hereditary numerical condition.

The current [Erdős catalogue #922](https://www.erdosproblems.com/922) is marked **PROVED**, while its formal-statement metadata does not certify a Lean development. The general proof resides in the printed literature, and a full GCL kernel replay has not been performed.

## GCL's current state

The **potential transformation** can be proved without the deep graph-coloring theorem. Let

$$
D(G)=\max_{H\subseteq_{\mathrm{ind}}G}
\left(|V(H)|-2\alpha(H)\right).
$$

Then the original hypothesis for a fixed nonnegative integer $k$ is precisely the inequality $D(G)\le k$. Every arbitrary subgraph on a given vertex set has independence number at least as large as the corresponding **induced** subgraph, because deleting edges cannot destroy an independent set. Therefore requiring the inequality for all subgraphs is equivalent to requiring it for all induced subgraphs.

The exact finite verifier at governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_922_923.py computes chromatic number and independence number by independent backtracking for **every one of 33,867 labelled simple graphs on one through six vertices**. It checks

$$
\chi(G)\le D(G)+2
$$

in every case, and compares the full hereditary hypothesis to the potential threshold for **269,747** graph-and-parameter pairs. It also checks the five-cycle and complete-graph sharpness families. These computations are exact but finite; they are not a substitute for the full Folkman theorem.

The full 2020 proof proceeds by contradiction from a smallest counterexample, uses a maximum-independent-set lemma of Hajnal and builds structural restrictions on induced even cycles and diamonds before giving a coloring contradiction. The complete argument is publicly available and mathematically established, but GCL has not independently formalized every reduction.

## What remains

The first bounded successor is a **source-equivalent potential lemma**. Inputs: a finite simple graph, its induced-subgraph independence numbers, and a nonnegative integer $k$. Prove the equivalence between the original all-subgraphs condition, the all-induced-subgraphs condition, and $D(G)\le k$. Acceptance requires a complete paper proof or no-placeholder pinned Lean code with literal axiom reporting, plus exact replay of the 269,747 quantified finite instances. An edge-deleted subgraph decreasing independence number, a failed parameter equivalence or a miscounted empty-graph case would falsify the implementation.

A separate independent proof-review task is to reproduce the 2020 proof of the universal inequality $\chi(G)\le D(G)+2$, including its minimal-counterexample assumptions, Hajnal's independent-set lemma, induced even-cycle and diamond exclusions and final coloring extension. The source must be matched to the actual theorem statement rather than treating the case $k=0$ or finite verification as the whole proof.

The stronger question of deleting a bounded number of vertices to make $G$ **itself bipartite** is a different problem: the half-stable property can be achieved separately in each induced subgraph and does not force a single global deletion set of exactly $k$ vertices. A 1999 theorem of Reed concerns a related bounded deletion function. It is not implied directly by the stated coloring bound.
