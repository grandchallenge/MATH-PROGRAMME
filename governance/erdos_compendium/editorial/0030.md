## The question in plain language

A *Sidon set* is a set of integers whose pairwise sums are unique up to swapping the summands, **including sums with a repeated summand**. Let $h(N)$ be the largest size of such a set contained in $\{1,\ldots,N\}$. [Erdős Problem #30](https://www.erdosproblems.com/30) asks whether, for every $\varepsilon>0$,

$$
h(N)=\sqrt{N}+O_{\varepsilon}(N^\varepsilon).
$$

The quantifier matters: the error must be smaller than **every** fixed positive power of $N$, not merely bounded by a single power such as $N^{1/4}$.

## A small example

The set $A=\{1,2,4\}$ is Sidon: its six unordered sums with repetition are $2,3,5,4,6,8$, all distinct. Thus $h(4)\ge3$. For a Sidon set of size $k$, the $k(k+1)/2$ unordered pair sums are different integers from 2 through $2N$; therefore

$$
\frac{k(k+1)}2\le2N-1.
$$

This elementary finite bound is much weaker than the conjectured asymptotic precision.

## What is known

The maintained registry reports a sharper upper bound of the form $\sqrt N+0.98183N^{1/4}+O(1)$ from Carter, Hunter and O'Bryant (2025), while the exact desired $O_\varepsilon(N^\varepsilon)$ error remains open. The source audit did not independently inspect the full modern paper, so the numerical coefficient is attributed to the registry rather than newly source-certified here.

## GCL's current state

[GCL audit #1299](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1299#issuecomment-6104178260) reconciles the repeated-summand interpretation with the pinned formal Sidon predicate, establishes the elementary pair-counting inequality and reports exhaustive integer enumeration of $h(N)$ for $1\le N\le12$. The pinned proof body is unfilled, and none of these finite calculations establishes the asymptotic conjecture.

## What remains

One needs a construction and upper estimate pinning the maximal Sidon size down to square-root scale with subpolynomial error **for every** positive exponent tolerance. Pair counting by itself stops at a substantially weaker leading constant and is not enough.
