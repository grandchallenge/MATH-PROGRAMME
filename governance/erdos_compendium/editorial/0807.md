## The question in plain language

The bipartition number tau(G) is the MINIMUM number of EDGE-DISJOINT complete bipartite subgraphs whose edge sets partition E(G). alpha(G) is size of largest edgeless vertex set. Conjecture: for G(n,1/2) almost surely tau(G)=n-alpha(G).

The biclique count is an EDGE PARTITION parameter. Graph edges cannot be counted twice among the complete bipartite pieces. The proposed almost-sure equality with n-alpha is disproved, and a stronger multiplicative improvement in alpha is known for random graphs.

## A small example

K4 has alpha=1; it admits a partition into three stars: edges from vertex 1 to {2,3,4}, then from vertex 2 to {3,4}, then edge {3,4}. Thus tau(K4)<=3=n-alpha(K4). This deterministic example is not contradictory to the random-graph asymptotic negative result.

This finite fixture tests the conventions. It does not certify any of the asymptotic or all-graphs assertions.

## What is known

Maintained #807 DISPROVED: Alon [Al15] proved random graphs almost surely tau(G)<=n-alpha(G)-1; Alon–Bohman–Huang [ABH17] stronger tau(G)<=n-(1+c)alpha(G) for absolute c>0. Crucial distinction edge PARTITION (disjoint edges), not cover with repetitions. Original proofs pending review.

The distinctions between established partial theorems, original open parts, and disproved universal claims have been preserved. Historical publications are attributed, rather than presented as new GCL mathematics.

Alon, Bohman, and Huang strengthen the negation of the original equality with the almost-sure estimate

$$

\tau(G)\leq n-(1+c)\alpha(G),

$$

where $c>0$ is absolute. This is an asymptotic probability statement about $G(n,1/2)$, not a pointwise statement for arbitrary graphs.
