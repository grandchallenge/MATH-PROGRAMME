## The question in plain language

Define A as the positive integers n such that for EACH prime p dividing n, there is some divisor d>1 of n with d≡1 (mod p). Erdős asked whether the natural density of A through N is exp(−(c+o(1))√(log N)·loglog N) for some c>0. The exact exponential constant and even precise scale remain OPEN. The d may depend on p, but must itself divide the same n.

## A small example

n=12 belongs to A: for p=2 choose d=3 (3≡1 mod2); for p=3 choose d=4 (4≡1 mod3). By contrast n=6 does not: for p=3 the divisors >1 are 2,3,6 and none is 1 mod3. A prime n=p also fails: its only divisor >1 is p, congruent 0 mod p. These are exact small membership fixtures.

## What is known

The maintained source attributes to Erdős a lower bound exp(−C√(log N) loglog N) for density and an upper exp(−(1+o(1))√(log N loglog N)). The exponent in the latter is sqrt(log N * loglog N), NOT sqrt(log N)*loglog N, so the two bounds differ by a factor about sqrt(loglog N) in scale. Erdős connected |A∩[1,N]| to upper bounds on possible orders of noncyclic simple groups, but the property is not an equivalence to the classification of such groups. Full 1974 arithmetic proof uninspected.
