## The question in plain language

Start with $1$, then repeatedly apply three rules:

$$
x\mapsto2x+1,\qquad x\mapsto3x+1,\qquad x\mapsto6x+1.
$$

Let $A$ be the smallest set of positive integers containing $1$ and closed under the rules. [Erdős Problem #1134](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1134) asks whether $A$ contains a **positive lower proportion** of the positive integers.

The externally proved answer is **no**: the density of $A$ is zero.

## A small example

Applying the maps to $1$ gives $3,4,7$. Later steps may reach the same integer through different expression trees, so it is important to count **distinct reachable integers**, not the number of sequences of operations.

An exact breadth-first closure algorithm enumerates every reachable integer at most $100,000$ and finds

$$
|A\cap[1,100000]|=14878.
$$

This finite fraction is about $14.878\%$. It does not establish the limiting density.

## What is known

Crampin and Hilton proved a much stronger asymptotic statement, reported by Klarner and expounded by Lagarias:

$$
|A\cap[1,X]|\ll X^{\tau+o(1)},
$$

where $\tau\approx0.900626<1$ is the unique positive root of

$$
6^{-\tau}+\sum_{k\ge0}(3\cdot2^k)^{-\tau}=1.
$$

Since $\tau<1$, the ratio $|A\cap[1,X]|/X$ tends to zero. This **disproves** positive lower density.

An older, general bound of Erdős for affine closure sets does not suffice here. Its direct exponent condition $2^{-\sigma}+3^{-\sigma}+6^{-\sigma}=1$ gives only $\sigma=1$.

Several related affine-closure questions due to Klarner remain open, and their status should not be transferred from this solved instance.

The original asymptotic proof has not been independently replayed.

## What remains
