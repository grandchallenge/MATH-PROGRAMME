## The question in plain language

Colour the positive integers with finitely many colours. Erdős #172 asks whether, for **arbitrarily large finite** sets $A$ of distinct positive integers, one can make **all finite sums and all finite products of distinct members of $A$ have the same colour**.

For $A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_m\}$, the natural strengthened interpretation is that every nonempty subset $I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}$ contributes both

$$
\sum_{i\in I}a_i
\quad\text{and}\quad
\prod_{i\in I}a_i,
$$

and all these values have one common colour. Clarify whether singleton subsets are required when comparing historical formulations; the current maintained page leaves “all sums and products of distinct elements” abbreviated.

## A small example

Take $A=\{2,3\}$. Its pair sum is $5$ and pair product is $6$, so the question for a two-element set demands that these values share a colour. Under the **nonempty-subsets** interpretation, the singleton values $2$ and $3$ must share that colour too. The latter is a strictly stronger finite fixture than checking $5$ and $6$ alone.

Requiring **arbitrarily large finite** $A$ does not imply the existence of one infinite $A$ with the same property: the finite witnesses can depend on the requested size.

## What is known

The problem over the **positive integers** remains **OPEN**. Hindman (1980) gave a seven-colouring showing that the infinite-set variant is false. Moreira (2017) proved that every finite colouring of the positive integers admits $x,y$ for which $x$, $x+y$ and $xy$ have the same colour, a special configuration substantially weaker than arbitrarily large finite sets.

Ryan Alweiss, [*Monochromatic Sums and Products over $\mathbb Q$*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08901) (2023), proves the corresponding **arbitrarily large** finite subset-sum and subset-product assertion for finite colourings of the **nonzero rationals**. A result over $\mathbb Q\setminus\{0\}$ does not by itself imply one over $\mathbb N$: its witnesses may be negative or fractional. Bowen and Sabok obtained the two-element rational case earlier.

## What remains

A bounded successor extracts the exact historical definition, then enumerates all sums and products of nonempty distinct-element subsets for finite example sets. Acceptance: a shared colour is checked across **both** operations, singleton inclusion is explicit, integers are positive, and the rational theorem is labelled as a **different ambient domain**, not a solved integer case.
