## The question in plain language

Colour all integers from 2 onward using finitely many colours. Must there always be a finite collection of **distinct denominators of one colour** whose reciprocals sum to exactly one?

That is [Erdős Problem #46](https://www.erdosproblems.com/46), an Egyptian-fraction Ramsey question. The answer is **yes**, proved by Ernest Croot in 2003.

## A small example

The distinct denominators 2, 3 and 6 give

$$
\frac12+\frac13+\frac16=1.
$$

If these integers are all assigned the same colour, they already form the required representation. But arbitrary colourings can separate them, so this observation is not a proof of the general theorem.

## What is known

Croot, *On a coloring conjecture about unit fractions*, *Annals of Mathematics* **157** (2003), 545–556, proved a stronger **finite-interval** statement: for any number $r$ of colours, a sufficiently large interval of denominators beginning at 2 forces a monochromatic unit-fraction representation of one. Restricting an arbitrary colouring of all positive integers to that interval gives the result immediately.

The registry also reports a generalization concerning monochromatic representations of other positive rational numbers. That extension has additional distinct-denominator bookkeeping and should not be substituted for the primary one-sum theorem without checking its construction.

The admitted frozen GCL formal-provider snapshot has no #46 statement. Reported external Lean status does not establish a protected GCL theorem certificate.

## What remains

The existence question is settled. Future work may formalize Croot's theorem or its rational-target corollaries, with full source and axiom verification, but should not call the original question open.
