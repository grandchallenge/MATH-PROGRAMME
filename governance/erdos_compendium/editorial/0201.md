## The question in plain language

For a fixed integer $k\ge3$, define $G_k(N)$ as the **largest guaranteed size** of a subset without any nonconstant $k$ term arithmetic progression, starting from an *arbitrary* $N$ element set of integers. Separately, let $R_k(N)$ be the largest progression-free subset of the interval $\{1,\ldots,N\}$.

Erdős asked whether these two extremal quantities are asymptotically identical for three-term progressions:

$$
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{R_3(N)}{G_3(N)}=1.
$$

The arbitrary ambient set in $G_k$ may contain very widely separated integers. This is much stronger than optimizing only within an interval.

## A small example

Every $N$ element integer set contains at least one $G_k(N)$ element progression-free subset by definition. Taking the interval itself as a test set shows $G_k(N)\le R_k(N)$.

The maintained [Erdős #201](https://www.erdosproblems.com/201) records the exact small discrepancy $G_3(5)=3$, $R_3(5)=4$; the set $\{1,2,3,4,5\}$ contains a four-element 3-progression-free subset, whereas a different five-element integer set has no such four-element subset. An additional finite inequality $G_3(14)\le7<R_3(14)=8$ is recorded. These are finite separations, not evidence for or against a limit of one.

## What is known

The asymptotic equality is **OPEN**. Riddell (1969) first investigated the problem. Komlós, Sulyok and Szemerédi (1975) established comparison up to a constant depending on $k$:

$$
R_k(N)\ll_k G_k(N).
$$

Together with $G_k(N)\le R_k(N)$, this gives the **same order up to constants** for fixed $k$. It does not force their ratio toward one. Roth's and Szemerédi's theorems give separate density estimates, but asymptotically sparse progression-free subsets can still differ by a nonvanishing constant factor.
