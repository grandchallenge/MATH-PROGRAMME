## The question in plain language

A nondecreasing sequence X_i≥2 is line-compatible if exactly m rich lines occur in a planar configuration of n distinct points, and the i-th rich line contains X_i points. Erdős asked whether the number f(n) of such sorted line-size sequences is at most exp(O(√n)); this is PROVED. A later question asking for lim log f(n)/√n is distinct and not resolved by the bound.

## A small example

Three non-collinear points yield exactly three rich lines of size 2: (2,2,2). Four points in general position yield six size-2 lines; exactly three of four collinear give one size-3 line and three size-2 lines, hence (2,2,2,3). Multiplicity and the condition 'containing at least two of the points' are essential.

## What is known

Original pinned teorth catalogue #733 and issue #2004 report PROVED; live maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/733 attributes the upper bound to Szemerédi–Trotter [SzTr83], and gives a lower exp(c√n). The exact primary incidence-theorem proof, sequence-encoding reduction and any limiting constant have not been independently read.
