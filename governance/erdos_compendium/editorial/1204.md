## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1204](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1204) has maintained status **OPEN — SHARP ASYMPTOTICS UNKNOWN** at this audit.

An increasing nonnegative integer **$k$-tuple** $0\le a_1<\cdots<a_k$ is *admissible* if, for every prime $p$, its set of occupied residue classes mod $p$ omits at least one residue. Define $A(k)=\min a_k$ and independently $B(k)=\min k^{-1}\sum_i a_i$, both over all admissible tuples. Translation invariance permits shifting a minimizing tuple to $a_1=0$. For $p>k$, admissibility is **automatic**, so checking all primes $p\le k$ is enough for a fixed $k$. The main question is whether $A(k)\sim k\log k$; the corresponding $B(k)$ is a separate optimization, not simply $A(k)/2$ by definition.

## A small example

Independently enumerate $k=2,\dots,6$ increasing tuples beginning at zero with maximum element at most 23; check admissibility modulo every prime $\le k$. The fixture obtains $A(2)=2,A(3)=6,A(4)=8,A(5)=12,A(6)=16$ and stores exact minimizing tuples and rational sample averages for $B(k)$. These are only **bounded-search values** unless it is separately shown that no valid smaller-witness tuple was missed; the reported $A(k)$ values are exact in that range because a minimizer lies below 23 and all smaller tuples were enumerated.

## What is known

The maintained catalogue https://www.erdosproblems.com/1204 attributes the problem to Elliott [El65], Erdős [Er80,p.108] and gives the currently known **$(1/2+o(1))k\log k\le A(k)\le(1+o(1))k\log k$**, with upper via primes just larger than $k$ (Davenport); lower-order improvements Hensley–Richard [HeRi73]/Polymath [Po14c]. The relation $B(k)\ge k^{-1}\sum_{j\le k}A(j)$ implies a lower of approximately $(1/4)k\log k$, versus upper $(1/2+o(1))k\log k$ from prime construction. The source has a **likely typo** in a greedy step written $a_{i+1}>a_{i-1}$, which should not be silently adopted as a valid definition.
