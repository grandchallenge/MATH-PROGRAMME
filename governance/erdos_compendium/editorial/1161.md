## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1161](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1161). How many permutations have a specified order, and which integer order is the most frequent?

$$f_k(n)=\#\{\sigma\in S_n:\operatorname{ord}(\sigma)=k\},\quad\max_k f_k(n)\sim(n-1)!.$$

Let f_k(n) be the number of permutations of S_n of order EXACTLY k (order = lcm of disjoint cycle lengths). Which k maximize f_k(n)? Let k be a positive integer and n sufficiently large; a conditional inequality f_k(n)≥(n-1)! has a divisibility criterion involving lcm(1,…,n-k). A verbal statement 'k minimal such that [1,..,n-k] divides k' must be source-parsed precisely: n-k may be zero/negative and minimality ranges over k.

## What is known

https://www.erdosproblems.com/1161 SOLVED by Adrian Beker [Be25d], 2025 arXiv:2510.11698 'The most probable order of a random permutation'. Primary abstract says max_m P(order=m)∼1/n so max f_k(n)∼(n-1)!, and for all sufficiently large n maximizers are exactly k least positive satisfying divisibility by all integers ≤n-k. The catalogue records an additional threshold f_k(n)≥(n-1)! implies divisibility. These are asymptotic / eventually exact, not a claimed exact small-n formula.
