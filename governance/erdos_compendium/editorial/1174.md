## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1174](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1174) is classified **NOT DISPROVABLE** in the maintained catalogue.

Two **distinct** infinite-graph edge-Ramsey questions are posed. First: can a $K_4$‑free graph $G$ have the property that **every** edge coloring with **countably many available colors** produces a monochromatic $K_3$? Second: can a graph with no clique of cardinality $\aleph_1$ force a monochromatic countable clique $K_{\aleph_0}$ under every countable edge coloring? Both quantify over **all** countable colorings; existence of one coloring avoiding the specified homogeneous clique refutes a candidate. Do not confuse vertex chromatic number with coloring the edges.

## A small example

No **finite** graph can satisfy the first edge-Ramsey condition with countably many colors: color **every edge differently**. This uses finitely, hence countably, many colors and prevents any monochromatic triangle. The fixture checks this on all 15 edges of $K_6$ and separately constructs the bipartite nine-edge graph $K_{3,3}$, which is $K_4$‑free. These elementary finite obstructions only show **genuinely infinite graphs are essential**. For an uncountable graph, distinct colors per edge may exceed the allowed countable palette.

Its **NOT DISPROVABLE** status is conditional-model evidence, not an unconditional ZFC existence proof or a theorem of full independence. The bibliographic identity and precise forcing assumptions of Shelah's argument need original-source examination, including whether the two graph properties are established under identical hypotheses. Their forbidden and forced clique cardinalities are very different.
