## The question in plain language

Let $L_o(G)$ be the set of lengths of odd cycles in a finite simple graph $G$. Erdős Problem 58 asks whether

$$
|L_o(G)|\le k
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\chi(G)\le2k+2,
$$

with equality only when $G$ contains $K_{2k+2}$.

The problem is sourced to Erdős’s 1990 chapter “Some of my favourite unsolved problems.” The exact chapter is bibliographically accessible, but its full text was unavailable here. Gyárfás’s primary 1992 paper explicitly records the attribution to Bollobás and Erdős and gives the relevant definition of $L_o(G)$.

Gyárfás proved a stronger structural theorem: if a 2-connected graph has minimum degree at least $2k+1$ and exactly $k$ odd cycle lengths, then it is $K_{2k+2}$. Applying this to the blocks of a graph gives

$$
\chi(G)\le2k+2,
$$

and shows that equality forces a complete block $K_{2k+2}$. The converse is immediate because $K_{2k+2}$ has chromatic number $2k+2$.

The numerical bound is sharp. In $K_{2k+2}$, every cycle length from $3$ through $2k+2$ occurs, so the odd lengths are exactly

$$
3,5,\ldots,2k+1,
$$

which are $k$ lengths. Thus $\chi(K_{2k+2})=2k+2$ realizes equality.

Gao, Huo, and Ma later strengthened the result: chromatic number at least $2k+3$ forces $k+1$ consecutive odd cycle lengths. This confirms that the original theorem is part of a stronger relation between chromatic number and the distribution of cycle lengths.

### Normalization and edge case

The phrase “at most $k$ different odd cycle lengths” means cardinality:

$$
|L_o(G)|\le k,
$$

not that every odd cycle has length at most $k$. The parameter $k$ is an integer, and $G$ is a simple graph. The source theorem is stated for $k\ge1$; the case $k=0$ is elementary.

For $k=0$, $G$ has no odd cycle. In each connected component, choose a root and color each vertex by the parity of its distance from the root. An edge joining equal parities would create an odd closed walk and hence an odd cycle. Therefore $G$ is bipartite and $\chi(G)\le2$.

## A small example

For $G=K_{2k+2}$, every integer cycle length $r$ with $3\le r\le2k+2$ occurs. The odd ones are

$$
3,5,\ldots,2k+1.
$$

Their number is

$$
\frac{(2k+1)-3}{2}+1=k.
$$

Also,

$$
\chi(K_{2k+2})=2k+2.
$$

Thus the upper bound cannot be improved.

Concrete check: for $k=2$, $K_6$ has odd cycle lengths $\{3,5\}$, exactly two lengths, and chromatic number $6=2\cdot2+2$.

For $k=1$, $K_4$ has exactly one odd cycle length, $3$, and chromatic number $4=2k+2$. For $k=0$, $K_2$ has no odd cycle and chromatic number $2$.

## What is known

Gyárfás’s Theorem 1 states that a 2-connected graph $G$ with

$$
\delta(G)\ge2k+1
\quad\text{and}\quad
|L_o(G)|=k\ge1
$$

must satisfy

$$
G=K_{2k+2}.
$$

The proof uses a longest odd cycle and analyzes how vertices outside it attach. If the graph were not complete, the attachment patterns generate more than $k$ distinct odd cycle lengths, contradicting the hypothesis.

This is the main reusable interface: a high minimum degree inside a 2-connected block forces the extremal complete graph.

Every graph decomposes into blocks. Gyárfás’s corollary says that if $|L_o(G)|=k$, then every relevant block is colorable with at most $2k+1$ colors unless it is $K_{2k+2}$. A complete block has chromatic number $2k+2$.

Hence

$$
\chi(G)\le2k+2.
$$

If equality holds, some block must be $K_{2k+2}$, so $G$ contains that clique. Conversely, containing $K_{2k+2}$ forces chromatic number at least $2k+2$, and the upper bound forces equality.

For “at most $k$” lengths, apply the result to the actual number $j=|L_o(G)|\le k$.

Gao, Huo, and Ma prove that if

$$
\chi(G)\ge2k+3,
$$

then $G$ contains $k+1$ consecutive odd cycle lengths. In particular, a graph avoiding all but $k$ odd lengths cannot have chromatic number $2k+3$ or larger.

This is stronger than merely counting odd lengths: it forces a consecutive interval in the odd-length spectrum.
