## The question in plain language

An *entire function* is a complex-valued function differentiable everywhere on the complex plane. Its derivative of order $n$ is denoted $f^{(n)}$. Erdős asked whether there exists one nonzero entire function $f$ such that, **whatever increasing infinite list of derivative orders we choose**, the union of the zero sets of those derivatives is dense in the complex plane:

$$
\overline{\bigcup_{k\ge1}\{z:f^{(n_k)}(z)=0\}}=\mathbb C.
$$

This is [Erdős Problem #906](https://www.erdosproblems.com/906). Read literally, the answer is trivially **yes**: a nonzero polynomial of degree $d$ has $f^{(n)}\equiv0$ whenever $n>d$, and the zero set of the zero function is the whole plane. Thus the meaningful historical problem requires $f$ to be **transcendental entire**, meaning not a polynomial.

The maintained catalogue labels the nontrivial interpretation **OPEN**. Besides the April 2026 online claims, a July 2026 arXiv manuscript by Eric Hou presents a full proof and an accompanying claimed machine-checked Lean formalization. These are important new evidence, but neither a manuscript claim nor an upstream Lean claim has yet been independently kernel- and source-audited by GCL. The difference between the catalogue status and the newer work is explicitly unresolved here.

## A small example

For $f(z)=z^3$, the fourth and every higher derivative vanish identically. Any increasing infinite sequence of nonnegative integers eventually contains an order at least four. Consequently the union of the corresponding zero sets equals $\mathbb C$. This demonstrates why the polynomial interpretation makes the problem vacuous.

In contrast, for $f(z)=e^z$, every derivative equals $e^z$ and **never vanishes**. This familiar transcendental entire function fails the condition completely.

A stronger exact reformulation is useful. Fix a nonempty open disk $U$ and let

$$
H_U=\{n\ge0:\text{the derivative }f^{(n)}\text{ has a zero in }U\}.
$$

The original universal-subsequence condition holds if and only if **for every nonempty open disk $U$, all but finitely many derivative orders belong to $H_U$**. Indeed, if the complement of $H_U$ were infinite, its elements would provide an increasing subsequence of derivative orders with no zero anywhere in $U$. Conversely, if every $H_U$ has finite complement, then every infinite list of derivative orders eventually hits every disk. This is the *cofinite hitting property*, not merely the assertion that infinitely many derivatives hit each disk.

## What is known

Erdős raised the problem in [Er56d] and [Er82e, p.72]. His 1982 remarks report an affirmative solution older than a decade but give no identifiable proof. Barth and Schneider (1972) constructed entire functions with prescribed zeros for **selected derivative orders**. Quanyu Tang's 2025 source analysis explains why this yields infinitely many successful derivative orders in each disk, a strictly **weaker** property than eventually **every** order having a zero there.

Related work of Boas and Reddy, *Zeros of successive derivatives of entire functions*, *Journal of Mathematical Analysis and Applications* **42** (1973), pp.466–473, constrains zero distributions for functions of finite order, but does not by itself supply the desired universal cofinite hitting property.

The [April–June 2026 discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/906) contains several independent affirmative **claims** addressing transcendental entire functions. One, attributed to Adriano, was posted before a GPT-5.5 Pro/Chojecki note; another, by Quanyu Tang, uses a planar Gaussian entire function and Edelman–Kostlan zero-density formulas combined with an Offord–Sodin concentration estimate and Borel–Cantelli. The probabilistic outline seeks a bound of the form

$$
\mathbb P\{f^{(n)}\text{ has no zero in }U\}
\le C_U e^{-c_U\sqrt n},
$$

for a fixed disk whose closure avoids the origin. Such a **summable** estimate would imply eventually every derivative has a zero in that disk, almost surely. It is important to establish the exact covariance-asymptotic hypotheses and the version of the Gaussian zero-statistics deviation inequality before asserting that the argument works.

A newer, more substantial proof claim is Eric Hou, [*Cofinite Zeros of High Derivatives*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20816), submitted July 23, 2026. Its Theorem 1.2 asserts existence of a transcendental entire function with the full cofinite property, even satisfying

$$
|f(z)|\le\sqrt{2}\exp(|z|^2).
$$

Rather than relying on the quoted Gaussian Offord estimate, this manuscript uses independent **bounded** random Fock coefficients, a saddle-point term estimate, a one-coordinate small-ball bound, and Jensen's formula to obtain summable zero-free-disk probabilities. Its [public code repository](https://github.com/erichou1/cofinite-derivative-zeros) claims a Lean 4 verification, including the growth bound. This has not been independently compiled or checked against the exact theorem statement by GCL.

The growth bound makes the manuscript's claim particularly consequential. Boas and Reddy (1973), Theorem 1 as quoted both in the source discussion and by Hou, excludes the required cofinite behavior for entire functions of order at most two and finite type. Hou's Section 4 asserts this historical theorem is **false as printed**, identifying an unproved uniformity step when the disk radius varies with the derivative order. This is a concrete mathematical **source inconsistency requiring independent adjudication**: we do not simultaneously certify both incompatible theorems.

At the time of this source audit, the [maintained #906 page](https://www.erdosproblems.com/906) still says OPEN and explicitly notes that claimed solutions exist in its comments. Neither these claims, Hou’s new proof and Lean assertions, nor the 1982 historical recollection has been independently source-proof replayed by GCL. The protected Formal Conjectures statement may explicitly require transcendence, so it must not silently be equated with the literal catalogue wording.

## GCL's current state

The **cofinite hitting equivalence** above is a complete elementary theorem of quantifier logic; it does not use any complex analysis. It gives an exact acceptance test for every proposed entire-function construction: a construction showing merely infinitely many successful derivatives for each disk is insufficient, because an infinite complement still supplies a forbidden subsequence.

The executable fixture at governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_906_907.py exhaustively checks **1,022 periodic hit/miss patterns** of periods one through nine. Every periodic pattern with even one missing residue admits an infinite arithmetic-progression subsequence avoiding the target disk; the only periodic pattern satisfying the cofinite property hits every residue. This finite discrete analogue tests the logical boundary but cannot construct a transcendental entire function or test arbitrary zero sets.

The polynomial and exponential examples are exact analytic sanity checks, not experimental evidence for the difficult transcendental existence question.

## What remains

The next bounded task is a **cofinite-hit logical equivalence** over a countable base of open disks. Inputs: the derivative-zero predicates indexed by natural-number orders and basic open disks. Prove that universal density for **all strictly increasing infinite sequences** is equivalent to finite complements for the hit sets of **every** basis disk. Acceptance is a complete mathematical proof or pinned no-placeholder Lean code with literal axiom reporting, plus exact replay of the 1,022 periodic fixtures. A single infinite missed-order subsequence or a failed implication falsifies the proposed equivalence.

The substantive source task is independent adjudication of the competing 2026 transcendental constructions and the printed 1973 Boas–Reddy theorem, beginning with Hou's fully documented July preprint. Exact inputs: the pinned arXiv manuscript version, the linked Lean git commit/toolchain/Mathlib dependencies, and the original 1973 Theorem 1. Compare the claimed bounded-coefficient Fock-series saddle bounds, small-ball estimate, Jensen gap, summable event probabilities, growth bound, and source semantics of the Lean theorem. Independently inspect the alleged missing uniformity in the 1973 proof before asserting any counterexample to an established theorem. For the earlier Gaussian route, independently prove almost-sure entire convergence, normalize the derivative covariance kernel and its Laguerre representation, obtain a uniform expectation lower bound on nonnegative compactly supported test functions and verify the precise Offord–Sodin deviation estimate including its normalizations. Establish a **summable probability bound for every member of one countable disk base**, then apply the first Borel–Cantelli lemma and countable intersection. A failure to obtain an order-uniform estimate, or proving only infinitely many rather than cofinally many zero hits, invalidates this approach. Stop before claiming a resolved transcendental theorem without that chain.
