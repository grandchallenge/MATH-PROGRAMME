## The question in plain language

Let $A$ be a subset of $\{1,\ldots,2n\}$ with *more than half* the integers. How many extra elements beyond $n$ are needed to guarantee that $A$ contains **all six pairwise sums** $b_i+b_j$ for four distinct integers $b_1,b_2,b_3,b_4$?

This is a question about a configuration **contained in** $A$, not necessarily a configuration whose original $b_i$ belong to $A$. The six pair sums need **not** be different: for example, $b_1+b_4$ can equal $b_2+b_3$ even when the $b_i$ are distinct.

An important choice is whether the $b_i$ can be arbitrary integers or must be *positive*. Write $g_k(n)$ for the least offset $t$ such that every subset $A\subseteq[2n]$ of size at least $n+t$ contains the pair sums of $k$ distinct **integers**. Write $h_k(n)$ for the corresponding requirement with $k$ distinct **positive integers**. These are not interchangeable problems.

## A small example

For $n=5$, take

$$
A=\{1,2,3,4,6,7,8,9,10\}.
$$

This is a nine-element subset of $[10]$ containing no complete collection of pairwise sums of four distinct **positive** integers. All ten nine-element subsets can be checked, and this is the only counterexample. Thus the positive threshold is $h_4(5)=5$, not $4$.

For $n=6$, all $66$ ten-element subsets of $[12]$ contain a valid positive four-tuple, while some nine-element subsets do not. Thus $h_4(6)=4$.

Our exact finite verifier uses the *set* of pair sums rather than incorrectly demanding six distinct sum values. It enumerates possible integer tuples, builds a bitmask of their required sum positions, and uses a subset-closure computation to determine whether each $A\subseteq[2n]$ contains some witness.

## What is known

Erdős's original question was motivated by Choi–Erdős–Szemerédi's 1975 results on dense sets and pairwise sums. The exact modern story for small values of $k$ has advanced rapidly in 2026:

- Wouter van Doorn, [arXiv:2605.00040](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00040), distinguishes the unrestricted $g_k$ and positive $h_k$ variants. The reported exact values include $g_3(n)=1$ for $n\ge3$, $g_4(n)=3$ for $n\ge2$, and $h_3(n)=2$ for $n\ge4$.
- John Erlbacher's [July 2026 frozen research release](https://github.com/demonstrandum-research/artifacts) reports a formal bound $h_4(n)=4$ for sufficiently large $n$ (originally $n\ge331{,}777$), together with further bounded results for $k=5$. Those formal and SAT artifacts have not been independently replayed by GCL.
- Wouter van Doorn and John Erlbacher, [*The cardinality of a set containing the pairwise sums of four positive integers*, arXiv:2609.39314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39314), submitted September 30, 2026 and updated October 1, prove the **sharp** positive variant

$$
h_4(n)=4\qquad(n\ge6).
$$

The authors report a formalized proof by Aristotle. Their work improves the earlier cutoff at large values of $n$. GCL has checked the statement and dated primary abstract but has not independently compiled the underlying formal source or replayed the full proof.

It would be inaccurate to mark all of Problem #866 solved: the general behavior for $k\ge5$ remains a separate research question.

## GCL's current state

The original [Erdős 1992 discussion, printed page 41](https://hrj.episciences.org/125/pdf) concerns distinct integers and a density surplus. Later literature explicitly separates unrestricted and positive variables. The frozen 2025 catalogue entry lacks the latest 2026 sharp results and should not be silently substituted for them. Moreover, any bound using the older unrestricted function must not be transferred automatically to the positive version.

The exact program governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_865_866.py computes both variants for $k=3,4$ and $2\le n\le7$ using integer masks and exhaustive subset closure. Among its verified results:

| $n$ | $g_4(n)$ (integer variables) | $h_4(n)$ (positive variables) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 3 | 4 |
| 4 | 3 | 4 |
| 5 | 3 | 5 |
| 6 | 3 | 4 |
| 7 | 3 | 4 |

In the unrestricted enumeration, candidate variables in $[-2n,2n]$ suffice: among three or more distinct integer variables with all pair sums positive, at most one can be nonpositive; two positive variables constrain the others by the $[1,2n]$ sum bounds. The program does not assume pair sums are distinct, an essential source-fidelity property.

These exhaustive checks verify only finitely many cases, not the general theorem or its formalization.

## What remains

There are two bounded successor obligations. First, formalize the distinction $g_k$ versus $h_k$ and prove that the finite witness-mask test has the same existential quantifiers as the source, **allowing repeated pair-sum values**. Acceptance: a reviewed equivalence proof or Lean code without placeholders, independently checked against the $n=5,6$ counterexamples and thresholds. Falsification: any finite set where the mask program disagrees with direct tuple enumeration.

Second, source-lock the September paper's formal proof, pin its exact Lean toolchain and dependencies, and obtain an actual compiler and axiom readback for the $h_4(n)=4$ statement. Stop short of claiming general $k\ge5$ results or GCL mathematical certification until these gates are satisfied.
