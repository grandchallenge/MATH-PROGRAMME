## The question in plain language

A Sidon set has a unique representation for every unordered sum of two elements, including when both summands are equal. Dense finite Sidon sets can have roughly $\sqrt N$ elements below $N$, but can **any prescribed finite Sidon set** be extended by adding larger numbers until it is nearly this dense?

[Erdős Problem #44](https://www.erdosproblems.com/44) asks whether, for every $N\ge1$, every Sidon set $A\subseteq[1,N]$, and every $\varepsilon>0$, there exist $M>N$ and $B\subseteq[N+1,M]$ such that $A\cup B$ remains Sidon and

$$
|A\cup B|\ge(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt M.
$$

The maintained public source continues to classify this as **OPEN**.

## A small example

Take $A=\{1,2,4\}$ and extend it with $B=\{8,13\}$. The combined set

$$
\{1,2,4,8,13\}
$$

is Sidon: its 15 unordered sums with repetition are

$$
2,3,4,5,6,8,9,10,12,14,15,16,17,21,26,
$$

all different. It has five elements below 14, exceeding $\sqrt{13}$. This provides one **finite** successful extension but does not answer the universal question.

## What is known

Classical Sidon-set constructions can be dense when we are free to choose all elements. Problem #44 is stronger: it fixes an **arbitrary initial set** $A$, forbids deleting or changing old elements, and requires every new element to exceed $N$.

There is an important quantifier issue. The extension limit $M$ must be allowed to depend on $N$, $A$ and $\varepsilon$. A shorthand saying only $M=M(\varepsilon)$ cannot literally be correct for every arbitrarily large $N$, because the theorem also requires $M>N$. The **actual frozen formal theorem** has nested quantifiers allowing the intended dependence, despite an ambiguous informal docstring.

## GCL's current state

[Source audit #1313](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1313#issuecomment-6133226199) compared the public problem, contributor discussion and pinned Formal Conjectures statement, and verified the finite 15-sum example by exact integer arithmetic. The research theorem remains unfilled in the frozen provider. No general Sidon extension theorem is claimed in GCL.

## What remains

Prove that every finite Sidon starting configuration can be extended to arbitrary near-optimal density at some larger cutoff, or establish a genuine obstruction. Bounded search may test algorithms but cannot prove the quantified infinite result.
