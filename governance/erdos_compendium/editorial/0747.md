## The question in plain language

Take a uniformly random three-uniform hypergraph on 3n labelled vertices with exactly ℓ(n) distinct triple edges. What number of random edges makes it have a perfect matching (n pairwise vertex-disjoint triples covering all 3n vertices) with probability tending to one? Shamir's problem, Erdős #747, is SOLVED in threshold scale: ℓ(n)∼n log n by Kahn's 2023 sharp result, after Johansson–Kahn–Vu 2008 determined the Θ(n log n) order. 'Almost surely' refers to n→∞ and a random edge sample, not every hypergraph with that many edges.

## A small example

With n=2 there are six vertices and a perfect matching is any partition into two disjoint triples. Choosing {1,2,3} and {4,5,6} succeeds, and there are binom(6,3)/2=10 distinct perfect matchings. Two triples sharing a vertex cannot form a perfect matching. This exact finite combinatorial fixture does not establish an asymptotic probability threshold.

## What is known

A necessary obstruction is an isolated vertex: a perfect matching covers every vertex. A random edge contains a given vertex with probability 3/(3n)=1/n, so after ℓ independently sampled edges a vertex is isolated with probability approximately exp(−ℓ/n); expected isolated count about 3n exp(−ℓ/n). This predicts transition near n log n but is only a heuristic until dependence and remaining matching obstructions are controlled. Johansson–Kahn–Vu [JKV08] proves threshold order, and Kahn [Ka23] proves sharp ℓ(n)∼n log n (also general uniformity). Full author proofs not independently replayed in GCL.
