## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #664](https://www.erdosproblems.com/664) asks whether a family of large sets that overlap pairwise in at most one element always admits a small-intersection **transversal**. More precisely, for a fixed constant $c$ less than one, the blocks $A_i$ have size greater than $c\sqrt n$ and satisfy $|A_i\cap A_j|\leq1$ for distinct blocks. Must there be a set $B$ meeting each block at least once but at most $C(c)$ times, with $C(c)$ independent of the growing system?

The maintained catalogue reports this general question **DISPROVED**, attributing the negative construction to Noga Alon. The stronger special version requiring every pair of ground points to lie on a block remains open; the two questions must not be conflated.

## A small example

In the seven-point **Fano plane**, label the points by residues modulo seven and make the seven lines the translates of $D=\{0,1,3\}$. Each line has three points, two different lines meet in exactly one point, and any two distinct points determine exactly one line. The whole ground set $B$ is a transversal with $|B\cap A_i|=3$ for each line.

This small positive example is NOT Alon's asymptotic counterexample. It only demonstrates the incidence vocabulary.

## What is known

For prime-power $q$, a projective plane has $n=q^2+q+1$ points and the same number of lines, with $q+1$ points on each line and a one-point intersection between any two distinct lines. If one replaces each line by a subset of its points, intersections can only decrease; in particular they remain of size at most one.

The [maintained #664 source](https://www.erdosproblems.com/664) says Alon used random subsets of projective-plane lines to obtain blocks of size at least $(2/5)\sqrt n$ for which every hitting set $B$ must meet SOME block in at least a constant multiple of $\log n$ points. That would defeat any uniform upper bound $C(c)$ for fixed $c<2/5$. The essential logarithmic transversal lower bound is not a formal consequence of ordinary projective-plane incidence: it depends on Alon's probabilistic construction.

**Primary-source limitation:** despite targeted searches, the original bibliographically identified Alon proof for this precise assertion was not directly located or inspected. The logarithmic result is therefore explicitly **catalogue-attributed**, not independently re-proved or certified here.

The [older Erdős 1981 variant](https://www.erdosproblems.com/history/664) requires every pair of ground points to occur in exactly one block. Randomly deleting points from lines generally destroys that property. Hence the reported general-family counterexample does not resolve the more restrictive version.
