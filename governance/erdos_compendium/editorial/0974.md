## The question in plain language

Fix \(n\ge2\) complex numbers \(z_1,\ldots,z_n\), with \(z_1=1\), and define their power sums
\[
s_k=\sum_{j=1}^n z_j^k,\qquad k\ge1.
\]
Suppose the sequence of power sums contains **infinitely many blocks of \(n-1\) consecutive zero values**. What can the original complex numbers be?

Erdős, reporting a conjecture of Turán in [Er65b, p.213], suggested they should be "essentially" the \(n\)-th roots of unity. The word **essentially** is mathematically important: the literal assertion is **false for even \(n\)**, whereas the precise classification is **PROVED**, originally by Robert Tijdeman in 1966. The maintained [Erdős Problem #974](https://www.erdosproblems.com/974) calls it **PROVED (LEAN)**, with the proviso that the source's formal-statement metadata and reported third-party Lean files still require GCL's own replay.

## The precise, proved classification

Tijdeman's theorem, as stated in the maintained problem and its [discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/974), is stronger than the original infinite-recurrence formulation. It suffices to assume **two distinct blocks of \(n-1\) consecutive vanishing power sums**.

- For **odd \(n\)**, the numbers \(z_1,\ldots,z_n\), up to permutation, are exactly the complete set of **\(n\)-th roots of unity**. The normalization \(z_1=1\) fixes the relevant rotation.
- For **even \(n=2m\)**, the numbers are, up to permutation, the vertices of **two regular \(m\)-gons on the same circle centered at the origin**. In particular, with \(z_1=1\), the circle has radius one. The relative angle between the polygons need not produce the complete \(n\)-th roots of unity.

This is a **necessary geometric classification**, not a claim that every arbitrary union of two regular \(m\)-gons satisfies the required vanishing-block hypothesis. The historical theorem in [Ti66] assumes distinct nodes; the later observation by Hu, Tang and Zhang shows why a vanishing block automatically enforces the distinctness and nonzero hypotheses.

## An explicit counterexample to the literal roots-of-unity reading

The even-\(n\) distinction is not decorative. Put \(n=2m\), with \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/m}\) and \(t=e^{i\pi/(2m)}\), and take the \(2m\) numbers
\[
1,\omega,\ldots,\omega^{m-1},
\qquad
t,t\omega,\ldots,t\omega^{m-1}.
\]
They are all distinct and have modulus one; the first is \(1\). Their power sums are
\[
s_k=(1+t^k)\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\omega^{jk}
 =
 \begin{cases}
 m(1+t^k),&m\mid k,\\
 0,&m\nmid k.
 \end{cases}
\]
For each \(q\ge0\), let \(K=(4q+2)m\). Since \(t^K=-1\), the power sum at \(K\) vanishes; the **\(2m-2\) neighboring indices**
\[
K-(m-1),\ \ldots,\ K-1,\ K+1,\ \ldots,\ K+(m-1)
\]
are *not* divisible by \(m\), so all of their power sums vanish as well. Consequently
\[
s_{K-(m-1)}=\cdots=s_{K+(m-1)}=0
\]
gives **infinitely many runs of \(2m-1=n-1\) consecutive zeros**.

Except in degenerate interpretations, this collection is **not** the full set of \(2m\)-th roots of unity: the second regular polygon is rotated by \(\pi/(2m)\), not by \(\pi/m\).

In the simplest case \(n=2\), take \(z_1=1,z_2=i\). Then
\[
s_k=1+i^k,\qquad s_{4q+2}=0
\]
for all \(q\ge0\). There are infinitely many required one-term zero runs, but \(\{1,i\}\ne\{1,-1\}\). It is a counterexample only to the **literal unqualified** reading, not to Tijdeman's two-polygon conclusion. For \(n=4\), the analogous construction has \(z=\{1,-1,e^{i\pi/4},-e^{i\pi/4}\}\) and zero blocks \(s_{8q+3}=s_{8q+4}=s_{8q+5}=0\).

## An elementary reduction: zero blocks force distinct, nonzero nodes

Here is a source-independent proof of the preliminary proposition that permits Tijdeman's classification to apply even if the input sequence is not initially assumed to have distinct elements.

Assume **one** run of \(n-1\) zeros:
\[
s_h=s_{h+1}=\cdots=s_{h+n-2}=0
\]
for some positive integer \(h\). Suppose there are only \(r<n\) *distinct nonzero values* \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r\) among the \(z_j\), with positive multiplicities \(m_1,\ldots,m_r\). Zero entries contribute nothing because \(h>0\). The first \(r\) equations imply
\[
\sum_{\ell=1}^r
m_\ell\lambda_\ell^h\lambda_\ell^a=0,
\qquad a=0,\ldots,r-1.
\]
The Vandermonde matrix \((\lambda_\ell^a)\) is invertible because the \(\lambda_\ell\) are distinct. Therefore \(m_\ell\lambda_\ell^h=0\) for every \(\ell\), impossible since \(m_\ell\ge1\) and \(\lambda_\ell\ne0\). There must consequently be at least \(n\) distinct nonzero nodes; since only \(n\) nodes were given, **all \(n\) are nonzero and pairwise distinct**.

The exact inequality \(r\le n-1\) is what makes one block of \(n-1\) consecutive zero equations sufficient. This argument is elementary, complete, and reusable for related linear-recurrence and Vandermonde problems.

## Why repeated zero runs constrain geometry

Power sums of \(n\) nodes satisfy a linear recurrence of order at most \(n\), with coefficients given by their elementary symmetric functions. Equivalently their ordinary generating function is the rational function
\[
\sum_{k\ge0}s_k t^k=\sum_{j=1}^n\frac1{1-z_jt}.
\]
A single run of \(n-1\) zeros forces powerful algebraic relations among those coefficients. Two distinct such runs rigidify the recurrence sufficiently for Tijdeman's classification, which uses the distribution and multiplicative properties of the nodes.

The Vandermonde argument above does **not**, on its own, establish that the \(\lambda_j\) have modulus one or form regular polygons. Those conclusions belong to the **published theorem** and require a separate reconstruction of its geometric/algebraic step. The explicit even-\(n\) family checks that the weaker polygonal classification is genuinely necessary.

## Source provenance and formalization boundaries

Tijdeman, [*On a conjecture of Turán and Erdős*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1385-7258(66)50044-0), *Proceedings of the Koninklijke Nederlandse Akademie van Wetenschappen, Series A* **69** (1966), pp.374–383 (also cited as *Indagationes Mathematicae*), supplies the established historical proof. The September 2025 forum discussion by Tao Hu, Quanyu Tang and Zhang independently reconstructed the argument, then recognized Tijdeman's priority. Its Proposition 1 removes the distinctness hypothesis and is independently reproduced above.

The catalogue's current "PROVED (LEAN)" label refers to third-party formalizations announced in the discussion (including one Aristotle-assisted 2026 development). At the same time its separate "Formalised statement?" metadata displays **No**. Those two labels concern different evidence channels and should not be interpreted as a completed, protected GCL kernel run. The exact Lean theorem must be checked for both parities, nonzero/distinct hypotheses, the normalization \(z_1=1\), correct block indexing and the two-polygon conclusion.

The 1965 Erdős source additionally assumed \(|z_j|\le1\) for \(j\ge2\), according to the independent source review, whereas the maintained problem page omits that bound. Under Tijdeman's precise conclusion and \(z_1=1\), every node lies on the unit circle, so this extra historical hypothesis does not alter the classified solutions. The discrepancy should nevertheless be documented, not silently erased.

## GCL's current state

The status **PROVED** is anchored in the 1966 refereed paper. The finite \(n=2\) example, the general two-polygon construction and the Vandermonde distinctness reduction are proved explicitly above.
