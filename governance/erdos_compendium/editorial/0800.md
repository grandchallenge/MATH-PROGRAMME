## The question in plain language

For every n-vertex graph G with no EDGE joining TWO vertices each having degree >=3, prove R(G)<=C*n with absolute C. R(G) is smallest N such that all 2-edge-colorings of K_N contain a monochromatic copy of G.

The key is to preserve the order of the quantifiers and the exact distinction between a finite example and a theorem about arbitrary large inputs. Replacing an extremum over every admissible set by an extremum over one convenient set would change the problem.

## A small example

The star K_(1,3) has center degree 3 and three leaves degree 1, so qualifies. The graph with two adjacent vertices each of degree 3 does NOT qualify, even when only those two vertices are high-degree. In K5 two color-complementary 5-cycles avoid a monochromatic K_(1,3); in every red/blue K6 some vertex has >=3 incident edges of one color (pigeonhole), so R(K_(1,3))=6.

The calculation illustrates the exact definitions and hypotheses. No single finite example establishes the reported asymptotic assertion.
