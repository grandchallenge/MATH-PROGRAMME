## The question in plain language

Let $p_1<p_2<\cdots$ be the primes and set $d_n=p_{n+1}-p_n$. Erdős asked for the second-moment estimate

$$
\sum_{n=1}^N d_n^2\ll N(\log N)^2.
$$

The implied constant must be independent of $N$. The bound concerns the **first $N$ consecutive prime gaps**, not all differences between primes in an interval.

## A small example

The primes $2,3,5,7,11$ have first four gaps $1,2,2,4$, whose squared sum is $1+4+4+16=25$. The prime-number theorem says the average gap near the prime with index $N$ is about $\log N$, but an average by itself does not control the average of **squared** gaps. A small number of unusually large gaps can dominate the latter.

Cauchy–Schwarz gives

$$
\sum_{n=1}^N d_n^2\ge \frac{(p_{N+1}-2)^2}{N},
$$

which by the prime-number theorem yields the matching-order **lower** bound $N(\log N)^2$. This does not prove the required upper bound.

## What is known

**OPEN.** Cramér (1936), assuming the Riemann hypothesis, obtained the weaker bound $O(N(\log N)^4)$. Selberg (1943), still conditional on RH, obtained a weighted estimate involving $\sum_{n\le N}d_n^2/n$. The conjectured scale would imply the pointwise bound

$$
d_N\ll N^{1/2}\log N
$$

for every $N$, by positivity of the summands. Such implications are a useful consistency test, but do not reverse.

The maintained [Erdős #233](https://www.erdosproblems.com/233) records the conjecture, historical conditional results and OEIS A074741. Its sharp upper bound is not currently established.

## What remains

A bounded task sieves primes through a selected cutoff, accumulates exact squared gaps and verifies both the telescoping and Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities. Acceptance: reproduce 25 for the first four gaps; report $N$, the last prime, the squared sum and the ratio to $N(\log N)^2$. A finite trend cannot settle the asymptotic bound.
