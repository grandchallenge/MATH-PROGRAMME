## The question in plain language

Let c(d) be the smallest integer K such that for EVERY k≥K, the d-dimensional unit cube can be partitioned into exactly k positive-volume cubes homothetic to the original, with possibly unequal sizes. Erdős asked for sharp estimates, particularly whether c(d)≫d^d. The problem remains OPEN, and one must not require all pieces to have the SAME size: that would allow only special counts k=t^d.

## A small example

A square can be divided into 2×2 equal quarter squares (k=4). Replacing one of those squares by four squares of half its side yields k=7. These are exact admissible homothetic tilings and illustrate how replacing one cube by 2^d subcubes increases the piece count by 2^d−1. The maintained site gives c(2)=6, which cannot be inferred from just examples k=4 and k=7; it requires proving all k≥6 are possible and k=5 is not.

## What is known

Hadwiger obtained lower c(d)≥2^d+2^(d−1); Connor–Marmorino (2018) improved it to c(d)≥2^(d+1)−1 for d≥3. Burgess–Erdős gave c(d)≪d^(d+1); Connor–Marmorino upper c(d)≤e²d^d if d+1 is composite and ≤1.8d^(d+1) if d+1 prime. Neither upper and lower proves c(d)≫d^d. Hudelson provided conditional smaller bounds depending on gcd(2^d−1,3^d−1). Historic original paper and exact tiling boundary conventions not independently checked.
