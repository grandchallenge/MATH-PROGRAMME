## The question in plain language

How complicated must the odd cycles of a graph be when four colours are needed to colour its vertices, but it contains no complete graph on four vertices?

[Erdős Problem #1091](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1091) contains two questions. First, must a $K_4$‑free graph of chromatic number $4$ contain an odd cycle with **at least two diagonals**? A diagonal is an edge joining two nonconsecutive vertices on the chosen cycle.

The stronger question asks whether excluding all small four-chromatic subgraphs forces an odd cycle with an arbitrarily large number of diagonals.

## A small example

Take the five-vertex cycle $C_5$ and join a new central vertex to all five cycle vertices. This is the *pentagonal wheel*. Its outer odd cycle requires three colours and its hub needs a fourth; it contains no $K_4$.

Exactly six distinct cycles of length five occur, and direct edge enumeration shows every such cycle has at most **two** diagonals, with some achieving two. The example explains why replacing “two diagonals” with “three diagonals” fails as a universal assertion. It does not resolve the more demanding condition that every small subgraph be three-colourable.

## What is known

The original one-diagonal question was proved by Larson (1979). **Voss (1982) proved the stronger two-diagonal assertion.**

For the much stronger local-to-global question, the [maintained catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1091) reports a negative result attributed to an internal OpenAI model, constructing arbitrarily large four-chromatic critical graphs with at most ten diagonals on any cycle. This newer mathematical proof has **not** been independently audited by GCL, so the statement is recorded as a reported external result rather than a verified new theorem.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source return #2362](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2362#issuecomment-6166417887) distinguishes the affirmative first question from the reported negative second question, and checks the six-vertex wheel exactly.

The original Voss paper and complete recent construction remain pending primary-source proof review.

## What remains

Review Larson (1979), Voss (1982) and the reported recent construction, including their diagonal definitions, graph-criticality, local chromatic assumptions and proof of a uniform ten-diagonal bound. A finite pentagonal wheel is not evidence for the asymptotic local-chromatic claim.
