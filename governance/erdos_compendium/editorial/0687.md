## The question in plain language

For x≥2, choose a single residue class modulo every prime p≤x. Let Y(x) be the largest y for which every integer 1,…,y is in at least one chosen class. How large can Y(x) be? Erdős asked for Y(x)=o(x²) or even x^(1+o(1)); neither upper improvement is established in the maintained source. The choice of residues may depend on x; this is not a single fixed system covering all positive integers.

## A small example

When x=3, the primes are 2 and 3. The choices 1 modulo 2 and 2 modulo 3 cover 1,2,3, giving Y(3)≥3. No choice can cover the first four: if the modulo-2 class is even, the remaining odd points 1 and 3 are different modulo 3; if it is odd, the remaining 2 and 4 are different modulo 3. Thus Y(3)=3. This finite calculation is not an asymptotic bound.

## What is known

The maintained Erdős catalogue attributes Y(x) ≪ x² to Iwaniec [Iw78] and a lower bound Y(x) ≫ x·log x·logloglog x/loglog x to Ford, Green, Konyagin, Maynard and Tao [FGKMT18], with constants and asymptotic ranges dependent on their original theorems. Those original papers and the exact Jacobsthal equivalence have not been independently re-read in this tranche. The finite expectation of residue coverage is not enough to prove maximal interval coverage; it is a highly structured covering-sieve problem. A prize quoted by the historical source is not evidence of a solution or an award.
