## The question in plain language

Erdős and Szemerédi ask whether addition and multiplication cannot both remain highly compressible on a large finite set of integers. For $A=\{a_1,\dots,a_n\}$, write

$$
A+A=\{a+a':a,a'\in A\},\qquad AA=\{aa':a,a'\in A\}.
$$

The conjecture is that, for every $\varepsilon>0$,

$$
\max(|A+A|,|AA|)\gg_\varepsilon |A|^{2-\varepsilon}.
$$

The original paper uses positive integers in increasing order and counts $a_i+a_j$ and $a_i a_j$ with $i\le j$. Because the operations are commutative, this is exactly the same set-counting convention as the usual sumset/product-set notation.

## A small example

The formulation change from positive integers to arbitrary integers is harmless asymptotically. Let $A\subset\mathbb Z$ have $n>1$ elements. At least one of

$$
A\cap\mathbb Z_{>0},\qquad -\bigl(A\cap\mathbb Z_{<0}\bigr)
$$

has at least $(n-1)/2$ elements. Call that positive set $B$. Then $B+B$ is either a subset of $A+A$ or the negation of such a subset, and $BB\subseteq AA$. Hence

$$
\max(|A+A|,|AA|)\ge \max(|B+B|,|BB|).
$$

A concrete example is

$$
A_n=\{1,2,4,\dots,2^{n-1}\}.
$$

Its product set is

$$
A_nA_n=\{2^k:0\le k\le 2n-2\},
$$

so $|A_nA_n|=2n-1$. The off-diagonal sums $2^i+2^j$ have distinct binary representations with two $1$’s, while diagonal sums are powers of two. Therefore

$$
|A_n+A_n|=\binom n2+n=\frac{n(n+1)}2.
$$

This example verifies directly that strong multiplicative structure forces substantial additive growth; it is illustrative, not a proof of the conjecture.

## What is known

The 1983 paper defines $f(n)$ to be the largest integer guaranteed for every $n$‑element positive-integer set. Its Theorem 1 proves

$$
n^{1+c_1}<f(n)<n^2\exp\left(-c_2\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\right)
$$

for positive constants $c_1,c_2$.

The lower bound already proves genuine expansion: at least one of the two sets has more than a fixed power strictly larger than $n$. The upper bound shows that the conjectured near-quadratic guarantee was not a trivial consequence of the number of available pairs. Erdős and Szemerédi explicitly say that the near-quadratic claim was far beyond their proof.

They also formulate higher-fold analogues involving $k$‑fold sums and products. Those variants are outside the exact two-operation statement audited here and should not be conflated with Problem 52.

Cushman’s 2026 arXiv revision states, for every finite $A\subset\mathbb R$,

$$
\max(|A+A|,|AA|)\gg_\eta |A|^{\frac43+\frac{10}{4407}-\eta}.
$$

The exponent equals

$$
\frac43+\frac{10}{4407}=\frac{1962}{1469}\approx1.3356.
$$

This applies directly to integer sets because every integer set is a real set. It substantially improves the historical lower-bound exponent, but it does not approach the conjectured exponent $2-\varepsilon$. The source is a primary author preprint; its theorem statement is evidence for the interface, not an independently checked proof.

Chang’s primary paper gives a useful extreme case: if the product set is only linear in size, then the sumset is quadratic in size. This confirms the basic rigidity principle but leaves open the difficult intermediate regime where both sets may have superlinear yet subquadratic growth.

$$
\max(|A+A|,|AA|)\gg_\eta n^{\frac{1962}{1469}-\eta}.
$$

**Method.**

1. Record the exact theorem quantifiers and hypotheses from the v2 preprint.
