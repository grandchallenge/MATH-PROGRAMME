## The question in plain language

The word critical has two different mathematical meanings, one about hitting all hyperedges with a small set and another about losing chromatic number upon an edge deletion. The minimum-degree-seven question has opposite answers under these definitions.

The term 3-critical 3-uniform hypergraph has two non-equivalent meanings. Transversal-critical: minimum edge-hitting set has size 3 and drops to at most 2 after deleting any edge. Chromatic-critical: chromatic number 3, dropping to <=2 after removing any edge or vertex. The minimum degree >=7 example is IMPOSSIBLE for transversal-critical, but EXISTS for chromatic-critical.

## A small example

Complete 3-uniform K5 has ten triples, chromatic number 3 (2-coloring five vertices forces a monochromatic three-set); after deleting any edge, choose precisely that triple as one color class and remaining two vertices as other, so the remaining graph is 2-colorable. Deleting any vertex produces K4^3, also 2-colorable. Every original vertex lies in six triples, not seven. This tests chromatic-critical definition but is NOT Li's nine-vertex witness.

This finite calculation is not an asymptotic or all-parameter proof.

## What is known

Maintained /latex/834 read 2026-10-14. [R. Li (2025), original arXiv manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24850) *On an Erdős-Lovász problem: 3-critical 3-graphs of minimum degree 7* proves transversal critical 3-graphs contain a vertex of degree <=6, and gives a chromatic-critical 3-uniform hypergraph on nine vertices with minimum degree 7. Registry SOLVED is ambiguous unless paired with definition. The author's public abstract confirms both formal interpretations; the full nine-vertex incidence list and its edge-deletion certificate have not been independently reconstructed here. An externally reported proof should not be mistaken for a GCL theorem certificate.

The complete three-uniform hypergraph on five vertices is chromatic three-critical. Five vertices in two colors force a monochromatic triple; deleting any edge permits two-coloring by putting the three vertices of that edge in one class and the other two in the other class. Deleting a vertex leaves a two-colorable four-vertex complete three-graph. Its degree is only six. Li's nine-vertex degree-seven example is different and must be separately replayed. Under transversal criticality Li instead rules out minimum degree seven.
