## The question in plain language

The chromatic number $\chi(G)$ is the minimum number of colours required to colour a graph's vertices without a monochromatic edge. Its Ramsey number $R(G)$ is the least integer $N$ such that every two-colouring of the edges of $K_N$ contains a monochromatic copy of $G$.

Erdős #87 asks whether, for every $\epsilon>0$ and sufficiently large $k$,

$$
R(G)>(1-\epsilon)^k R(k)
$$

holds for **every** graph $G$ with $\chi(G)=k$, where $R(k)=R(K_k)$. The stronger conjecture asks whether $R(G)>cR(k)$ for a fixed positive $c$ independent of $k$. The order of these quantifiers is important.

## A small example

For $G=K_k$, equality $R(G)=R(k)$ holds. It does not follow that every graph with chromatic number $k$ has Ramsey number at least $R(k)$. Faudree and McKay calculated the pentagonal wheel $W$ has chromatic number four and $R(W)=17$, whereas $R(4)=18$. This refutes an older, stronger conjecture but not the revised large-parameter question.

For fixed $k=4$, the proposed inequality can tolerate a discrepancy such as $17/18$; an asymptotic claim is not decided by a single small value.

## What is known

Both revised formulations are **OPEN**. The historical source is Erdős's 1995 problem list cited on [Erdős #87](https://www.erdosproblems.com/87), which also records the Faudree–McKay finite result and Wigderson's observation that a probabilistic red-blue colouring yields $R(G)\gg2^{k/2}$ when $\chi(G)=k$.

Because the standard diagonal bound gives $R(k)\le4^k$, the displayed exponential comparison is automatic if $\epsilon\ge3/4$. This does not address the interesting arbitrarily small positive $\epsilon$ regime. The exact wheel computation and the original Erdős 1995 printed statement have not been independently page-checked here.

## GCL's current state

The pinned catalogue and maintained page (January 2026) classify this problem **OPEN**. The original source assignment is [#1356](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1356). This article distinguishes the failed strict inequality $R(G)\ge R(k)$ from both open weakened versions, without promoting secondary bibliographic remarks to independent source-proof certification.

The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1356#issuecomment-6158437755). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded verification task is to encode the small pentagonal-wheel example as a two-colour Ramsey SAT problem. Acceptance requires a two-colouring of $K_{16}$ without a monochromatic wheel, a complete certificate that every two-colouring of $K_{17}$ contains one, and a check that the wheel is four-chromatic. Stop at $k=4$ and make no inference about large $k$.
