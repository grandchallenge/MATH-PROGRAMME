**Editorial status:** UNACCEPTED_DRAFT. Erdős Problem #492 is **DISPROVED**. The original uniform-distribution conjecture was refuted by W. M. Schmidt in 1969; the full original counterexample construction has not been independently reconstructed in this audit. Positive special cases remain valid.

## The question in plain language

Normally, the fractional part $\{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor$ records where $x$ falls between consecutive integers. Consider a *different ruler*, whose tick marks are an arbitrary increasing sequence of positive integers

$$
A=\{a_1<a_2<a_3<\cdots\},\qquad
\frac{a_{i+1}}{a_i}\longrightarrow1.
$$

Within the interval $[a_i,a_{i+1})$, define the normalized position

$$
h_A(x)=\frac{x-a_i}{a_{i+1}-a_i},\qquad
a_i\le x<a_{i+1}.
$$

This lies in $[0,1)$ and resets to zero at each tick mark. The ratio assumption means consecutive tick marks become close *relative to their size*; it does not mean that their absolute spacing becomes constant or monotone.

Erdős, following a question of W. J. LeVeque, asked whether for **every** such fixed sequence $A$, and for **almost every** real number $\alpha>0$, the values

$$
h_A(\alpha),h_A(2\alpha),h_A(3\alpha),\ldots
$$

are **uniformly distributed in $[0,1)$**. Uniform distribution means that for each subinterval $[u,v)\subseteq[0,1)$,

$$
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N
\#\{1\le n\le N:h_A(\alpha n)\in[u,v)\}=v-u.
$$

The phrase *almost every* refers to **Lebesgue measure in $\alpha$**. The quantifiers must stay in this order: for each eligible $A$, ask whether the conclusion holds for almost all $\alpha$. The conjecture is **false in general**, as Schmidt proved. This does not mean that every choice of tick marks fails.

For finitely many initial $n$ where $\alpha n<a_1$, the function can be left undefined or assigned an arbitrary value without affecting asymptotic uniform distribution.

## A small example

Take every positive integer as a tick mark, $A=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}$. Then $a_i=i$, $a_{i+1}/a_i=(i+1)/i\to1$, and

$$
h_A(x)=\{x\}.
$$

For every **irrational** $\alpha$, the ordinary sequence $\{\alpha n\}$ is uniformly distributed, by the classical Weyl theorem. Since the rational numbers form a set of Lebesgue measure zero, this example satisfies the conjectured conclusion.

In fact, take **equally spaced tick marks** $a_i=di$ for a fixed positive integer $d$. Then

$$
h_A(x)=\left\{\frac{x}{d}\right\}
$$

for $x\ge d$. When $\alpha/d$ is irrational, the resulting sequence is uniformly distributed. This follows directly from the finite geometric sum

$$
\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N e^{2\pi i k\alpha n/d}
=\frac{e^{2\pi i k\alpha/d}(1-e^{2\pi i k\alpha N/d})}
{N(1-e^{2\pi i k\alpha/d})}
\longrightarrow0
$$

for each nonzero integer $k$; the denominator stays nonzero. Weyl's criterion gives the desired result.

This is a **complete positive special case**, not a contradiction of Schmidt's refutation. The failure occurs for more complicated tick-mark sequences satisfying the same asymptotic ratio condition.

## What is known

### Erdős and LeVeque's original question

In *Some unsolved problems* (1961), printed p.238, Problem I.31, Erdős attributes the problem to W. J. LeVeque and spells out the tick-mark interpolation and the uniform-distribution requirement. The original page was visually inspected. LeVeque's earlier paper, *On uniform distribution modulo a subdivision*, *Pacific Journal of Mathematics* **3** (1953), pp.757–771, gives special positive results. Its complete proof has not been reconstructed in this audit.

The maintained [Erdős #492](https://www.erdosproblems.com/492) records two subsequent positive theorems. Davenport and LeVeque (1963) establish equidistribution under a monotonicity condition on successive *gap lengths* $a_{i+1}-a_i$. Davenport and Erdős (1963) establish a further result under a quantitative growth hypothesis $a_n\gg n^{1/2+\varepsilon}$ for some $\varepsilon>0$, with the other source assumptions as in the original papers. These bibliographic and result summaries are catalogue-reported here: the complete original 1963 proof texts have **not** been independently read. They should not be merged into an unrestricted theorem.

### Schmidt's negative resolution

W. M. Schmidt, *Disproof of some conjectures on Diophantine approximations*, *Studia Scientiarum Mathematicarum Hungarica* **4** (1969), pp.137–144, is the resolution cited by the catalogue. The title, author, year and pages are corroborated in Schmidt's scholarly bibliography and in subsequent Erdős papers. The maintained catalogue classifies the original general claim as **DISPROVED**.

**Critical limit of this source audit:** Schmidt's complete 1969 article was not accessible through the material retrieved, so the actual exceptional tick-mark sequence, its limit properties and the measure-theoretic refutation have **not** been independently checked. This article accordingly states the established literature classification but does not fabricate an explicit counterexample or advertise GCL reproduction of the proof. A faithful mathematical replay of the negative result still requires Schmidt's original construction or a verified exact reprint.

Independently verify the measure-theoretic quantifiers and determine whether failure holds on a positive-measure set or under some stronger condition. Until that is done, the explicit counterexample remains an acknowledged source-verification gap.

Second, formalize the interpolation function with a precise half-open interval convention, prove the arithmetic-progression case using Weyl's criterion, and verify that changing finitely many initial terms does not affect asymptotic frequency. This is a bounded theorem suitable for automated checks, but it does not reverse a known refutation.
