## The question in plain language

For a fixed small graph $H$, the **extremal number** $\mathrm{ex}(n,H)$ is the maximum number of edges that a finite simple graph on $n$ vertices can contain while avoiding every copy of $H$.

Erdős asked about one specially constructed bipartite graph $H_k$. Take a central vertex $x$, connect it to $k$ vertices $y_1,\ldots,y_k$, and for **each unordered pair** of distinct indices $\{i,j\}$ introduce its own additional vertex $z_{\{i,j\}}$, joined to both $y_i$ and $y_j$. There are **no other edges**. For fixed $k\ge4$, must

$$
\mathrm{ex}(n,H_k)=O_k(n^{3/2})?
$$

This is [Erdős Problem #926](https://www.erdosproblems.com/926). The answer is **yes**: Füredi proved it in 1991. Alon, Krivelevich and Sudakov improved the dependence of the upper bound on $k$ in 2003. The power $3/2$ is sharp for fixed $k$.

## A small example

For $k=4$, the graph $H_4$ has the central vertex, four vertices of type $y$, and six vertices of type $z$:

$$
|V(H_4)|=1+4+\binom42=11,
\qquad
|E(H_4)|=4+2\binom42=16.
$$

For every pair $i<j$, the cycle

$$
x-y_i-z_{\{i,j\}}-y_j-x
$$

is a quadrilateral. Therefore $H_k$ contains a four-cycle for every $k\ge2$. Any graph with no four-cycle automatically avoids $H_k$.

This supplies an asymptotic **lower bound** of order $n^{3/2}$ through incidence graphs of finite projective planes. For a projective plane of prime order $q$, make a bipartite graph joining each point to the lines that contain it. It has

$$
2(q^2+q+1)\text{ vertices},\qquad
(q+1)(q^2+q+1)\text{ edges}.
$$

Two distinct points lie on exactly one common line, so this bipartite incidence graph contains **no** four-cycle. Its edge count grows like the three-halves power of its vertex count.

The exact finite verifier builds these graphs for $q=2,3,5,7$. Their respective vertex and edge counts are $(14,21)$, $(26,52)$, $(62,186)$ and $(114,456)$. They are examples illustrating the known matching lower exponent, not proofs of the deep upper theorem.

## What is known

Erdős posed the graph in [*Some unsolved problems in graph theory and combinatorial analysis*](https://www.erdosproblems.com/926), in *Combinatorial Mathematics and Its Applications* (1971), printed pp.103–104. The original uses a separate intermediate vertex for every pair among the $y$ vertices. This matters because the site's abbreviated phrase about a unique $z_i$ for every pair could be misread as allowing different pairs to share an intermediate vertex. The intended graph has precisely $\binom{k}{2}$ distinct vertices of type $z$.

Zoltán Füredi, [*On a Turán type problem of Erdős*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01375476), *Combinatorica* **11** (1991), pp.75–79, proved that if a graph has at least an appropriate multiple of $k^{3/2}n^{3/2}$ edges, it contains the prescribed graph. Füredi names it $L^k$ and explicitly identifies it with the **lowest three levels of the Boolean lattice** on $k$ symbols: the empty set, singleton sets, and two-element subsets, with edges joining sets that differ by one element. The peer-reviewed publisher abstract confirms this exact vertex-and-edge construction and the $k^{3/2}n^{3/2}$ threshold.

The stronger published result is Noga Alon, Michael Krivelevich and Benny Sudakov, [*Turán numbers of bipartite graphs and related Ramsey-type questions*](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0963548303005741), *Combinatorics, Probability and Computing* **12** (2003), pp.477–494. Their [author-hosted complete manuscript](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~krivelev/TuranRam.pdf), **Theorem 6.1**, establishes for generalized graphs $L_t^{k,s}$ that

$$
\mathrm{ex}(2n,L_t^{k,s})
\le
2^{1+1/t}(s+1)^{1/t}\,
k\,n^{2-1/t}.
$$

For $t=2$ and $s=1$, this generalized graph is exactly $H_k$. Substitution gives

$$
\mathrm{ex}(2n,H_k)\le4k n^{3/2}.
$$

Since the graph extremal function is monotone in the number of vertices, passing between even and arbitrary orders gives $\mathrm{ex}(N,H_k)=O(kN^{3/2})$. The improved factor is **linear in $k$**, rather than Füredi's older $k^{3/2}$ factor. GCL inspected the source's Theorem 6.1 and its probability-based proof pages.

The graph $H_k$ is **2-degenerate**: remove each degree-two pair vertex, then the remaining $y$ vertices each have degree one, and finally remove $x$. However the original Erdős question about **all** bipartite 2-degenerate graphs is broader; the known bound for this specific $H_k$ must not be promoted automatically to all such graphs. The related problem with the central vertex removed is [#1021](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1021).

The [maintained #926 catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/926) marks the result PROVED. A separate scholarly [source assessment](https://erdosproblems.ai/wiki/problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0926) reports a 2026 external Lean formalization link for Füredi's argument, but this GCL batch did not independently pin, build or axiom-audit that external code. The classical published mathematics is established without making a false local kernel-certification claim.

## GCL's current state

The definition of $H_k$ is now an exact finite graph specification. Its vertex set consists of one empty-set vertex, $k$ singleton vertices and one vertex for every unordered pair of singleton indices. Its edges are precisely the inclusions between consecutive levels of that three-level Boolean lattice. It therefore has

$$
1+k+\binom{k}{2}\text{ vertices},
\qquad k+2\binom{k}{2}=k^2\text{ edges}.
$$

The direct theorem substitution is independent of the probabilistic argument. In Theorem 6.1 of Alon–Krivelevich–Sudakov, take $t=2$ and $s=1$, then use the identification $L_2^{k,1}\cong H_k$. This gives the stated upper bound for an even number of vertices and its asymptotic for fixed $k$ consequence for all graph orders.

The executable certificate governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_926_927.py constructs $H_k$ for $3\le k\le8$. It verifies exact vertex and edge counts, degrees, the bipartition, the four-cycle witness and an explicit 2-degenerate elimination order. It also constructs four exact finite projective-plane incidence graphs for prime orders $2,3,5,7$, checks their regularity and that no two lines share more than one point, and hence verifies the absence of four-cycles. These are bounded graph checks. They neither reconstruct Füredi's full theorem nor AKS Theorem 6.1.

## What remains

The first formalizable task is the **graph identification**: prove that the stated $H_k$ is isomorphic to the bottom-three-level Boolean-lattice incidence graph $L_2^{k,1}$, with one distinct pair vertex for every unordered pair, and verify that it contains $C_4$ and is 2-degenerate. Acceptance requires an elementary mathematical proof or pinned no-placeholder Lean with an actual axiom report, and exact replay for $3\le k\le8$. A missing distinct pair vertex, an extra edge, or an incorrect count of $k^2$ edges falsifies the specification.

The next proof task is the **theorem specialization**, tracking the author's parameter $2n$ and the fixed variables $t=2,s=1$. Verify that the conclusion $\mathrm{ex}(2n,H_k)\le4kn^{3/2}$ transfers to all integer orders and that constants depend on $k$ as claimed. Full independent replication would require checking the random-common-neighborhood argument and the greedy distinct-pair-vertex assignment in the original 2003 proof, not just reading the theorem statement.

The exact sharp constant in the extremal problem and stronger uniform dependence on $k$ are separate refinements. The classical yes-or-no question is already proved. No GCL new extremal result or independent local formalization is asserted.
