## The question in plain language

Erdős asked for the largest subset $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ from which one cannot choose $k+1$ pairwise coprime integers. The natural candidate is the set of integers divisible by at least one of the first $k$ primes:

$$
E(N,k)=\{m\le N:\ p_i\mid m\text{ for some }i\le k\}.
$$

It cannot contain $k+1$ pairwise coprime integers: every selected number would need a different witness among the first $k$ primes. Erdős conjectured that this construction is always extremal.

The formulation appears explicitly in Erdős’s 1965 paper, §3, p. 183, and is equivalent to the registered problem after imposing $N\ge p_k$.

## A small example

Take $t=209$, so $k=t+3=212$, and list the relevant primes:

$$
1289,1291,1297,1301,1303,1307,1319,1321,1327,1361.
$$

Choose

$$
1{,}752{,}967\le N<1{,}754{,}329,
$$

because

$$
1321\cdot1327=1{,}752{,}967
<
1{,}754{,}329
=
1289\cdot1361.
$$

Define $B$ to be the integers at most $N$ divisible by one of $p_1,\ldots,p_{208}$, and define

$$
C=\{p_i p_j:209\le i<j\le217\}.
$$

There are

$$
|C|=\binom92=36
$$

such products, and the upper endpoint ensures they all lie below $N$.

Set $A=B\cup C$. This is the competing family.

A pairwise coprime subfamily of $B$ has size at most $208$: each member contains a prime among $p_1,\ldots,p_{208}$, and no such prime can divide two selected members.

Each member of $C$ is supported on two of the nine primes $p_{209},\ldots,p_{217}$. Pairwise coprime products must have disjoint supports, so at most

$$
\left\lfloor\frac92\right\rfloor=4
$$

members of $C$ can be selected pairwise coprimely.

Consequently every pairwise coprime subfamily of $A$ has size at most

$$
208+4=212.
$$

Thus $A$ satisfies the original forbidden-configuration condition for $k=212$.

The Erdős set contains $B$, plus the multiples of $p_{209},p_{210},p_{211},p_{212}$ that are not already in $B$.

Within the chosen interval, these additional elements are:

- the four primes;
- their four squares;
- $8+7+6+5$ admissible products with the later primes.

Hence the non-$B$ portion has size

$$
4+4+8+7+6+5=34.
$$

The competitor has $36$ elements in its corresponding exceptional part. Therefore

$$
|A|=|B|+36
>
|B|+34
=
|E(N,212)|.
$$

So the universal conjecture is false, and in fact

$$
f(N,212)\ge |E(N,212)|+2.
$$

## What is known

The 1994 paper of Ahlswede and Khachatrian introduces

$$
f(n,k,s)
$$

as the largest size of a subset of integers avoiding $k+1$ pairwise coprime members. For $s=1$, their $E(n,k,1)$ is exactly the multiples of the first $k$ primes.

They identify this as Erdős’s conjecture and state it for all $n,k\in\mathbb N$. Thus the later catalogue condition $N\ge p_k$ is a nontrivial-domain normalization, not a change to the extremal mechanism.

The direct 1962 Hungarian source is bibliographically identified as *Remarks on Number Theory IV: Extremal Problems in Number Theory I*, Mat. Lapok 13 (1962), 228–255. The English 1965 exposition is the directly inspected primary statement source.

The 1995 Ahlswede–Khachatrian paper proves a complementary result: for every fixed $k$, there is a threshold $n(k)$ such that for all $N>n(k)$, the Erdős set is uniquely optimal. This does not repair the original universal conjecture; it says that the counterexamples occur only below a $k$-dependent threshold.

The distinction is essential:

$$
\text{universal claim: false},
$$

while

$$
\forall k\ \exists n(k)\ \forall N>n(k),\quad f(N,k)=|E(N,k)
$$

is proved in the primary 1995 source.

A bounded formalization should first encode the explicit $k=212$ construction. The key reusable ingredients are the support-packing argument for products of two primes and the finite decomposition of $E(N,212)$.
