## The question in plain language

A sieve with bounded reciprocal weight may leave many integers uncovered, but its formulation must exclude 1. The problem has different answers in the ranges C below, equal to, and above 1.

Given A subset [1,x] with sum_{a in A} 1/a<=C, ask for a polynomial-logarithmic lower bound on count of m<=x divisible by NO a. Literal input permits 1, and A={1} kills every m for C>=1. The mathematically intended nontrivial question therefore REQUIRES a>=2, not a silent historical substitution.

## A small example

For x=12 and A={2,3}, the uncovered integers are 1,5,7,11. Conversely A={1} is admissible under the literal wording at C=1 and leaves zero survivors. The discrepancy is substantive.

Finite exact example: x=12 and A={2,3} with harmonic mass 5/6. Uncovered {1,5,7,11}, four out of 12. With A={1} mass=1 and zero uncovered. Union bound via counting at most floor(x/a) multiples proves the corrected lower bound x(1-C) for C<1.

## What is known

Maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/784, SOLVED, explicitly highlights the 1 loophole and defines corrected H_C(x) over A subset {2,...,floor x}. Reported: 0<C<1 union bound H_C(x)>=(1-C)x, C=1 H_1(x) asymp x/log x (Ruzsa lower, Saias upper), and fixed C>1 Weingartner [We25] H_C(x) asymp x^{exp(1-C)}/log x. These force polynomial-log conjecture false for C>1 but true for 0<C<=1.
