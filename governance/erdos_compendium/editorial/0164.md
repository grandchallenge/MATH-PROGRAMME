## The question in plain language

A set $A$ of integers **greater than one** is *primitive* when no distinct element of $A$ divides another. Erdős asked whether, among all finite or infinite primitive sets, the weighted reciprocal sum

$$
f(A)=\sum_{a\in A}\frac1{a\log a}
$$

is largest when $A$ is the set of all primes. The logarithm is the natural logarithm. The exclusion of one is necessary because $\log1=0$.

This is a global extremal inequality for arbitrary primitive sets, not a statement that primes give the largest summand one element at a time.

## A small example

The set $A=\{4,9\}$ is primitive because neither divides the other, but the set $\{2,4\}$ is not, as $2$ divides $4$. The prime set is primitive by unique factorization. Although individual composite terms may be smaller than nearby prime terms, infinitely many composite numbers could in principle compete in aggregate; that is why the theorem is nontrivial.

Erdős proved in 1935 that $f(A)$ converges for any primitive set and is uniformly bounded. Convergence alone does not identify the maximizing set.

## What is known

**PROVED.** Jared Duker Lichtman, [*A proof of the Erdős primitive set conjecture*](https://doi.org/10.1017/fmp.2023.16), *Forum of Mathematics, Pi* 11 (2023), e18, proved

$$
f(A)\le\sum_{\substack{p\text{ prime}}}\frac1{p\log p}
$$

for every primitive set of integers greater than one. The primary Cambridge publisher article supplies the precise hypotheses, and its arXiv version is [arXiv:2202.02384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.02384).

A later, alternative proof by Alexeev, Barreto, Li, Lichtman, Price, Shah, Tang and Tao is cited by the [maintained Erdős #164](https://www.erdosproblems.com/164), updated May 2026. The maintained site reports Lean verification, but GCL has **not** independently replayed that third-party formal project and cannot equate a reported Lean proof with protected GCL kernel admission. The 2023 published proof already settles the original conjecture independently of the newer route.
