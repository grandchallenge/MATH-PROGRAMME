## The question in plain language

Choose the least integer $f(k)$ so that every red/blue colouring of the integers from 1 to $f(k)$ contains a monochromatic **descending wave** of length $k$: an increasing sequence whose consecutive gaps never increase.

Brown, Erdős and Freedman proposed the \quadratic formula $f(k)=k^2-k+1$. This exact conjecture is **false**. Alon and Spencer proved in 1989 that the correct growth rate has cubic rather than \quadratic order.

## A small example

The increasing sequence $1,3,4$ has gaps 2 and 1, so it is a descending wave. Reflect the surrounding interval $[5]$ by the map $x\mapsto 6-x$ and then write the transformed entries in increasing order. The image is $2,3,5$, whose gaps are 1 and 2: an **ascending** wave.

The reflection preserves the two colours, although it reverses the sequence order. Consequently, every descending wave in one colouring corresponds to an ascending wave in the reflected colouring of the same interval, and conversely. This simple reversible mapping matters because the original 1989 paper is about *ascending*, not descending, waves.

## What is known

A sequence

$$

x_1<x_2<\cdots <x_k

$$

is descending when

$$

x_j-x_{j-1}\geq x_{j+1}-x_j
\quad (2\leq j<k).

$$

Equivalently, $2x_j\geq x_{j-1}+x_{j+1}$. The conjectured exact formula was $k^2-k+1$, and Brown, Erdős and Freedman established a lower bound of that order together with a cubic upper bound in [*Quasi-progressions and descending waves*](https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/781), *JCTA* (1990), 81–95.

N. Alon and J. Spencer, [*Ascending waves*](https://www.cs.tau.ac.il/~nogaa/PDFS/Publications1/Ascending%20waves.pdf), *Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series A* **52** (1989), 275–287, prove for their ascending-wave threshold positive constants $c_1,c_2$ with

$$

c_1k^3\leq f(k)\leq c_2k^3.

$$

This is the article's original published abstract theorem, not merely a later catalogue paraphrase. Reflection of the interval proves that the ascending and descending 2-colouring thresholds are **identical**. Thus the cubic growth carries over without adjusting asymptotic constants, even though the direction of the gap inequalities changes.

The lower cubic bound for arbitrarily large $k$ contradicts the proposed universal \quadratic identity. It does not assert any exact value of $f(k)$ for a particular fixed $k$ without additional calculation.
