## The question in plain language

Take any $n$ distinct points in the plane. Draw an edge between two points if they are exactly one unit apart. How many points can we always select so that no selected pair is unit distance apart?

Write $f(n)$ for the **smallest possible** size of a largest such selection, over every possible configuration of $n$ points. [Erdős Problem #1070](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1070) asks for a good estimate of $f(n)$ and, specifically, whether

$$
f(n)\ge\frac n4
$$

for every $n$.

Unlike [Problem #1066](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1066), there is no minimum-separation requirement on the original points. This is an essential difference.

## A small example

Three points at the corners of a unit equilateral triangle form a graph $K_3$. Every two vertices are adjacent, so a largest independent set has just one vertex. Every three-point graph has at least one independent vertex; hence $f(3)=1$.

The celebrated seven-vertex *Moser spindle* has independence number two, so it gives $f(7)\le2$, an upper ratio $2/7$. Neither example goes below one quarter.

To **disprove** the proposed universal one-quarter lower bound, just one realizable finite unit-distance graph with independence ratio strictly below $1/4$ would suffice.

## What is known

The maintained catalogue still labels #1070 **open** and records Croft's measurable lower bound $f(n)\ge0.22936n$ and subsequent asymptotic upper constructions.

There is a significant later development: on June 26, 2026, **Ákos Dúcz and Dániel Varga** posted [*A unit-distance graph in the plane with independence ratio below 1/4*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28157). Its abstract expressly claims a finite graph with independence ratio strictly less than $1/4$, building on geometric fractional chromatic-number techniques.

If the paper's construction and proof survive independent verification, it refutes the particular one-quarter conjecture. However, even such a counterexample would not determine the exact asymptotic value of $f(n)/n$.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source audit #2341](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2341#issuecomment-6157264288) flags a **status conflict** between the older catalogue badge and the newer author preprint. It checks the exact distinction between #1070 and the separated-point problem #1066, and proves the elementary $f(3)=1$ example.

The newer proof is an *external research claim*, not a newly obtained GCL mathematical result. GCL has not checked its enormous finite unit-distance construction or every lemma in its fractional-colouring argument.

## What remains

The immediate mathematical-editorial task is to verify the Dúcz–Varga proof, including the existence of a genuine finite Euclidean unit-distance graph, the independence bound and all analytic or limiting steps used to infer it. A verified counterexample should update the quarter-bound status without falsely declaring the whole extremal function determined.
