## The question in plain language

Arrange $n$ distinct points in $d$-dimensional Euclidean space. How few different positive distances can occur between pairs?

Write $f_d(n)$ for the minimum number among all such configurations. [Erdős Problem #1083](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1083) asks, for each fixed $d\ge3$, whether the correct order is

$$
f_d(n)=n^{2/d-o(1)}.
$$

This differs from nearby [Problem #1084](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1084), which counts the **maximum number of contacts** after forbidding distances below one.

## A small example

Choose a $q\times q\times q$ grid in three dimensions. Its $n=q^3$ points have integer squared distances between $1$ and $3(q-1)^2$. Therefore at most $3(q-1)^2$ distinct positive distances can occur.

For $q=2$, eight cube corners yield precisely three squared-distance types $\{1,2,3\}$. For $q=3$, the 27 points yield precisely nine types

$$
\{1,2,3,4,5,6,8,9,12\}.
$$

More generally, a $d$-dimensional $q$-grid has $q^d$ points and at most $d(q-1)^2$ distinct distances. It provides the familiar upper construction of order $n^{2/d}$, not a matching lower bound.

## What is known

**The conjectural sharp exponent remains open.** Erdős established the elementary bounds of order between $n^{1/d}$ and $n^{2/d}$. The maintained catalogue credits Solymosi and Vu (2008) with a $3$-dimensional lower bound proportional to $n^{3/5}$ and, for $d\ge4$, a lower exponent of the form $2/d-c/d^2$.

The catalogue also explains a strengthening of the three-dimensional statement obtained by combining the method with the planar Guth–Katz distinct-distances theorem. The precise theorem hypotheses and dependence on fixed $d$ need primary-paper review.
