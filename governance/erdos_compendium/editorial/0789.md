## The question in plain language

This is a length-rigidity problem for additive equalities: can a large subset ensure that equal sums use the same number of terms? Repetition is an essential unresolved interpretation in the compact formulation.

Given finite A subset Z with |A|=n, maximize h(n) universally guaranteeing B subset A such that an equality of sums of r and s elements of B implies r=s. The printed catalogue does not explicitly require all summands to be distinct, but its stated bounds h(n)>> (n log n)^{1/3} are incompatible with UNRESTRICTED REPETITION: any distinct positive u,v in B give v copies of u = u copies of v (after dividing by gcd as needed), with unequal lengths. Even B={0} fails (0=0+0). This is a substantive source-normalization hold, not a new disproof of intended problem.

## A small example

With unrestricted repetition, the set {2,3} fails because 2+2+2=3+3, although the distinct-subset convention does not have this collision. This single example exposes the necessary source-fidelity distinction.

Finite exact counterfixture: B={2,3}, 2+2+2=3+3 has r=3 vs s=2; B={1,2,3} has 1+2=3 if distinct terms are allowed but different cardinalities. For subsets with at most one use of each member on each side, {2,3} has no distinct-cardinality equality between its different subsets, illustrating the semantic split. In Z, negative and zero elements create further exceptions.
