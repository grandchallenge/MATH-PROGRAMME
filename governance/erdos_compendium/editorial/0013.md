## The question in plain language

How large can a set $A$ of integers between 1 and $N$ be if no **smaller** member divides the sum of two **larger** members? More precisely, whenever $a,b,c\in A$ and $a<\min(b,c)$, we require $a$ **not** to divide $b+c$. The two larger summands may be equal.

[Erdős Problem #13](https://www.erdosproblems.com/13), posed by Erdős and Sárközy, conjectured an upper bound of $N/3+O(1)$. The conjecture was **proved** by Benjamin Bedert in 2023. A separate problem allowing sums of $r$ larger elements remains an open generalization.

## A small example

Choose

$$
A=\{\lfloor 2N/3\rfloor+1,\ldots,N\}.
$$

This contains $N-\lfloor2N/3\rfloor$ integers, about one third of the interval. If $a,b,c\in A$ and $a<b,c$, then

$$
2a<b+c\le2N<3a.
$$

There is no integer multiple of $a$ strictly between $2a$ and $3a$, so $a$ cannot divide $b+c$. For $N=9$, the set $\{7,8,9\}$ is one concrete example. This proves that **one third is achievable**, not that no other construction can be larger.

## What is known

Bedert, *On a problem of Erdős and Sárközy about sequences with no term dividing the sum of two larger terms*, arXiv:2301.07065 (2023), proves the stronger estimate

$$
|A|\le \lfloor N/3\rfloor+1
$$

for every sufficiently large $N$. Together with the tail construction above, this determines the asymptotic leading constant. The detailed published argument was not independently reproduced for this article.

## What remains

The **original two-larger-summand question is resolved**. Remaining tasks involve formal verification of the published upper theorem and separate study of the generalized sum-of-multiple-elements question. Neither is a reason to label the historical #13 statement open again.
