## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1185](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1185) is **DISPROVED** in the maintained catalogue.

Fix $\delta>0$ and $k\ge3$. Erdős–Mauldin asked whether some **$m=m(\delta,k)$** guarantees for every sufficiently large $N$, whenever $A,B\subseteq[1,N]$ have $|A|\ge\delta N$ and $|B|\ge m$, there is a nontrivial $k$-term AP in $A$ whose positive common difference belongs to $B-B$. The phrase $|B|\ge m$ is essential; a failure for one $B$ of size two cannot refute the theorem for arbitrary large $m$.

## A small example

For $N=20$, let $A$ contain all ten odd integers and $B=\{1,4\}$. Then the only positive difference in $B-B$ is 3, but all three-term APs lying among odd numbers have **even** step. The finite obstruction is exact, but $|B|=2$ fails to address arbitrarily large $m$ and is **not** a standalone proof of the published counterexample.

## What is known

The catalogue https://www.erdosproblems.com/1185 reports **DISPROVED already for $k=3$**, via Furstenberg [Fu81]'s construction of an infinite $B$ whose difference set is not $2$-intersective. A $2$-intersective set forces a 3-AP difference in any positive-density set. The full passage from an infinite nonintersectivity example to the exact uniform finite-$N$ quantifiers requires primary-source review; the site attributes the original motivation to Erdős–Mauldin [Er80,p.92].
