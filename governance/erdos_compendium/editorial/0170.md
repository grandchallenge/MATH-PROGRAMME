## The question in plain language

A *sparse ruler* is a set $A\subseteq\{0,\ldots,N\}$ of marks such that **every distance** $0,1,\ldots,N$ occurs as a difference of two marks. Write $F(N)$ for the smallest number of marks:

$$
F(N)=\min\{|A|:\ A\subseteq\{0,\ldots,N\},\ \{0,\ldots,N\}\subseteq A-A\}.
$$

Erdős and Gál asked about the normalized minimum $F(N)/\sqrt N$. The limit is known to exist; Erdős #170 asks for **its exact value**.

## A small example

Take $N=5$ and $A=\{0,1,3,5\}$. The positive differences are $1,2,3,4,5$: for example, $5-1=4$ and $3-1=2$. Thus $F(5)\le4$. Counting the $\binom{|A|}{2}$ unordered pairs gives the general bound $\binom{|A|}{2}\ge N$, and hence $F(N)\ge(1+\sqrt{1+8N})/2$.

This elementary lower bound yields limiting constant at least $\sqrt2$, weaker than the best known constant. A ruler with repeated distances is permitted: every required distance need occur **at least once**, not exactly once.

## What is known

The existence of

$$
L=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{F(N)}{\sqrt N}
$$

was established by Erdős and Gál (1948). The precise constant is still **OPEN**. The maintained [Erdős #170](https://www.erdosproblems.com/170) gives the interval

$$
1.56\le L\le\sqrt3.
$$

Leech (1956) supplied the lower bound; Wichmann (1963) supplied the upper. Computational work by Pegg (2020) suggests that the upper value $\sqrt3$ may be exact, but computations on a finite collection of rulers do not prove an asymptotic extremal constant.

The related problem where the marks are not required to lie in $\{0,\ldots,N\}$ is different and should not be substituted for the protected statement.

## What remains

A bounded successor enumerates minimum rulers through $N=12$ by exact bitmask difference coverage, checks the witness for $N=5$, and derives the pair-count lower bound. Acceptance: an exact independent difference-set checker and witnesses for each optimum. Stop without extrapolating numerical ratios to $L$ or claiming to improve the $1.56$ and $\sqrt3$ bounds.
