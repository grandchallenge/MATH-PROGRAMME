## The question in plain language

For a fixed graph $H$, the Turán number $\mathrm{ex}(n,H)$ is the largest possible number of edges in an $n$ vertex graph without a copy of $H$. Erdős and Simonovits proposed that, for a **bipartite** graph $H$, the estimate

$$
\mathrm{ex}(n,H)=O_H(n^{3/2})
$$

holds **if and only if** $H$ is *2-degenerate*: every nonempty subgraph of $H$ has a vertex of degree at most two.

The adjective *2-degenerate* is stronger than merely having average degree below three; the definition applies to **all** subgraphs, not just $H$ itself.

## A small example

Every tree is 1-degenerate: any nonempty subgraph with an edge has a leaf, while edgeless subgraphs have isolated vertices. The complete bipartite graph $K_{3,3}$ is **not** 2-degenerate because its minimum degree equals three. This makes 3-regular bipartite graphs natural candidates for testing the proposed reverse implication.

However, finding one dense $H$ free graph is not the same as proving an upper bound on $\mathrm{ex}(n,H)$ for all $n$. A counterexample to the conjecture must violate the implication from the *upper-bound property* back to degeneracy.

## What is known

**DISPROVED.** Oliver Janzer's [*Disproof of a Conjecture of Erdős and Simonovits on the Turán Number of Graphs with Minimum Degree 3*](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnac076), published in *International Mathematics Research Notices* 2023, 8478–8494, constructs for **every** $\varepsilon>0$ a 3-regular bipartite graph $H$ satisfying

$$
\mathrm{ex}(n,H)=O_H(n^{4/3+\varepsilon}).
$$

Choose, for example, $\varepsilon=1/12$. Then $4/3+1/12=17/12<3/2$; thus the constructed non-2-degenerate $H$ nevertheless has $\mathrm{ex}(n,H)=O(n^{3/2})$. This directly refutes the “only if” half of the conjecture. The primary publisher abstract and [arXiv:2109.06110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06110) support the precise quantifiers.

## What remains

Formalize the elementary contradiction given a theorem supplying a 3-regular bipartite $H$ with an $n^{4/3+\varepsilon}$ upper estimate for any fixed $\varepsilon>0$. Verify mechanically that a 3-regular graph has a subgraph of minimum degree three, and that $n^{17/12}\le n^{3/2}$ for $n\ge1$. Acceptance: quantified contradiction to the reverse implication; the deep Janzer construction remains an explicitly imported source theorem.
