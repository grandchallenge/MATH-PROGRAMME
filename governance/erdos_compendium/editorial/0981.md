# Erdős #981 — Average eventual thresholds for quadratic character sums

## The question and the quantifiers

For an **odd prime** $p$ let $\chi_p(n)=(n/p)$ denote the Legendre symbol, with value $0$ when $p\mid n$, $1$ for nonzero squares modulo $p$, and $-1$ for nonsquares. Write

$$
S_N(p)=\sum_{n=1}^N\chi_p(n).
$$

For each real $\varepsilon>0$, define the **eventual one-sided threshold**

$$
F_\varepsilon(p)=
\min\{m\ge1:\ S_N(p)<\varepsilon N
\text{ for every integer }N\ge m\}.
$$

Erdős asked whether, for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, there is a constant $c_\varepsilon>0$ such that

$$
\sum_{\substack{p<x\\p\text{ odd prime}}}
F_\varepsilon(p)\sim c_\varepsilon\frac{x}{\log x}.
$$

**The catalogue status is PROVED**, crediting P. D. T. A. Elliott (1969). But the exact theorem printed in Elliott's paper concerns a **related two-sided eventual threshold**; the paper states that its method also proves the original one-sided version, without printing the adapted argument. That distinction is an important verification obligation, not grounds to relabel an established literature result open. The maintained [problem #981](https://www.erdosproblems.com/981) was corrected in December 2025 to distinguish the intended eventual-time question from a separate first-passage variant.

## Three different thresholds

Keep separate the following quantities, all for fixed odd $p$ and fixed $\varepsilon>0$:

- **First-passage time:** $H_\varepsilon(p)=\min\{m\ge1:S_m(p)<\varepsilon m\}$.
- **Eventual one-sided time (Erdős):** $F_\varepsilon(p)$ as above, requiring the inequality at **every later** index.
- **Eventual two-sided time (Elliott's displayed theorem):**

$$
G_\varepsilon(p)=
\min\{m\ge1:\ |S_N(p)|<\varepsilon N
\text{ for every integer }N\ge m\}.
$$

The definitions imply the elementary inequalities

$$
H_\varepsilon(p)\le F_\varepsilon(p)\le G_\varepsilon(p).
$$

**They need not be equal.** In particular, an asymptotic mean for $H_\varepsilon$ does not automatically give the same asymptotic for $F_\varepsilon$. Likewise $F_\varepsilon\le G_\varepsilon$ alone does not prove that their mean constants agree, or even that both means converge. The proof must control the appropriate *eventual* tail.

## Two exact finite examples

For $p=7$, the first six Legendre symbols are

$$
\chi_7(1),\ldots,\chi_7(6)=(1,1,-1,1,-1,-1),
$$

and the partial sums are $(1,2,1,2,1,0)$. With $\varepsilon=1/2$, the inequality $S_N<N/2$ first holds at $N=3$, but **fails again at $N=4$**, because $S_4=2=N/2$. It then holds for every $N\ge5$ (periodicity bounds all later sums). Thus

$$
H_{1/2}(7)=3,\qquad
F_{1/2}(7)=5.
$$

For $p=5$, the first four symbols are $(1,-1,-1,1)$, with sums $(1,0,-1,0)$. With $\varepsilon=1/5$, the one-sided inequality holds for every $N\ge2$, but the **absolute-value** inequality fails at $N=3$, since $|S_3|=1\ge3/5$. Hence

$$
F_{1/5}(5)=2,\qquad G_{1/5}(5)=4.
$$

These are **exact counterexamples to the equivalence of the three definitions**, not counterexamples to their asymptotic theorems. They can be recomputed with modular exponentiation and rational arithmetic, without floating-point tolerances.

## Why each eventual threshold is finite

The Legendre symbol is periodic modulo $p$ and has zero sum over a complete residue system:

$$
\sum_{a=1}^{p}\chi_p(a)=0.
$$

Consequently $S_{N+p}(p)=S_N(p)$ for every positive integer $N$. Since all partial sums lie between $-(p-1)$ and $p-1$, eventually

$$
|S_N(p)|<\varepsilon N.
$$

In particular the sets defining $F_\varepsilon$ and $G_\varepsilon$ are nonempty and all three thresholds are finite. For example, $G_\varepsilon(p)\le\lfloor p/\varepsilon\rfloor+1$ is a coarse explicit bound.

This pointwise bound is **not uniform enough for the desired average as $p\to\infty$**. Its size grows linearly with $p$, and summing it over prime moduli would give a much larger upper estimate than $x/\log x$. The mean theorem needs averaged cancellation across characters.

Two boundary cases are elementary. If $\varepsilon>1$, then $S_N(p)\le N<\varepsilon N$ for all $N\ge1$, hence $F_\varepsilon(p)=1$ for every odd prime and $c_\varepsilon=1$. If $\varepsilon=1$, the first quadratic nonresidue causes $S_N(p)<N$ and this strict inequality persists for every subsequent $N$. Thus

$$
F_1(p)=n_2(p),
$$

where $n_2(p)$ is the least quadratic nonresidue. The $\varepsilon=1$ assertion is exactly the quadratic mean-value result of [problem #980](https://www.erdosproblems.com/980), not an additional unresolved conjecture. The interesting analytic regime is $0<\varepsilon<1$.

## Elliott's actual 1969 theorem

P. D. T. A. Elliott, [*A conjecture of Erdős concerning character sums*](https://doi.org/10.1016/1385-7258(69)90006-7), *Proceedings of the Koninklijke Nederlandse Akademie van Wetenschappen, Series A* **72** (1969), pp.164–171 (also *Indagationes Mathematicae* **31**), states on printed p.165:

$$
\frac{1}{\pi(x)}
\sum_{\substack{p\le x\\p\text{ odd}}}G_\varepsilon(p)
\longrightarrow c_G(\varepsilon)
\qquad(0<\varepsilon\le1).
$$

The actual author defines $G$ using the **absolute value** in the introductory paragraph on p.164. Immediately before his theorem, he reproduces Erdős's **one-sided** definition and says that *simple changes in the argument* prove a similar result for that earlier definition. The adaptation is **reported by the source author, not written out** in full in the paper. The community catalogue and Tang–Zhang 2026 explicitly credit Elliott with proving the original one-sided conjecture. Thus **PROVED** has established literature provenance, while the detailed one-sided proof is a bounded task for independent verification.

The mean can be expressed using the limiting distribution of the threshold, formally as

$$
\mathbb E(G_\varepsilon)
=\sum_{m\ge0}\Pr(G_\varepsilon>m).
$$

For each **fixed** cutoff $m$, the event is determined by finitely many Legendre symbols and a limiting density can be obtained from quadratic reciprocity and primes in residue classes. But one cannot interchange the infinite tail sum and the limit over primes without a **uniform tail estimate**. Elliott's proof employs character-sum moments, sieve bounds and the distribution of primes in suitable congruence classes to secure that missing uniformity. Replaying those estimates is the mathematical center of the independent audit. The limit constant is positive because every threshold is at least one.

## Why Tang and Zhang's theorem is separate

Quanyu Tang and Hao Zhang, [*Average first-passage times for character sums*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24631), arXiv:2512.24631v2 (18 January 2026), prove the asymptotic for

$$
H_\varepsilon(p)=\min\{m:S_m(p)<\varepsilon m\}.
$$

That is a genuine **first-passage** theorem and gives a valid result for a different random variable. Their paper was prompted by the website's earlier misstatement of the problem as first-passage. The page was then corrected to Erdős's actual **eventual-time** requirement. The two results are compatible, but they must **not** be counted as two independent proofs of the same quantified assertion. The $p=7$ example already proves the definitions differ.

There is an additional transcription error in the old website commentary, which at one point printed an ordinary binomial coefficient where the **Legendre symbol** was intended. The canonical source now uses $(n/p)$, and a formal statement must use a quadratic character, not binomial coefficients.
