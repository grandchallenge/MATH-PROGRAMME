## The question in plain language

The maintained Erdős #707 asks whether every finite Sidon set embeds into a perfect difference set modulo p²+p+1 for some PRIME p. Sidon means all unordered two-term sums (including repetitions) are different. The answer is DISPROVED. Historical Hall 1947 and Alexeev–Mixon 2025 supply five-point examples, and an externally published Lean formalization has been reported, but GCL has not audited its proof dependencies.

## A small example

An elementary positive control is {1,2,4} modulo 7. Its six ordered differences between distinct members are 1,2,3,4,5,6 modulo 7 in some order, exactly once each, so this small Sidon set already forms a perfect difference set. The negative example {1,2,4,8,13} is a Sidon set with all 15 unordered sums distinct; Alexeev–Mixon prove that it cannot be extended to any cyclic perfect difference set.

## What is known

Primary author abstract: Boris Alexeev and Dustin G. Mixon, 'Forbidden Sidon subsets of perfect difference sets, featuring a human-assisted proof', arXiv:2510.19804 (2025). They also describe earlier Hall 1947 nonextension of {1,3,9,10,13}. Directly checked GCL-pinned Formal Conjectures 707.lean at commit 85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020. Its parent theorem concerns ANY positive modulus, a stronger claim than the particular p²+p+1 modulus, with separate prime and prime-power variants. Main negative theorem and variants have `by sorry` placeholders in the GCL-pinned snapshot. An external Lean project is linked, but its proof and axioms were not independently replayed. The exact relationship among allowed moduli must remain explicit.
