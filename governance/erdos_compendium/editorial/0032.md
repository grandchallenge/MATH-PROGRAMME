## The question in plain language

An *additive complement* to the primes is a set $A$ such that **every sufficiently large integer** can be written $n=p+a$ with $p$ prime and $a\in A$. [Erdős Problem #32](https://www.erdosproblems.com/32) asks how sparse such an $A$ can be. In particular, can the number of its elements up to $N$ grow more slowly than $(\log N)^2$ while still covering all sufficiently large integers? A separate historical bound asks for something on the scale of $\log N$.

A statement that almost every integer has a representation is weaker than the eventual **every-integer** requirement.

## A small example

A **finite** set of shifts can never work. For instance take $A=\{1,2\}$. The number $n=11$ is not of the form prime plus one of these two shifts: $11-1=10$ and $11-2=9$ are composite. In fact there are arbitrarily large such exceptions.

For any finite list $A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_k\}$, choose distinct auxiliary primes $q_1,\ldots,q_k$. The Chinese remainder theorem gives arbitrarily large integers $n$ satisfying

$$
n\equiv a_i\pmod{q_i}\quad(1\le i\le k).
$$

Taking $n$ sufficiently large makes every positive difference $n-a_i$ a **proper multiple** of $q_i$, hence composite. So any true additive complement must be infinite. This necessary condition says nothing about the optimal sparseness of infinite complements.

## What is known

The maintained record distinguishes the unresolved sparse-complement questions from a separate published density-related result associated with Ruzsa. Their quantifiers must not be exchanged. The existing [source audit](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1301#issuecomment-6104178501) does not claim to have independently verified the historical primary paper's exact pages, so its reported source outcomes are attributed rather than promoted.

## GCL's current state

[GCL audit #1301](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1301#issuecomment-6104178501) confirms that the pinned formal predicate uses eventual existence of a prime-plus-shift representation, and derives the finite-complement obstruction above using elementary modular arithmetic. It does not give an example of an infinite complement satisfying the hoped-for sparse bounds or a kernel-checked proof of the conjecture.

## What remains

Build an infinite set with the required eventual coverage and exceptionally slow counting growth, or establish a matching obstruction. The Chinese remainder theorem shows why finite sets fail but leaves the logarithmic versus squared-logarithmic scale unresolved.
