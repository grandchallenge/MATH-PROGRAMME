## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1147](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1147) concerns the following mathematical question and its exact quantifiers:

$$
A_\alpha=\left\{n\ge2:\|\alpha n^2\|<\frac1{\log n}\right\}.
$$

For irrational alpha>0 define A_alpha={n>=2: distance(alpha*n²,Z)<1/log n}. Is A_alpha an asymptotic additive basis of ORDER TWO, i.e. do ALL sufficiently large integers belong to A_alpha+A_alpha? Formula at n=1 has log 1=0 and requires a finite-prefix convention. Strict inequality matters only for boundary equality; preserve it in source.

## A small example

For alpha=sqrt(2), high-precision decimal calculation of n=2..500 includes 211 qualifying members; sums of two members from this TRUNCATED set miss 23 targets in 4..1000, e.g. 781. Those missing sums in bounded ranges are NOT proof of failure as an asymptotic basis, which requires arbitrarily large absent targets; published result is external.

## What is known

https://www.erdosproblems.com/1147 DISPROVED. Konieczny [Ko18] *Sets of recurrence as bases for the positive integers* Theorem A(A4): more generally for any epsilon(n)->0, such thin quadratic-fractional-part sets are not additive bases of order 2 for almost all alpha. Proposition 1.3 gives a SPECIFIC counterexample alpha=sqrt(2), not only almost-everywhere. Reference arXiv:1504.02410. Original 2018 proof not independently replayed; catalog update Jan 2026.
