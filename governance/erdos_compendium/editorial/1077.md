## The question in plain language

A graph is nearly regular if its vertices have degrees of comparable size. More precisely, a graph with positive minimum degree $\delta$ and maximum degree $\Delta$ is **$D$-balanced** when $\Delta\le D\delta$.

[Erdős Problem #1077](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1077) originally asked whether every graph with at least $n^{1+\alpha}$ edges contains a large $D$-balanced subgraph with comparably many edges, on more than $n^{1-\alpha}$ vertices.

The difficulty is that the literal statement is not quite the best mathematical formulation. The maintained catalogue discusses a mismatch in the exponent and quantifiers. The correct general scale of the largest guaranteed nearly regular subgraph is proportional to $n^\alpha$, not $n^{1-\alpha}$.

## A small example

A complete bipartite graph $K_{a,b}$ connects every vertex on one side to every vertex on the other. Set

$$
a=\lceil2n^\alpha\rceil,\qquad b=n-a,
\qquad0<\alpha<\frac12.
$$

For sufficiently large $n$, its $ab$ edges exceed $n^{1+\alpha}$. We can prove that **every** $D$-balanced subgraph $H$ with an edge, not just a complete bipartite subgraph, has at most $(D+1)a$ vertices.

Suppose $H$ has $a'\le a$ vertices on the small side and $b'$ on the large side, with positive minimum degree $\delta$ and maximum degree $\Delta\le D\delta$. Count its edges twice:

$$
b'\delta\le |E(H)|\le a'\Delta\le Da'\delta.
$$

It follows that $b'\le Da'$ and hence

$$
|V(H)|=a'+b'\le(D+1)a=O_D(n^\alpha).
$$

An isolated vertex would force $\delta=0$ and therefore all degrees zero under the $D$-balance definition, contradicting the existence of an edge.

Because $\alpha<1/2$, this upper bound is smaller than $n^{1-\alpha}$ for sufficiently large $n$. This is an elementary, independently checked obstruction to the **literal** original vertex-count exponent.

## What is known

Erdős and Simonovits investigated regularizing dense graphs in 1970. The [maintained catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1077) marks the original question **disproved** and explicitly discusses likely transcription or intent issues.

It credits Jiang and Longbrake (2025) with the corrected lower-bound scale: a $6$-balanced subgraph on at least a constant multiple of $n^\alpha$ vertices, with suitable edge-density control. That general positive theorem is external and has not been independently proved here.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source audit #2348](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2348#issuecomment-6158390580) now includes the full bipartite double-counting proof, correcting an earlier incomplete argument. The [frozen Lean transcription](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1077.lean) labels the literal assertion false but contains an unproved theorem body.

The obstruction above is a bounded rigorous mathematical argument. It is not an independent replay of the sharp Jiang–Longbrake theorem or a claim to settle every possible corrected version.

## What remains

Primary-source review must determine what Erdős and Simonovits intended by the parameters $\varepsilon,\alpha,D$, then verify Jiang and Longbrake's precise quantifiers, construction and $6$-balanced theorem. A corrected mathematical statement should be admitted alongside, not confused with, the disproved original.
