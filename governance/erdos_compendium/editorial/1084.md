## The question in plain language

Suppose $n$ points in $d$ dimensions are separated by distances of at least one. What is the **largest number of pairs** exactly one unit apart?

This maximum, denoted $f_d(n)$, is the *contact number*. Equivalently, place nonoverlapping congruent balls with centers at the points and count pairs that touch. [Erdős Problem #1084](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1084) asks for contact-number estimates across dimensions.

The planar special case is a **solved theorem**, while the broad higher-dimensional problem remains open.

## A small example

Place points in a hexagonal patch of the triangular lattice. In axial integer coordinates, take

$$
P_r=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2:
\max(|a|,|b|,|a+b|)\le r\}.
$$

Choose two Euclidean unit vectors at angle $60^\circ$ as the coordinate basis. The squared distance between axial points is

$$
(\Delta a)^2+\Delta a\Delta b+(\Delta b)^2.
$$

For radius $r$, this patch has $N=3r^2+3r+1$ points and $9r^2+3r$ pairs at unit distance. Exact enumeration for $r=1,2,3,4$ gives respectively

| Points | Contact pairs |
|---:|---:|
| 7 | 12 |
| 19 | 42 |
| 37 | 90 |
| 61 | 156 |

These finite examples attain the published planar optimum at those sizes. They do not themselves prove optimality among **all** point configurations.

## What is known

**Harborth (1974)** proved the exact planar contact formula for every $n\ge2$:

$$
f_2(n)=\left\lfloor3n-\sqrt{12n-3}\right\rfloor.
$$

The hexagonal cases above agree because $12N-3=(6r+3)^2$. In dimension one, $f_1(n)=n-1$ for $n\ge1$.

In three dimensions, asymptotic contact estimates have a leading $6n$ term, with boundary corrections of order $n^{2/3}$. The maintained catalogue reports a Bezdek–Reid upper bound $f_3(n)<6n-0.926n^{2/3}$. In higher dimensions, kissing-number bounds control the possible contacts per vertex, but do not determine the exact maximum.
