## The question in plain language

For fixed $k\ge3$, let $r_k(N)$ be the maximum number of integers one can choose from $1,\ldots,N$ without forming a nontrivial arithmetic progression of length $k$. Erdős #142 requests an **asymptotic formula** for $r_k(N)$, rather than merely proof that its density tends to zero.

A sharp asymptotic formula would identify the main growth function and sufficient error control. Even a correct order-of-magnitude estimate would be a major advance, though it is weaker than an equivalent with a precise leading constant.

## A small example

To find $r_3(5)$, list every three-term progression in $\{1,2,3,4,5\}$, including $1,2,3$, $2,3,4$, $3,4,5$, and $1,3,5$. The set $\{1,2,4,5\}$ contains no three-term progression, giving $r_3(5)\ge4$. A five-element set is the entire interval and is forbidden, so $r_3(5)=4$. This small exact value cannot determine an asymptotic formula.

## What is known

**OPEN, even for $k=3$.** Szemerédi's theorem establishes $r_k(N)=o(N)$. Subsequent quantitative upper bounds, including Kelley–Meka for three-term progressions, Green–Tao for four-term progressions, and Leng–Sah–Sawhney for longer lengths, constrain its growth but do not provide the exact extremal asymptotics. Behrend-type constructions yield important lower bounds for $k=3$. The gap remains wide. Problem #139 is a proved density theorem and must not be confused with the open asymptotic request.

The maintained [Erdős #142](https://www.erdosproblems.com/142) and original [issue #1411](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1411) classify the problem **OPEN**. The frozen [Formal Conjectures 142 entry](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/142.lean) represents a requested $\Theta$ comparison to an unspecified function rather than supplying a proved explicit formula; its proof is absent. This formulation is less precise than the plain-language request for an asymptotic equivalent, a distinction future formalization must resolve.

The original source assignment has an authenticated [RESULT/1 report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1411#issuecomment-6166661234). This is unadjudicated source evidence, not a proof certificate or accepted article.

## What remains

Compute exact values of $r_3(N)$ through $N=20$ using integer-programming or exhaustive search with proof certificates. Acceptance: all forbidden triples are included, witness subsets match the upper bound, and the output clearly distinguishes exact finite data, order-of-magnitude statements and asymptotic equivalence.
