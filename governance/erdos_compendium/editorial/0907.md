## The question in plain language

Suppose a real function $f$ may be highly discontinuous, but every **translation difference**

$$
\Delta_t f(x)=f(x+t)-f(x)
$$

is continuous as a function of $x$ for each fixed positive translation $t$. Can its discontinuity arise from **nothing more than an additive function**?

An additive function $H:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ satisfies

$$
H(x+y)=H(x)+H(y)
$$

for all real $x,y$, but need not be continuous: pathological examples exist using a Hamel basis of the real numbers over the rational numbers. Erdős conjectured that every such $f$ decomposes as

$$
f(x)=g(x)+H(x)
$$

with $g$ continuous and $H$ additive. This is [Erdős Problem #907](https://www.erdosproblems.com/907), **proved affirmatively** by N. G. de Bruijn in 1951.

## A small example

Take any discontinuous additive function $H$ on the real line, and set $g(x)=x^2$. Define

$$
f(x)=x^2+H(x).
$$

In general $f$ is discontinuous, but

$$
f(x+t)-f(x)=2tx+t^2+H(t).
$$

For each fixed $t$, the right side is a continuous affine function of $x$, regardless of the discontinuity of $H$. Thus the existence of continuous differences **does not make $f$ itself continuous**, but is compatible with the asserted continuous-plus-additive decomposition.

One explicit algebraic interface is the rational vector space $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$. Define $H(a+b\sqrt2)=b$ for rational $a,b$. It is additive, satisfies $H(1)=0$ and $H(\sqrt2)=1$, and cannot equal any scalar multiple of the identity. Extending a basis of this two-dimensional rational subspace to a Hamel basis of $\mathbb R$ extends $H$ to a noncontinuous additive map on all of $\mathbb R$.

The exact finite program checks the polynomial-plus-additive difference identity for 392 sample pairs in this number field, and the commutation of mixed differences for 1,568 triples, using rational arithmetic without numerical approximations.

## What is known

N. G. de Bruijn, [*Functions whose differences belong to a given class*](https://pure.tue.nl/ws/files/4316577/597498.pdf), *Nieuw Archief voor Wiskunde*, series 2, volume **23** (1951), pp.194–218, proves Erdős's conjecture as **Theorem 1.1**. The original publisher's PDF has been visually checked: the first printed page (p.194) states the exact conclusion for **every real translation**, and the introduction explicitly credits an Erdős conjecture motivated by an earlier theorem of Mary L. Boas and R. P. Boas Jr.

The maintained [Erdős #907](https://www.erdosproblems.com/907) specifies only positive $t$. This is equivalent to the original all-real translation hypothesis. For a negative $t=-s$, $s>0$,

$$
\Delta_{-s}f(x)=-\Delta_s f(x-s),
$$

which is continuous because a translated continuous function remains continuous; $\Delta_0f$ is identically zero. No extra regularity hypothesis on $f$ is necessary.

De Bruijn's paper also treats related **difference properties** for other function classes and intervals. Its introductory Theorem 1.2 concerns bounded additive defects, whereas Theorem 1.3 extends the main decomposition to an interval. These are related but not interchangeable hypotheses. In particular the measurable-difference analogue of [problem #908](https://www.erdosproblems.com/908) has an **additional almost-invariant remainder** and is a separate theorem.

The maintained catalogue marks #907 **PROVED (LEAN)** following an April 2026 Aristotle formalization report, yet the same page's external metadata says **“Formalised statement? No”**. This inconsistency calls for a pinned source and genuine kernel/axiom readback before formal certification. The published de Bruijn theorem is sufficient to classify the original mathematical problem as proved; the online formalization label is not itself a GCL proof.

## GCL's current state

The exact finite verification fixture governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_906_907.py uses pairs of rational coefficients as exact representations of $a+b\sqrt2$. Addition and multiplication follow

$$
(a+b\sqrt2)(c+d\sqrt2)
=(ac+2bd)+(ad+bc)\sqrt2.
$$

With $H(a+b\sqrt2)=b$ and $f(x)=x^2+H(x)$, the executable verifies the exact identity

$$
\Delta_t f(x)=2tx+t^2+H(t)
$$

for 392 choices of $x,t$, plus commutativity $\Delta_s\Delta_t f=\Delta_t\Delta_s f$ for 1,568 choices of $x,s,t$. The latter identity is immediate for **any** function because finite translations commute; the verification serves as an algebraic regression, not an independent proof of the universal decomposition theorem.

A useful uniqueness observation can also be proved abstractly. If $f=g+H=g'+H'$ with $g,g'$ continuous and $H,H'$ additive, then $H-H'=g'-g$ is **both continuous and additive**. Every continuous real additive function is linear, so the two decompositions differ by a term $cx$. This gives uniqueness **up to an arbitrary continuous linear additive component**, not absolute uniqueness of $g$ and $H$.

## What remains

An accessible bounded theorem is the **positive-to-all-translations reduction** and uniqueness modulo linear terms. Inputs: an arbitrary real function $f$ with continuous $\Delta_t f$ for every positive $t$; show the same property for negative and zero translations. Assuming any two continuous-plus-additive decompositions, prove their additive parts differ by $x\mapsto cx$.
