## The question in plain language

For n distinct points A in R^4, let f(n) maximize over A the minimum, over x∈A, of the number of OTHER points y at a common distance d(x)>0 from x, where the distance may depend on x. Erdős asked whether f(n)≤n/2+O(1). Swanepoel (2013) proved this in a stronger directed-incidence form, bounding Σ_x #{y∈A: |x−y|=d(x)} by n²/2+O(n) for ANY choice of radii d(x).

## A small example

A regular tetrahedron has four vertices and can be embedded in R^4. Each vertex sees the three other vertices at the same distance, so f(4)≥3, larger than n/2=2 but consistent with an additive O(1) term. For n=2, any pair yields f(2)=1. Finite small-n examples cannot determine the uniform additive constant in the proved inequality.

## What is known

Avis–Erdős–Pach [AEP88] gave n/2+2≤f(n)≤(1+o(1))n/2. Swanepoel [Sw13] sharpened the upper error to O(1) and established a directed spherical incidence bound; summing n radii gives f(n)≤n/2+O(1).
