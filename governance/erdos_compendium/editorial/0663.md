
## The question in plain language

Erdős Problem #663 asks for the least prime missing from a product of consecutive integers. Let $P(n,k)=\prod_{i=1}^k(n+i)$ and let $q(n,k)$ be the smallest prime not dividing $P(n,k)$. For each fixed integer $k\geq2$, is $q(n,k)\leq(1+o(1))\log n$? The catalogue says OPEN. The established elementary bound has $k$ instead of 1.


## A small example

For $n=20$, $k=2$, the product $21\cdot22=462$ has factors 2, 3, 7 and 11. The first missing prime is 5. For $n=209$, the factor $n+1=210$ ensures all primes through 7 divide the product, so the missing prime is at least 11.


## What is known

If every prime $p\leq y$ divides $P(n,k)$ then their product does too. Taking logs gives $\vartheta(y)\leq k\log n+O_k(1)$. By the prime number theorem, $\vartheta(y)\sim y$, which gives $q(n,k)\leq(k+o(1))\log n$. For a lower example put $n=M-1$ with $M$ the product of all primes through $y$. Then every such prime divides $n+1$, so $q(n,k)>y$ and $\log n\sim y$. Thus coefficient one would be sharp.
