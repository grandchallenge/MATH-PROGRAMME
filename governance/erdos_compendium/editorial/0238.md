## The question in plain language

Fix **any two positive real constants** $c_1,c_2$. Erdős asked whether, for every sufficiently large $x$, one can find **more than $c_1\log x$ consecutive primes** no larger than $x$ with every two distinct primes in the chosen block separated by more than $c_2$.

Because these primes occur in increasing order, the condition on every pair is equivalent to requiring **every adjacent gap within the block** to exceed $c_2$. Here “consecutive primes” means a contiguous segment in the entire prime sequence, not merely primes forming a progression.

## A small example

The consecutive primes $7,11,13,17$ have gaps $4,2,4$. They constitute a block with every pair farther than one, but not a block with every pair farther than two, because the gap from $11$ to $13$ equals two. The inequality is **strict**.

A block of $m$ consecutive primes has $m-1$ internal gaps. Replacing the desired number of primes by the number of gaps causes an off-by-one error in the finite search.

## What is known

**OPEN for arbitrary $c_1,c_2>0$.** Erdős (1949) proved the assertion for any fixed $c_2>0$ provided $c_1$ is **sufficiently small depending on $c_2$**. The maintained [Erdős #238](https://www.erdosproblems.com/238), last updated July 2026, preserves the stronger two-parameter conjecture as open.

The existence of arbitrarily large prime gaps is insufficient by itself: the question concerns a run of **many consecutive prime gaps** all longer than the threshold. A proof of isolated gaps, or a positive density of individually long gaps, does not immediately imply a contiguous run of length growing as $\log x$.

## GCL's current state

Pinned and live classification: **OPEN**. Original issue [#1507](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1507). The source's dependence of the proven small constant $c_1$ on $c_2$ is retained; the original 1949 analytic proof was not independently reviewed.

The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1507#issuecomment-6181124682). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded task sieves primes to selected cutoffs and computes the longest run of consecutive gaps strictly above $c_2$ for several fixed thresholds. Acceptance: count the **primes** as one more than the number of gaps and preserve strict greater-than comparisons. Finite runs do not establish the all-constant asymptotic assertion.
