## The question in plain language

The Johnson graph records whether equal-size subsets differ in precisely one element. A coloring of this graph encodes the older problem of using all colors on the k-subsets of every (k+1)-set. The known obstruction leaves prime-minus-one parameters as open candidates.

Does there exist k>2 with chi(J(2k,k))=k+1 where Johnson graph vertices are k-element subsets of [2k] and adjacent when intersection size k-1? The original coloring formulation asks that each (k+1)-vertex underlying set has all k+1 colors across its k-subsets. The lower bound k+1 follows from its (k+1)-cliques, but the existence question remains unresolved for potential k=p-1 where p is prime.

## A small example

For k=2, J(4,2) is line graph of K4, and its chromatic number is three by 3-coloring the six edges in three disjoint matchings. Any three underlying elements give three mutually adjacent two-sets. For k=3, a four-element subset gives a clique of four in J(6,3), but this only proves chi>=4, not strict >4.

This finite calculation is not an asymptotic or all-parameter proof.

## What is known

Maintained /latex/835 read 2026-10-14. It reports explicit negative computations for 3<=k<=8 and Ma–Tang proved chi(J(2k,k))>k+1 for all k>2 NOT of the form prime-minus-one. This leaves infinitely many candidate k=p-1 with p prime (not all automatically solved), and excludes many small ones separately. Registry special VERIFIABLE is NOT proof of the full existential claim. Exact source for 'this website' computed chromatic numbers and Ma–Tang paper requires source lock.

Fixing a (k+1)-element underlying set S creates k+1 distinct k-subsets. Every two differ by one element, forming a clique in the Johnson graph and forcing at least k+1 colors. For k=2, J(4,2) is the line graph of K4 and admits a three-coloring corresponding to the three perfect matchings. Ma–Tang's source-reported theorem excludes k>2 unless k+1 is prime; the necessity of primality does not prove sufficiency.
