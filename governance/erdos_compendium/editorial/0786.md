## The question in plain language

A multiplicative analogue of independent subset sums requires that equal products contain the same number of factors. Whether the factors can repeat is a critical distinction that changes the answer.

A subset A of positive integers is required to have the property that equality between products of r and s selected factors forces r=s. Does A have density arbitrarily close to one, or can A subset [1,N] have (1-o(1))N members? The exact definition MUST specify whether factors may repeat. Distinct-within-each-product and repetition-allowed versions are mathematically inequivalent.

## A small example

Every number congruent to 2 modulo 4 has 2-adic valuation one. Therefore a product of r such numbers cannot equal a product of s such numbers unless r=s. The family has natural density one quarter.

Exact example A={2,6,10,...} numbers congruent 2 mod 4: v_2(a)=1 for every a, hence equality of products implies same number of factors even ALLOWING repetitions. Density 1/4. Conversely, A={2,4} satisfies 2*2=4 with factor counts 2 vs 1 when repetition is allowed, but this collision may vanish when every factor in each product must be distinct. It is an exact witness that conventions differ.
