## The question in plain language

Write consecutive primes as \(p_r<p_{r+1}\), and let \(g_r=p_{r+1}-p_r\) be their gap. Erdős conjectured that for **infinitely many** indices \(r\), at least two distinct integers \(n\) strictly between those primes have every prime divisor strictly less than \(g_r\). Such integers are called \(g_r\)-smooth, with the strict inequality important. The problem is open. [Original-source catalogue, #932](https://www.erdosproblems.com/932).

## A small example

Between the consecutive primes \(23\) and \(29\), the gap is \(6\). The interior integers are \(24,25,26,27,28\). Both \(24=2^3\cdot3\) and \(25=5^2\) have all prime factors below \(6\). Also \(27=3^3\) qualifies. Thus this gap has **three** qualifying integers. This is only a finite illustration; no number of computed gaps establishes the required infinitude.

## What is known

The catalogue reports that Erdős could establish that the set of prime indices \(r\) for which **even one** qualifying integer occurs has density zero. This sparsity does not contradict the conjecture: an infinite set can have density zero. The website records a Lean formalization of the *problem statement* in the Google DeepMind Formal Conjectures project; formalizing the conjecture is not the same as formally proving it.

The published problem discussion contains heuristics connected to Cramér-type prime-gap sizes and the scarcity of very smooth integers. The remarks suggest that requiring two is near a delicate probabilistic threshold, but they are **heuristics**, not mathematical deductions from Cramér's conjecture or a verified theorem. [Discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/932).

## Why the problem is difficult

Two unlike distributions must coincide. First, a prime gap must be sufficiently large relative to its location. Second, that same interval must contain two integers all of whose prime factors lie **below the gap's own length**, generally a very small smoothness threshold compared with the integers themselves. Classical statements on maximal prime gaps and average smooth-number counts do not automatically give their required joint occurrence.

There is a useful exact reformulation: with \(P^+(m)\) denoting the largest prime factor of \(m\), the event is
\[
\#\{m\in\mathbb Z:p_r<m<p_{r+1},\ P^+(m)<g_r\}\ge2.
\]
The cardinality is finite for each gap and decidable by factorization, but the quantifier *infinitely many gaps* is not decidable by a finite search.

## GCL's current state

The explicit \(23,29\) witness is independently checkable by elementary factorization. The zero-density claim is an attributed historical statement from the maintained catalogue, not a newly supplied proof. No unconditional infinite family of gaps containing two qualifying integers is established here.
