## The question in plain language

Let h(n)=greatest universal lower bound on number of DISTINCT circumradii among all triangles formed from n planar points satisfying no three collinear AND no four concyclic. Unlike #827 which chooses a k-subset all of whose triple radii are distinct, here count ALL distinct radii across the n points. Determine h(n). OPEN.

## A small example

Points (0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,2) have four triangles whose squared circumradii are 1/2,5/2,5/4,1, all different. Points (0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,3) have four triangles with squared radii 1/2,25/2,5/2,5/2, so only THREE distinct circumradii; no three collinear and not all four cocircular. Thus $h(4)\le3$, and repetition remains possible even under no-four-concyclic condition.

The example can be checked exactly. A finite witness does not replace the universal, asymptotic, or infinitely-often quantifiers in the original question.

## What is known

Maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/831 OPEN, cites related #104 and #506 but gives no quantitative theorem; none may be invented. General position excludes degeneracies and four concyclic points, yet different triangles CAN share a circumradius with different centers. Need derive elementary finite constraints before any asymptotic claims.

For a triangle $PQR$, the exact squared circumradius is
$$
R^2=\frac{|PQ|^2|PR|^2|QR|^2}{4\det(Q-P,R-P)^2}.
$$
Points $(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,3)$ are in the required general position but produce four triangles with squared radii $1/2,25/2,5/2,5/2$. Hence only three radii are distinct. The absence of four cocircular points does **not** imply every triangle has a distinct radius, because circles can have different centers but equal radii.
