## The question in plain language

A *maximal* Sidon subset $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ is one to which **no further integer from the interval** can be added while keeping all unordered pair sums distinct. “Maximal” means maximal **under inclusion**, not maximum possible cardinality.

Erdős, Sárközy and Sós asked whether, for every large $N$, there exists such a maximal Sidon set of cardinality only

$$
O(N^{1/3}).
$$

This is a problem about finding unusually small sets which nevertheless cannot be enlarged, not about how many points can fit into a largest Sidon set.

## A small example

The set $A=\{1,2,4\}$ is Sidon. It may or may not be maximal in a specified ambient interval: to decide, inspect each unchosen integer $x\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\setminus A$, compute all pair sums of $A\cup\{x\}$ and reject $x$ only when a duplicate sum appears. A set that cannot be enlarged by **one** point is maximal, even if another Sidon set in the same interval is much larger.

Adding a candidate $x$ creates new sums $x+a$ for $a\in A$ and $2x$, so testing only pairs of old elements misses precisely the obstruction relevant to maximality.

## What is known

The sharp $O(N^{1/3})$ upper existence bound remains **OPEN**. Imre Z. Ruzsa constructed maximal Sidon sets with

$$
|A|\ll(N\log N)^{1/3},
$$

reported in his [1998 paper](https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/156), [Ru98b]. The logarithmic factor cannot be dropped by merely absorbing it into the implied constant. The maintained [Erdős #156](https://www.erdosproblems.com/156) also records the elementary lower bound that every maximal Sidon set has cardinality $\gg N^{1/3}$, obtained by counting the integer candidates forbidden by each short additive collision.

Thus lower and upper bounds agree in their power of $N$, but a logarithmic gap remains. The problem is distinct from #155, which concerns *largest* Sidon sets of order roughly $\sqrt N$.

## What remains

An exhaustive finite successor enumerates Sidon subsets of $[1,N]$ for $N\le16$, retaining precisely those for which every missing candidate introduces a repeated unordered pair sum. Acceptance: verify each candidate's Sidon property and maximality separately, report the smallest maximal cardinality and a witness for every tested $N$, and compare numerically with $N^{1/3}$ without asserting an asymptotic improvement. Source verification should locate the exact theorem/page in Ruzsa's 1998 publication.
