## The question in plain language

Let $W_2(k)$ be the smallest $N$ for which every two-colouring of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ contains a monochromatic arithmetic progression of length $k$. [Erdős #138](https://www.erdosproblems.com/138) asks whether

$$
\lim_{k\to\infty} W_2(k)^{1/k}=+\infty.
$$

Equivalently, does $W_2(k)$ eventually exceed $C^k$ for **every fixed** $C>1$? This is much stronger than saying van der Waerden numbers are exponentially large, and the full limit is stronger than a limsup claim.

## A small example

The two-colour van der Waerden number $W_2(3)=9$: every binary colouring of nine consecutive integers contains a monochromatic three-term progression, whereas some colouring of eight avoids one. A distinct three-colour example is

$$
0,0,1,0,0,1,1,2,2,
$$

which avoids monochromatic three-term progressions on nine positions. Yet merging its three colours down to two in any of the eight possible binary relabellings creates such a progression. The [GCL audit](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1407#issuecomment-6098796505) checked all 16 candidate progressions and all relabellings. A result for three colours therefore does not automatically give one for two.

## What is known

Berlekamp's 1968 construction gives a lower bound of the form $W_2(p+1)\ge p\,2^p$ for prime $p$. This is an exponential-scale bound on a subsequence, not proof of superexponential growth. Later work concerns stronger multi-colour variants; a proof that $W_3(k)$ grows superexponentially does not by itself settle the two-colour question.

For $r,s\ge2$, a useful composition inequality is

$$
W_{rs}(k)\le W_r(W_s(k)).
$$

It follows by viewing a colour from an alphabet with $rs$ possibilities as a pair, first finding a progression monochromatic in its first coordinate, then a subprogression monochromatic in the second. The inequality places the larger-colour quantity below an *iterate*; it does not provide the missing lower bound on $W_2(k)$.

## GCL's current state

[Source audit #1407](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1407#issuecomment-6098796505) checks the pinned formal statement, finite examples and the colour-composition lemma. It notes a particularly important transcription error: an adjacent formal-source description prints a tower $p^{2^p}$ whereas the actual auxiliary theorem is the **product** $p\,2^p$. The historical DOI and source-volume discrepancies in earlier returns are also corrected by this audit. None is a kernel-certified proof of the headline.

## What remains

Find genuinely superexponential lower bounds for the **two-colour** quantity with sufficiently uniform control over all large $k$, or refute the proposed full-limit divergence. Transfers that apply only to more colours or an iterated argument leave a substantive gap.
