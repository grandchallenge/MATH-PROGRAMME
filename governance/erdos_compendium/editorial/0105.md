## The question in plain language

Let $A$ and $B$ be disjoint finite sets of points in the Euclidean plane, with $|A|=n$ and $|B|=n-3$. Assume $A$ is **not collinear**. Erdős and Purdy asked whether some straight line determined by two distinct points of $A$ contains **no** point of $B$.

This is not merely a question about the number of lines spanned by $A$. The same line may contain three or more points of $A$; a single point of $B$ on that line makes it forbidden. The two colour classes must have the **exact** specified sizes.

## A small example

With $n=3$, the second set has size zero. Any three noncollinear points form a triangle, and all three of its side lines avoid $B$ vacuously. To disprove the universal statement one needs a noncollinear $A$ and a smaller set $B$ meeting **every** line determined by a pair of $A$ points.

A counterexample to the stronger historical version with $|B|=n-2$ is not sufficient: removing one point from $B$ might expose an unblocked line. The exact three-point difference is therefore essential.

## What is known

**DISPROVED.** The maintained [Erdős #105](https://www.erdosproblems.com/105) credits Wu Xichuan with several explicit finite counterexamples. The original mathematical source is linked as [forum post #1430](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/105#post-1430). An independent third-party Lean project provides a formalized counterexample in [Erdos105.lean](https://github.com/plby/lean-proofs/blob/main/src/latest/ErdosProblems/Erdos105.lean), including explicit integer-coordinate point sets; its file explains that a projective construction was translated into finite affine coordinates.

The maintained source reports the third-party proof as Lean-verified, but GCL has **not** run the declared Lean toolchain or audited all dependencies. A theorem displayed online with a proof is evidence of formalization, not a protected GCL kernel-replay receipt. The result does not settle whether the assertion becomes true with $|B|=n-4$, or with $|B|=n-O(1)$.

The original Erdős–Purdy 1995 paper and the 1983 Beck/Szemerédi–Trotter bounds are separate historical results, including a weaker affirmative statement when $|B|$ is only a sufficiently small positive fraction of $n$.

A bounded independent replay is to extract the exact two finite coordinate sets from the cited Lean witness and check them with integer determinants. Acceptance: $|A|=|B|+3$, disjointness, at least one noncollinear triple in $A$, and for **every** pair of distinct $A$ points, some $B$ point collinear with them.
