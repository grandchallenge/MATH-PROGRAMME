## The question in plain language

For a positive integer $n$, let

$$
s(n)=\sigma(n)-n=\sum_{\substack{d\mid n\\d<n}}d
$$

be the sum of its **proper positive divisors**. Erdős, Granville, Pomerance and Spiro conjectured that $s$ cannot carry a set of positive integers of density zero back to a set of positive density:

$$
A\subseteq\mathbb N,\quad
\#(A\cap[1,x])=o(x)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\#\{n\le x:s(n)\in A\}=o(x).
$$

This remains **open for arbitrary density-zero $A$**. The direction matters: the question concerns the *preimage* $s^{-1}(A)$, not the image $s(A)$. [Erdős Problem #955](https://www.erdosproblems.com/955).

## A small example

*Exact examples*

For a prime $p$, its only proper positive divisor is $1$, so $s(p)=1$. Conversely, if $n>1$ is composite, it has a proper divisor $d>1$, whence $s(n)\ge1+d>1$. Thus

$$
s^{-1}(\{1\})=\{\text{positive primes}\}.
$$

The singleton $\{1\}$ has density zero and its preimage (the primes) has density zero as well. This is an illustrative special case, not the general theorem. With the conventional definition $s(1)=0$; this boundary value is irrelevant to asymptotic densities on the positive integers.

Other examples are $s(6)=1+2+3=6$, so $6$ is perfect, and $s(12)=1+2+3+4+6=16$. Neither fixed points nor isolated large fibers decide a claim quantified over **every** zero-density set.

## An equivalent positive-density formulation

The conjecture is equivalent to the assertion that **the image under $s$ of every set of positive upper density has positive upper density**. To see one direction, suppose $A$ has density zero but $B=s^{-1}(A)$ has positive upper density. Then $s(B)\subseteq A$ has zero density, contradicting the image assertion. Conversely, if $B$ has positive upper density but $s(B)$ has zero density, apply the preimage assertion to $A=s(B)$; its preimage contains $B$, a contradiction. This is a direct logical equivalence, not a proof of either version.

The original Erdős–Granville–Pomerance–Spiro paper stated the positive-upper-density image formulation as **Conjecture 4** in *On the normal behavior of the iterates of some arithmetic functions* (1990), p.169, with the preimage formulation used subsequently. A modern source index provides locations, but the printed original still requires independent page-image fidelity inspection before GCL admits its primary-source receipt. [Research source index](https://erdosproblems.ai/wiki/library/arithmetic_functions/erdos_1990_normal_behavior_iterates_arithmetic_functions/conjecture_4).

## What is known

*What has been proved*

Pollack, Pomerance and Thompson, *Divisor-sum fibers*, **Mathematika 64** (2018), pp.330–342, proved a substantial **uniform sparse-target theorem**. If $\varepsilon(x)\to0$ and $\mathcal A_x$ contains at most $x^{1/2+\varepsilon(x)}$ positive integers, then

$$
\#\{n\le x:s(n)\in\mathcal A_x\}=o(x).
$$

Their paper expressly presents this as a weak form of the EGPS conjecture. The result applies to target sets of sufficiently small *cardinality* (uniformly over such targets), not arbitrary sets whose natural density is merely zero. [Author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03120); [published paper](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579317000535).

For a fixed infinite $A$ with counting function $A(y)=y^{1/2+o(1)}$, this theorem implies the desired conclusion: since $s(n)\ll n\log\log n$, only target values up to $O(x\log\log x)$ can arise for $n\le x$. Truncating $A$ at that level still yields at most $x^{1/2+o(1)}$ target values. The $o(1)$ statement depends on the theorem's uniformity and must not be replaced by a bound with an unspecified fixed power.

The catalogue lists additional structural special cases: Pollack proved the conjecture for $A$ equal to the primes; Troupe did so for sets with abnormally many prime factors and for sums of two squares. These results depend on the arithmetic structure of the sets and do not establish the general claim. The [maintained catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/955) still classifies the problem as **OPEN**.

## Why the problem is difficult

An individual fiber $\{n:s(n)=m\}$ can be large, yet a single large fiber does not contradict the conjecture: it may occupy negligible density. The question demands **uniform collective control** of the fibers above every sparse target set, even targets constructed adversarially to capture unusually large fibers. A theorem that works for all sets with at most $x^{1/2+o(1)}$ candidate values is useful but leaves an enormous range of zero-density targets whose counting function can approach $x$ arbitrarily closely.

There is also an instructive asymmetry. The catalogue reports examples in which a zero-density set $A$ has $s(A)$ of positive density; therefore the naive statement that $s$ preserves density zero under **images** is false. This is no counterexample to the stated *preimage* conjecture.
