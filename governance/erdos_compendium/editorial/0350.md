## The question in plain language

Take a finite set $A$ of positive integers. Form every sum obtained by choosing each member either zero times or one time. The set is called dissociated, or subset-sum-distinct, when two different choices never give the same sum. Erdős Problem 350 asks whether this strong additive separation forces the reciprocal total

$$
\sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{n}<2.
$$

The point is not that the elements are individually large. A dissociated set may begin with $1$, but adding many small integers creates collisions among subset sums. The question asks for a uniform bound on the total reciprocal mass of every finite set that avoids those collisions.

## A small example

Let $A=\{1,2,4\}$. Its subsets have sums

$$
0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7,
$$

so all eight sums are different. Exact rational arithmetic gives

$$
\frac11+\frac12+\frac14=\frac74<2.
$$

This example also reaches the sharper finite bound reported for the original proof: with $|A|=3$, $2-2^{1-|A|}=2-2^{-2}=7/4$. The same pattern is the power-of-two set $\{1,2,4,\ldots,2^{n-1}\}$, whose subset sums are all integers from $0$ through $2^n-1$ and whose reciprocal sum is $2-2^{1-n}$.

The check was performed with exact, not floating-point, arithmetic. A reproducible core is:

```python
from fractions import Fraction
from itertools import combinations

A = (1, 2, 4)
sums = [sum(c) for r in range(4) for c in combinations(A, r)]
assert sorted(sums) == list(range(8))
total = sum((Fraction(1, n) for n in A), Fraction(0))
assert total == Fraction(7, 4) and total < 2
```

This verifies one finite instance and illustrates equality in the stronger finite estimate. It does not prove the universal statement, the equality classification, or any Lean theorem.

## What is known

The maintained [Erdős Problems entry](https://www.erdosproblems.com/350), read on 2026-10-12, states the finite positive-integer problem and describes it as proved. It attributes the proof to C. Ryavec, says that Ryavec did not appear to publish it, and says that the proof is reproduced in S. J. Benkoski and P. Erdős, “On weird and pseudoperfect numbers,” *Mathematics of Computation* 28 (1974), 617–623. The linked primary source copy is [the 1974 paper](https://combinatorica.hu/~p_erdos/1974-24.pdf). The entry also reports the sharper inequality $\sum 1/n\leq 2-2^{1-|A|}$, with equality for the power-of-two initial segment.

The primary paper by F. Hanson, J. M. Steele, and F. Stenger, “Distinct sums over subsets,” *Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society* 66 (1977), 179–180, gives a strengthening. Its opening statement assumes $1\leq a_1<a_2<\cdots<a_n$ and all $0$–$1$ subset sums distinct, then proves for every real $s>0$ that

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{a_i}\right)^s<\frac{1}{1-2^{-s}}.
$$

At $s=1$ this implies the original strict inequality. The paper’s proof uses the generating-function inequality forced by distinct subset sums and an integral substitution; this is external mathematical evidence, not a GCL certification.
