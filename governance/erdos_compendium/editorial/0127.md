## The question in plain language

A bipartite subgraph of an undirected graph consists of edges crossing some two-colour partition of its vertices. Its maximum possible number of edges is the graph's **maximum cut**. For each number $m$ of edges, consider the minimum of the maximum-cut size over all graphs with exactly $m$ edges, allowing arbitrarily many vertices.

Edwards proved that every such graph contains a bipartite subgraph with at least

$$
\frac m2+\frac{\sqrt{8m+1}-1}{8}
$$

edges. Erdős #127 asks whether for infinitely many $m$ there is an **unbounded improvement** beyond this expression, uniformly over all graphs with that edge count. The excess $f(m)$ must be guaranteed for **every** graph of that edge count, not merely witnessed by one convenient graph.

## A small example

The triangle $K_3$ has three edges and maximum cut two. Its Edwards expression equals

$$
\frac32+\frac{\sqrt{25}-1}{8}=2,
$$

so the excess is zero. Complete graphs give infinitely many edge counts where Edwards's lower bound is tight, up to rounding. This does not preclude a different infinite sequence of edge counts on which the excess grows.

For an arbitrary graph $G$, choosing a uniformly random vertex bipartition retains each edge with probability one half. This proves the elementary bound $\mathrm{MaxCut}(G)\ge m/2$, but not Edwards's square-root correction.

## What is known

**PROVED.** Noga Alon, [*Bipartite subgraphs*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01261315), *Combinatorica* 16 (1996), 301–311, establishes the required unbounded excess. The original [Princeton author publication record](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/bipartite-subgraphs/) states that there exists an absolute constant $c>0$ such that, for every sufficiently large integer $t$, every graph with **exactly $2t^2$ edges** contains a bipartite subgraph with at least

$$
t^2+\frac t2+c\sqrt t
$$

edges. Edwards's bound at $m=2t^2$ is strictly below $t^2+t/2$. Hence the excess is at least $c\sqrt t$, which tends to infinity. This is an exact source-level deduction of the required subsequence.

Alon also establishes an $O(m^{1/4})$ general upper order for the excess, and his paper gives sharp-order bounds for maximum cuts in triangle-free graphs. These related results do not determine the best leading constant in the arbitrary-graph excess.

## What remains

A bounded proof-replay task is to substitute $m=2t^2$ into Edwards's expression and prove

$$
\frac{\sqrt{16t^2+1}-1}{8}<\frac t2
$$

for every positive integer $t$. Consequently, importing Alon's theorem gives $f(2t^2)\ge c\sqrt t$ without any false inference from other edge counts. Independently enumerate graphs on at most five vertices to compute exact maximum cuts and compare with Edwards's bound. Acceptance: all graphs at a fixed $m$ contribute to the minimum, and the triangle gives equality; stop at these semantic checks.
