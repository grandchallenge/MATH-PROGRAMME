## The question in plain language

List the positive **squarefree integers** in increasing order $s_1<s_2<\cdots$. A squarefree integer has no repeated prime factor, so no prime square divides it. Erdős asked how large the gap $s_{n+1}-s_n$ can be.

Two related questions remain: whether **for every $\varepsilon>0$** and sufficiently large $s_n$,

$$
s_{n+1}-s_n\ll_\varepsilon s_n^\varepsilon,
$$

and whether the much sharper near-logarithmic estimate holds:

$$
s_{n+1}-s_n
\le(1+o(1))\frac{\pi^2}{6}\frac{\log s_n}{\log\log s_n}.
$$

The second expression is not interchangeable with the first. The constant $\pi^2/6$ is a proposed sharp upper scale.

## A small example

The first positive squarefree numbers are $1,2,3,5,6,7,10,11,13,14,15,\ldots$. The numbers $8=2^3$ and $9=3^2$ are **not** squarefree, so the gap between $7$ and $10$ equals three. A squarefree-gap certificate must check **every integer between consecutive squarefree numbers**, not merely find a large number divisible by a prime square.

If $p^2\mid m$, then $m$ fails the squarefree predicate. Trial division by all prime squares up to $m$ is sufficient for finite fixtures, but it gives no asymptotic gap bound.

## What is known

Both unconditional questions are **OPEN**. Erdős (1951) constructed infinitely many gaps of size at least

$$
(1+o(1))\frac{\pi^2}{6}\frac{\log s_n}{\log\log s_n},
$$

showing why the conjectured near-logarithmic upper coefficient would be optimal.

Filaseta and Trifonov (1992) proved gaps of size at most $s_n^{1/5+o(1)}$. Pandey (2024) improved the exponent to $1/5-c$ for some absolute $c>0$, but this is still far from **every positive power** $\varepsilon$. Granville (1998) showed that the ABC conjecture implies the weaker bound $s_{n+1}-s_n\ll_\varepsilon s_n^\varepsilon$; the ABC conditional implication is not an unconditional proof.

The maintained [Erdős #208](https://www.erdosproblems.com/208), edited October 2025, preserves both open questions and these partial estimates. The average density $6/\pi^2$ of squarefree numbers does not alone control maximal gaps; a positive average density can coexist with long empty intervals.

## What remains

A bounded task enumerates squarefree integers up to $N=100000$, computes every gap and records its endpoints and all excluded intervening integers with square divisors. Acceptance: the initial gap from $7$ to $10$ is three, exact sieve results match a second trial-division implementation, and no finite graph of gap ratios is presented as an asymptotic theorem. A proof-level task should identify the precise Filaseta–Trifonov and Pandey exponent hypotheses.
