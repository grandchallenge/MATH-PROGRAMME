## The question in plain language

Fix chromatic number k≥4. Among n-vertex k-CRITICAL graphs, meaning all proper subgraphs have chromatic number below k, let $f_k(n)$ be the smallest number of edges whose deletion makes such a graph bipartite. Erdős–Hajnal–Szemerédi conjectured $f_k(n)$→∞ as n→∞. This is DISPROVED: Rödl–Tuza showed $f_k(n)=\binom{k-1}{2}$ for all sufficiently large admissible n (as recorded in the maintained catalogue), a constant depending on k.

## A small example

The odd cycle $C_5$ is 3-critical, and deleting ONE edge makes it a path and hence bipartite, so f_3(5)=1. For $K_4$, which is 4-critical, deleting two same-part edges leaves $K_{2,2}$, showing $f_4(4)\le2$; at least two deletions are needed because a bipartite graph on four vertices has at most four edges versus K4's six. Thus $f_4(4)=2$, and the asymptotic source value 3 for k=4 applies only for sufficiently LARGE n.

## What is known

The maintained #744 credits Rödl–Tuza [RoTu85] with the constant extremal result $f_k(n)=\binom{k-1}{2}$ for large n. Gallai and Lovász obtained earlier n-dependent upper bounds, superseded on the divergence question. The distinction between k-critical and merely k-chromatic graphs is essential; changing to all k-chromatic graphs changes the minimization and permits irrelevant isolated vertices. Original Rödl–Tuza proof pages are not independently replayed.
