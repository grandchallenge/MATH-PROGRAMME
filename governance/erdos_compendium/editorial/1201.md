## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1201](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1201) has maintained status **OPEN** at this audit.

Write $P(m)$ for the **largest prime divisor** of $m>1$. Fix $\epsilon,\eta>0$ and ask whether a **fixed integer block length $k=k(\epsilon,\eta)$**, independent of $n$, exists so that the lower natural density of positive integers $n$ satisfying
$$P\bigl(n(n+1)\cdots(n+k)\bigr)>n^{1-\epsilon}$$
is at least $1-\eta$. There are **$k+1$ consecutive factors**, not $k$, under the displayed convention. The natural density may require a liminf; a finite-count ratio is not a limiting density. Small positive $n$ and exponent $\epsilon\ge1$ are degenerate and should not replace the nontrivial $0<\epsilon<1$ regime.

## A small example

The phase24 integer-factorization fixture samples $2\le n\le30,100,300$ and $k=0,2,5$ with $\epsilon=2/5$; for $N=30$, successes are **25 of 29** for $k=0$ and **29 of 29** for $k=2$ or $5$. Here the largest factor of the product is computed directly and compared against $n^{3/5}$. These bounded frequencies are not a natural-density assertion, and cannot support a claim valid for every $\epsilon,\eta>0$.

## What is known

The catalogue https://www.erdosproblems.com/1201 labels it **OPEN**, Erdős [Er80,p.107], and records Erdős's **partial case $\epsilon=1/2$**. That is not a proof for arbitrarily small positive $\epsilon$ and arbitrary $\eta$. Current site has comments but no reviewed external theorem in the captured maintained statement. Proof of a high-density large-prime factor along even a short block requires careful sieve and shifted-smooth-number estimates; a bound for a single factor is not automatically uniform in the fixed short block.
