## The question in plain language

A *Sidon set* has unique unordered sums of two elements, counting repeated summands as legitimate pairs. Finite Sidon subsets of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ can have about $\sqrt N$ elements, but making one **infinite** Sidon set with that density scale is more delicate. [Erdős Problem #39](https://www.erdosproblems.com/39) asks whether some infinite Sidon set $A$ satisfies, for **every** $\varepsilon>0$, a lower growth estimate proportional to $N^{1/2-\varepsilon}$ for its counting function at sufficiently large cutoffs.

The quantifiers refer to one fixed infinite set, not a separate optimal finite set chosen for each $N$.

## A small example

The set $\{1,2,3\}$ fails the strong Sidon condition because

$$
1+3=2+2.
$$

The repeated 2 on the right is allowed and creates a genuine collision. In contrast $\{1,2,4,8\}$ has no repeated unordered pair sum.

For a finite Sidon set of size $k$ inside $[1,N]$, all $k(k-1)/2$ positive differences of distinct pairs are different and lie in $[1,N-1]$. Thus

$$
\frac{k(k-1)}2\le N-1.
$$

This is a necessary finite constraint, not a construction of a dense infinite Sidon set.

## What is known

The maintained problem record reports the infinite density question as OPEN and attributes partial constructions to Ruzsa and other additive-number-theory work. The [source audit](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1308#issuecomment-6104193314) has not independently rechecked the exact leading exponent in all those primary papers, so no more precise published construction rate is asserted here.

## GCL's current state

[Audit #1308](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1308#issuecomment-6104193314) establishes the positive-difference injection lemma and highlights the necessity of checking that the frozen finite Sidon predicate includes repeated summands. The source file's research theorem is unfilled; finite examples are not a global solution.

## What remains

Produce one infinite Sidon set with the proposed near-square-root growth **at all sufficiently large cutoffs**, or establish that none can attain it. Finite difference-counting bounds do not supply the required compatible infinite construction.
