## The question in plain language

Can a set of positive integers be unusually small while giving every selection of its members a different sum? A set with $n$ members produces $2^n$ possible subsets. Erdős proposed that if all those sums differ and the largest selected integer is $N$, then $N$ must remain at least a *fixed positive fraction* of $2^n$, regardless of how large $n$ becomes.

The original question was about a universal constant, not simply whether the necessary numbers grow exponentially.

## A small example

The numbers $1,2,4,8$ give the ordinary four-bit binary system: all 16 subset sums are distinct. But the set $\{3,5,6,7\}$ also has 16 distinct subset sums, with largest number only 7 rather than 8. Clever arrangements can save space.

The delicate question is whether an improvement can become *arbitrarily large relative to $2^n$* as the number of elements increases.

## What is known

**The original conjecture is disproved.** A 2026 computer-assisted formal development demonstrates that for every $\varepsilon>0$, some sum-distinct sets satisfy $N\leq\varepsilon 2^n$. Thus no fixed positive constant works as Erdős proposed.

The disproof is attributed to AI-generated work in the FrontierMath Erdős benchmark, preserved in the author-maintained [Lean proof repository](https://github.com/tadamcz/erdos1). The contemporary [Formal Conjectures statement](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/main/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1.lean) now records the negation. A local proof placeholder in that particular source file is not itself a checked proof.

This says **no constant-factor lower bound** exists. It does *not* say arbitrarily long sum-distinct sets can be made polynomially small.

## GCL's current state

[GCL's source investigation](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1270) reconciled the original conjecture with the later disproof. A [mathematical verification record](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/3225#issuecomment-6856158672) independently reconstructed the **conditional binary-block lemma**: if a family of positive integer weights has no nonzero integer relation with each coefficient of absolute value below $2^k$, replacing each weight by its $k$ binary blocks produces $dk$ distinct weights with distinct subset sums and a controlled upper bound. This statement assumes a relation-free family exists; it does not construct the large families needed for the full disproof.

GCL subsequently performed an [actual source-pinned Lean replay](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/actions/runs/43541084973) of the author's Lean 4.28.0 development, using the author's exact source revision and Mathlib lock. The build succeeded, and both the conditional lemma and the final declaration disproving the original universal-constant conjecture passed type and axiom checks. The compiler reported only the standard axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`; a deliberately altered test removing the relation-freedom hypothesis was rejected. The [preserved compiler receipt and critical review](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/tree/main/governance/erdos_proof_verification/evidence/0001/LEAN_REPLAY_43541084973) are publicly inspectable.

**These are different levels of verification.** GCL has reconstructed and critically tested the bounded lemma, and has independently run Lean's checker on the source author's development. GCL has **not independently reconstructed and mathematically adjudicated every deep lattice and cube-gadget construction** in the full disproof; it has not obtained independent human referee endorsement or MATHCERT certification. An accepted Lean kernel replay validates the specified formal claims under their recorded axioms; it does not by itself establish that every informal scientific interpretation has been independently verified.

## What remains

The original yes/no conjecture has been disproved. Follow-up mathematics concerns the sharp quantitative constructions and bounds, the independent mathematical auditing of the source's deeper argument, and any separately authorized certification. The bounded binary-block transfer lemma is a verified component, not a substitute for this wider audit.
