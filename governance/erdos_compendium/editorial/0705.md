## The question in plain language

For finite planar unit-distance graphs, does there exist a fixed girth threshold k such that every graph of girth at least k has chromatic number ≤3? The maintained catalogue says DISPROVED: O'Donnell in 1999 constructs 4-chromatic finite planar unit-distance graphs of arbitrarily large girth. Girth refers to shortest cycle length, not vertex count; the graph uses edges for EXACT distance one.

## A small example

A regular unit equilateral triangle has girth 3 and chromatic number 3. This is not a counterexample to a high-girth 3-colourability claim. The Moser spindle has chromatic number 4 and short cycles, also insufficient. The required counterexamples must maintain χ≥4 while forcing the girth above ANY requested bound.

## What is known

The maintained source credits Wormald 1979 with a 6448-vertex example of girth 5 and χ4, O'Donnell 1994 with a 56-vertex example of girth 4, and his 1999 Rutgers thesis 'High girth unit-distance graphs' with arbitrarily large girth and chromatic number four. The GCL-pinned FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/705.lean directly inspected: theorem erdos_705 uses answer(False) ↔ the existence of such a universal k, but the proof body is 'by sorry'. Thus the FALSE status is recorded from O'Donnell's external mathematics, not pinned Lean certification. Full thesis proof not independently reconstructed.
