## The question in plain language

Let $A$ be a subset of **all real numbers** containing no three distinct points in arithmetic progression: whenever $a,c\in A$ are distinct, their midpoint $(a+c)/2$ is not in $A$.

Erdős asked whether the complement $\mathbb R\setminus A$ must contain some **one-sided infinite** arithmetic progression, meaning all real numbers $x,x+d,x+2d,\ldots$ for one fixed $x\in\mathbb R$ and **nonzero** real $d$. The progression is allowed to have negative step. To disprove this, construct a midpoint-free set meeting **every** such infinite progression.

## A small example

The finite set $\{0,1,3\}$ has no three-term arithmetic progression, but its complement contains many infinite progressions, such as $10,11,12,\ldots$. A finite witness can never refute the conjecture, because every finite set misses a distant infinite progression entirely.

A set meeting all progressions must work across **uncountably many** starting points and real steps; listing only rational progressions is insufficient. This distinguishes the real-vector-space question from Erdős #198, in which the relevant positive-integer progressions form a countable family.

## What is known

**DISPROVED.** James E. Baumgartner, [*Partitioning vector spaces*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165(75)90016-3), *Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series A* 18 (1975), 231–233, proves a theorem for **every vector space over $\mathbb Q$**: it contains a subset with no three-term arithmetic progression yet meeting every infinite arithmetic progression. Taking the rational vector space $V=\mathbb R$ proves the assertion false.

The theorem uses the **axiom of choice**, including selection of a Hamel basis of $\mathbb R$ over $\mathbb Q$. The source does not provide a simple explicitly computable subset of all reals analogous to the factorial sequence for positive integers. This matters: a proof for a countable dense subgroup of $\mathbb R$ does not automatically handle arbitrary real steps.

The [maintained Erdős #199](https://www.erdosproblems.com/199) labels the conjecture **DISPROVED**. Its statement is genuinely different from #198: the ambient set is $\mathbb R$, and avoiding only three-term arithmetic progressions is the assumed property.

## What remains

A bounded task formalizes the elementary reduction from Baumgartner's rational-vector-space theorem to the real-line counterexample. Acceptance: show that $\mathbb R$ is a $\mathbb Q$ vector space, that rational affine progressions with an arbitrary **real step** are covered, and that the theorem's no-three-term property is the exact midpoint prohibition. Explicitly list the choice assumptions; stop before claiming an effective or Borel construction.
