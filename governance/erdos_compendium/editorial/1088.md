## The question in plain language

Suppose we have many points in $d$-dimensional space. Can we find $n$ of them so that **every pair** among the selected points has a different distance?

[Erdős Problem #1088](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1088) defines $f_d(n)$ as the number of points sufficient to guarantee such an $n$-point *rainbow-distance* subset, regardless of the arrangement. The question emphasizes fixed $n\ge3$ as dimension $d$ grows:

$$
f_d(n)=2^{o(d)}\quad\text{?}
$$

This is not the same as requiring all distances in the **whole original point set** to be distinct.

## A small example

Three equally spaced points at positions $0,1,2$ on a line have three pair distances $1,1,2$. They are **not** a rainbow-distance triple.

But selecting points at positions $0,1,3$ gives distances $1,2,3$, all different. Hence a set such as $\{0,1,2,3\}$ can contain a rainbow triple even though the full four-point set has repeated distances.

The known sharp finite values cited by the catalogue, such as $f_2(3)=7$ and $f_3(3)=9$, require arguments covering **every** point set of that size. Individual examples cannot establish that guarantee.

## What is known

The maintained catalogue reports a general exponential-in-dimension guarantee of the form $f_d(n)\le c_n^d$ attributed to Erdős and Straus. For $n=3$, stronger results establish

$$
f_d(3)=\frac{d^2}{2}+O(d),
$$

which is polynomial in $d$ and thus subexponential.

**The general fixed-$n$ question remains open.** An affirmative result for triples cannot be extended to arbitrary target sizes without additional proof.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source audit #2359](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2359#issuecomment-6162322007) fixes the order of the quantifiers and distinguishes the rainbow-selection problem from the neighboring distinct-distance problems.

The current catalogue says a formalized statement exists, but the frozen formal-conjectures snapshot does not contain a #1088 file. The version discrepancy requires a source refresh rather than an unsupported claim that no formalization exists.

## What remains

Review Erdős's original 1975 statement, the modern proof of the $d^2/2+O(d)$ triple result, and the claimed general exponential bound. Reconcile the current formal representation separately, then study whether the rainbow-extraction argument can extend to general fixed target $n$.
