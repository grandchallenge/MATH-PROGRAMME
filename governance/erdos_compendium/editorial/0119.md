## The question in plain language

Choose an infinite sequence $z_1,z_2,\ldots$ on the complex unit circle, so that $|z_j|=1$. Form the polynomials

$$
p_n(z)=\prod_{j=1}^n(z-z_j),
\qquad
M_n=\max_{|z|=1}|p_n(z)|.
$$

Erdős #119 contains **three** distinct growth questions: must $\limsup M_n$ be infinite? Must $M_n>n^c$ occur for infinitely many $n$ and some fixed $c>0$? Finally, must there be $c>0$ such that for **all sufficiently large** $N$,

$$
\sum_{n\le N}M_n>N^{1+c}?
$$

The first two concern an unbounded subsequence or individual peaks. The third concerns the **sum of all initial values**. An occasional large peak does not automatically settle the sum question.

## A small example

Take $z_j=1$ for every $j$. Then $p_n(z)=(z-1)^n$, and the maximum on the unit circle is at $z=-1$, giving $M_n=2^n$. All three proposed growth conclusions hold dramatically for this particular sequence. The real problem is a universal assertion over **every** sequence $(z_j)$.

In general the point $z$ maximizing $|p_n(z)|$ can vary with $n$; replacing $\max_{|z|=1}$ by evaluation at one fixed point invalidates the definition.

## What is known

**PARTIALLY SOLVED: the first two statements are proved, the third remains OPEN.** Gerold Wagner proved a quantitative form of the first, obtaining $M_n>(\log n)^c$ infinitely often for some positive constant $c$.

József Beck proved a polynomial peak bound: for some $c>0$, and arbitrarily large $N$,

$$
\max_{n\le N}M_n>N^c.
$$

His primary source is [*The modulus of polynomials with zeros on the unit circle: A problem of Erdős*](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1991/134-3/p03), *Annals of Mathematics* 134 (1991), 609–651. This solves the second question as recorded in the maintained catalogue.

The third cumulative question, $\sum_{n\le N}M_n>N^{1+c}$ eventually, is **not settled** by Beck's peak bound. A hypothetical single high peak among many small terms illustrates the logical gap: to force an $N^{1+c}$ sum, one needs enough mass, not merely one peak of unspecified exponent $c$.

The pinned catalogue labels #119 generically **SOLVED**, reflecting the historical resolution of the headline question; the maintained [Erdős #119](https://www.erdosproblems.com/119), edited January 2026, explicitly retains the third question as open. Its mixed status must not be flattened into a full solution.

## What remains

A bounded task is to verify $M_n=2^n$ for the constant sequence $z_j=1$, prove the exact finite geometric-sum formula $\sum_{n\le N}2^n=2^{N+1}-2$, and formalize the distinction between “infinitely often peaks” and “eventual cumulative growth.” Acceptance: quantify the fixed exponent $c$ and preserve every existential/universal order. Stop before claiming a solution of the third problem.
