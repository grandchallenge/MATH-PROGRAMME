**Editorial status:** UNACCEPTED_DRAFT. Erdős Problem #511 is **DISPROVED**. The claim that a monic complex polynomial's lemniscate can have only a bounded number of connected components whose diameters exceed any fixed threshold greater than one was refuted by Christian Pommerenke in 1961. Linhang Huang independently rediscovered the stronger closed-lemniscate theorem in September 2025 and explicitly acknowledged Pommerenke's priority.

## The question in plain language

Given a monic nonconstant complex polynomial

$$
f(z)=\prod_{j=1}^n(z-a_j),
$$

consider its **open** lemniscate

$$
E_f^\circ=\{z\in\mathbb C:|f(z)|<1\}.
$$

For a fixed number $t>1$, can there be only a bounded number of connected components of $E_f^\circ$ whose Euclidean diameters exceed $t$, regardless of the degree $n$?

The **diameter** of one connected component $C$ is

$$
\mathrm{diam}(C)=\sup_{z,w\in C}|z-w|.
$$

A degree-independent upper bound would say that for every fixed $t>1$ there is a constant $B(t)$ with at most $B(t)$ large components, even when $n$ becomes arbitrarily large.

**The answer is no.** Source interpretation requires care because published proofs often discuss the **closed** set

$$
E_f=\{z:|f(z)|\le1\}
$$

instead. Components of $E_f^\circ$ need not match components of $E_f$, particularly where different branches meet at boundary critical points. The distinction cannot be erased by changing an inequality sign.

## A small example

Consider the familiar polynomial

$$
f_n(z)=z^n-1,\qquad n\ge2.
$$

The strict inequality $|z^n-1|<1$ is equivalent to

$$
|z|^{2n}<2\mathrm{Re}(z^n).
$$

In particular, $z=0$ is **excluded**, and $\mathrm{Re}(z^n)>0$. Writing $z=re^{i\theta}$, every point in the open lemniscate lies in one of $n$ disjoint angular sectors where $\cos(n\theta)>0$. Within each sector, the permitted radii are

$$
0<r<(2\cos(n\theta))^{1/n}.
$$

Each sector's portion is connected: one can move radially to a sufficiently small positive radius, travel along a small circular arc inside the sector, and move outward again. Thus **the open lemniscate has exactly $n$ components**.

Along the sector's central ray, its component contains every radius $0<r<2^{1/n}$. Its diameter is therefore at least $2^{1/n}>1$. By contrast, the **closed** lemniscate includes the origin, where $|f_n(0)|=1$; all the petals meet at that point, so the whole closed set is connected.

This is an exact, reusable demonstration:

$$
\#\pi_0(E_{f_n}^\circ)=n,
\qquad
\#\pi_0(E_{f_n})=1.
$$

It also illustrates why these polynomials **do not alone disprove #511**. Since $2^{1/n}\to1$, the large components obtained here need not exceed a *fixed* threshold $t>1$ for arbitrarily large $n$. The historic counterexample needs a more flexible construction.

## What is known

### Original 1958 and 1961 formulations

P. Erdős, F. Herzog and G. Piranian, *Metric properties of polynomials*, *Journal d'Analyse Mathématique* **6** (1958), 125–148, [primary scan](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1958-05.pdf#page=18), printed p.142, contains two related but distinct conjectures. Its Problem 8 asks whether the sum of component excess diameters $\sum_C\max(0,\mathrm{diam}(C)-1)$ is bounded. Its Problem 9 asks for a bounded number of components of diameter greater than one **when all zeros lie in the unit disk**.

Erdős's [1961 problem on printed p.247](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1961-22.pdf#page=27) drops the restriction on all zeros and asks, for each $c>0$, about components of diameter exceeding $1+c^2$. These pages were visually inspected. Since every $t>1$ can be written $1+c^2$, this is the same family of positive thresholds used in the maintained statement. The 1958 restricted-root question should not be substituted for it.

### Pommerenke's 1961 disproof

Christian Pommerenke, *On metric properties of complex polynomials*, *Michigan Mathematical Journal* **8** (1961), 97–115, [DOI 10.1307/mmj/1028998561](https://doi.org/10.1307/mmj/1028998561), Theorem 1 on printed p.98, gives a strikingly stronger result. For any

$$
0<d<4,\qquad k\in\mathbb N,
$$

there exists a monic polynomial whose **closed** lemniscate has at least $k$ different connected components of diameter at least $d$. Thus there is no universal degree-independent component-count bound below diameter four.

The complete original Pommerenke PDF could not be directly inspected during this pass. The precise theorem wording and page were checked through an [annotated account of the original publisher scan](https://erdosproblems.ai/wiki/library/analysis/pommerenke_1961_metric_properties_complex_polynomials); that account is secondary evidence and does not replace rereading the 1961 proof.

### Huang's independent 2025 reconstruction

Linhang Huang, *Many lemniscates with large diameter*, [arXiv:2509.11597v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.11597), dated September 16, 2025, gives an independently developed proof of the same closed-lemniscate theorem. The complete five-page primary preprint was inspected, including Theorem 1.1 and the proof on pp.2–4.

The construction proceeds through **logarithmic capacity**. A line segment of Euclidean length $d$ has capacity $d/4$, explaining the special threshold four. The proof uses a transformed Joukowski map to build a capacity-one domain of diameter at least $d<4$, packs separated Jordan domains with large diameters inside it, then applies the **Hilbert Lemniscate Theorem** to approximate their union by a polynomial sublevel set.

The initial approximating polynomial need not be monic. If its leading coefficient has modulus at least one, Huang rescales the complex variable by an appropriate root of that coefficient. This makes the polynomial monic without decreasing the component diameters. The proof's named ingredients are genuine analytic theorems; this article explains the reduction without independently reproving every capacity and Hilbert-approximation lemma.

Huang's note added explicitly credits Pommerenke's prior 1961 solution and states that the rediscovered argument would not be submitted to a journal. The source's priority is therefore unambiguous.

Pólya's 1928 projection/capacity estimate shows that the limiting diameter four is also a natural **upper** threshold for components of unit lemniscates. The maximal diameter statement is different from the unbounded count at every fixed lower threshold.

### A precise open/closed boundary

The modern [Erdős #511 catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/511) labels the question **DISPROVED** while displaying the strict open set $|f|<1$ and describing Pommerenke and Huang's closed-level theorem. The explicit $z^n-1$ example above shows that **closed and open component counts can differ dramatically**.

To make an independent proof of the exact strict formulation, one should track **strict margins** in the Jordan-domain and polynomial-approximation construction so that each long component persists for some nearby level strictly below one. The source reports the broader disproof, but this editorial audit has not separately certified that technical open-level transfer. This is a source-fidelity task, not grounds to relabel the established historical result as open.

## What remains

Retrieve Pommerenke's full 1961 Theorem 1 proof and explicitly validate the open-versus-closed threshold bridge. For the bounded editorial mathematics, test the strict $n$‑petal decomposition and the single closed connected component of $z^n-1$, preserving the fact that the petal radius approaches one. This guards against two common false inferences: counting petals without distinguishing the boundary, and using $2^{1/n}>1$ as a counterexample for fixed $t>1$.
