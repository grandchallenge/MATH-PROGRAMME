## The question in plain language

For a graph $H$, the *Turán number* $\mathrm{ex}(n,H)$ is the maximum number of edges among all $n$ vertex graphs containing no copy of $H$. Erdős and Simonovits conjectured that if $H$ is bipartite with minimum degree $r\ge3$, then for some constant $\epsilon=\epsilon(H)>0$,

$$
\mathrm{ex}(n,H)\gg_H n^{\,2-1/(r-1)+\epsilon}.
$$

The lower-bound inequality is **existential over dense $H$ free graphs**, whereas a matching upper bound for a carefully chosen $H$ can disprove this assertion.

## A small example

For $r=3$, the predicted exponent is $2-1/(3-1)=3/2$, with a *strict* power improvement $n^\epsilon$. A 3-regular bipartite graph has minimum degree three, but it is not enough to know that some such $H$ exists: one must show its Turán number grows **too slowly** for any prescribed positive improvement over $3/2$.

Janzer's construction supplies, for every fixed small $\delta>0$, a 3-regular bipartite graph $H$ with $\mathrm{ex}(n,H)\ll n^{4/3+\delta}$. Choosing $\delta=1/12$ gives the exponent $17/12<3/2$. Thus even the unamplified $3/2$ lower scale fails for that $H$.

## What is known

**DISPROVED.** Oliver Janzer, [*Disproof of a conjecture of Erdős and Simonovits on the Turán number of graphs with minimum degree 3*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06110), *International Mathematics Research Notices* (2023), 8478–8494, gives the $r=3$ counterexample. His separate 2023 paper [*Rainbow Turán number of even cycles, repeated patterns and blow-ups of cycles*](https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/147) provides the failure for all **even** $r\ge4$.

The maintained [Erdős #147](https://www.erdosproblems.com/147) distinguishes the disproved conjectured exponent $2-1/(r-1)$ from a different probabilistic lower exponent $2-2/r+\epsilon$. Janzer proposes the latter as the sharp scale for suitably chosen regular bipartite $H$; proving that conjecture for even values of $r$ does **not** settle its remaining odd-degree versions.

The pinned and maintained catalogues record #147 **DISPROVED**. The original authenticated issue is [#1416](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1416). The $r=3$ exponent contradiction follows immediately from the stated primary theorem and was checked, but the full 2023 extremal construction has not been independently reproduced.

## What remains

A bounded task formalizes the implication from Janzer's $n^{4/3+\delta}$ upper estimate to the failure of every $n^{3/2+\epsilon}$ lower estimate for its selected $H$. Acceptance: verify positive constants, dependence on the fixed graph $H$, the $\delta=1/12$ exponent calculation, and the exact universal versus existential order. Stop before promoting the still-conjectural sharp exponent for all degrees.
