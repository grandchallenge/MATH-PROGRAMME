## The question in plain language

A Sidon set has unique unordered pair sums. A generalized Sidon set of type $B_2[r]$ permits at most $r$ representations of each integer as $a+b$, where $a\le b$ belong to the set. One can impose an analogous restriction on *positive differences* $a-b$, where $a>b$.

Let $F_r^{+}(N)$ be the largest size of a set in $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ satisfying the sum restriction, and let $F_r^{-}(N)$ be the largest size under the positive-difference restriction. [Erdős Problem #863](https://www.erdosproblems.com/863) asks whether the limiting square-root coefficients $c_r$ and $c'_r$, if they exist, must differ for every $r\ge2$, with the sum coefficient larger.

The qualifier *if they exist* matters. One can prove a strict separation of asymptotic bounds without proving the existence of either limiting coefficient.

## A small example

For $r=2$, put $t=\lfloor r/2\rfloor=1$. The known construction for the sum problem gives the lower coefficient

$$
L_r=\frac{r+t}{\sqrt{r+2t}}=\frac32.
$$

The positive-difference problem has an upper coefficient $U_r=\sqrt r=\sqrt2$. Thus any eventual limits would satisfy $c_2\ge1.5>\sqrt2\ge c'_2$.

For general $r\ge2$, the gap is exact:

$$
L_r^2-U_r^2
=\frac{(r+t)^2}{r+2t}-r
=\frac{t^2}{r+2t}>0.
$$

Our finite coefficient fixture checks this rational identity without floating-point error for every $2\le r\le12$. It also separately enumerates small $B_2[r]$ sum and difference sets under the stated diagonal conventions.

## What is known

Cilleruelo, Ruzsa and Trujillo, [*Upper and Lower Bounds for Finite* $B_h[g]$ *Sequences*](https://doi.org/10.1006/jnth.2001.2767), *Journal of Number Theory* **97** (2002), 26–34, prove in Theorem 2.1 a lower construction which, for $h=2$ and $g=r$, yields

$$
F_r^{+}(N)\ge
\left(\frac{r+\lfloor r/2\rfloor}
{\sqrt{r+2\lfloor r/2\rfloor}}+o(1)\right)\sqrt N.
$$

A classical Erdős–Turán-type positive-difference counting argument gives

$$
F_r^{-}(N)\le(\sqrt r+o(1))\sqrt N.
$$

Together they prove a **strict separation between the lower limit for sums and the upper limit for differences**:

$$
\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{F_r^{+}(N)}{\sqrt N}
\ge L_r>\sqrt r
\ge\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{F_r^{-}(N)}{\sqrt N}.
$$

Consequently, *if* the two limits defining $c_r,c'_r$ exist, then $c_r>c'_r$. This is the affirmative resolution recorded by the [maintained Erdős catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/863).

The important source-fidelity distinction is between a theorem on $\liminf$ and $\limsup$ and a statement presupposing actual limits. The former is enough to settle the conditional original question; claiming existence of all $c_r,c'_r$ from these inequalities alone would go beyond the evidence.
