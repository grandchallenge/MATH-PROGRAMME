## The question in plain language

Colour the edges of the complete graph $K_n$ red and blue. Erdős #162 asks for a sharp logarithmic constant in a *balanced large-subsets threshold*: the smallest $k$ for which there exists a colouring such that **every** induced subgraph on at least $k$ vertices has more than $\alpha$ of its edges in **each** colour.

For a fixed $0\le\alpha<1/2$, the intended research question is whether this threshold satisfies

$$
F(n,\alpha)\sim c_\alpha\log n
$$

for some positive constant $c_\alpha$. This is a sharpening of known $\Theta_\alpha(\log n)$ estimates, not an instruction to maximize $k$.

## A small example

The current maintained page prints **“largest $k$”**. Read literally, the statement is undefined: every $k>n$ is admissible because there are no induced subgraphs on at least $k$ vertices. Even if one artificially restricts $k\le n$, any sufficiently balanced colouring makes $k=n$ admissible when $\alpha<1/2$, so the largest threshold is $n$, not logarithmic.

The printed range also includes $\alpha=1/2$ with **strictly more** than half of all edges required of each colour. That is impossible for a nonempty edge set, because the red and blue proportions sum to one. These are independent quantifier and endpoint defects.

## What is known

**OPEN as an intended corrected asymptotic problem; ill-posed literally.** The [maintained Erdős #162 discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/162) contains an explicit April 2026 observation identifying the “largest” versus “smallest” error and the forbidden endpoint. Earlier comments in December 2025 clarified that the colouring quantifier should be **existential** and $H$ should be *induced*.

The site attributes upper and lower bounds of the form $c_1(\alpha)\log n<F(n,\alpha)<c_2(\alpha)\log n$ to the probabilistic method. These can only be statements about the corrected **minimum** threshold at $0<\alpha<1/2$, not the literal maximum.

A closely related 2011 hypergraph discrepancy theorem of Conlon, Fox and Sudakov applies to three-uniform hypergraphs, with different exponents. It does **not** determine the sharp two-colour graph constant $c_\alpha$ asked for here. At $\alpha=0$ with strict “more than,” the condition becomes nonmonochromaticity and also needs separate treatment.
