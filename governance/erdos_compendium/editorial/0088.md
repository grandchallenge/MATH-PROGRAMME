## The question in plain language

A graph is Ramsey-like if it has neither a very large clique nor a very large independent set. Erdős and McKay conjectured that a graph $G$ with $n$ vertices and no clique or independent set of size $\epsilon\log n$ has an **induced** subgraph containing exactly $m$ edges for **every** integer in a long initial interval:

$$
0\le m\le\delta n^2.
$$

Here $\epsilon>0$ is fixed and $\delta>0$ may depend on it. Once vertices are selected, all edges among them must be retained. Arbitrarily deleting unwanted edges would change the question.

## A small example

In a five-cycle, one selected vertex induces zero edges, two adjacent vertices induce one edge, and three consecutive vertices induce two edges. Thus several successive edge counts are realized. However, a complete graph on $n$ vertices only realizes counts $\binom{t}{2}$ and misses most intermediate integers. The complete graph violates the Ramsey-like hypothesis for large $n$.

The conjecture asserts substantially more than density alone: it prescribes **each** integer in an interval of length proportional to $n^2$.

## What is known

**PROVED.** Matthew Kwan, Ashwin Sah, Lisa Sauermann and Mehtaab Sawhney, [*Anticoncentration in Ramsey graphs and a proof of the Erdős–McKay conjecture*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02874), establish refined bounds on the distribution of induced-edge counts of random vertex subsets of Ramsey graphs. Their 2022 primary abstract explicitly identifies the Erdős–McKay conjecture as a consequence.

Erdős and McKay previously proved a version with only $\delta(\log n)^2$ in place of $\delta n^2$. The original historical statement also assumed positive density of edges; the maintained source records that earlier Erdős–Szemerédi work implies this from the no-large-clique/no-large-independent-set hypothesis. The complete proof invokes several deep anticoncentration and Fourier-analytic tools and has not been independently reproduced here.

## What remains

A bounded source-semantics fixture is to enumerate graphs on five or six vertices and compute all induced-edge counts by examining vertex subsets. Acceptance: a second implementation using adjacency matrices agrees on every count, including $m=0$, and rejects arbitrary noninduced edge deletion. This checks the **definition**, not the asymptotic theorem. An independent reading of Kwan–Sah–Sauermann–Sawhney's technical lemmas remains a separate optional proof-audit undertaking.
