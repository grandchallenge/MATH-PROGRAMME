## The question in plain language

A *triple system* is a hypergraph whose edges contain exactly three vertices. It is properly coloured if no edge is monochromatic. A finite triple system $F$ is *obligatory* if **every** (possibly enormous) triple system requiring more than countably many colours contains a copy of $F$. [Erdős #593](https://www.erdosproblems.com/593) asks for a characterization of the obligatory finite triple systems.

A major trap: being two-colourable is a **necessary but not sufficient** condition for being obligatory. A frozen historical formal statement mistakenly presented the equivalence as the conjecture; the later upstream provider corrected this.

## A small example

Take four vertices $\{0,1,2,3\}$ with two triples $\{0,1,2\}$ and $\{0,1,3\}$. Colour 0 and 1 red, 2 and 3 blue. Neither edge is monochromatic, so the system is two-colourable. Yet the two triples share **two** vertices, so the system is *not linear* (a linear triple system allows distinct edges to share at most one vertex).

As the [GCL source audit](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1864#issuecomment-6098975477) explains, classical partition theory constructs a linear triple system with uncountable chromatic number; this four-point non-linear system cannot embed in it. Thus it is not obligatory. The finite colour check is elementary; the high-chromatic host rests on a separately cited infinite combinatorial theorem.

## What is known

Erdős and Hajnal studied compulsory subhypergraphs under large chromatic-cardinal conditions. Two-colourability alone does not classify them. A subsequent claimed general classification has been discussed in external work, but a GCL protected adjudication accepted only particular elementary reductions and left the global kernel/proof assessment outstanding. Such work cannot be silently promoted to GCL-certified resolution.


## GCL's current state

The [authenticated source audit](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1864#issuecomment-6098975477) reconciles the frozen statement, primary literature and bounded elementary checks. These observations are not a new GCL mathematical certification.

## What remains

The open source-record obligation is the *exact* finite-family characterization under the correct subhypergraph embedding and chromatic-cardinal quantifiers, with rigorous reconciliation of any purported resolution. The counterexample above refutes an erroneous candidate classification; it does not provide the desired full classification.
