## The question in plain language

A graph is **regular** if every vertex has the same number of neighbours. Every clique and every independent set is regular, but there are many other examples. Erdős, Fajtlowicz and Staton ask how large a regular **induced** subgraph one can always find in an arbitrary $n$ vertex graph.

Let $F(n)$ be the largest guaranteed order. Erdős #82 asks whether

$$
\frac{F(n)}{\log n}\longrightarrow\infty.
$$

The word *induced* is important: the selected vertices must retain exactly the edges they had in the original graph, without selectively deleting edges to manufacture regularity.

## A small example

A cycle $C_5$ is 2-regular, so all five vertices form a regular induced subgraph. A star $K_{1,4}$ has an induced independent set of four leaves, which is 0-regular, but the full star is not regular. A clique and an independent set always provide trivial regular induced subgraphs.

Ordinary Ramsey theory guarantees a clique or independent set of logarithmic order. Proving that the **worst** graph always has a substantially larger regular induced subgraph is the open issue; exhibiting a single highly regular graph does not suffice.

## What is known

The conjecture remains **OPEN**. Alon, Krivelevich and Sudakov, [*Large nearly regular induced subgraphs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.2106), proved the historical bounds

$$
c\log n\le F(n)\le O\bigl(\sqrt n(\log n)^{3/4}\bigr).
$$

Dyson and McKay's [2026 primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08215) sharpens the worst-case *upper* bound to a constant multiple of $\sqrt n$ and investigates exact small Ramsey thresholds.

A further **8 August 2026 preprint** by Ariel Edgardo Levy, [*Sharp asymptotics for regular induced subgraphs of type-based random graphs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08169), states in its Theorem 1.2 that

$$
F(n)\le(\sqrt{2e}+o(1))\sqrt n.
$$

Its Theorem 1.3 gives a matching bound **inside a specified type-based random-graph family**. That is not a lower bound for every graph and does **not** settle the proposed superlogarithmic growth. The new preprint postdates the maintained catalogue's April 2026 revision; its full proof is not independently certified here.

## What remains

A bounded verification task is to enumerate graphs on at most six vertices and calculate the maximal size of a regular induced subgraph, then take the minimum across graphs of each order. Acceptance: retain all edges induced by each vertex subset, allow degree zero, and produce a certificate for both minimizer and all-graph bound. This finite exercise tests the definition only.
