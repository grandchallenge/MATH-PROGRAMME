## The question in plain language

Colour every ordered triple from three large sets with one of two colours. Must there be an infinite rectangular block of triples all having the same colour?

[Erdős Problem #1128](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1128) asks this when each of three sets $A,B,C$ has cardinality $\aleph_1$. The proposed conclusion requires subsets

$$
A_1\subseteq A,\quad B_1\subseteq B,\quad C_1\subseteq C
$$

of cardinality $\aleph_0$ **each**, with the entire product $A_1\times B_1\times C_1$ monochromatic.

The proposition has been **disproved** externally, although its original counterexample was apparently not published.

## A small example

Colour the eight triples $(i,j,k)\in\{0,1\}^3$ by the parity of $i+j+k$:

$$
\mathrm{colour}(i,j,k)=(i+j+k)\bmod 2.
$$

The full $2\times2\times2$ cube contains both colours. Thus the whole finite cube is not monochromatic.

However, this is **not** a counterexample to the actual question. The original sets must have cardinality $\aleph_1$, and their monochromatic subproduct need only be countably infinite. A finite example cannot address these cardinal requirements.

## What is known

The maintained catalogue attributes the question to Erdős and Hajnal, and credits **Prikry and Mills (1978)** with a negative solution. It cautions that the original construction appears to have been **unpublished**.

The negative result is instead reported in later sources attributed to **Todorčević (1994)** and **Komjáth (2025)**. As a consequence, a source-fidelity review must use those actual published theorem statements rather than invent or assume the existence of a full Prikry–Mills paper.

This is a product-Ramsey phenomenon involving three coordinates and uncountable starting sets; it is not simply the ordinary finite Ramsey theorem.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source return #2399](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2399#issuecomment-6180919709) records the exact $\aleph_1$ and $\aleph_0$ quantifiers and reproduces the finite parity colouring.

The original mathematical counterexample and its precise set-theoretic hypotheses have not yet been independently reconstructed. The negative disposition is **source-attributed**, not a GCL-certified construction.
