## The question in plain language

Let $H$ be a fixed finite simple graph. The Erdős–Hajnal conjecture asks whether there is a constant $c(H)>0$ such that every $n$‑vertex graph with no induced copy of $H$ contains a clique or independent set of size at least $n^{c(H)}$.

Erdős and Hajnal introduced the problem in their 1989 paper *Ramsey-type theorems*. Their exact formulation was slightly disjunctive: for sufficiently large $n$, every graph either contains $H$ as an induced subgraph or contains a homogeneous set of size at least $n^\varepsilon$. Thus the catalogue formulation is equivalent after adjusting the exponent to handle finitely many small graphs.

The general conjecture is still open. Erdős and Hajnal proved the subpolynomial bound

$$
\max\{\alpha(G),\omega(G)\}\ge \exp(c_H\sqrt{\log n}),
$$

later improved by Bucić, Nguyen, Scott, and Seymour to

$$
2^{c_H\sqrt{\log n\log\log n}}.
$$

Many structured cases are known. Erdős and Hajnal settled graphs $H$ with at most four vertices. Chudnovsky and Safra proved the bull case with exponent $1/4$. Chudnovsky, Scott, Seymour, and Spirkl proved the $C_5$ case. Nguyen, Scott, and Seymour proved the $P_5$ case in 2026. Alon, Pach, and Solymosi’s substitution theorem then implies the conjecture for every graph on five vertices.

Several six-vertex cases are now known, but these do not constitute a solution for arbitrary $H$. The appropriate disposition is therefore `BOUNDED_TASK_DESIGNED`, with the universal theorem explicitly marked `PARTIAL`.

### Six-section mathematical explanation

The exact assertion is

$$
\forall H\ \exists c(H)>0\ \forall n\ge1\ \forall G
\left[
|V(G)|=n\land H\not\hookrightarrow_{\mathrm{ind}}G
\Rightarrow
\max\{\alpha(G),\omega(G)\}\ge n^{c(H)}
\right].
$$

The constant depends on $H$, but not on $n$ or $G$. “No copy of $H$” means no induced copy, not merely no ordinary subgraph.

## A small example

Take $G=2K_2$ with

$$
V(G)=\{a,b,c,d\},
\qquad
E(G)=\{ab,cd\}.
$$

It has no induced $P_4$: every four-vertex induced subgraph is $2K_2$, which has only two edges, whereas $P_4$ has three. Also,

$$
\omega(G)=2,
\qquad
\alpha(G)=2,
\qquad
|V(G)|^{1/2}=4^{1/2}=2.
$$

Thus this example attains equality in the elementary $P_4$‑free benchmark $\max\{\alpha(G),\omega(G)\}\ge\sqrt{|V(G)|}$.

The computation was independently enumerated: no induced $P_4$ was found, and both the maximum clique and maximum independent set have size $2$.

## What is known

A homogeneous set is either a clique or an independent set. Complementation exchanges these two possibilities:

$$
\alpha(G)=\omega(\overline G),
\qquad
\omega(G)=\alpha(\overline G).
$$

Therefore the conjecture for $H$ is equivalent to the conjecture for $\overline H$. This symmetry is used repeatedly in the known case reductions.

The original 1989 result gives, for every fixed $H$,

$$
\max\{\alpha(G),\omega(G)\}
\ge
\exp(c_H\sqrt{\log n})
$$

whenever $G$ is $H$‑free and $n$ is sufficiently large.

The later Bucić–Nguyen–Scott–Seymour result replaces this by

$$
2^{c_H\sqrt{\log n\log\log n}}.
$$

Both bounds are superlogarithmic but subpolynomial, so neither proves the conjectured $n^{c(H)}$ bound.

If $H_1$ and $H_2$ satisfy the Erdős–Hajnal property, then substituting $H_2$ for a vertex of $H_1$ preserves the property. This reduces the classification problem to prime graphs.

The primary literature now verifies all graphs on at most five vertices through:

- the at-most-four-vertex theorem;
- the bull theorem;
- the $C_5$ theorem;
- the $P_5$ theorem;
- complementation and vertex substitution.

This is a finite classification result, not a solution for all finite graphs.

## What remains

Use the explicitly unresolved pair identified by Chudnovsky–Scott–Seymour–Spirkl:

$$
\mathcal H=\{C_8,\overline{C_8}\}.
$$

Input: all non-isomorphic graphs $G$ on at most $9$ vertices.

Target: test the finite pure-pair assertion

$$
G\text{ is }\mathcal H\text{-free}
\Longrightarrow
\exists A,B\subseteq V(G)
$$

with $A\cap B=\varnothing$, $|A|,|B|\ge\lfloor |V(G)|/4\rfloor$, and $A,B$ either complete or anticomplete.

Method: exhaustive generation of non-isomorphic graphs; exact induced-subgraph tests for $C_8$ and $\overline{C_8}$; exact search over unordered vertex pairs $(A,B)$.

Acceptance: a machine-checkable certificate for every graph through order $9$, including the graph hash, canonical labeling, selected $A,B$, and adjacency relation.

Falsification: one $\mathcal H$‑free graph with no such pair, together with its canonical adjacency matrix and exhaustive certificate.

Stopping rule: stop after all graphs through order $9$ are certified or the first counterexample is independently reproduced. This is only a bounded certificate and does not imply the asymptotic conjecture.
