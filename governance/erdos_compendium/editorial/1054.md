## The question in plain language

List the positive divisors of an integer $m$ in increasing order. Add the first $k$ of them, starting at $1$. For a given number $n$, let $f(n)$ be the *smallest* $m$ for which this sum equals $n$, if one exists.

[Erdős Problem #1054](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1054) asks whether $f(n)$ becomes small relative to $n$: might $f(n)=o(n)$, perhaps only for almost all $n$, and could the ratios $f(n)/n$ nevertheless be arbitrarily large? These are different assertions. The function is undefined at $n=2$ and $n=5$, so its domain convention also matters.

## A small example

The divisors of $m=4$ are $1,2,4$. Their sum is $1+2+4=7$, so $f(7)\le4$. In fact the smaller integers $m=1,2,3$ do not provide a divisor prefix summing to $7$, so $f(7)=4$.

Similarly $m=15$ has smallest divisors $1,3,5$, which sum to $9$, and $m=12$ has prefix $1,2,3,4$ summing to $10$. We checked the smallest witnesses for these and other small values by enumerating all $m\le1000$.

Why is $n=2$ impossible? The first divisor is $1$, and adding any second divisor contributes at least $2$, producing at least $3$. For $n=5$, two divisors would have to be $1,4$, but any integer divisible by $4$ also has divisor $2$. Three or more divisors have prefix sum at least $1+2+3=6$. This gives elementary universal exclusions.

## What is known

The [maintained catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1054) reports that the universal $f(n)=o(n)$ conjecture has been refuted by an argument of Terence Tao, published in discussion of related Problem #468. Its stated upper-density bound for the set where $f(n)\le\delta n$ prevents an unrestricted little-o conclusion.

A separate June 2026 discussion proposes a resolution of the remaining unbounded-limsup question, using substantial analytic input. A posted solution or Lean file with explicit unproved external assumptions does not establish independent mathematical certification. The catalogue continues to label #1054 open. The original formulation is attributed to Guy's *Unsolved Problems in Number Theory*, problem B2.

## GCL's current state

[Authenticated source audit #2325](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2325#issuecomment-6156558086) verifies elementary divisor-prefix examples and checks the [frozen Lean transcription](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1054.lean).

The frozen definition returns the sentinel value zero when $f(n)$ is undefined; it contains proofs of the exclusions at $2$ and $5$, but its overall asymptotic variants remain unproved. Notably, one Lean theorem named as if it concerned $3$ actually asserts the case $5$. GCL records this naming discrepancy rather than silently treating it as a mathematical theorem change.
