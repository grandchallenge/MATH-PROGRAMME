## The question in plain language

Let A be n distinct integers ≥2 and I a block of M=max(A) consecutive nonnegative integers. Erdős–Surányi ask for the smallest uniform g(n) that permits a subset B of I whose product is divisible by the product of all elements of A. The maintained source asks whether g(n)≤(2+o(1))n, perhaps even 2n. It is OPEN. Crucially PRODUCT divisibility is not a matching of each a to a distinct multiple: that is #709.

## A small example

For A={2,3} and I={1,2,3}, B={2,3} gives product 6 divisible by product 6. For A={2,3,4}, I={1,2,3,4}, choosing all four gives product 24, again divisible by 24. The maintained source states exact g(2)=2 and g(3)=4, but these two examples alone prove neither universal claim.

## What is known

The maintained source attributes g(n)≥(2−o(1))n and g(3)=4 to Erdős–Surányi [ErSu59], using products of pairs of primes for the lower-bound construction. The source also asks a separate variant with exactly n chosen numbers and a stretched interval c_n M, giving c_2=1, c_3=√2. A SOURCE-FIDELITY issue needs primary-paper clarification: if 'subset B with |B|=g(n)' literally requires EXACTLY g(n) selected positions in EVERY interval, it cannot work for instances with M<g(n), whereas an 'at most g(n)' certificate can. Because M≥n+1, an asymptotic g(n)≈2n need not fit in the smallest intervals. Neither a false inconsistency claim nor an implicit switch to at-most is acceptable without historical source lock.
