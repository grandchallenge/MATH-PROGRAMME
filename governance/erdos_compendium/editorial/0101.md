## The question in plain language

Take $n$ points in the real plane with **no five on one line**. How many different straight lines can each contain *exactly four* of the points? Let $M_4(n)$ be the maximum. [Erdős #101](https://www.erdosproblems.com/101) asks whether

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{M_4(n)}{n^2}=0.
$$

Thus the question is whether four-point-rich lines are *subquadratic*, uniformly over all admissible planar configurations. It does not say that only finitely many such lines can exist.

## A small example

A four-point line uses $\binom42=6$ point pairs. Since each distinct pair determines exactly one line, the simple counting bound is

$$
6M_4(n)\le\binom n2,\qquad M_4(n)\le\frac{n(n-1)}{12}.
$$

This is only $O(n^2)$ and **does not** give the required $o(n^2)$.

An exact 30-point construction in [the GCL source audit](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1370#issuecomment-6098728078) starts with integer points $(x,y,z)$ satisfying $x^2+y^2+z^2\in\{1,5\}$ and applies $(x,y,z)\mapsto(x+100z,y+10000z)$. Its checked geometry yields 12 four-point lines, with no five collinear. It is a finite illustration, not an asymptotic counterexample.

## What is known

Solymosi and Stojaković, *Many Collinear k-Tuples with no k+1 Collinear Points*, *Discrete & Computational Geometry* **50** (2013), 811–820, [arXiv:1107.0327](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.0327), construct nearly quadratic numbers of lines with exactly $k$ points for fixed $k\ge4$. For $k=4$ their lower bounds have exponent approaching 2, while still being consistent with $M_4(n)=o(n^2)$. This rules out any fixed power-saving upper bound $O(n^{2-\delta})$ with $\delta>0$.

The original formulation in Erdős, *Research problems*, *Periodica Mathematica Hungarica* **15** (1984), Problem 36, covers all fixed $k>3$ and also asks a separate growth-rate question; #101 isolates its four-point subquadratic question.

## GCL's current state

[Source audit #1370](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1370#issuecomment-6098728078) reconciles the pinned formal statement, corrects older erroneous bibliographic identifiers for Solymosi–Stojaković, and provides a finite exact collinearity-preserving projection. It expressly rejects an unsupported attempt to exclude all algebraic-group constructions by a degree argument. The global asymptotic claim remains unproved in this record.

## What remains

One must improve the universal quadratic bound to a quantity that is $o(n^2)$ without contradicting constructions with $n^{2-o(1)}$ rich lines. Abstract pair-counting and finite pictures cannot bridge that difference.
