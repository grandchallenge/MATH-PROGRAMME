## The question in plain language

Arrange all positive divisors of an integer $N$ in increasing order:

$$
1=d_1<d_2<\cdots<d_{\tau(N)}=N.
$$

Each quotient $d_{i+1}/d_i$ measures how far apart two consecutive divisors are, on a multiplicative scale. For a fixed $\alpha>1$, [Erdős Problem #1099](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1099) asks whether there are infinitely large $N$ along which

$$
h_\alpha(N)=
\sum_{i=1}^{\tau(N)-1}
\left(\frac{d_{i+1}}{d_i}-1\right)^\alpha
$$

stays bounded. This is a **bounded liminf**, not boundedness for every integer.

## A small example

For $N=12$ the divisors are

$$
1,2,3,4,6,12.
$$

The successive quotients are $2,3/2,4/3,3/2,2$. Setting $\alpha=2$ gives, using exact rational arithmetic,

$$
h_2(12)=1+\frac14+\frac19+\frac14+1
=\frac{47}{18}.
$$

The first quotient is at least two for every $N>1$, so $h_\alpha(N)\ge1$. The problem is whether the entire sum can stay uniformly small along an infinite sequence of numbers.

## What is known

The maintained catalogue credits **Vose (1984)** with a positive answer by constructing a suitable sequence of integers.

Erdős had suggested factorials $N=j!$ and least-common-multiples $N=\mathrm{lcm}(1,\ldots,j)$ as natural candidates. According to the catalogue, whether **those particular two sequences** satisfy the bound is still open.

Erdős also studied the related quantity formed by summing the raw consecutive-divisor ratios and subtracting $\tau(N)+\log N$. It is important not to conflate this secondary question with the proved existence of *some* bounded‑$h_\alpha$ sequence.

[Authenticated source audit #2370](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2370#issuecomment-6167053395) reproduces the exact divisor list and the calculation $47/18$.

The source credits Vose's theorem but the proof and explicit sequence have not undergone independent primary-paper review. A current formalization is reported, without a matching file in the protected older snapshot.

## What remains

Read Vose's construction and verify its dependence on $\alpha$, the sequence of integers selected, and the distinction between a bounded liminf and all‑$N$ bounds. Treat factorial and least-common-multiple cases as separate open problems.
