## The question in plain language

Let $A\subseteq\mathbb N$ be **infinite** and have asymptotic density zero: $A(N)=|A\cap[1,N]|=o(N)$. Let $A+A$ be the set of **distinct** pairwise sums of two members, allowing an element to be added to itself. Erdős asked whether

$$
\limsup_{N\to\infty}
\frac{|(A+A)\cap[1,N]|}{|A\cap[1,N]|}\ge3.
$$

The numerator is the sumset **truncated at $N$**, not the entire global sumset. The conclusion is a limsup, not a bound for every sufficiently large cutoff.

## A small example

If $A=\{1,2,3\}$, then $A+A=\{2,3,4,5,6\}$. Its five distinct sums illustrate how addition expands a set. But $A$ is finite, and so cannot be used as a witness for the infinite-set theorem.

An infinite example is the powers of two, whose density is zero. Its pairwise sums are largely distinct. That example illustrates a high sumset-to-set ratio but does not prove the **minimum universal** limsup factor.

## What is known

**PROVED.** Freiman (1973) established the factor three for every infinite density-zero set. Erdős had earlier indicated that a factor two was easier, and that three should be best possible; Mann's 1960 result provides a route to the weaker bound.

The maintained [Erdős #245](https://www.erdosproblems.com/245) identifies Freiman as the source of the affirmative result. The density-zero condition and exact truncated-count denominator are indispensable to the cited formulation. It is not a theorem about the finite ratio $|A+A|/|A|$ for one chosen small set.

## What remains

A bounded task enumerates sparse sets and counts their initial segments and their **distinct** pair sums not exceeding each cutoff. Acceptance: permit $a+a$, never count sums greater than the cutoff, and verify the input set's density-zero requirement separately from the sumset calculation. Finite data are not a proof of the universal infinite limsup.
