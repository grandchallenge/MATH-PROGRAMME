## The question in plain language

Let A be a set of positive integers and let M_A={n≥1: a divides n for at least one a∈A}, the union of their multiples. Erdős asks for a general necessary and sufficient condition on A such that M_A has natural density one. Such sets A are called Behrend sequences. The general problem is OPEN; the classical reciprocal-sum test holds in important special cases, but not for arbitrary sets with overlapping divisibility.

## A small example

Take A={2p:p prime}. Then Σ_{a∈A}1/a=(1/2)Σ_p1/p diverges. Nevertheless M_A consists of every even integer ≥4, and no odd integer, so its natural density is exactly 1/2. Thus divergence of Σ_{a∈A}1/a is NOT sufficient for arbitrary A. For A={2,3}, inclusion-exclusion gives density 1/2+1/3−1/6=2/3. If 1∈A then M_A is all positive integers, so density one trivially.

## What is known

For A a set of pairwise coprime integers greater than one, the CRT turns nondivisibility for finite prefixes into the product ∏_{a∈A}(1−1/a), giving the familiar density-one criterion Σ_{a∈A}1/a=∞; passing from finite prefixes to the infinite density requires justification, not merely a formal product. The maintained source describes lacunary block sequences and cites a theorem by Gérald Tenenbaum [Te96]: under two-sided bounds 1<C1<n_{i+1}/n_i<C2 and block widths η_i=i^(−β), the threshold is β=log 2 (strict cases β< and β>). Without that two-sided growth assumption, the claimed threshold can fail. The main necessary-and-sufficient characterization remains OPEN and the original Te96 proof was not independently inspected.
