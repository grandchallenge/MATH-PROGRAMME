## The question in plain language

An infinite set of positive integers may be required to have distinct sums of three of its elements, ignoring permutations. [Erdős Problem #41](https://www.erdosproblems.com/41) asks whether such an infinite set must have arbitrarily large cutoffs where its counting function is **small relative to** $N^{1/3}$, specifically a zero lower limit after that normalization.

A crucial ambiguity is whether summands may repeat. The pinned Formal Conjectures definition compares sums of three-element **subsets** (three distinct entries); the classical strong triple-Sidon convention compares three-element **multisets** (repetitions allowed). The source audit has not yet established which convention the original historical text intended.

## A small example

For the set $B=\{1,2,3\}$, there is only one three-element subset, so uniqueness among such subsets holds vacuously. But two different triple multisets give equal sums:

$$
1+1+3=1+2+2=5.
$$

Therefore the frozen subset-based condition is genuinely weaker than the strong multiset condition, already in this tiny finite example. The example does **not** disprove any conjecture about infinite sets.

## What is known

The maintained problem registry records the global question as OPEN. The [source audit](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1310#issuecomment-6104193801) confirms the exact frozen definition and the elementary implication from strong multiset uniqueness to the weaker subset uniqueness, while warning that its converse fails. It does not establish that the original Erdős wording permits repeated summands; primary-source semantic checking remains necessary.

## What remains

Determine and document the historical three-sum convention, then address the actual infinite-set density statement under that precise definition. A finite mismatch between subsets and multisets is a real formalization hazard, but not a global proof or counterexample.
