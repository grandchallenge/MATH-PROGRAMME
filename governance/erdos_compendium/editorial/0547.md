## The question in plain language

For a finite tree $T$ with $t$ vertices, its ordinary two-color Ramsey number $R(T)$ is the least $N$ such that every red-blue coloring of the edges of $K_N$ contains a monochromatic (not necessarily induced) copy of $T$.

S. A. Burr and Paul Erdős asked whether, for **every tree with at least two vertices**,

$$
\boxed{R(T)\le2t-2.}
$$

This bound is sharp in general: stars $K_{1,t-1}$ with even $t$ satisfy $R(K_{1,t-1})=2t-2$.

One must state $t\ge2$. For the one-vertex tree $K_1$,

$$
R(K_1)=1>0=2(1)-2.
$$

Thus the unrestricted modern sentence "if $T$ is a tree on $n$ vertices" is literally false at $n=1$, although the mathematically intended nontrivial-tree conjecture is true.

The problem was historically difficult because trees can have very different shapes: paths, stars, double stars and highly branching structures. The bound proposes **one linear function** controlling all of them.

## A small example

The three-vertex path $P_3$ satisfies $R(P_3)=3$, which is stronger than the general bound $2(3)-2=4$.

The four-vertex star $K_{1,3}$ satisfies

$$
R(K_{1,3})=6=2(4)-2.
$$

A coloring of $K_5$ as a red five-cycle and its blue complement (also a five-cycle) avoids a monochromatic $K_{1,3}$: every vertex has exactly two neighbors in each color. For $K_6$, every vertex has five incident edges, at least three of one color, giving a monochromatic three-edge star. This proves the exact equality.

There is also a stronger **odd-order** consequence of the 2026 theorem. When $t$ is odd and $t\ge3$,

$$
\boxed{R(T)\le2t-3.}
$$

The difference of one vertex comes from an integer parity argument applied to the exact extremal edge bound, not from the older large-$t$ theorem alone.

### The all-orders 2026 Erdős–Sós theorem

The decisive new ingredient is the full Erdős–Sós theorem, Erdős Problem #548. Its original formulation says that **every graph whose average degree exceeds $t-2$ contains every tree on $t\ge2$ vertices**. Equivalently, for every $t$-vertex tree $T$ and every $N$-vertex graph $H$ that **does not** contain $T$,

$$
\boxed{2e(H)\le(t-2)N.}
$$

A complete mathematical exposition is David R. Wood, *The Erdős–Sós Theorem*, [arXiv:2609.17877v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.17877), posted September 15, 2026. Its seven-page original PDF was retrieved and inspected. **Theorem 1, printed p.1**, states the universal tree-embedding result. **Theorem 2, printed p.2**, derives the general multicolor Ramsey bound.

Wood explicitly credits the discovery to **GPT-6 Astra**, working in the FrontierMath Erdős project, and reports its formal verification in Lean. The primary formalization is available in [Tom Adamczewski's proof repository](https://github.com/tadamcz/erdos548). Its README and formalization metadata were retrieved through authenticated GitHub and describe the checked declaration, comparator, and proof provenance. The mathematical proof uses a count of marked vertex orderings, reversible transformations and an induction on rooted trees.

The formal statement surfaced to the benchmark includes a slightly stronger numeric hypothesis than the classical strict-edge threshold in one parity. The proof development itself contains the **sharp tree-free edge count** above; Wood's theorem directly states that sharp form. This distinction matters for the odd-order refinement, which should not be inferred from the weaker compared benchmark statement alone.

The source reports a machine-checked Lean derivation with standard axioms; GCL has **not independently rebuilt the pinned external source and its transitive Mathlib dependencies**, checked the comparator run locally, or issued a separate GCL kernel certificate. ### The Ramsey bound follows by one count

Fix a tree $T$ on $t\ge2$ vertices, and let

$$
N=2t-2.
$$

Consider any red-blue edge coloring of $K_N$. Suppose neither color contains $T$. The graph of edges of **each** color is then $T$-free. The sharp Erdős–Sós theorem gives

$$
e_{\mathrm{red}}\le\frac{(t-2)N}{2},
\qquad
e_{\mathrm{blue}}\le\frac{(t-2)N}{2}.
$$

Adding,

$$
\binom N2=e_{\mathrm{red}}+e_{\mathrm{blue}}
\le(t-2)N.
$$

But for $N=2t-2$,

$$
\binom N2=\frac{(2t-2)(2t-3)}2>(t-2)(2t-2).
$$

This contradiction proves **$R(T)\le2t-2$ for every $t\ge2$**.

The same argument works for $q$ colors. If a $q$-coloring contains no monochromatic copy of $T$, applying the tree-free edge bound to every color class gives

$$
\binom N2\le\frac{q(t-2)N}{2}.
$$

Choosing $N=q(t-2)+2$ violates the inequality, yielding the general bound

$$
\boxed{R_q(T)\le q(t-2)+2.}
$$

This is Wood's Theorem 2, with $q=2$ recovering the requested problem. It is a **short rigorous consequence** of the externally established Erdős–Sós theorem, not a new independent proof of that difficult theorem.

### An extra vertex saved when the tree order is odd

Suppose $t\ge3$ is odd and choose $N=2t-3$, also odd. Then $(t-2)N$ is **odd**. If both color graphs avoided $T$, their integer edge counts would each satisfy

$$
e_{\mathrm{color}}\le
\left\lfloor\frac{(t-2)N}{2}\right\rfloor.
$$

Hence

$$
e_{\mathrm{red}}+e_{\mathrm{blue}}
\le(t-2)N-1,
$$

whereas

$$
\binom N2=(t-2)N.
$$

This is impossible, so $R(T)\le2t-3$. The parity improvement is compatible with the exact star values: when $t$ is even the star reaches $2t-2$, and when $t$ is odd it reaches $2t-3$.
