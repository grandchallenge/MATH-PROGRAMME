**Editorial status:** UNACCEPTED_DRAFT. Erdős Problem #497 is **SOLVED**, by D. J. Kleitman's 1969 asymptotic theorem on the number of antichains. This is an enumeration problem, not merely Sperner's theorem bounding the size of an individual antichain.

## The question in plain language

Consider the set of all subsets of $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$. An **antichain** is a family $\mathcal F$ of these subsets such that no **two distinct** members are comparable by inclusion. For example, all subsets of the same size constitute an antichain, but $\{\{1\},\{1,2\}\}$ does not: one member is included in the other.

Let $D(n)$ denote the *total number of different antichains* on $[n]$. This includes the empty family. How quickly does $D(n)$ grow as $n$ becomes large?

The central binomial coefficient

$$
M(n)=\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
$$

enters naturally: Sperner's theorem says every single antichain has at most $M(n)$ members. The original question asks much more: determine the logarithmic order of the **number of possible antichains**.

Kleitman's result is

$$
\boxed{D(n)=2^{(1+o(1))M(n)}.}
$$

Equivalently, $\log_2D(n)/M(n)\to1$ as $n\to\infty$. The relative error $o(1)$ belongs **inside the exponent**. This does not assert an exact closed formula for $D(n)$ at every finite $n$.

## A small example

For $n=2$, the universe of subsets is

$$
\varnothing,\quad\{1\},\quad\{2\},\quad\{1,2\}.
$$

There are exactly **six** antichains: the empty family; each of the four singleton families; and the family $\{\{1\},\{2\}\}$. All other pairs are comparable, and no three-member family is an antichain.

Small exact values are

| $n$ | $D(n)$ | $M(n)$ |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 2 | 1 |
| 1 | 3 | 1 |
| 2 | 6 | 2 |
| 3 | 20 | 3 |
| 4 | 168 | 6 |

For $n=0$ there are two antichains, the empty family and the singleton family $\{\varnothing\}$. This edge case is important when counting monotone Boolean functions.

A useful lower bound holds exactly at every $n$. Take the middle layer

$$
L_n=\left\{S\subseteq[n]:|S|=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\right\}.
$$

Every subfamily of $L_n$ is an antichain because distinct sets of equal size cannot include one another. There are $M(n)$ members in $L_n$, hence

$$
D(n)\ge2^{M(n)}.
$$

Kleitman's theorem says the corresponding upper bound is asymptotically just as strong **on the scale of the exponent**.

## What is known

### The original 1961 formulation

Erdős, *Some unsolved problems* (1961), printed p.239, Problem II.1, [original scan](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1961-22.pdf#page=19), explicitly raises the enumeration problem after stating Sperner's theorem. He writes a lower bound from the subsets of the middle layer and asks whether for every $\varepsilon>0$ and sufficiently large $n$,

$$
D(n)<\exp\bigl((1+\varepsilon)(\log2)M(n)\bigr).
$$

This is exactly the upper asymptotic content of the later Kleitman theorem. The printed primary page was directly inspected. Note that Erdős's other questions in the same section about *union-free* set systems are **different** problems: forbidding $A\cup B=C$ is not the antichain condition.

### Kleitman's resolution

D. J. Kleitman, *On Dedekind's problem: The number of monotone Boolean functions*, *Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society* **21** (1969), 677–682, DOI [10.1090/S0002-9939-1969-0241334-6](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1969-0241334-6), established

$$
\log_2D(n)=(1+o(1))\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}.
$$

The author-paper text available through the [research article record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/243666530_On_Dedekind%27s_Problem_The_Number_of_Monotone_Boolean_Functions) confirms both the assertion and stronger explicit error estimates. The AMS publisher PDF returned HTTP 403 in this audit, so the **complete 1969 proof pages were not independently retrieved or reconstructed**. The asymptotic is published mathematics, not a new GCL proof.

### The antichain–Boolean-function bridge

There is an exact correspondence between antichains and monotone Boolean functions on the Boolean lattice. Given an antichain $\mathcal F$, form its **upward closure**

$$
U_{\mathcal F}=\{T\subseteq[n]:\exists S\in\mathcal F,\ S\subseteq T\}.
$$

The indicator $1_{U_{\mathcal F}}$ is monotone with respect to set inclusion. Conversely, the inclusion-minimal sets on which a monotone Boolean function has value $1$ form a unique antichain. The two operations are inverse to one another. In particular, the empty family gives the constant-zero function and $\{\varnothing\}$ gives the constant-one function.

This exact equivalence explains the alternative name **Dedekind's problem** and permits independent finite enumeration using either antichains or upward-closed families. It does *not* alone establish Kleitman's asymptotic upper bound.
