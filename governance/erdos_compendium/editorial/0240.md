## The question in plain language

Can one choose an **infinite set of primes** $P$ so that, when the positive integers having no prime factors outside $P$ are listed in increasing order,

$$
1=a_1<a_2<a_3<\cdots,
$$

their **consecutive gaps** tend to infinity?

$$
a_{j+1}-a_j\longrightarrow\infty.
$$

The integers $a_j$ are often called $P$ smooth numbers. The prime set $P$ must be **infinite**; choosing finitely many permitted primes makes the gaps large for easier reasons.

## A small example

For a finite prime set $P=\{2\}$, the sequence is $1,2,4,8,16,\ldots$, and the differences grow without bound. This demonstrates the phenomenon but does not answer the question because $P$ is finite.

When $P$ is infinite, it contains infinitely many individual prime numbers, which themselves are members of the smooth sequence. The challenge is to choose $P$ sparse enough that these extra numbers do not create infinitely many bounded gaps.

## What is known

**PROVED.** Tijdeman (1973) showed that, for each fixed $\varepsilon>0$, one can choose an infinite set of primes $P$ such that the associated sequence satisfies a strong estimate of the form

$$
a_{j+1}-a_j\gg_\varepsilon a_j^{1-\varepsilon}.
$$

The prime set and implicit constants may depend on the chosen exponent. The maintained source does not assert that one single prime set satisfies this bound for all exponents simultaneously. This is much stronger than simply forcing divergence of the gaps.

For a **finite** $P$, classical results of Pólya and Tijdeman already gave quantitative gap information; the theorem for infinite prime sets is the substantial advance. The maintained [Erdős #240](https://www.erdosproblems.com/240) attributes the original question to Wintner, communicated to Erdős, and the definitive positive answer to Tijdeman.

## What remains

A bounded task enumerates smooth numbers for finite sample prime sets and verifies their gaps and multiplicative closure. A proof-level successor must audit the exact infinite $P$ in Tijdeman's source and verify the precise ordering: for each fixed positive $\varepsilon$, an infinite suitable set $P$ may be chosen. Acceptance: finite examples are never called infinite-prime-set constructions.
