## The question in plain language

A *Sidon set* is a set of integers where an unordered sum of two elements, including a repeated element, determines the summands uniquely. [Erdős Problem #42](https://www.erdosproblems.com/42) asks whether for **every fixed positive integer** $M$ and all sufficiently large $N$, every Sidon set $A$ in $[1,N]$ has another Sidon set $B$ of size $M$ in the same interval with

$$
(A-A)\cap(B-B)=\{0\}.
$$

The maintained external registry reports an affirmative solution and Lean verification. GCL has **not** independently replayed the reported deep proof.

## A small example

In $[1,4]$, the set $A=\{1,2,4\}$ is Sidon because its six unordered sums with repetition are $2,3,5,4,6,8$, all different. Its positive differences are 1, 2 and 3. Every two-element set $B$ inside this short interval has a difference from this list, so none is compatible.

This does **not** refute the theorem, since $N$ must be sufficiently large depending on $M$. Finite small cases cannot reverse the quantifiers.

## What is known

The [current contributor discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/42) attributes initial cases $M=1,2,3$ to Daniil Sedov and a later general construction to GPT 5.5 Pro prompted by Harjas Sandhu. The public page labels the result SOLVED (LEAN). Attribution to that contemporary work is important; it is not a classical published proof.

There is a source-definition difference: the frozen Formal Conjectures theorem quantifies over **inclusion-maximal** Sidon sets $A$, whereas the public problem quantifies over **every** Sidon set. There is a simple logical bridge. Every Sidon set in a finite interval extends to an inclusion-maximal Sidon superset $A'$, and $A-A\subseteq A'-A'$. A $B$ whose nonzero differences avoid those of $A'$ also works for $A$. That elementary bridge does not reproduce the difficult construction of $B$.
