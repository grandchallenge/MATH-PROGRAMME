## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1171](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1171) is classified **NOT DISPROVABLE** in the maintained catalogue.

For each finite $k<\omega$, the question is whether the **ordinal** $\omega_1^2$ has the positive $(k+1)$-color pair-partition property
$$
\omega_1^2\to(\omega_1\cdot\omega,3,\ldots,3)_{k+1}^{2},
$$
where there are **$k$ copies of the target $3$** after the distinguished target $\omega_1\cdot\omega$. The large homogeneous first-color set must have order type $\omega_1\cdot\omega$; writing cardinal $\aleph_1$ in its place loses ordinal information. For $k=0$ this is a degenerate one-color instance; the genuinely difficult versions have multiple colors.

## A small example

As a precise finite bookkeeping example, $k=1$ means two colors, while $k=2$ means three colors: one large-target color and two triangle-target colors. Merely renaming two red/blue edge colors cannot prove a three-color Ramsey property. The phase-20 fixture records this arity and the distinct ordinals $\omega_1\cdot\omega$ and $\omega_1^2$; no finite calculation decides a forcing-model statement.

## What is known

The maintained page lists the status **NOT DISPROVABLE** and reports that Baumgartner [Ba89b] proved, assuming a variant of **Martin's axiom**, a positive two-color arrow $\omega_1\cdot\omega\to(\omega_1\cdot\omega,3)^2$. That has a *smaller ambient ordinal* than the one in the general problem, but only two colors. It does not automatically establish the full family of $(k+1)$-color arrows for arbitrary finite $k$. No independent model/forcing proof or ordinal implication has been checked by GCL.
