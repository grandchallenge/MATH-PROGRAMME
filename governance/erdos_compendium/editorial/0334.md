## The question in plain language

For an integer $n$, call a positive integer smooth at level $y$ if every prime dividing it is at most $y$. Erdős Problem 334 asks how small one can make a common smoothness bound while still representing every sufficiently large $n$ as

$$
n=a+b
$$

with both $a$ and $b$ having no prime divisor exceeding $f(n)$. Equivalently, if $P^+(m)$ denotes the largest prime factor of $m$, with $P^+(1)=1$, the target is to understand the least scale of $f(n)$ for which

$$
P^+(ab)\leq f(n)
$$

can always be arranged. The quantifier “every sufficiently large” matters: checking many values of $n$ cannot by itself establish the asymptotic assertion.

The original source phrases the challenge through the least integer not representable with prime factors below a given bound. In the source’s notation, Erdős conjectured that this least exception eventually exceeds every fixed power of the bound. The modern problem page presents the equivalent smooth-summand formulation and records the historical $n^{1/3}$ question.

## A small example

Here is an exact finite check, included to make the definitions concrete. Direct trial division of every summand pair $a,n-a$ for $2\leq n\leq20$ gives the least common smoothness bound

$$
1,2,2,2,3,2,2,2,3,2,2,3,3,3,2,2,2,3,2
$$

in order for $n=2,3,\ldots,20$. For example, the entries requiring $3$ can be witnessed by

$$
11=2+9,\quad 13=1+12,\quad 14=2+12,\quad 15=3+12,\quad 19=1+18.
$$

The factors are explicit: $9=3^2$, $12=2^2\cdot3$, and $18=2\cdot3^2$, while $1$, $2$, and $3$ have no prime factor above $3$. The same exact enumeration supplies the other entries; for instance $20=4+16$ has smoothness bound $2$. This verifies a finite lemma, namely that every integer from $2$ through $20$ has a representation with bound at most $3$. It does not establish a bound for all larger integers, identify the optimal function, or reproduce Balog’s asymptotic argument.

## What is known

The primary historical source is Erdős, “Some new problems and results in number theory,” *Number Theory (Mysore, 1981)*, Lecture Notes in Mathematics 938 (1982), p. 55. Its problem 4 asks about the least exception for binary sums and conjectures growth beyond every fixed power. The retrieved PDF has SHA-256 `e3fb7fca8b27e2fb8ca63f4063a61a1603ab368c41e420310ce2159842930622`.

The principal verified theorem is A. Balog, “On additive representation of integers,” *Acta Mathematica Hungarica* 54 (1989), 297–301, especially the theorem on p. 297. For each fixed $\varepsilon>0$, Balog proves that every sufficiently large $N$ can be written as $N=a+b$ with

$$
P^+(ab)\leq N^{4/(9\sqrt e)+\varepsilon}.
$$

The exponent is approximately $0.2695691821$, so this is stronger than the originally mentioned $N^{1/3}$ scale. The proof uses a deep distribution result of Fouvry for sequences in residue classes with large moduli; Balog explicitly says that the method does not reach even an $N^\varepsilon$ bound in the conjectural subpolynomial direction. The complete volume scan used here is [available from REAL-J](https://real-j.mtak.hu/7451/1/MTA_ActaMathHung_54.pdf), with SHA-256 `12db2f333c5cd10c4f50dd152fed0a13a9d0d1af42c2da82fff83c0f7c935095`.

Thus the external record supports a proved polynomial upper bound and an open improvement problem. The live page also says that $f(n)\leq n^{o(1)}$ is considered likely, but that sentence is a conjectural outlook, not a theorem. Its two forum comments are user reports and explicitly not verified proof expositions; they do not change the dated status.

## What remains

The smallest useful successor is a source-locked normalization and bounded formalization-preparation task. It should first fix whether $a,b$ range over positive naturals, define $P^+(1)$, and choose one exact asymptotic statement: either Balog’s theorem with its $\varepsilon$ and sufficiently-large threshold, or Erdős’s least-exception conjecture. It should then translate only that statement into a theorem interface and test the translation against the wording and page locations in the two primary sources.

Acceptance should require a line-by-line quantifier record, a source quotation or transcription checked against the scans, and an executable finite checker for a declared range. Falsification is any mismatch in positivity, the product condition, the order of quantifiers, or the exponent. The task should stop when the interface and finite checker agree with the sources, or immediately when the primary statement cannot be read unambiguously. A formal theorem proved with `sorry` would be a source artifact, not a proof of the mathematics.
