**Editorial status:** UNACCEPTED_DRAFT. Erdős Problem #528 remains **OPEN**. It asks for the exact connective constant governing the number of self-avoiding walks on the integer lattice. The **existence** of that constant has been known since the 1950s; the difficulty is to determine its **value**, notably on the square lattice. High-precision computations do not constitute an exact mathematical expression.

## The question in plain language

Start at the origin of the integer lattice $\mathbb Z^k$. At each step, move one unit along a coordinate axis, in either the positive or negative direction. A walk is **self-avoiding** if it never visits the same lattice vertex twice.

Let $c_n(k)$ denote the number of self-avoiding walks taking exactly $n$ steps from the origin. Two walks that make different ordered sequences of steps are counted separately, even if they are related by rotation or reflection. The zero-step walk is counted once, $c_0(k)=1$.

The number of walks grows rapidly with $n$. Erdős asks us to determine

$$
\boxed{\mu_k=\lim_{n\to\infty}c_n(k)^{1/n}.}
$$

This number is called the **connective constant**. It measures how many admissible continuations are available *on average, in the exponential-growth sense*, after self-avoidance is taken into account.

The statement is not asking us to establish the existence of the limit. That result was already known when Erdős wrote down the problem. Nor does it identify walks up to lattice symmetry. Changing the underlying lattice changes the connective constant.

The most prominent open case is $\mu_2$, the constant for the **square lattice**. An exact formula for the connective constant of a different lattice, such as the honeycomb lattice, does not settle the square-lattice question.

## A small example

In $\mathbb Z^2$, the first step has four possible directions. Thus

$$
c_0(2)=1,\qquad c_1(2)=4.
$$

The second step cannot immediately return to the origin, leaving three choices after every first step, so $c_2(2)=4\cdot3=12$.

At three steps the same simple count still works, giving $c_3(2)=36$. At the fourth step, however, some otherwise non-reversing choices would return to the origin by completing a four-edge square. The count becomes

$$
\boxed{c_0(2),c_1(2),c_2(2),c_3(2),c_4(2)
=1,4,12,36,100.}
$$

The naive formula $4\cdot3^{n-1}$ fails already at $n=4$. Self-avoidance imposes more constraints than merely prohibiting an immediate reversal.

There is one dimension where the exact constant is elementary. On the line $\mathbb Z$, once a walk starts left or right it must continue in that direction forever, or it would revisit a vertex. Hence

$$
c_n(1)=2\quad(n\ge1),\qquad
\mu_1=\lim_{n\to\infty}2^{1/n}=1.
$$

This exact one-dimensional solution does not extend to $\mathbb Z^2$, where walks can bend around previously visited sites.

## What is known

### The existence theorem

The [original Erdős problem](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1961-22.pdf#page=34), printed p.254 of *Some unsolved problems* (1961), explicitly says that the exponential growth limit had already been observed. The original source page was directly examined.

Hammersley and Morton, *Poor Man's Monte Carlo*, *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society, Series B* **16** (1954), 23–38, [DOI 10.1111/j.2517-6161.1954.tb00145.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1954.tb00145.x), are credited with the early existence theory. The publisher's bibliographic and abstract records were checked, but the entire original 1954 proof was not re-examined.

There is a short direct argument using **submultiplicativity**. Every self-avoiding walk of length $m+n$ has a self-avoiding prefix of length $m$ and a self-avoiding suffix of length $n$. Translate the suffix so it starts at the origin. This determines an *injective* mapping into pairs of shorter walks: distinct long walks cannot have the same pair of translated segments.

Not every pair can be joined without a collision, so we have only the upper inequality

$$
\boxed{c_{m+n}(k)\le c_m(k)c_n(k).}
$$

Taking logarithms turns it into a subadditive sequence. Fekete's lemma then establishes

$$
\boxed{
\log\mu_k=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log c_n(k)}n
=\inf_{n\ge1}\frac{\log c_n(k)}n.
}
$$

Thus existence is rigorous, and every finite value $c_n(k)^{1/n}$ gives an **upper bound** on $\mu_k$. None gives an exact value without an additional argument.

### Elementary dimension-dependent bounds

There are at least $k^n$ walks that use only **positive coordinate directions**. All such walks are self-avoiding because the sum of their coordinates strictly increases at every step.

Conversely, after the first of at most $2k$ steps, immediate reversal is forbidden, leaving at most $2k-1$ choices at each later step. Therefore

$$
k^n\le c_n(k)\le2k(2k-1)^{n-1},
$$

and consequently

$$
\boxed{k\le\mu_k\le2k-1.}
$$

These bounds are rigorous but do not locate the constant precisely. H. Kesten, *On the number of self-avoiding walks*, *Journal of Mathematical Physics* **4** (1963), 960–969, improved the high-dimensional understanding, obtaining the expansion reported in the maintained catalogue,

$$
\mu_k=2k-1-\frac1{2k}+O(k^{-2})
\quad(k\to\infty).
$$

Kesten's original full proof was not independently reconstructed in this audit.

### Numerical accuracy is not an exact formula

The [maintained Erdős #528 catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/528) lists historical rigorous bounds and numerical studies. Jesper Lykke Jacobsen, Christian R. Scullard and Anthony J. Guttmann, *On the growth constant for square-lattice self-avoiding walks*, [arXiv:1607.02984](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.02984), report

$$
\mu_2\approx2.63815853032790(3).
$$

This is a **high-precision numerical estimate** with a stated uncertainty, not a theorem identifying an exact algebraic or transcendental value. Their study also ruled out an earlier candidate exact expression at the twelfth decimal place. The original authors' abstract was checked.

The existence of a closed form on the *honeycomb* lattice must not be transferred to the square lattice. The local geometry and admissible walks differ.

## GCL's current state

The authenticated [original #528 source return](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1799#issuecomment-6227152077) preserves OPEN and distinguishes the established existence proof from the unsolved value problem.

An exact finite fixture enumerates ordered lattice paths with a visited-vertex set, cross-checks selected small counts and verifies submultiplicative inequalities. It also prepares endpoint-distance histograms useful for #529. The protected Formal Conjectures snapshot has no #528 statement and no GCL Lean kernel proof is claimed.

## What remains

The bounded mathematical interface is a reproducible counting algorithm, the splitting injection, and rigorous bounds derived from the finite counts. The genuine open boundary is an exact determination or much sharper analytic characterization of $\mu_k$ on the hypercubic lattice, especially $\mu_2$. Running the enumeration for larger $n$ does not itself establish an exact limit.

No unselected research Solve campaign is launched by this editorial work.
