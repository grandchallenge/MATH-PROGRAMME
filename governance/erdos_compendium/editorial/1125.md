## The question in plain language

Suppose a function $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ satisfies

$$
2f(x)\le f(x+h)+f(x+2h)
$$

for **every $x\in\mathbb R$ and every $h>0$**. Must $f$ be nondecreasing, even when it is not assumed measurable or continuous?

[Erdős Problem #1125](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1125) answers **yes**, a theorem due to Laczkovich (1984) extending Kemperman's earlier measurable-function case.

## A small example

For $f(x)=ax+b$, the inequality becomes $3ah\ge0$. Since $h>0$, this holds for every $x,h$ exactly when $a\ge0$, precisely the condition for an affine function to be nondecreasing.

This exact reduction, checked on rational samples, illustrates the condition but **does not** prove the general theorem for arbitrary nonmeasurable functions.

## What is known

Miklós Laczkovich, *On Kemperman's inequality*, *Colloquium Mathematicum* **49** (1984), pp. 109–115, DOI [10.4064/cm-49-1-109-115](https://doi.org/10.4064/cm-49-1-109-115), proved the result without a measurability assumption. Bibliographic details have been independently checked against the publisher.
