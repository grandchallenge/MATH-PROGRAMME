## The question in plain language

A **simple cycle** is a closed loop of three or more distinct vertices in a graph. Erdős asked whether a graph with $n$ vertices and at least $2n-2$ edges must contain a cycle $C$ together with a vertex $v$ **outside the cycle** that is adjacent to three distinct vertices of $C$.

This is [Erdős Problem #916](https://www.erdosproblems.com/916). It was **proved** by Carsten Thomassen in 1974. Such a cycle and its three extra spokes form a special subdivision of the complete four-vertex graph $K_4$. The word **special** matters: an arbitrary subdivision of $K_4$ does not require one branch vertex to be directly adjacent to three points on a common cycle.

The theorem is about a finite simple undirected graph and distinct cycle vertices. Extra edges, disconnected components and non-induced cycles are allowed. If a graph has more than $2n-2$ edges, selecting an arbitrary $2n-2$ of them and applying the exact-threshold theorem also establishes the conclusion.

## A small example

Take four vertices. At the threshold $2n-2=6$, the graph is the complete graph $K_4$. Its triangle on any three vertices is a cycle, and its fourth vertex is adjacent to all three triangle vertices. This realizes the promised structure.

To see why **one fewer edge is insufficient**, start with two vertices $u,w$, add the edge $uw$, and connect every other vertex to both $u$ and $w$, but do not add edges among those remaining vertices. The resulting graph has

$$
1+2(n-2)=2n-3
$$

edges. Every cycle is a triangle through $u,w$ or a quadrilateral with those two vertices and two of the others. Any vertex outside one of these cycles is adjacent to at most the two central vertices $u,w$, never to three cycle vertices. Thus the precise bound is sharp for every $n\ge4$.

A brute-force exact verifier enumerates **all 33,866 labelled simple graphs on two through six vertices**, and identifies precisely 5,001 graphs meeting the edge threshold. It finds a suitable cycle-and-outside-vertex witness for every one.

## What is known

The original question was raised by Erdős in [Er67b]. Dirac's earlier result, G. A. Dirac, *In abstrakten Graphen vorhandene vollständige 4-Graphen und ihre Unterteilungen*, *Mathematische Nachrichten* (1960), pp.61–85, ensured a subdivision of $K_4$. That is a weaker graph configuration because its three branches need not be single edges from one chosen vertex.

Carsten Thomassen, [*A minimal condition implying a special K4-subdivision in a graph*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01238666), *Archiv der Mathematik* **25** (1974), pp.210–215, settled Erdős's stronger question affirmatively. The maintained [problem #916 source bibliography](https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/916) identifies this paper as [Th74]. A later paper by Thomassen and Toft, [*Non-separating induced cycles in graphs*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0095-8956(81)80025-1), *Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B* **31** (1981), pp.199–224, independently summarizes and extends related special-subdivision results.

The 1974 source also analyzes graphs close to the sharp threshold. A modern [graph-decomposition research introduction](https://pure-oai.bham.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/187099766/1_s2.0_S0095895623000059_main.pdf) reports that Thomassen characterized the graphs with exactly $2n-3$ edges that avoid this special $K_4$ subdivision using assemblies, or **cockades**, built from triangles and complete bipartite $K_{3,3}$ graphs with small clique overlaps. This structural near-extremal theorem is stronger than the single explicit sharpness example above. Its precise decomposition axioms need direct comparison with the original 1974 paper before formal admission.

The [maintained Erdős #916](https://www.erdosproblems.com/916) is marked PROVED and currently lists no formalized statement. It reports interest in a formal proof, but neither a pinned Lean statement nor a machine-checked proof is part of GCL's present source lock. The published 1974 proof is imported as established mathematics, not independently replayed in the finite computation.

## GCL's current state

The required witness is an exact finite combinatorial object: a simple cycle represented by a sequence of distinct vertices, together with a vertex not on that cycle whose graph-neighbor set meets the cycle in at least three vertices. Once such a witness is found, its validity can be checked in time polynomial in the size of the given cycle, without trusting how it was discovered.

The executable exact fixture at governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_916_917.py enumerates every labelled simple graph for $2\le n\le6$. For each graph meeting the edge threshold, it enumerates all possible simple cycles up to rotation and reversal, then checks each outside vertex. It reports **5,001 eligible graphs and 5,001 witnesses**, with no counterexample. For $n=4,5,6,7$ it separately constructs and verifies the sharpness family with $2n-3$ edges and no witness.

These checks establish the sharp low-order examples and the correct quantifiers for cycle membership, but not the general 1974 extremal theorem for all graph orders.

## What remains

A bounded verification task is the **special-subdivision witness predicate**. Fix the input as a finite simple graph and show that a simple cycle plus one outside vertex adjacent to three distinct vertices on that cycle yields a subdivision of $K_4$ with three unsubdivided spokes. Acceptance is a complete elementary proof or pinned no-placeholder Lean definition and theorem with literal axioms, plus replay of all 5,001 qualifying graphs and the explicit $2n-3$ sharpness construction. A repeated cycle vertex, missing spoke, or outside vertex lying on the cycle falsifies the witness.

The deeper source obligation is an independent reconstruction of Thomassen's 1974 proof, including its exact minimal edge hypothesis and near-extremal graph-decomposition characterization. Obtain the original printed Theorems and proof pages before claiming the full structural result has been independently checked. The earlier Dirac topological subdivision and the special direct-spoke subdivision must remain separate statements.
