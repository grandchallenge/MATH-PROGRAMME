## The question in plain language

A collection of paths between two vertices is **edge-disjoint** if no edge is used twice. It is **internally vertex-disjoint** if the paths have no vertices in common except their two endpoints. The second property is stronger.

Bollobás and Erdős asked whether every graph on

$$
v=1+t(m-1)
$$

vertices with at least

$$
1+t\binom m2
$$

edges must contain **two vertices joined by $m$ disjoint paths**, for every integer $m\ge2$ and positive integer $t$.

This is [Erdős Problem #915](https://www.erdosproblems.com/915). The original word **disjoint** is ambiguous, and the two interpretations have different answers. For *internally vertex-disjoint* paths the conjecture is **false** from $m=5$ onward. For *edge-disjoint* paths the claimed threshold is **true**, and even a stronger result is known. The maintained catalogue therefore calls the original problem **SOLVED**, rather than uniformly proved or disproved.

## A small example

Take $t$ distinct complete graphs $K_m$ and identify exactly **one vertex** from all of them, leaving all other vertices separate. The result, often called a **clique bouquet**, has

$$
1+t(m-1)
$$

vertices and

$$
t\binom m2
$$

edges, **one edge fewer** than the conjectured threshold.

It contains no pair joined by $m$ internally vertex-disjoint paths. Two vertices in the same clique have at most $m-1$ such paths, since at most $m-1$ edges leave either endpoint inside its block. Vertices in different cliques must route through the single shared vertex, which prevents two internally vertex-disjoint connections. Similarly, no pair has $m$ edge-disjoint paths: every vertex outside the shared center has degree $m-1$, and any edge-disjoint paths from it need distinct incident edges.

This demonstrates that a **positive result at one additional edge would be sharp**. It does not establish that positive result. Indeed, Leonard found a 57-vertex graph with **141 edges** and no pair joined by five internally vertex-disjoint paths, directly contradicting the original $m=5$, $t=14$ threshold, since $57=1+14(5-1)$ and $141=1+14\binom52$.

## What is known

The original question comes from Bollobás and Erdős, [*Extremal problems in graph theory*](https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/915), *Matematikai Lapok* (1962), pp.143–152, and Erdős [Er67b, p.4]. The maintained [problem #915](https://www.erdosproblems.com/915) explicitly separates **vertex-disjoint** and **edge-disjoint** versions.

For the vertex-disjoint version, let $k_m(v)$ denote the minimum edge count that forces two vertices with at least $m$ internally vertex-disjoint paths in every graph with $v$ vertices. The small cases were established by Bártfai (1960) for $m=3$ and Bollobás (1966) for $m=4$; in particular,

$$
k_3(2t)=3t-1,\quad
k_3(2t+1)=3t+1,\quad
k_4(v)=2v-1.
$$

John L. Leonard, [*On a conjecture of Bollobás and Erdős*](https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/915), *Periodica Mathematica Hungarica* (1973), pp.281–284, gives the $m=5$ counterexample described above. Sørensen and Thomassen, [*On k-rails in graphs*](https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/915), *Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B* (1974), pp.143–159, prove

$$
k_5(v)=\left\lfloor\frac{8v}{3}\right\rfloor-3
\quad\text{for }v\ge13.
$$

Mader (1973) supplies failures for every $m\ge6$. Consequently the general vertex-disjoint conjecture cannot be rescued merely by checking finitely many smaller cases.

For the edge-disjoint version, define $\ell_m(v)$ analogously. Mader, [*Ein Extremalproblem des Zusammenhangs von Graphen*](https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/915), *Mathematische Zeitschrift* (1973), pp.223–231, gives a stronger degree-sensitive theorem; the catalogue records its corollary

$$
\ell_m(v)=\left\lfloor\frac m2(v-1)+1\right\rfloor
$$

for all $m\ge2$. Substituting $v=1+t(m-1)$ recovers the conjectured edge threshold, or a stronger one. This source theorem has not been independently reconstructed or kernel-certified by GCL.

## GCL's current state

A useful exact semantic check is the distinction between the two types of Menger connectivity. For **nonadjacent** endpoints $u,v$, the maximum number of internally vertex-disjoint $u$–$v$ paths equals the smallest number of other vertices whose removal disconnects them. If endpoints **are adjacent**, that direct edge supplies one independent path; remove that edge and apply the nonadjacent-vertex separator calculation to count all remaining paths. This avoids incorrectly treating an adjacent pair as having an ordinary finite vertex separator without special handling.

The executable fixture at governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_914_915.py exhausts all relevant labelled graphs with three to six vertices for the small $m=2,3$ thresholds, checking **28,661 graph-and-parameter instances**. It computes internal connectivity by exact separator enumeration, with separate accounting for an endpoint edge. It also validates the algebraic clique-bouquet vertex and edge counts for $2\le m\le8$ and $t\in\{1,2,3,5\}$.

This is finite source-statement regression, not an independent computational replay of Leonard's much larger 57-vertex counterexample, the full Sørensen–Thomassen formula, or Mader's universal theorem.

## What remains

First source-lock the ambiguous word **disjoint** in the original 1962 and 1967 texts, preserving two explicitly different conjectures. A bounded lemma suitable for formalization is the clique-bouquet construction, including the single common articulation vertex, exact counts and upper bounds of $m-1$ on both path-connectivity notions. Another is the adjacent-endpoint correction to the standard vertex form of Menger's theorem. Acceptance requires complete elementary proofs or pinned no-placeholder Lean with actual axiom readback, plus all finite fixture cases.

For the **vertex-disjoint negative branch**, retrieve Leonard's 1973 original paper and reconstruct its 57-vertex, 141-edge graph as an explicit labelled edge list, then independently verify every unordered pair has internal path-connectivity at most four. For the **edge-disjoint affirmative branch**, independently review Mader's degree-sensitive theorem, the exact strict inequality and integer rounding that yield the displayed formula. These branches must not be merged into a single unjustified universal claim.
