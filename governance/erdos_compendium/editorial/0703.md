## The question in plain language

Define T(n,r) as the maximum number of subsets in a family of {1,...,n} such that distinct members never meet in EXACTLY r points. Erdős asked about its size, especially whether for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that T(n,r)<(2−δ)^n whenever εn<r<(1/2−ε)n. The maintained catalogue says this positive answer was PROVED by Frankl–Rödl [FrRo87]. Distinguish r fixed from r proportional to n.

## A small example

For n=4,r=1, the family {∅,{1},{2},{3},{4}} has five sets; any two distinct singleton sets are disjoint and the empty set meets none, so no pair meets in one point. Another valid construction for any r is the full family of all subsets with size strictly less than r, since no intersection can have size r.

## What is known

The maintained source attributes to Frankl–Füredi [FrFu84b] exact extremal descriptions for FIXED r and sufficiently large n, subject to separate parity conditions on n+r. It attributes the crucial proportional-r exponential saving to Frankl and Rödl, 'Forbidden intersections', Transactions AMS 300 (1987), 259–286, independently corroborated in Rödl's author publication listing. It does not say that T(n,r) has one exact closed formula for arbitrary n and growing r. A source claim that the result implies exponential growth in Euclidean distance-graph chromatic number is a related consequence, not the same theorem. No GCL protected admitted FC #703 file.
