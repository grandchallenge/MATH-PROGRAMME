## The question in plain language

Erdős Problem #257 asks whether every infinite set of positive integers $A$ gives an irrational number

$$
S(A)=\sum_{a\in A}\frac{1}{2^a-1}.
$$

The word “every” is the whole difficulty: $A$ may be dense, sparse, made of primes, or have arbitrarily complicated common divisors. It is not enough to settle one natural set or one family with extra structure. The original source packet writes $A\subseteq\mathbb N$, but $a=0$ makes the displayed denominator zero. This article therefore uses $\mathbb N_{>0}$. The pinned Lean file quantifies over `Set ℕ` and relies on Lean’s division-by-zero convention, where its $a=0$ summand is $0$; that is a formalization convention, not the ordinary mathematical domain.

For positive $a$, expand the geometric series and collect the coefficient of $2^{-m}$. If

$$
f_A(m)=\bigl|\{a\in A:a\mid m\}\bigr|,
$$

then nonnegative-term reindexing gives

$$
S(A)=\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{f_A(m)}{2^m}.
$$

For $A=\mathbb N_{>0}$, $f_A(m)=\tau(m)$, the divisor-counting function. For the prime set, $f_A(m)=\omega(m)$, the number of distinct prime divisors. These identities explain why a question about a subseries becomes a question about arithmetic functions, but they do not themselves prove irrationality.

## A small example

Take the finite set $A=\{1,2,3\}$. Its direct sum is exactly

$$
S(A)=1+\frac13+\frac17=\frac{31}{21}.
$$

For $M=12$, let $R_M=\sum_{m=1}^{M}f_A(m)2^{-m}$. Exact rational arithmetic gives

$$
R_{12}=\frac{6045}{4096},\qquad S(A)-R_{12}=\frac{31}{86016}.
$$

Every term arising from $a\in A$ has the geometric expansion $\sum_{k\geq1}2^{-ak}$. The terms with $ak>M$ are bounded by the full geometric tail $\sum_{j>M}2^{-j}=2^{-M}$ for each $a$, so

$$
0\leq S(A)-R_M\leq |A|2^{-M}=\frac{3}{4096}.
$$

This independently checks both the divisor-count reindexing and a useful finite tail estimate. A short reproducible check is:

```python
from fractions import Fraction
A = {1, 2, 3}
S = sum((Fraction(1, 2**a - 1) for a in A), Fraction())
M = 12
R = sum((Fraction(sum(m % a == 0 for a in A), 2**m)
         for m in range(1, M + 1)), Fraction())
print(S, R, S - R, Fraction(len(A), 2**M))
```

It prints `31/21 6045/4096 31/86016 3/4096`. This is a finite arithmetic check, not evidence that the infinite sum is irrational and not a proof of the problem.

## What is known

The full-support case is classical. In “On arithmetical properties of Lambert series” (1948), p. 63, Paul Erdős states the theorem that for every integer $|t|>1$, the Lambert series $f(1/t)=\sum_{n\geq1}1/(t^n-1)$ is irrational. Taking $t=2$ proves the case $A=\mathbb N_{>0}$. The same paper’s identity $\sum_{n\geq1}d(n)t^{-n}=\sum_{n\geq1}(t^n-1)^{-1}$ is the source of the divisor-count formulation. This is a published classical solution of one special case, not the universal assertion.

Erdős’s “On the irrationality of certain series” (Math. Student 36, 1968, 222–226) gives another substantial special case. The unnumbered theorem on printed p. 222 assumes an increasing sequence $n_1<n_2<\cdots$ with $\gcd(n_i,n_j)=1$ for $i\ne j$ and $\sum_i1/n_i<\infty$; it concludes that, for every integer $t\geq2$,

$$
\sum_{i\geq1}\frac{1}{t^{n_i}-1}
$$

is irrational. Thus it applies at $t=2$ to infinite pairwise-coprime $A$ whose reciprocal sum converges. The proof occupies pp. 223–225. The paper says that the coprimality hypothesis is superfluous by more complicated arguments, but gives no such argument there. That sentence is a research remark, not a proof of #257.

The prime-support case is now a published-source result. Tao and Teräväinen, “Quantitative correlations and some problems on prime factors of consecutive integers,” arXiv:2512.01739v2 (2026), Theorem 1.3 on printed p. 4, proves unconditionally that

$$
\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{\omega(n)}{2^n}=\sum_p\frac{1}{2^p-1}
$$

is irrational. The paper says that a similar modification also handles the prime-power set, but explicitly leaves those details to the reader. The maintained problem page calls both cases settled; the prime case matches the theorem, while the prime-power wording is currently source-limited and should not be promoted here to an independently checked proof.

Kovač and Tao’s “On several irrationality problems for Ahmes series” (arXiv:2406.17593v4, Theorem 2.5 and Corollary 2.6, printed p. 7) concerns sums with variable integer shifts in the denominators. Its rationality constructions and its negative result for a shifted “Type 3” property do not alter the fixed denominator $2^a-1$ in Problem #257. Treating that neighboring result as a counterexample to #257 would change the problem.

## What remains

The original universal statement still asks for irrationality for every infinite $A\subseteq\mathbb N_{>0}$. Existing theorems require either $A=\mathbb N_{>0}$, pairwise coprimality plus reciprocal convergence, or the special prime structure. A future proof must control the unrestricted functions $f_A(m)$ without silently importing one of those hypotheses. The finite identity and tail bound are useful infrastructure, but they do not supply an irrationality criterion.

There are also documentary obligations. The $\mathbb N$ versus $\mathbb N_{>0}$ convention should be normalized in any formal proof; the live page’s prime-power claim needs a semantic check against a complete proof; and a formal compilation at the pinned provider revision would still be a formal replay, not by itself GCL mathematical certification. The safe current classification is therefore: open original problem; classical solved subcases; an unconditional published prime subcase; a source-reported but detail-omitted prime-power extension; and no accepted universal proof.
