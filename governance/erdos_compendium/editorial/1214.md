## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1214](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1214) currently has source status **PROVED — CORRALES-RODRIGÁÑEZ–SCHOOF**.

For integers $x,y\ge1$, suppose for **every** integer $n\ge1$ the set of distinct prime divisors of $x^n-1$ equals that of $y^n-1$. Must $x=y$? This compares **prime supports**, not full prime-power valuations or numerical equality. The displayed domain $x,y\ge1$ presents a convention issue at $x=1$, since $1^n-1=0$ and the set of prime divisors of 0 is not ordinarily defined. Source-normalize to $x,y>1$ unless an explicit support-of-zero convention is provided.

## A small example

Compare $(x,y)=(2,3)$ for $n=1,\dots,6$. At $n=1$, $2^1-1=1$ has no prime divisors while $3^1-1=2$ has prime support $\{2\}$, immediately distinguishing the bases. Phase25 also lists the distinct-prime supports of all six exponents for both. This rejects one particular unequal pair but does not reproduce the general Corrales-Rodrigáñez–Schoof theorem.

## What is known

The maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/1214 reports this Erdős Banff 1988 question **PROVED YES** by Corrales-Rodrigáñez–Schoof [CoSc97], originally on prime divisors of $x^n-1$. The theorem is substantially stronger than a few small-exponent distinctions. A condition valid for **all** $n\ge1$ may force the bases equal even though distinct bases can share prime divisors for many individual exponents. The precise statement and handling of $x=1$ need primary proof verification, and formalization is not independently kernel-run.
