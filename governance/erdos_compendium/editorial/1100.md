## The question in plain language

List the positive divisors of an integer $N$ in increasing order. How often do **two neighboring divisors** have no common prime factor?

Write $\tau_\perp(N)$ for the number of adjacent pairs $(d_i,d_{i+1})$ with

$$
\gcd(d_i,d_{i+1})=1.
$$

[Erdős Problem #1100](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1100) asks several related but separate questions about the growth of this statistic.

## A small example

For $N=30$, the ordered divisors are $1,2,3,5,6,10,15,30$. Four consecutive pairs are coprime:

$$
(1,2),\ (2,3),\ (3,5),\ (5,6).
$$

Therefore $\tau_\perp(30)=4$. There are three distinct prime factors of $30$, so $\omega(30)=3$.

For $N=12$, the consecutive pairs $(1,2),(2,3),(3,4)$ are coprime, whereas $(4,6)$ and $(6,12)$ are not. Hence $\tau_\perp(12)=3$ and $\omega(12)=2$.

These examples illustrate the statistic; neither predicts its average or maximum for large numbers.

## What is known

The maintained catalogue credits Erdős and Hall (1978) with foundational bounds and Erdős–Simonovits with results concerning

$$
g(k)=\max_{\substack{\omega(N)=k\\N\text{ squarefree}}}
\tau_\perp(N).
$$

It reports exponential-in‑$k$ lower and upper estimates, respectively of the rough forms $(\sqrt2+o(1))^k$ and $(2-c)^k$.

The major **open** questions include whether

$$
\frac{\tau_\perp(N)}{\omega(N)}\to\infty
$$

for *almost all* integers $N$, and whether a proposed slowly growing upper bound holds for **every** $N$. The first statement concerns density-one typical behavior; the second concerns rare extremal values. The restriction to **squarefree** $N$ applies to the definition of $g(k)$, not to the other questions.

The original published asymptotic arguments and recent online proof claims have not been independently assessed.

## What remains

Review the original Erdős–Hall and Erdős–Simonovits bounds, investigate the status of the two online proof claims, and compute reproducible larger finite tables. None of these finite experiments can prove density-one convergence.
