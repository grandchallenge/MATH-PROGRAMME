## The question in plain language

Select as many distinct integers as possible from $\{1,\ldots,N\}$, while forbidding a certain configuration: **no $r$ of the selected integers, where $r\ge3$ is fixed, may have the same greatest common divisor for every pair**.

For example, $6,10,14$ form a forbidden triple because

$$
\gcd(6,10)=\gcd(6,14)=\gcd(10,14)=2.
$$

By contrast, $6,10,15$ do **not** form a forbidden triple:

$$
\gcd(6,10)=2,\qquad
\gcd(6,15)=3,\qquad
\gcd(10,15)=5.
$$

Define

$$
f_r(N)=\max\{|A|:A\subseteq[1,N],\
\text{no }r\text{ distinct members of }A
\text{ have one common pairwise gcd}\}.
$$

How large can $f_r(N)$ be? The conjecture is that for each fixed $r\ge3$, there is a constant $C_r>0$ such that

$$
\boxed{
f_r(N)\le
\exp\left(C_r\frac{\log N}{\log\log N}\right)
=N^{C_r/\log\log N}
}
$$

for sufficiently large $N$. The value of $C_r$ may depend on $r$, but **not** on $N$.

This is not the same as avoiding $r$ pairwise **coprime** integers. That would forbid only common gcd one; the actual condition forbids **every** possible shared gcd $d\ge1$.

## A small example

The two triples above illustrate how the greatest common divisor connects the question to a classical problem about intersecting sets.

Factor an integer $a\ge1$ into primes. For each prime $p$ dividing $a$, record one **layer** for each occurrence of $p$ in its factorization:

$$
L(a)=\{(p,j):p\text{ prime},\ 1\le j\le v_p(a)\},
$$

where $v_p(a)$ is the exponent of $p$ in $a$.

For example,

$$
L(12)=\{(2,1),(2,2),(3,1)\},
\qquad
L(18)=\{(2,1),(3,1),(3,2)\}.
$$

Their intersection is $\{(2,1),(3,1)\}=L(6)$, corresponding to $\gcd(12,18)=6$. In general,

$$
\boxed{L(a)\cap L(b)=L(\gcd(a,b)).}
$$

This identity is exact because the valuation of $\gcd(a,b)$ is $\min(v_p(a),v_p(b))$ at every prime.

A collection of $r$ sets is a **sunflower** if the intersection of every pair is the same set, called its *core*. Consequently,

$$
\boxed{
\gcd(a_i,a_j)=d\text{ for every }i\ne j
\iff
L(a_1),\ldots,L(a_r)
\text{ form a sunflower with core }L(d).
}
$$

This is an equivalence between an arithmetic configuration and a set-theoretic one. The prime-power layers are essential: recording only the *distinct* prime factors loses multiplicities. For instance, $2,4,8$ have the same squarefree prime support, but $\gcd(2,8)=2$ while $\gcd(4,8)=4$.

## What is known

### The original Erdős bounds and the normalization of the extremal function

Paul Erdős, *On a Problem in Elementary Number Theory and a Combinatorial Problem*, *Mathematics of Computation* **18** (1964), 644–646, [original paper](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1964-10.pdf), introduced this configuration. The complete original three-page paper was obtained and its first page visually inspected.

Erdős originally used $f_r^{\mathrm{force}}(N)$ for the **least** size of a subset of $[N]$ guaranteed to contain the forbidden $r$-tuple. Therefore,

$$
f_r^{\mathrm{force}}(N)=f_r(N)+1
$$

with the modern maximum-avoiding-set definition used in this article. This off-by-one distinction has no effect on the asymptotic bounds, but matters in exact finite tests.

The 1964 paper established a lower bound of the form

$$
f_r(N)\ge
\exp\left(c_r\frac{\log N}{\log\log N}\right)
$$

and an upper bound $f_r(N)\le N^{3/4+o(1)}$. H. L. Abbott and D. Hanson improved the upper exponent to $1/2$ in *An extremal problem in number theory*, *Bulletin of the London Mathematical Society* **2** (1970), 324–326, [DOI 10.1112/blms/2.3.324](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms/2.3.324).

These bounds show that Erdős's conjectured exponential-in-$\log N/\log\log N$ scale is plausible, but the historical square-root upper estimate was not the final word.

### Sunflowers and the modern subpolynomial bound

The exact layer identity above allows one to use **sunflower theorems** to constrain the size of an arithmetic family with no constant-pairwise-gcd $r$-tuple.

The classical Erdős–Rado sunflower bound for families of $k$-element sets includes a factorial $k!$. The original conjectured stronger sunflower bound replaces this factorial growth by a constant raised to the $k$th power. A suitable improved bound would yield Erdős's desired $N^{C_r/\log\log N}$ estimate.

Modern sunflower results yield the established weaker but still **subpolynomial** estimate, as recorded in the [April 29, 2026 catalogue update](https://www.erdosproblems.com/535):

$$
\boxed{
f_r(N)\le
\exp\left(
O_r\left(\frac{\log N\log\log\log N}{\log\log N}\right)
\right)=N^{o(1)}.
}
$$

This is much smaller than $N^{1/2+o(1)}$. The proof requires more than simply applying a sunflower lemma to all integers in $[N]$ at once: the numbers must be organized by factorization scale and size, with careful counting of small and large prime factors.

The modern sunflower literature includes Ryan Alweiss, Shachar Lovett, Kewen Wu and Jiapeng Zhang, *Improved bounds for the sunflower lemma*, *Annals of Mathematics* **192** (2020), 1105–1186, [DOI 10.4007/annals.2020.192.3.5](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2020.192.3.5), and the quantitative variant used by Bell, Chueluecha and Warnke. The precise extension from a fixed-uniform family to the necessary bounded-size set family is a source-sensitive step, not an automatic quotation of the original 2020 theorem.

### Why the stronger historical auxiliary conjecture is different

In his [1973 survey, printed p.123](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1973-21.pdf#page=7), Erdős records Abbott's warning that the **ordinary** sunflower conjecture did not appear sufficient for the intended arithmetic argument. Erdős then formulates a stronger auxiliary problem about numbers with exactly $k$ prime factors **counted with multiplicity**, written $\Omega(a)=k$.

It forbids $r$ numbers with constant pairwise gcd $d$ **and** an additional quotient condition $\gcd(a_i/d,d)=1$ for each chosen number. A conjectural exponential-in-$k$ upper bound for such families would imply the desired bound on $f_r(N)$. This stronger statement must not be confused with either the usual squarefree-support sunflower theorem or the original condition on arbitrary integers.

### An April 2026 exposition, not a solved-status change

An [April 2026 discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/535) by Hrishi Sunder, Sourish Kumrawat and Kireet Cheri presents the known upper estimate and an explicit lower-bound constant $\log(r-1)$, using prime-block choices and prime-power divisibility layers. The authors disclose assistance from ChatGPT-5.5 Thinking. A subsequent correction identifies Bell–Chueluecha–Warnke, rather than relying on Alweiss–Lovett–Wu–Zhang alone, for the specific sunflower statement used.

The independent catalogue commentary considers the note an exposition of the **already known** bounds, not a solution or newly established priority. Its arguments are useful as a mathematical roadmap, but their provenance cannot be elevated to peer-reviewed acceptance or to a GCL original theorem.

## What remains

The scientific objective is to eliminate the extra $\log\log\log N$ factor in the proven upper-bound exponent or identify an obstruction. A reasonable first target is a stronger **structured sunflower lemma** for prime-power layer families with the relevant arithmetic constraints. It would require a theorem with explicit hypotheses, not an extrapolation from small cases.
