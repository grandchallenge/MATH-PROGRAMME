## The question in plain language

How many points in $d$ dimensions are needed to guarantee at least $n$ **different positive distances** among their pairs?

Write this threshold as $g_d(n)$. [Erdős Problem #1089](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1089) asks particularly what happens as the dimension becomes very large while the required number $n$ of different distances stays **fixed**.

It is closely related to [Problem #1083](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1083), which instead starts with a fixed number of points and asks how few distances they can determine.

## A small example

On a line, the four distinct points $0,1,2,3$ determine three positive distances: $1,2,3$. In contrast, the three points $0,1,2$ determine only $1$ and $2$. In fact, any four distinct collinear points determine at least three distances by examining the gaps between consecutive ordered points. Hence $g_1(3)=4$.

The interesting asymptotic regime is not dimension one, but dimension $d\to\infty$ at fixed distance target $n$.

## What is known

**The limiting question is solved.** The maintained catalogue gives the two bounds

$$
\binom{d+1}{n-1}+1
\ \le\ g_d(n)\ \le\
\binom{d+n-1}{n-1}+1.
$$

The upper bound is attributed to Bannai, Bannai and Stanton (1983), while the lower bound is attributed to the Aletheia research reported by Feng and colleagues (2026). The original proofs of these bounds have not been independently replayed by GCL.

The limit follows from the bounds by elementary algebra. For every **fixed** positive integer $m$,

$$
\binom{d+c}{m}=\frac{d^m}{m!}+O_m(d^{m-1})
$$

when $c$ is fixed. Taking $m=n-1$ and squeezing between the two binomial expressions yields

$$
\boxed{\lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{g_d(n)}{d^{n-1}}
=\frac1{(n-1)!}.}
$$

For example, the limiting constant for $n=3$ is $1/2$.

[Authenticated source audit #2360](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/2360#issuecomment-6162322585) separates the externally established geometric bounds from the elementary asymptotic deduction.

The maintained catalogue reports a newer formalized statement, but there is no #1089 file in the protected older formal-conjectures snapshot. The original 1983 and 2026 proof sources and current formal version require independent review.

## What remains

Verify the mathematical hypotheses and precise integer boundaries in both published bounding theorems. The fixed‑$n$ squeeze is straightforward once those inequalities are granted. Do not present it as an original GCL theorem, and do not extend the limit to growing $n$ without new uniform estimates.
