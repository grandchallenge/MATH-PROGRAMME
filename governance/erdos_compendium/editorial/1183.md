## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1183](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1183) asks the following. The maintained status is **OPEN**.

Two-color **every subset** of $[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}$. Define $f(n)$ as the guaranteed maximum size of a **monochromatic family** closed under both **union AND intersection**. Define $F(n)$ analogously with closure under **union only**. The family need not be a full sublattice of $2^{[n]}$; it must simply be internally closed under the specified operations. Ask for sharp sizes, whether $F(n)\ge n^{\omega(n)}$ for some diverging $\omega(n)$, and an upper bound $F(n)<(1+o(1))^n$. One-color classes alone are **not** necessarily closed families.

## A small example

For $n=3$, the Boolean lattice has eight subsets and **256** red/blue colorings. Exhaustively enumerate all nonempty families of the eight subsets and test closure under union and under both union/intersection. There are respectively **121** union-closed and **73** meet-and-join-closed nonempty families. Minimizing the largest available monochromatic closed family across all colorings yields **$f(3)=F(3)=2$**, exactly. This finite coincidence does not predict superpolynomial $F(n)$ as $n\to\infty$.

## What is known

The maintained catalogue https://www.erdosproblems.com/1183 lists this Erdős–Ulam question **OPEN** [Er78,p.39]. A maximal chain of $n+1$ nested subsets is automatically closed under both operations, so by the pigeonhole principle it contains a monochromatic subchain of at least $\lceil(n+1)/2\rceil$ members. This is the elementary lower bound noted by Erdős; it does not imply strong growth. A Howorka result on **cardinality-class colorings**, cited without a located reference, gives superpolynomial $F(n)$ for a restricted class of colorings and does **not** settle arbitrary 2-colorings.
