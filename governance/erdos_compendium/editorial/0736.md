## The question in plain language

Given a graph G that needs ℵ₁ colours, is it possible to build a graph of **every** cardinal chromatic number m while using only finite graph patterns that already occur in G? This is Walter Taylor's question, [Erdős #736](https://www.erdosproblems.com/736). The maintained source labels the universal statement **NOT PROVABLE**.

## A small example

A triangle-free graph has no triangle among any of its finite subgraphs; a graph that uses only its finite patterns must also be triangle-free. This illustrates the meaning of pattern containment, but finite triangle-freeness alone says nothing about arbitrarily high cardinal chromatic numbers.

## What is known

The maintained source credits Komjáth–Shelah [KoSh05] with a relative-consistency result: in a suitable model there is an ℵ₁-chromatic graph G whose finite-subgraph patterns cannot be realized by any graph H with chromatic number exceeding ℵ₂. Therefore the unconditional demand for every cardinal m fails in that model. This is a model-theoretic **not-provable** result, not an elementary finite counterexample or a proof that the statement fails under every possible set-theoretic axiom system.
