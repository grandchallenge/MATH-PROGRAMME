## The question in plain language

For fixed n and m, define the minimal guaranteed independence alpha(G) over n-vertex G such that EVERY induced m-vertex subgraph has an independent set of size at least log n. The maintained statement inconsistently names f(m,n) and f(n); retain two-variable f(m,n) for clarity. Erdős–Hajnal conjectured f((log n)^2,n)>=n^(1/2-o(1)), and f((log n)^3,n)>> (log n)^3.

The function has two arguments: both the ambient order n and the subset scale m matter. The hypothesis concerns EVERY induced m-vertex subgraph, while the conclusion concerns ONE global independent set. The two specific strong predictions were refuted; the extremal optimization and its known logarithmic estimates remain mathematically meaningful.

## A small example

In complete graph K4, every induced 2-vertex subgraph has alpha=1, while in empty graph on 4 vertices every induced 2-vertex subgraph has alpha=2 and global alpha=4. These fixtures distinguish alpha(G) from minimum alpha over induced m-subgraphs; they are not asymptotic evidence.

This finite fixture tests the conventions. It does not certify any of the asymptotic or all-graphs assertions.

## What is known

Maintained #804 reports Alon–Sudakov [AlSu07] bounds f((log n)^2,n) between c (log n)^2/loglog n and C (log n)^2, and f((log n)^3,n) asymptotic (log n)^2/loglog n. Thus both ambitious conjectural comparisons fail. 'DISPROVED' refers to subquestions, not the existence of the f optimization. The [authors' 2007 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.4099) and [Princeton publication record](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.20264) directly establish the cubic-scale independence result; complete proofs, and the exact squared-scale rounding, have not been independently replayed. quantifier and parameter rounding need independent source audit.

The distinctions between established partial theorems, original open parts, and disproved universal claims have been preserved. Historical publications are attributed, rather than presented as new GCL mathematics.

The recorded Alon–Sudakov bounds compare the two regimes

$$
\frac{(\log n)^2}{\log\log n}
\ll f((\log n)^2,n)\ll(\log n)^2
$$

and

$$
f((\log n)^3,n)\asymp \frac{(\log n)^2}{\log\log n}.
$$

The notation $f(m,n)$ makes the two independent size parameters explicit. Exact floor and ceiling conventions are part of the outstanding original-source review.
