## The question in plain language

Two-colour Ramsey theory asks how large a complete graph must be before any red-blue colouring forces either a red copy of a fixed graph $G$ or a blue copy of another graph $H$. Write this number $r(G,H)$.

We call $G$ **Ramsey size-linear** if there is a constant $C_G$ such that

$$
r(G,H)\le C_G e(H)
$$

for *every* graph $H$ without isolated vertices. Erdős #79 asks whether infinitely many graphs fail this property even though every **proper subgraph** has it.

## A small example

$K_3$ is Ramsey size-linear: Sidorenko's theorem gives $r(K_3,H)\le 2e(H)+1$ for graphs $H$ without isolated vertices. By contrast, $K_4$ is not Ramsey size-linear. In fact every proper subgraph of $K_4$ is Ramsey size-linear. The original question asks for infinitely many such minimal obstructions, rather than a second isolated example.

A tree is Ramsey size-linear. Indeed, every graph $H$ without isolated vertices embeds in a complete graph on at most $2e(H)$ vertices. Applying Chvátal's exact tree-versus-complete-graph Ramsey formula bounds $r(T,H)$ linearly in $e(H)$.

## What is known

**PROVED.** Yuval Wigderson's paper [*Infinitely many minimally non-Ramsey size-linear graphs*](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.05931v2), revised 5 May 2025, establishes exactly this assertion in **Theorem 1**. Its proof is short and directly inspectable.

The three crucial inputs are: every forest is size-linear (Lemma 2); every graph with $e(G)\ge2v(G)-2$ is not size-linear (Lemma 3); and graphs of arbitrarily large girth with average degree at least four exist (Lemma 4). If there were finitely many minimal obstructions, select a high-girth graph avoiding all their cycle lengths. Its average degree forces non-size-linearity, so an inclusion-minimal non-size-linear subgraph gives a new obstruction, contradiction.

Wigderson explicitly emphasizes that this is a nonconstructive proof. Identifying a concrete new minimal obstruction other than $K_4$ is still an open *successor question*, not the original assertion.

## What remains

A bounded proof-replay task is to formalize the minimal-obstruction contradiction assuming three explicit lemmas, with a parameter $g$ greater than every forbidden cycle length. Acceptance: prove the resulting graph cannot contain any member of a *finite* obstruction list and still contains a new inclusion-minimal non-size-linear subgraph. Reject arguments that assume a computable minimal-obstruction classifier, or claim an explicit example, which the paper does not provide.
