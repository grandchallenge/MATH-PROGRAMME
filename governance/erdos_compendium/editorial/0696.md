## The question in plain language

For n≥1 define h(n) as the maximum length of an increasing chain of PRIME DIVISORS p_i|n satisfying p_{i+1}≡1 mod p_i; define H(n) similarly using ALL positive divisors d_i|n. Estimate their typical sizes and ask whether H(n)/h(n)→∞ for almost every integer n. The catalogue now calls this multi-part question SOLVED (externally LEAN-reported) because it attributes comparable upper and lower estimates on the scale of the iterated logarithm log_* n, which would preclude a ratio tending to infinity.

## A small example

Let n=2·3·7·29=1218. The prime factors form the chain 2,3,7,29, since 3≡1 mod2, 7≡1 mod3 and 29≡1 mod7. Thus h(1218)≥4 and H(1218)≥4. Including divisor 1 gives a five-term divisor chain 1,2,3,7,29, so H(1218)≥5 under the usual convention that 1 counts as a positive divisor. The finite chain has no implications about almost-all growth.
