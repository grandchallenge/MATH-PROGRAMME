## The question in plain language

The arithmetic condition that every product plus one has a squared prime factor is rigid enough to force one of two residue classes near the optimum. Sawhney's reported stability theorem addresses all sufficiently large N, not an exact statement for every small finite N.

Among A subset [1,N] satisfying ab+1 NOT squarefree for ALL a,b in A, including diagonal, ask whether the maximizing examples are the residue class 7 (mod25); residue 18 (mod25) is symmetric via quadratic roots of -1 modulo25. The theorem concerns all SUFFICIENTLY LARGE N, not every small N. This classification is distinct from problem #844, which forbids squarefree ab without +1.

## A small example

Take A={7,32,57}: all ≡7 (mod25), so for every a,b in A including equal terms, ab+1 ≡7²+1≡0 (mod25), not squarefree. If we mix 7 and 18, then 7*18+1=127, prime and squarefree, so the two residues cannot generally be combined. The eventual extremizer may be either full residue class, not just 7.

This finite calculation tests definitions, not a result quantified over all large N or infinite sets.

## What is known

Maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/848 read 2026-10-14 labels DECIDABLE but says problem SOLVED for sufficiently large N by Sawhney, with a note proving: for some c>0, any valid A with |A|>=(1/25-c)N must lie entirely in either residue 7 (mod25) or 18 (mod25). For either residue A, every ab+1 divisible by 25 since 7²+1=50 and 18²+1=325 and both roots satisfy x²=-1 mod25. Published note title/source URL and full stability proof require primary review. Separate van Doorn bound ≈0.108 reduced by Weisenberg to ≈0.105 does not establish exact 1/25.

Both residue classes 7 and 18 modulo 25 solve the necessary local congruence because

$$

7^2+1=50,\qquad18^2+1=325,

$$

both divisible by 25. If all elements of A are in either ONE of those residue classes, every ab+1 is divisible by 25 and hence not squarefree. The classes cannot be freely mixed: $7\cdot18+1=127$ is squarefree. The source reports an eventual STABILITY theorem putting every sufficiently large near-maximizer within a single class, a much stronger assertion than the earlier numerical density upper bounds. The registry tag DECIDABLE must not obscure this source-reported proof.
