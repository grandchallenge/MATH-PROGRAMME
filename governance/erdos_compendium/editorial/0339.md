## The question in plain language

Take a set $A$ of positive integers that is an additive basis of order $r$: every sufficiently large positive integer can be made by adding at most $r$ members of $A$, and no smaller bound has this property. Repetition is allowed in this ordinary sumset. Erdős and Graham asked whether the same order still has many representations when repetition is forbidden. In other words, define the restricted r-fold sumset by $r\times A=\{a_1+\cdots+a_r:a_i\in A\text{ and the }a_i\text{ are pairwise distinct}\}$. Must this set have positive lower asymptotic density?

Here “positive lower density” means that the eventual lower proportion of represented integers is nonzero: $\liminf_{x\to\infty}|(r\times A)\cap[1,x]|/x>0$. The maintained problem page records a companion question as well: if the ordinary r-fold sumset has positive upper density, must the restricted r-fold sumset have positive upper density? These are density assertions, not claims that every large integer has a distinct representation.

## A small example

Let $A$ be the odd positive integers and take $r=2$. Every sufficiently large odd integer is already in $A$, while every sufficiently large even integer is a sum of two odd integers, so $A$ is a basis of order $2$. For the restricted sumset, the exact calculation is transparent: if $n$ is even and $n\ge4$, then $n=1+(n-1)$, and the two summands are distinct odd integers. No odd integer is a sum of two odd integers, and $2$ has no representation by two distinct positive odd integers.

I checked this with exact integer enumeration of all pairs from $\{1,3,5,\ldots,21\}$ whose sum is at most $20$. The represented values are exactly $4,6,8,10,12,14,16,18,20$. This finite check illustrates both the parity and the distinctness condition. It does not prove a positive density statement for an arbitrary basis, and it does not reproduce the general theorem’s argument.

## What is known

Its live bibliography identifies the paper as *A proof of two Erdős' conjectures on restricted addition and further results*, Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik 560 (2003), 199–220, DOI [10.1515/crll.2003.055](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.2003.055). The live history shows the explanatory status was added on 20 October 2025, while the page currently says it was last edited 14 October 2025; this is presentation history, not a new mathematical result.

The primary paper’s abstract states the two relevant conclusions: positive upper density of an ordinary sumset implies positive upper density of the corresponding restricted sumset, and an additive basis of order $r$ has an r-fold restricted sumset of positive lower density. The maintained problem discussion identifies the direct implication as Theorem 4. I could not read the publisher’s complete 2003 text in this timebox, so the exact printed page on which Theorem 4 appears is not independently locked here; only the article’s journal page range and theorem number are reported. A later primary paper by the same authors, [*Answer to a Question by Burr and Erdős on Restricted Addition, and Related Results*](https://www.cmls.polytechnique.fr/perso/plagne/Erdos-Burr.pdf), was readable and confirms the notation separating repeated and pairwise-distinct sums.
