## The question in plain language

Let $A$ be a subset of the first $N$ positive integers. Say $A$ is **non-dividing** if no element $a\in A$ divides the sum of any nonempty set of **distinct** elements of $A\setminus\{a\}$. Define $F(N)$ to be the maximum size of such a set.

Erdős asked for the correct growth rate, and specifically whether

$$
F(N)>N^{1/2-o(1)}.
$$

This is a lower-growth question about the **largest** valid set, not the existence of any particular small construction. The “nonempty” condition prevents the empty subset sum zero from making the definition vacuous, because every positive integer divides zero.

## A small example

The set $A=\{2,3\}$ is non-dividing: $2$ does not divide $3$, and $3$ does not divide $2$. By contrast, $\{2,3,5\}$ fails because $5$ divides the sum $2+3=5$. One must check sums of subsets, not merely divisibility of pairs of chosen elements.

A non-dividing set is **non-averaging**: if an element $a$ equalled the average of $t$ other distinct chosen elements, their sum would be $ta$ and hence divisible by $a$, contradicting the hypothesis. The converse need not hold because divisibility can occur with quotient different from the subset size.

## What is known

**The proposed square-root lower growth is DISPROVED; the true order of $F(N)$ remains OPEN.** Erdős, Lev, Rauzy, Sándor and Sárközy showed

$$
F(N)<3\sqrt N+1,
$$

and gave lower constructions of order at least $N^{1/5}$, discussed in their 1999 work. The stronger 2024 theorem of Huy Tuan Pham and Dmitrii Zakharov, [*Sharp bound for the Erdős–Straus non-averaging set problem*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14624), proves that every non-averaging subset of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ has size at most $N^{1/4+o(1)}$. Since every non-dividing set is non-averaging, we obtain

$$
F(N)\le N^{1/4+o(1)}.
$$

This rules out $F(N)>N^{1/2-o(1)}$ for large $N$. However, an upper bound $N^{1/4+o(1)}$ does not prove matching **lower** constructions for the more restrictive non-dividing property.

The maintained [Erdős #131](https://www.erdosproblems.com/131), edited September 2025, explicitly distinguishes these conclusions. It retains the umbrella “estimate $F(N)$” problem as OPEN, while identifying the proposed square-root subquestion as answered negatively.

## What remains

A bounded task is to enumerate subsets of $\{1,\ldots,12\}$ and compare two exact predicates: non-dividing and non-averaging. Acceptance: $\{2,3\}$ passes, $\{2,3,5\}$ fails, and every set satisfying non-dividing also satisfies non-averaging. Provide at least one explicit set illustrating that the reverse logical implication is not being assumed. Stop at finite semantics, not an asymptotic theorem.
