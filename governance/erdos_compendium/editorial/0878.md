## The question in plain language

Let $n$ have distinct prime divisors $p_1,\ldots,p_t$. For each such prime, take the largest power of that prime not exceeding $n$, and add the resulting powers. This defines Erdős's unusual arithmetic function

$$
f(n)=\sum_{p\mid n}p^{\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor}.
$$

A related function $F(n)$ allows us to choose positive integers $a_1,\ldots,a_s\le n$ whose prime factors all divide $n$, subject to pairwise coprimality. We maximize their sum. The summand $1$ is permitted and contributes one when $n\ge1$. Hence $F(n)\ge f(n)$ for $n>1$.

[Erdős Problem #878](https://www.erdosproblems.com/878) is a collection of questions about the typical sizes, maximal orders, equality cases, and averages of these two functions. The conjectures are not equivalent and cannot be resolved together by checking a finite range.

## A small example

Take $n=210=2\cdot3\cdot5\cdot7$. The largest powers of its prime divisors that do not exceed 210 are $128$, $81$, $125$, and $49$. Thus

$$
f(210)=128+81+125+49=383.
$$

But $1$, $125$, $128$, and $189=3^3\cdot7$ are pairwise coprime. Each is at most $210$, and all their prime factors divide $210$. Their sum is

$$
F(210)\ge1+125+128+189=443.
$$

Our exact support-mask dynamic program independently finds **$F(210)=443$**, and a separate exhaustive calculation establishes $\max_{n\le210} f(n)=383$. Consequently, the equality of the two running maxima is **false at cutoff 210**. This disproves the strongest all-cutoffs version, not the original assertion about equality infinitely often.

## What is known

The primary source is P. Erdős, [*On Two Unconventional Number Theoretic Functions and on Some Related Problems*](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1984-16.pdf), *Calcutta Mathematical Society* jubilee volume, Part I (1984), pp.113–121. The paper makes several precise distinctions:

- **Theorem 3**, printed p.115, proves that $F(n)/n$ tends to infinity off a set of natural density zero. The proposed stronger typical lower bound of order $n\log\log n$ is not thereby established.
- **Theorem 4**, printed p.116, establishes that the following upper limit is exactly one. Erdős explicitly conjectures that the ratio actually tends to one; a limit superior is weaker than a full limit.

$$
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{\max_{n\le x}f(n)}{x\log x/\log\log x}=1.
$$

- The original **question (6)** on printed p.114 asks whether the two running maxima agree for *infinitely many* $x$. Erdős speculates that they might agree for all sufficiently large $x$, or even for every $x$. The cutoff-210 counterexample rules out that last, strongest possibility only.
- **Theorems 6 and 7**, printed pp.117–118, concern the sums of $f(n)/n$ and provide oscillation and upper bounds. Theorem 7 gives an upper estimate of order $x\log\log\log x$ for these sums. A proposed improvement to a fourth iterated logarithm remains separate.

In the [May 2026 updated catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/878), Kevin Barreto observed that the 1984 mean upper bound implies $f(n)=o(n\log\log n)$ for almost every $n$. The catalogue records the all-cutoffs counterexample at $210$ but continues to classify the multi-part problem **open**. The 1984 primary paper and its weaker infinite-cutoffs question should retain priority over a compressed modern paraphrase.

## GCL's current state

GCL implemented a reproducible exact optimizer in governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_878.py. It represents the available prime divisors of $n$ by bits. For each nonempty subset of those prime divisors, it finds the largest integer at most $n$ with precisely that prime support. A dynamic program partitions available supports into disjoint groups and maximizes the sum, adding the legal summand $1$.

The program computes $f(n)$ and $F(n)$ for every $1\le n\le250$. It checks all optimized witnesses for pairwise coprimality and source-prime support, and it cross-checks the optimizer against an independently programmed direct subset search for $n\le25$. The verified counterexample at 210 is exact, including the stronger result that 443 is the optimal $F(210)$, not just a lower bound.

There is a semantic subtlety: the single number $1$ may be included once; it has no prime factors and is coprime to all other permitted numbers. Neither functions' asymptotic questions are decided by the finite optimizer, and no reproduction of Erdős's original analytic arguments is claimed.

## What remains

A bounded successor is a **finite support-partition optimality lemma**. Given $n$, its distinct prime factors, and a table that gives the largest allowable integer for each nonempty prime support, prove that $F(n)$ equals one plus the maximum weight over partitions into mutually disjoint nonempty supports.

Acceptance requires a complete proof or pinned Lean theorem covering the legal summand $1$, exact computation of $F(210)=443$, independent validation of $\max_{n\le210}f(n)=383$, and an actual source-hash/axiom report if formalized. Falsification would be an admissible set with sum greater than the purported partition optimum or a failure of the support mapping. Stop at this finite reduction: proving asymptotic density statements, infinitely many equal running maxima, or the proposed iterated-logarithm mean bound requires separate analytic work.
