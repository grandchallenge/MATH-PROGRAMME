## The question in plain language

Is it possible to choose an **infinite** set $S$ of positive integers with two seemingly conflicting properties? It must be Sidon, meaning every unordered sum $s_1+s_2$ (including repeated elements) determines its pair uniquely. It must also be an *asymptotic basis of order three*, meaning every sufficiently large positive integer can be written as

$$
n=s_1+s_2+s_3,\qquad s_1,s_2,s_3\in S.
$$

The three summands in the basis condition **may repeat**, unlike the uniqueness requirement for unordered two-term sums. Requiring representation of every *positive* integer rather than every sufficiently large one would be a different claim.

## A small example

A small finite Sidon set such as $\{1,2,4\}$ has six distinct unordered pair sums and three-fold sumset contained in $[3,12]$. It is not an asymptotic basis, because a finite set has bounded sums. A naive greedy infinite Sidon sequence, by contrast, may be too sparse to represent all sufficiently large integers by three terms. The challenge is to preserve uniqueness while retaining additive coverage.

## What is known

**PROVED.** Cédric Pilatte's [*A solution to the Erdős–Sárközy–Sós problem on asymptotic Sidon bases of order 3*](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0010437X24007140), *Compositio Mathematica* 160 (2024), 1418–1432, constructs precisely such a set. The published primary abstract explicitly states both properties and that every sufficiently large $n$ is a sum of three elements of the Sidon set.

Pilatte's construction uses ideas from Ruzsa's infinite Sidon sequence and a deep result of Sawin on the $\mathbb F_q[t]$ analogue of Montgomery's conjecture for convolutions of the von Mangoldt function. The argument is not a simple consequence of a finite cyclic-group construction: a finite Sidon basis does not automatically concatenate into an infinite Sidon sequence without cross-block sum collisions.

Javier Cilleruelo's earlier [*Sidon basis*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.5351) obtained a $B_2[2]$ asymptotic basis of order three and separate near-basis results for genuine Sidon sets. A $B_2[2]$ set permits two representations of some sums, so that earlier result did **not** resolve the exact Sidon problem.

## What remains

A bounded semantic task checks two-term Sidon uniqueness and three-term coverage separately on finite truncations of proposed infinite sets. Acceptance: unordered sums including $s+s$ are unique, and an indicated finite interval is covered by $S+S+S$ with repeated summands allowed. This validates statement conventions, not the existence of one infinite set meeting both properties. A substantive proof audit would verify Pilatte's Section 4 Sidon property and Section 5 basis argument under their exact hypotheses.
