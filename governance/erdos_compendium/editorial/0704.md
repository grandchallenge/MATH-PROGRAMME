## The question in plain language

Let G_n be the infinite unit-distance graph of Euclidean R^n: vertices are all points and edges connect pairs exactly distance 1. Estimate its chromatic number χ(G_n), and does lim_{n→∞}χ(G_n)^(1/n) exist? The maintained source remains OPEN for a sharp exponential base and existence of the limit, even though EXPONENTIAL GROWTH ITSELF IS PROVED.

## A small example

In R^1, edges connect points differing exactly 1. Colour x by parity of floor(x). If y=x+1, then floor(y)=floor(x)+1 and the colours differ. Therefore χ(G_1)=2, since the graph has an edge and admits a two-colouring. The finite regular simplex of n+1 points in R^n has every pair at distance 1, so χ(G_n)≥n+1, but this polynomial lower bound is much weaker than known exponential estimates.

## What is known

Frankl–Wilson 1981 proved χ(G_n)≥(1+o(1))1.2^n; Raigorodsky 2000 improved its exponential lower base to 1.239... . Larman–Rogers 1972 proved χ(G_n)≤(3+o(1))^n, also re-proved by Prosanov 2020. These bounds establish exponential growth of chromatic number, but do not settle the EXISTENCE of χ(G_n)^(1/n) or its precise value. The cube-colouring bound (2+sqrt(n))^n is weaker.
