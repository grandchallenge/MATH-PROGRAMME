## The question in plain language

Can n points in R² determine at least c n DISTINCT distances, each occurring among more than n unordered pairs of distinct points? Erdős and Pach asked this in #756, which is PROVED by Bhowmick [Bh24]: a construction has floor(n/4) different distances, each occurring at least n+1 times. A stronger demand for EVERY distance except the diameter is a SEPARATE unresolved question.

## A small example

Four vertices of a unit square have four unit-distance pairs (its sides) and two diagonal-distance pairs. Since n=4, even the most frequent distance occurs only n, not strictly more than n. The square is not a counterexample to the existence claim for arbitrarily large n; its example demonstrates how to count UNORDERED pairs and distinguish ≥n from >n.

## What is known

The maintained source credits Bhowmick [Bh24] with the floor(n/4) ≥n+1 construction and more generally floor(n/(2(m+1))) distinct distances each occurring at least n+m pairs, for fixed m and n sufficiently large. Clemen–Dumitrescu–Liu [CDL25] studied lattice point frequency as a separate construction; the 'all distances except largest' version remains unproved. Hopf–Pannowitz [HoPa34] bounded diameter pairs by n, but this does not impose the same limit on smaller distances. External LEAN status reported, not a GCL protected kernel replay.
