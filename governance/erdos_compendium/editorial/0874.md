## The question in plain language

For a finite set of positive integers $A$, let $r^{\wedge}A$ be the collection of sums of $r$ **pairwise distinct** elements of $A$. The set is *admissible* if no integer is simultaneously a sum of $r$ elements and a sum of $s$ elements when $r\ne s$. The summands need not be uniquely determined; only their **number** must be.

Erdős asked how many members such a set can have inside $\{1,\ldots,N\}$. In particular, is the largest admissible set, when $N$ is sufficiently large, a terminal interval of consecutive integers ending at $N$? This exact eventual-extremal statement is stronger than the asymptotic claim that the maximum has size approximately $2\sqrt N$.

## A small example

For $N=20$, the terminal set

$$
T=\{13,14,15,16,17,18,19,20\}
$$

is admissible. Sums of exactly $r$ different members are consecutive integers, and the interval of sums of length $r$ finishes before the interval of sums of length $r+1$ begins. One cannot enlarge this terminal set to nine members without violating the required separation.

The exact finite verifier in governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_873_874.py uses two independent admissible-set maximization procedures for $N\le14$, followed by an incremental search through $N=36$. Every tested global maximum agrees with the terminal-interval construction. A finite table cannot establish an eventual theorem by itself.

## What is known

The primary proof is Jean-Marc Deshouillers and Gregory A. Freiman, [*On an Additive Problem of Erdős and Straus, 2*](https://www.numdam.org/item/AST_1999__258__141_0.pdf), *Astérisque* **258** (1999), pp.141–148. Its Theorem 1 on printed page 142 states that there is an effectively computable integer $N_0$ such that, for every $N\ge N_0$ and every admissible $A\subseteq[1,N]$,

$$
|A|\le 2\sqrt{N+\tfrac14}-1=\sqrt{4N+1}-1.
$$

The right-hand side is precisely the size achieved by the largest admissible terminal interval after taking its integer floor. The theorem therefore proves the original eventually-optimal terminal-interval conjecture. It does **not** assert that the terminal construction is uniquely extremal for every $N$, or that the proof has been independently formalized by GCL.

The authors' argument invokes their earlier structural result on large admissible sets. Theorem 2 of the 1999 article quotes this prerequisite explicitly, with a density condition $|A|>1.96\sqrt N$ and conclusions about containment in short arithmetic progressions modulo an integer $q$. Replaying Theorem 1's full proof requires this structural input.

## GCL's current state

GCL independently proved the *terminal-interval criterion*. Let $T_{N,k}=\{N-k+1,\ldots,N\}$. The restricted sumset of length $r$ for this interval is itself an unbroken interval:

$$
r^{\wedge}T_{N,k}
=\left[r(N-k+1)+\frac{r(r-1)}2,\quad
rN-\frac{r(r-1)}2\right]\cap\mathbb Z.
$$

To prevent different cardinalities from giving the same sum, it is enough, and necessary, to separate the intervals for consecutive values $r$ and $r+1$. For $1\le r<k$, their endpoints are disjoint exactly when

$$
N\ge (r+1)(k-r).
$$

The greatest right-hand side is $\lfloor(k+1)^2/4\rfloor$. Because $(k+1)^2$ is a square, the equivalent integer condition is $(k+1)^2\le4N+1$. Hence the maximum admissible terminal-interval length is

$$
k_{\rm tail}(N)=\lfloor\sqrt{4N+1}\rfloor-1.
$$

The formula is exact for terminal intervals for **all** $N$. The 1999 paper supplies the separate and much deeper theorem that *no other* admissible set can beat this cardinality when $N$ is sufficiently large. The finite computations reproduce the formula through $N=36$ using exact integer arithmetic.

## What remains

A bounded formalization task is the terminal-interval criterion, stated using restricted sums of **distinct** members and both endpoint inequalities. Input: $N,k$ positive integers with $1\le k\le N$ and $T_{N,k}=[N-k+1,N]\cap\mathbb Z$. Target: prove the sumset-interval identity and the exact equivalence between admissibility and $(k+1)^2\le4N+1$.

Acceptance requires a complete paper proof or compiled Lean statement without placeholders, exact checks for the small cases and the $N=36$ fixture, and a source-locked dependency map from the 1995 structural theorem to the 1999 Theorem 1. A counterexample to any endpoint inequality or a mismatch under repeated summands would invalidate the proposed formal target. Stop without claiming the global 1999 theorem is re-proved unless its imported structural lemma is independently verified.
