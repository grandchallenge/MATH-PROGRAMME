## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1157](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1157). How many hyperedges can a uniform hypergraph have when no small group of vertices supports a prescribed number of edges?

$$\operatorname{ex}_r(n,\mathcal F),\quad\mathcal F=\{H:|V(H)|=k,\ |E(H)|=s,\ H\ r\text{-uniform}\}.$$

For r-uniform hypergraphs, let F be the family of ALL r-graphs with k vertices and s edges, and ex_r(n,F) the largest number of edges in an n-vertex r-graph containing no member of F. This is equivalently forbidding s distinct edges supported on at most k vertices, subject to exact definitions and isolated vertices. Maintained question's introduction says 'Let t,k,r≥2' but uses s without binding it and does NOT use t in ex_r; flag source TYPO/variable mismatch, require 1≤s≤binom(k,r), k≥r. Don't confuse the cited Brown–Erdős–Sós t-uniform general conjecture with this r-uniform extremal quantity.

## A small example

For r=2,k=3,s=3 forbidden family is K3; Mantel's theorem ex_2(5,K3)=6, verified by exhaustive enumeration of 1024 labelled graphs. For r=3,k=4,s=2, any two 3-edges on 4 vertices violate, giving ex_3(4,F)=1. These exactly computed cases do not settle general asymptotics.

## What is known

https://www.erdosproblems.com/1157 OPEN, Brown–Erdős–Sós [BES73], Vardi [Va99,3.64]. For k>r,s>1 a general lower bound ex_r(n,F)≫_{k,s} n^{(rs-k)/(s-1)} is reported. Their broader conjecture ex_t(n,F)=o(n^t) for r>t≥2,s≥3,k≥(r-t)s+t+1 concerns separately parameterized t-uniform graphs and is linked #1178. Special 3-uniform (k,s) cases #716,#1076. Bound's significance depends on exponent and parameter conditions.
