## The question in plain language

Two players build a set of integers from $\{2,3,\ldots,n\}$, taking turns choosing a new integer. A choice is legal only if no two chosen integers divide one another. The game stops when no legal choice remains. **Prolonger** wants as many moves as possible; **Shortener** wants as few as possible. [Erdős Problem #872](https://www.erdosproblems.com/872) asks whether optimal play must last at least a positive fraction of $n$, and, more ambitiously, almost $n/2$ moves.

The key ambiguity is who moves first. The original statement, attributed to Erdős's 1992 problem collection (p. 47), does not fix this. The pinned FormalConjectures file chooses **Prolonger first**. This is mathematically consequential even for small $n$.

A set with no distinct $a,b$ satisfying $a\mid b$ is called *primitive*. In poset language, it is an antichain under divisibility. A completed game is a **maximal** antichain (no further element can be added), not necessarily a **maximum** antichain (largest possible cardinality). These two notions must not be confused.

## A small example

Let $D_n=\{2,\ldots,n\}$. A state is a chosen primitive set $S\subseteq D_n$. Define its legal moves by

$$
\mathcal L_n(S)=\{x\in D_n\setminus S:\
  \forall s\in S,\ x\nmid s\ \text{and}\ s\nmid x\}.
$$

The terminal value is $|S|$. With $V_P$ and $V_S$ denoting values when Prolonger or Shortener moves next, respectively,

$$
V_P(S)=\begin{cases}|S|,&\mathcal L_n(S)=\varnothing,\\
\max_{x\in\mathcal L_n(S)}V_S(S\cup\{x\}),&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}
$$

$$
V_S(S)=\begin{cases}|S|,&\mathcal L_n(S)=\varnothing,\\
\min_{x\in\mathcal L_n(S)}V_P(S\cup\{x\}),&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
$$

The recursion terminates because each move strictly enlarges $S$. An independently coded second algorithm stores only the remaining **legal** choices, removing a chosen integer and every integer comparable with it. The two encodings are equivalent because once a candidate becomes illegal, later moves cannot make it legal again. Thus its deletion from the working pool is safe.

The offline fixture checks these independent minimax encodings for every $2\le n\le30$, without any probabilistic approximation. Both algorithms agree in all 58 first-player cases.

## What is known

Selected exact values are:

| $n$ | Prolonger first | Shortener first | $\pi(n)$ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| 9 | 5 | 4 | 4 |
| 15 | 7 | 6 | 6 |
| 20 | 9 | 8 | 8 |
| 24 | 11 | 9 | 9 |
| 25 | 11 | 10 | 9 |
| 30 | 13 | 11 | 10 |

The two game values first differ at $n=9$. Moreover, at $n=25$ Shortener-first optimal play lasts **ten** moves even though $\pi(25)=9$. Thus the observation that Shortener-first values may be *close to* $\pi(n)$ must not be silently strengthened into equality. The full reproducible table covers all $2\le n\le30$.

There is a simple universal upper bound sharper than the $n-1$ bound in the pinned formal file. Partition $D_n$ into chains by odd part: for each odd $q\le n$, consider $q,2q,4q,\ldots$ restricted to $D_n$. Every integer belongs to exactly one chain, and distinct members of one chain divide one another. A primitive set contains at most one member of each chain. There are $\lceil n/2\rceil$ odd $q\le n$, so **every completed game** satisfies

$$
|S|\le\left\lceil\frac n2\right\rceil.
$$

Conversely, every prime $p\in(n/2,n]$ is isolated in the divisibility comparability graph on $D_n$: no other integer in $D_n$ divides $p$ or is divisible by $p$. Therefore **every maximal primitive set** contains all these primes, yielding the elementary lower bound

$$
|S|\ge\pi(n)-\pi(\lfloor n/2\rfloor).
$$

An independent exhaustive terminal-antichain enumerator checked that the *minimum* size of a maximal primitive set equals $\pi(n)$ for $2\le n\le22$. This is finite evidence only; the pinned formal provider explicitly treats the corresponding all $n$ assertion as an open variant.

## GCL's current state

The maintained [problem page](https://www.erdosproblems.com/872) and its [dated revision history](https://www.erdosproblems.com/history/872) record the question as open. The historical connection is to saturation games: in the graph analogue, players add edges without creating a triangle. The graph-game results cited there are analogies, not numerical bounds for the divisibility game.

The April 2026 discussion reports a Prolonger-first Shortener strategy yielding at most $(23/48+o(1))n$ moves, contradicting the near $n/2$ target under that convention. A later participant reports a stronger upper coefficient below $0.19$ and a lower bound of order $n\log\log n/\log n$. These are **forum or manuscript claims**, not independently reproduced asymptotic proofs or accepted published theorems in this audit. Neither supplies a positive linear lower bound, so the weak question remains open on the evidence examined.

The protected source `FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/872.lean` at commit `85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020`, blob `e1c94bfaf9ff2964bfa2664d9af467ea42d6d26b`, defines a genuine finite minimax game with Prolonger moving first, and separates weak and strong questions. Both target theorem bodies remain `sorry`; the source also contains an open variant about minimum maximal primitive-set size. Its own comment notes that the first-player convention is not innocuous.

## What remains

GCL has independently derived the two elementary terminal-size bounds, implemented two different exact minimax recursions, and replayed them for $n\le30$. A third independent enumeration verifies minimum maximal-antichain sizes for $n\le22$. These results can serve as a regression suite for the formal minimax definition, but do **not** prove any positive asymptotic fraction or certify the forum strategies.

A bounded formalization task is to prove that the **chosen-antichain recursion** and the **remaining-legal-moves recursion** have equal values, with explicit turn-order parameter, and to certify the exact values $V_P(\varnothing)=5$, $V_S(\varnothing)=4$ for $n=9$. This is small enough to falsify a misinterpreted formal model. Acceptance requires exact-head Lean compilation, a literal axiom report, and agreement with the independent Python certificates. Stop if the two recursions disagree or if the statement's first-player convention differs.

A separate bounded mathematical task is to source-lock the strongest available Shortener strategy, reproduce its density estimate with explicit errors, and establish the strategy against **adaptive** Prolonger moves. A static primitive-set construction alone does not certify a winning strategy. This source and adversarial-quantifier boundary is the main remaining obstacle for the reported asymptotic upper bounds.
