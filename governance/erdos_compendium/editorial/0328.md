## The question in plain language

Let $A$ be a set of natural numbers, and let $r_A(n)$ count ordered pairs $(a,b)$ from $A$ with $a+b=n$. Erdős Problem 328 asks whether a uniform bound on $r_A$ can always be improved inside finitely many pieces. More precisely, if $C>0$ and

$$
r_A(n)\le C\quad\text{for every }n,
$$

must there be a finite number $t=t(C)$ such that $A$ can be partitioned into $A_1,\ldots,A_t$ with

$$
r_{A_i}(n)<C\quad\text{for every }i\text{ and every }n?
$$

The important quantifier is that $t$ may depend on $C$, but not on the particular set $A$. Equivalently, imagine coloring the elements of $A$ with a bounded number of colors: can every color class have strictly fewer than $C$ ordered additive representations at every integer? The maintained problem page records the answer as no for every $C$, and attributes the question to Erdős and Newman.

## A small example

The finite set $A=\{1,2,4,8\}$ already shows the basic obstruction caused by ordered representations. The following calculation uses only exact integer arithmetic and enumerates every ordered pair.

```python
from collections import Counter

A = [1, 2, 4, 8]
r = Counter(x + y for x in A for y in A)
print(sorted(r.items()))
print(max(r.values()))
print(r[3], [(x, y) for x in A for y in A if x + y == 3])
assert max(r.values()) == 2
assert r[3] == 2
```

The verified output is

```text
[(2, 1), (3, 2), (4, 1), (5, 2), (6, 2), (8, 1), (9, 2), (10, 2), (12, 2), (16, 1)]
2
2 [(1, 2), (2, 1)]
```

Thus the powers-of-two pattern has at most two ordered representations in this finite sample, while two distinct elements in the same part already force two representations of their sum. The finite calculation does not establish anything about all natural numbers, infinite sets, or the universal quantifier over $C$. It is a check of the local counting convention only.

## What is known

For $C=1$, the negative answer is elementary but for a different reason: the singleton $A=\{a\}$ has $r_A(2a)=1$, so no partition of $A$ can make the representation count strictly less than one. The distinct-pair observation applies to $C=2$: if a part contains distinct $x$ and $y$, then $(x,y)$ and $(y,x)$ give two representations of $x+y$. The catalogue attributes the general negative answer to Theorem 2 of J. Nešetřil and V. Rödl, “Two proofs in combinatorial number theory,” *Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society* 93 (1985), 185–188, printed pp. 186–187. The paper constructs sequences with bounded additive representations that retain a representation obstruction under every finite partition. However, its $B_2^k$ convention does not plainly count ordered pairs, whereas the pinned Lean `sumRep` does. The paper is primary evidence for the classical disproof, but the implication to the exact ordered-pair formal statement has not been source-verified. We must not promote it as a proved semantic bridge.

The construction is not a powers-of-two shortcut for general $C$. Nešetřil and Rödl use ordered graphs with a Ramsey property. They form an ordered graph from special graphs $G_k^r$, then encode edges by sums of distinct powers of three. The key graph lemma is stated on page 186; the authors say its proof follows from their earlier partite-construction work rather than reproducing it in this short paper. This is a published external proof route, but the omitted lemma is an important dependency when assessing formal reuse.

The live page also lists Erdős’s earlier 1980 result for $C=3,4$ and infinitely many other values. That historical result is narrower than the Nešetřil–Rödl theorem and should not be substituted for it. Nešetřil–Rödl is the reported general literary resolution, with the $C=1$ case handled separately. Matching its representation convention to the pinned Lean theorem remains an explicit source-normalization task.

## What remains

The most useful bounded successor is a two-stage interface audit. First, formalize the exact $C=2$ witness $A=\{2^j:j\in\mathbb N\}$: prove its ordered representation count is at most two, and prove that every finite partition has an infinite part containing two distinct elements, hence a representation count at least two. This is a reusable elementary lemma and directly tests whether `sumRep` agrees with the paper’s ordered-pair convention.

Second, if broader formal coverage is desired, isolate the stated Nešetřil–Rödl graph lemma as an explicit dependency and translate its edge-to-sum encoding into the $B_k^2$ conclusion for one fixed parameter $k\ge2$. Acceptance would require matching the quantifiers, strict inequality, and partition notion; failure of the graph lemma translation, or a mismatch in `sumRep`, would falsify the proposed interface rather than the published theorem. Stop after one fixed-parameter interface or at the first unresolved source dependency; do not start an exhaustive Lean build.
