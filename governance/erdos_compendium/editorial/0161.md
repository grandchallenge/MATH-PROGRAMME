## The question in plain language

Colour the edges of the complete $t$ uniform hypergraph on $n$ vertices red or blue. A vertex subset $X$ is *balanced at tolerance* $\alpha$ when at least an $\alpha$ proportion of its $\binom{|X|}{t}$ edges have **each** colour. Define $F^{(t)}(n,\alpha)$ as the least threshold $m$ for which some colouring makes every $X$ with $|X|\ge m$ balanced.

Erdős asked whether, for fixed $t$, changing $\alpha$ between zero and one half causes one abrupt change in the growth of this threshold or several. The distinction is asymptotic in $n$, not mere continuity of a finite-valued step function. The **original threshold and endpoint conventions require review**, as explained below.

## A small example

For $t=2$, the hyperedges are ordinary graph edges. With $n=4$, a colouring whose six edges are split three red and three blue may be balanced on the full vertex set at $\alpha=1/3$; this does not guarantee the condition on any three-vertex subset. Every eligible subset must be tested.

At **exactly $\alpha=0$**, however, the literal condition “at least zero edges of each colour” holds for every subset under *every* colouring, so the literal minimum threshold is one. It cannot be a Ramsey threshold. The catalogue's comparison with the ordinary Ramsey function therefore needs an explicit nonmonochromatic or **positive-colour-count** convention at zero.

## What is known

The broad parameter-jump question remains **OPEN**. Conlon, Fox and Sudakov, [*Large almost monochromatic subsets in hypergraphs*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11856-011-0016-6), *Israel Journal of Mathematics* 181 (2011), 423–432, prove a sharp square-root-logarithm scale for the three-uniform, fixed-positive-tolerance discrepancy problem. The [maintained Erdős #161](https://www.erdosproblems.com/161), edited January 2026, records $F^{(3)}(n,\alpha)\ll_\alpha\sqrt{\log n}$ for fixed positive $\alpha$, with matching order in the relevant regime; it says the jump pattern at positive $\alpha$ is settled for triples, while the larger-uniformity problem remains.

**Source-fidelity qualification:** Conlon–Fox–Sudakov's paper concerns guaranteed almost-monochromatic subsets in *every* colouring, whereas the displayed problem is phrased as existence of a colouring making all large subsets balanced. These are complementary Ramsey/discrepancy perspectives; their exact quantifier relation must be proved rather than assumed. The literal zero-tolerance issue makes a direct theorem-to-statement transfer especially unsafe.

## What remains

A bounded task is to enumerate two-colourings for $n\le5$ and $t=2,3$, compute the least threshold satisfying the literal definition for $\alpha=0,1/4,1/3$, and formalize the complementarity between a balanced-all-large-subsets colouring and unavoidable nearly monochromatic subsets. Acceptance: report the trivial literal value at $\alpha=0$, identify all empty-edge cases, preserve strict versus non-strict inequalities, and stop without claiming any resolution of the higher-uniformity asymptotic jump problem.
