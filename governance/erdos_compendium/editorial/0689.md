## The question in plain language

For each sufficiently large n, Erdős asks whether one can select one residue class modulo every prime p≤n so that **each** integer 1,…,n belongs to at least TWO selected classes. The two matches must use distinct primes, not repeated counting of a class. A generalized question replaces two by any fixed r. The maintained status is OPEN and a claimed solution in its public discussion is explicitly not automatically verified or editorially adopted.

## A small example

For n=5, exhaustive exact enumeration over one residue each modulo primes 2,3,5 finds maximum minimum coverage exactly one, not two. At n=10 the same search over 2,3,5,7 yields maximum minimum coverage zero: no assignment even covers every position once. These small failures do not refute a statement that concerns sufficiently large n. Each assignment has finitely many possibilities: ∏_{p≤n}p.

## What is known

The related single-cover problem is #687. Choosing residues at random gives expected coverage of a fixed integer Σ_{p≤n}1/p, which grows like loglog n, but high average does not guarantee a uniform minimum of two for every one of n positions. For each prime p, its chosen class hits either floor(n/p) or ceil(n/p) positions, so a necessary (not sufficient) total-incidence condition is Σ_{p≤n}ceil(n/p)≥2n.
