## The question in plain language

Given n planar points, when can we put **strictly positive** weights on them such that every line through two or more of the points has the same total weight? Erdős Problem [#735](https://www.erdosproblems.com/735) records Murty's proposed four-class classification, proved by Ackerman, Buchin, Knauer, Pinchasi and Rote (2008).

## A small example

If no three points lie on one line, assign weight 1 to every point: each rich line contains two points and weighs 2. If n−1 points are on one line, assign them weight 1 and give the remaining point weight n−2. Both the long line and each pair-line with the off-line point then weigh n−1.

## What is known

The maintained source reports four types: all collinear, general position, n−1 collinear, and a special configuration involving a triangle, its angle bisectors and incentre, up to projective equivalence. The exact scope of the final type is attributed to the original 2008 paper rather than reconstructed here. A mathematical formulation uses an incidence matrix M for all rich lines and asks for a vector w with positive entries satisfying M w=c·1.
