## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1193](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1193) is **DISPROVED AS LITERALLY STATED** in the source as of 15 October 2026.

For $A\subseteq\mathbb N$, let $(1_A*1_A)(n)$ be the number of **ordered** pairs $(a,b)\in A^2$ with $a+b=n$. Given any positive nondecreasing $g(n)$, ask whether the set of $n$ satisfying $(1_A*1_A)(n)=g(n)$ has **lower density zero**, and whether its **upper density** is bounded by some universal $c<1$. The literal question does **not** restrict $A$ to a sparse basis or impose a sublinear growth condition on $g$, even though Erdős likely intended a restriction.

## A small example

The phase23 exact check enumerates all ordered pairs $(a,b)$ of nonnegative integers summing to each $n=0,\dots,20$, giving precisely $n+1$ solutions. The proof is elementary for **every** nonnegative $n$, since the pairs are $(0,n),(1,n-1),\dots,(n,0)$. This is a rigorous general counterexample to the **literal unrestricted** statement, not just a finite numerical coincidence.

## What is known

The maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/1193 calls **both** questions *trivially false* and notes that Er80,p.100 omitted intended restrictions. If $\mathbb N$ includes zero, take $A=\mathbb N_0$, giving $(1_A*1_A)(n)=n+1$ identically. If $\mathbb N$ is positive, take $A=\{1,2,\dots\}$ and $g(n)=n-1$ for every $n\ge2$ (extend by positive monotone values for finitely many initial indices if required). In both conventions the equality holds for all sufficiently large $n$, so lower/upper agreement densities are **both one**.
