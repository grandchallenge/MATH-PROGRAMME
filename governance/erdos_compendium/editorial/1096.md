## The question in plain language

Choose a real number $q>1$ and form every finite sum of **distinct nonnegative powers** of $q$:

$$
\sum_{i\in S}q^i,\qquad S\subset\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\ \text{finite}.
$$

Arrange the distinct sums in increasing order $0=x_1<x_2<\cdots$. [Erdős Problem #1096](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1096) asks whether, when $q$ is sufficiently close to $1$ from above, the consecutive gaps approach zero:

$$
\lim_{j\to\infty}(x_{j+1}-x_j)=0.
$$

This means **all sufficiently late gaps** are small, not merely that some infinite subsequence of gaps approaches zero.

## A small example

For the exact rational value $q=11/10$, consider only powers $q^0,\ldots,q^8$. Their $2^9=512$ subsets produce 512 distinct sums, with a minimum consecutive gap of $46729/100000000$ and maximum gap $1$ *within this finite collection*.

Adding more allowed powers introduces additional sums and can split gaps, but a finite set cannot establish the behavior of the entire infinite increasing sequence. The finite maximum of one in this truncated example is not a counterexample to convergence of full-sequence gaps.

## What is known

**The original sufficiently-small-$q$ question was proved.** The [maintained catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1096) credits Erdős and Komornik (1998) with convergence of the gaps to zero for

$$
1<q<\sqrt{q_1}\approx1.175,
$$

where $q_1$ is the second smallest Pisot–Vijayaraghavan number.

The distinction between a limit and a lower limit is important. The catalogue reports Feng's 2016 criterion that, for $1<q<2$, the *liminf* of the consecutive gaps is zero exactly when $q$ is not a Pisot number. Under additional hypotheses involving $q^2$, Feng also proves convergence of **all** consecutive gaps to zero over a larger interval.

A Pisot number is an algebraic integer greater than one whose other algebraic conjugates lie strictly inside the complex unit disk. Certain Pisot parameters obstruct overly broad extensions of the original theorem.

It also computes a bounded rational example without mistaking it for a proof of the infinite property.

## What remains

Audit the Erdős–Joó–Komornik, Erdős–Komornik and Feng primary papers for the exact interval endpoints, the $q^2$ Pisot condition and the quantification over the **full** infinite spectrum. Keep finite subset-sum experiments separate from those theorems.
