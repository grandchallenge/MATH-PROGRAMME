## The question in plain language

Erdős Problem 378 asks about squarefree entries in a row of Pascal's triangle. An integer is squarefree when no prime square divides it. For a fixed integer $n$, look at the binomial coefficients

$$
\binom{n}{k}, \qquad 1\leq k<n.
$$

The endpoints $k=0$ and $k=n$ are deliberately omitted from the question: both coefficients are $1$, so they would contribute two automatic examples. Let $S(n)$ be the number of squarefree coefficients in this internal part of row $n$. For each fixed integer $r\geq0$, the problem asks whether the set

$$
\{n\in\mathbb N:S(n)\geq r\}
$$

has a natural density, and whether that density is positive. “Density” here means the limiting proportion among the first $N$ integers as $N$ tends to infinity.

The distinction between internal entries and the whole row is the key normalization. Since the two endpoints are always squarefree, saying that the whole row has exactly $2m+2$ squarefree entries is equivalent to saying that $S(n)=2m$. The primary theorem below is phrased using the whole row, while the Erdős question is phrased using internal entries.

## A small example

Take $n=10$. Exact arithmetic gives the internal row

$$
10,\ 45,\ 120,\ 210,\ 252,\ 210,\ 120,\ 45,\ 10.
$$

The factorizations relevant to squarefreeness are

$$
10=2\cdot5,\qquad 210=2\cdot3\cdot5\cdot7,
$$

while $45=3^2\cdot5$, $120=2^3\cdot3\cdot5$, and $252=2^2\cdot3^2\cdot7$ are not squarefree. Therefore the squarefree internal positions are exactly $k=1,4,6,9$, and $S(10)=4$. Including the two endpoint $1$'s gives six squarefree entries, which is $2m+2$ with $m=2$.

This check is reproducible without floating-point computation:

```python
from math import comb
from sympy import factorint

n = 10
squarefree = []
for k in range(1, n):
    factors = factorint(comb(n, k))
    if all(exponent == 1 for exponent in factors.values()):
        squarefree.append(k)
print(squarefree, len(squarefree))
```

The verified output is `[1, 4, 6, 9] 4`. It illustrates the indexing and checks one finite row only; it neither proves an asymptotic density nor supplies the analytic estimates needed for the general result.

## What is known

The relevant primary source is Andrew Granville and Olivier Ramaré, “Explicit bounds on exponential sums and the scarcity of squarefree binomial coefficients,” *Mathematika* 43 (1996), 73–107, DOI [10.1112/S0025579300011608](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300011608). The accessible author-hosted PDF has SHA-256 `2fc57e71c90e5246a661464b82a3bd7e33514660e3cf1bb0d6bdadb2ff3fad63`.

Their introduction records the central-binomial result as Theorem 1. In displayed form, the claim is

$$
\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\quad n>4\implies \neg\mathrm{Squarefree}\left(\binom{2n}{n}\right).
$$

More directly relevant here, Theorem 5 on printed p. 75 states that the sequence of integers $n$ for which row $n$ of Pascal's triangle has exactly $2m+2$ squarefree entries has an asymptotic density, denoted by $\eta_m$. It also states that, for a constant $\tau_4>0$ and $m\geq1$,

$$
0<\eta_m\ll\exp\left(-\frac{\tau_4\sqrt m}{\log(2m)}\right).
$$

For the original question, internal count at least $r$ means the full row has at least $r+2$ squarefree entries. The complementary exact-count cases are therefore $2m+2<r+2$, equivalently $0\leq m\leq\lfloor(r-1)/2\rfloor$. Thus the live problem page records the resulting density as

$$
1-\sum_{0\leq m\leq\lfloor(r-1)/2\rfloor}\eta_m.
$$

It is positive because $\eta_{r+1}>0$ gives a positive-density class with more than enough squarefree internal entries. This is the source-level resolution of the question, not a claim that GCL has independently certified the paper's proof.

The live [Erdős Problems entry for #378](https://www.erdosproblems.com/378), accessed 2026-10-13, attributes the original question to Erdős and Graham, *Old and New Problems and Results in Combinatorial Number Theory* (1980), p. 72, and identifies Granville–Ramaré as the resolving source. Its two discussion comments likewise point to Theorem 5, but the page warns that comments are user contributions and are not proof verification.
