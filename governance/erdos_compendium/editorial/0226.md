## The question in plain language

Does there exist a **nonlinear entire function** $f:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C$ such that, for every real number $x$,

$$
x\in\mathbb Q \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f(x)\in\mathbb Q?
$$

An entire function is complex differentiable everywhere, not merely smooth on the real line. It is easy to preserve rationality with $f(z)=z$; the challenge is to do so with a genuinely nonlinear, even **transcendental**, entire function.

## A small example

The polynomial $f(z)=z^2$ sends every rational to a rational, but it also sends the irrational $\sqrt2$ to $2$. It fails the reverse implication. Conversely, $f(z)=z+1$ satisfies both implications because adding one preserves rationality, but it is linear. Checking only that rational inputs produce rational outputs is therefore insufficient.

More generally, if $A$ and $B$ are countable dense subsets of the real line, the source problem asks for an entire function that maps **exactly the preimage** of $B$ to $A$, not just a function whose image contains all of $B$.

## What is known

**PROVED.** Karl Barth and W. J. Schneider constructed transcendental entire functions that realize exact membership equivalences between any two countable dense sets. Their published [*Entire Functions Mapping Arbitrary Countable Dense Sets and Their Complements Onto Each Other*](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s2-4.3.482), *Journal of the London Mathematical Society* (1972), generalizes the original real-line question, while their 1970 and 1971 works establish the relevant cases. In particular, choosing $A=B=\mathbb Q$ solves Erdős's request.

The result is stronger than a real-variable interpolation through finitely or countably many prescribed values: it controls the **whole membership predicate** on the real axis. Constructing such an entire function requires careful approximation and interpolation so that unintended irrational inputs do not map into the rational target set.

## What remains

A bounded semantic fixture tests basic candidate functions such as $z$, $z+1$ and $z^2$ on exact rational samples and selected irrational algebraic numbers, recording why they pass or fail the biconditional. A proof-level successor must reproduce Barth–Schneider's approximation lemma and the avoidance of unintended target preimages. Acceptance: no finite sample is presented as proving an **all-real** biconditional.
