## The question in plain language

Colour every edge of a complete graph $K_N$ with one of $r$ colours. Define $R(n;k,r)$ as the least $N$ such that **every** such colouring has an $n$ vertex subset on which at least one colour contains **no** complete graph $K_k$.

Erdős and Gyárfás asked, as currently printed, whether for fixed $r$ there is $C(r)>1$ with

$$
R(n;3,r)<C(r)^{\sqrt n}
$$

for all sufficiently large $n$. Beware: this is not the usual multicolour Ramsey number asking for a monochromatic clique; it asks for a **large subset omitting a monochromatic triangle of some colour**.

## A small example

When $r=2$ and $k=3$, a subset qualifies if it has **no red triangle or no blue triangle**. A colouring in which every $n$ vertex subset contains both a red and a blue triangle is therefore a counterexample at that $N$. It is not enough to find one monochromatic triangle somewhere in the graph.

For a random red-blue edge colouring, a prescribed triangle is monochromatic red with probability $1/8$, independently of other **edge-disjoint** triangles. The complement event of having no red triangle can thus be bounded using a collection of many such triangles.

## What is known

**THE LITERAL PRINTED CONJECTURE IS DISPROVED; THE INTENDED HISTORICAL PROBLEM IS UNCLEAR.** Antonio Girão observed, as recorded on [Erdős #129](https://www.erdosproblems.com/129), that already

$$
R(n;3,2)\ge c^n
$$

for an absolute $c>1$. To see the mechanism, any complete graph on $n$ vertices contains a constant times $n^2$ edge-disjoint triangles: remove edges of triangles greedily until the remainder is triangle-free and apply Mantel's bound. In a random two-colouring, the chance that a given $n$ subset has no red triangle or no blue triangle is at most $2(7/8)^{\Omega(n^2)}$. There are at most $N^n$ such subsets. For $N=\lfloor e^{cn}\rfloor$ and sufficiently small $c>0$, the union bound is less than one, showing a colouring with no qualifying subset.

This exponential **lower** bound contradicts every $C^{\sqrt n}$ upper bound for $r=2$. However, Erdős and Gyárfás reportedly claimed a substantially different lower growth estimate in their original presentation. The maintained site therefore retains an **OPEN/AMBIGUOUS** classification pending recovery of what formulation was originally intended. We should not assert that an unknown corrected question is solved.
