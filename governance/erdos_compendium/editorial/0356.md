## The question in plain language

Let $a = (a_1,\ldots,a_k)$ be a strictly increasing list of positive integers, with every term at most $n$. A consecutive sum is obtained by choosing two indices $u$ and $v$ with $1 \leq u \leq v \leq k$ and adding the entries from $a_u$ through $a_v$. The question is whether there is one fixed constant $c>0$ such that, for every sufficiently large $n$, some such list has at least $cn^2$ different consecutive-sum values. The number of intervals is at most quadratic, so the question asks whether a positive proportion of the possible interval positions can produce different integers.

This is Erdős Problem 356, attributed to Erdős and Graham. The exact source question is recorded in [the original issue](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1626) and in the frozen catalogue entry [at commit `536408716724a2402bdfa7c1d764bf83d64a8c89`](https://github.com/teorth/erdosproblems/blob/536408716724a2402bdfa7c1d764bf83d64a8c89/data/problems.yaml). The entry is the object with Git blob `4e60263a80af6207aca494d2e1388e44fa372889`; its catalogue fields say “proved” and separately report `formal_status: Lean`, while its `formalized` field says “no.” Those metadata are evidence to reconcile, not the mathematical statement.

## A small example

Take $a=(1,2,4,8)$. Enumerating the ten intervals gives, in interval order,

$$
1,2,4,8,3,6,14,7,12,15.
$$

After sorting, this is $\{1,2,3,4,6,7,8,12,14,15\}$, with ten distinct values. Thus the exact count is $4\cdot5/2=10$, equal to the number of intervals. This was checked by integer enumeration: for every pair $(u,v)$, compute `sum(a[u:v+1])`, sort the resulting list, and verify that it equals `[1,2,3,4,6,7,8,12,14,15]` and has length ten.

The example illustrates the object being counted and provides a reproducible finite arithmetic check. It does not establish the Erdős--Graham assertion: one finite list gives no constant valid for all large $n$, and this particular binary sequence does not supply a construction bounded by $n$ at every scale.

## What is known

The primary resolving source is Adrian Beker, “On a problem of Erdős and Graham about consecutive sums in strictly increasing sequences.” The work appeared first as arXiv:2311.10087v1, submitted 16 November 2023, and was published in the *Bulletin of the London Mathematical Society* 56 (2024), 2749--2759, DOI [10.1112/blms.13098](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms.13098). The arXiv v1 PDF was read as a nine-page primary source; its downloaded-byte SHA-256 was `9f490cf7ecb609e0471bb130ba3ba21b152e2f49ea96cd7376331ee1cc156143`.

Beker’s Theorem 1.2 states the affirmative result with stronger quantification: there is a constant $c_1>0$ such that for every positive integer $n$ there are integers $1\leq a_1<\cdots<a_k\leq n$ whose consecutive sums contain at least $c_1n^2$ distinct values. The proof first treats random perturbations $a_i=3i+\varepsilon_i$, where the signs are independent and symmetric, by bounding the expected additive energy of the partial-sum set. It also gives an explicit family, with a parameter $b$ in the stated logarithmic range, and derives a positive quadratic lower bound. The paper’s Proposition 1.5 supplies a separate upper bound, while its final section leaves the optimal constant and typicality questions open.

The original problem source is P. Erdős and R. Graham, *Old and New Problems and Results in Combinatorial Number Theory*, Monographie 28, Université de Genève, 1980, p. 58, cited as `ErGr80` by the maintained [problem page](https://www.erdosproblems.com/356). The maintained page currently says “PROVED,” attributes the affirmative solution to Beker, links the paper, and records “Formalised statement? No.” Its [LaTeX source view](https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/356) also records the related permutation problem and other follow-up questions; those are not part of the registered theorem.
