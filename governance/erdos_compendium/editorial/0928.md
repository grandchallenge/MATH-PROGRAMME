## The question in plain language

Let $P^+(m)$ denote the largest prime factor of $m$. For fixed $0<\alpha,\beta<1$, Erdős asked whether the set of integers $n$ with

$$
P^+(n)<n^\alpha,\qquad P^+(n+1)<(n+1)^\beta
$$

has a natural density. The expected value is $\rho(1/\alpha)\rho(1/\beta)$, the product of the separate smooth-number densities. The problem remains open.

## A small example

At $\alpha=\beta=1/2$, the pair $80=2^4\cdot5$, $81=3^4$ satisfies both strict inequalities: $5<\sqrt{80}$ and $3<9$. In contrast the pair $48,49$ fails because $P^+(49)=7=\sqrt{49}$. Strict inequalities matter in finite tests.

## What is known

For one integer the Dickman function $\rho(u)$ describes the density of integers with largest prime factor less than $n^{1/u}$. It satisfies $\rho(u)=1$ for $0\le u\le1$, and $u\rho'(u)+\rho(u-1)=0$ for $u>1$.

Teräväinen [Te18] proved the predicted **logarithmic** density for the simultaneous conditions. Logarithmic density weights integers proportionally to $1/n$, whereas natural density counts all integers up to $N$ equally. Existence of the former does not prove existence of the latter. Wang [Wa21] proved the natural-density product law *conditional* on an Elliott–Halberstam conjecture for friable numbers. The requisite unproved hypothesis must not be omitted. These are the distinctions recorded in the [maintained original-source catalogue](https://www.erdosproblems.com/928).

## Why the problem is difficult

The events that $n$ and $n+1$ are smooth have correlations invisible to separate one-variable density estimates. Their coprimality does not imply independence. Upgrading logarithmically averaged estimates to unweighted interval averages requires stronger joint distribution control.

## GCL's current state

The equality of the **natural** density with the Dickman product is unproved unconditionally. No finite search can establish its asymptotic existence. The example above checks definitions only; the logarithmic-density theorem and conditional result are literature statements, not original proofs presented here.
