## The question in plain language

Erdős Problem #347 asks whether one can build an increasing sequence of positive integers whose successive ratios approach $2$, while its finite subset sums cover almost every natural number, even after deleting finitely many terms. If $A$ is the sequence and $A'$ is any cofinite subsequence, the relevant set is

$$
P(A')=\left\{\sum_{x\in B}x:B\subseteq A'\text{ finite}\right\}.
$$

“Density $1$” means that the proportion of integers up to $X$ which belong to $P(A')$ tends to $1$ as $X$ grows. It does not mean that every sufficiently large integer is represented. The question therefore asks for a sequence that is close to doubling, but whose subset sums still have only a vanishing proportion of gaps after any finite deletion.

The maintained [Erdős Problems entry](https://www.erdosproblems.com/347) records the exact displayed question, tags it as number theory and complete sequences, and currently says that the answer is affirmative. Its wording uses a nondecreasing sequence of integers; the explicit construction discussed below is positive and strictly increasing.

## A small example

The construction in the primary source starts with $M_0=10$ and

$$
k_0=4+\left\lceil\log_2\log_2(16)\right\rceil=6.
$$

Its first block is

$$
10,20,40,80,160,311.
$$

The next scale is

$$
M_1=\left\lfloor\left(2^6-\frac32\right)10\right\rfloor=625.
$$

For the greedy argument, the associated digit set is

$$
B_0=\{0,10,20,\ldots,310\}\cup\{321,331,\ldots,621\}.
$$

It has $63$ elements. Exact integer enumeration checked every $x$ from $0$ through $625$: choosing the largest member $b$ of $B_0$ with $b\leq x$ always leaves $0\leq x-b\leq10$. The check therefore verifies the first finite instance of the local covering lemma used by the construction. For example, $x=625$ uses $b=621$ and leaves $4$; just below the first shifted digit, $x=320$ uses $b=310$ and leaves $10$.

This calculation does not establish density $1$, the limiting ratio, or the statement for every cofinite subsequence. It is only a transparent finite check of one exact-arithmetic component of the proof.

## What is known

The original reference attached to the live entry is P. Erdős and R. Graham, *Old and new problems and results in combinatorial number theory* (1980), cited there as [ErGr80]. It is the source of the problem statement, not a proof of the later affirmative result. A related primary paper is S. A. Burr and P. Erdős, “Completeness properties of perturbed sequences,” *Journal of Number Theory* 13 (1981), 446–455, [doi:10.1016/0022-314X(81)90036-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-314X(81)90036-6). That paper studies subset-sum completeness and strong completeness; it is useful context, but it should not be cited as independently proving this exact ratio-limit problem.

The decisive source located for this audit is Enrique Barschkis, “A Sequence with Doubling Ratio and Full-Density Subset Sums,” dated January 21, 2026. The [author’s LaTeX source](https://github.com/ebarschkis/ErdosProblem/blob/20afb2b3e34eecd7c64807a7e2f64e0057a36e35/Problem347/note.tex) has blob `3a00fe38a57bf4bab34127b5cf4e1965ce4a8893` and SHA-256 `bf930dce2008febe10a424f96e1e7f21ef70c33567b997276d51fa4d7bae5cbc`; the accompanying [PDF](https://github.com/ebarschkis/ErdosProblem/blob/20afb2b3e34eecd7c64807a7e2f64e0057a36e35/Problem347/347.pdf) has Git blob `833d3dfb1c06f91169d7a8c1a019480c4506e0d1` and the retrieved file’s SHA-256 is `a5c563a0eeaa248bd15d678cc194d48765043d3177b7b658176e350ae005ea99`.

The proof concatenates blocks containing powers of $2$ times $M_n$, plus one adjusted final term. The block length grows slowly, while $M_{n+1}$ is approximately $(2^{k_n}-3/2)M_n$. Within and between blocks, the ratio tends to $2$. A greedy expansion chooses one digit from each block, leaving a bounded remainder. Most digit vectors contain enough copies of a special digit; replacing that digit by its shifted companion corrects the remainder. The source then bounds the exceptional digit vectors by an exponential decay times a fixed polynomial, yielding density $1$ for every tail and hence for every cofinite subsequence.

## What remains

The source-level theorem is available and matches the registered affirmative question in its main quantifiers: one sequence, ratio limit $2$, and density $1$ after every finite deletion. A bounded successor task should now audit the semantic bridge between the Barschkis construction and the pinned formal interface. In particular, check positivity and indexing, the meaning of “cofinite subsequence,” the use of `Monotone` rather than strict monotonicity, and whether `range ι` with finite complement exactly expresses finite deletion.

The same task can formalize the finite greedy-cover lemma for arbitrary $n$, then isolate the exceptional-vector estimate. Acceptance should require matching hypotheses and a proof that the finite estimates extend from scale endpoints to all cutoffs. It should stop if the formal statement needs material changes or if the source proof’s omitted details cannot be supplied from the locked note. This is a bounded verification and reusable-lemma opportunity, not a new claim that GCL has certified the theorem.
