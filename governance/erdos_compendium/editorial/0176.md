## The question in plain language

Colour the integers $1,\ldots,N$ using signs $f(n)\in\{-1,1\}$. For a fixed length $k$, every arithmetic progression $P=\{a,a+d,\ldots,a+(k-1)d\}$ has *discrepancy* $\bigl|\sum_{n\in P}f(n)\bigr|$.

Define $N(k,\ell)$ as the smallest $N$ that forces some progression of length $k$ to have discrepancy at least $\ell$ **for every sign colouring**. Erdős #176 asks for good upper bounds, particularly whether $N(k,ck)$, $N(k,2)$, or $N(k,\sqrt{k})$ can be bounded by $C^k$ with constants appropriate to the chosen threshold.

## A small example

For $k=3$, every sum of three signs is odd, so its absolute value is either one or three. Asking for discrepancy at least **two** is therefore equivalent to asking for discrepancy **three**, i.e. a monochromatic progression. In general every discrepancy has the same parity as $k$, so noninteger thresholds such as $ck$ must be interpreted via $\lceil ck\rceil$ with the parity restriction applied.

When $\ell=k$, the condition exactly forces a monochromatic progression of length $k$. Thus $N(k,k)$ equals the ordinary van der Waerden number $W(k)$ for two colours.

## What is known

The proposed broad exponential **upper bounds are OPEN**. Spencer (1973) proved, for $k=2^tm$ with $m$ odd,

$$
N(k,1)=2^t(k-1)+1.
$$

The formula concerns the threshold one; it is **not** an exponential bound at a proportional threshold $ck$.

Erdős proved exponential **lower** bounds for $N(k,ck)$, with the base depending on $c$. Zach Hunter's observation on the [maintained #176](https://www.erdosproblems.com/176) derives stronger lower bounds via the Lovász local lemma, involving the binomial tail

$$
\sum_{j>(1+c)k/2}\binom kj.
$$

For $c\to1$, the associated lower exponential base approaches two. These are obstructions to small $N$, not upper bounds answering Erdős's question. The statement “$N(k,2)\le C^k$” requires a named constant independent of $k$ and should not be inferred from finite examples.

## What remains

A bounded fixture exhaustively enumerates sign colourings of $[1,N]$ for small $N$, calculates discrepancies on **every** nonconstant arithmetic progression of length $k$, and compares the formula for threshold one for $k=2,3,4$ with brute force. Acceptance: parity restrictions and rounding are explicit, $\ell=k$ is equivalent to the two-colour van der Waerden problem, and all results are labelled finite. Stop before extrapolating a universal exponential upper bound.
