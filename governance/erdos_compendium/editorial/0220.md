## The question in plain language

For a positive integer $n\ge2$, list the integers less than $n$ that are coprime to $n$ in increasing order:

$$
1=a_1<a_2<\cdots<a_{\varphi(n)}<n.
$$

Erdős asked whether the squared **internal gaps** between consecutive such integers satisfy

$$
\sum_{1\le j<\varphi(n)}(a_{j+1}-a_j)^2
\ll \frac{n^2}{\varphi(n)}.
$$

The implied constant is independent of $n$. The sum is over the displayed representatives **without a cyclic wrap-around gap** from $a_{\varphi(n)}$ to $n+1$.

## A small example

For $n=10$, the coprime residues below ten are $1,3,7,9$. The internal gaps are $2,4,2$, and their squared sum is $4+16+4=24$. The right-hand scale is $10^2/\varphi(10)=25$.

For a prime modulus $n=p$, every integer $1,\ldots,p-1$ is a reduced residue, and the internal gaps are all one. The left side is then $p-2$, far below an inappropriate worst-case model with a few huge gaps. There is no need to count a gap past the final reduced residue in the original question.

## What is known

**PROVED.** Hugh Montgomery and Robert Vaughan (1986) established the inequality. They proved the stronger family, for **every real $\gamma\ge1$**,

$$
\sum_{1\le j<\varphi(n)}
(a_{j+1}-a_j)^\gamma
\ll_\gamma \frac{n^\gamma}{\varphi(n)^{\gamma-1}}.
$$

The constant may depend on $\gamma$ but not $n$. Taking $\gamma=2$ recovers the displayed conjecture, while $\gamma=1$ trivially bounds the sum of internal gaps by $n$. Higher powers control rare large reduced-residue gaps more severely.

The maintained [Erdős #220](https://www.erdosproblems.com/220) identifies the 1986 result and historical Erdős sources back to 1940. The complete analytic argument has not been independently reconstructed here.
