## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1177](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1177) asks the following. The maintained status is **OPEN**.

For a finite **3-uniform** hypergraph $G$, define $F_G(\kappa)$ as 3-uniform hypergraphs of chromatic number $\kappa$ avoiding $G$. Erdős–Galvin–Hajnal ask **three separate** things: (i) $F_G(\aleph_1)$ nonempty implies a witness with at most $2^{2^{\aleph_0}}$ vertices; (ii) if $F_G(\aleph_1)$ and $F_H(\aleph_1)$ are both nonempty, they have a **common** witness; (iii) nonempty $F_G(\kappa)$ for one uncountable $\kappa$ implies nonempty $F_G(\lambda)$ for **every** uncountable $\lambda$. Weak hypergraph chromatic means no monochromatic edge; source-verify this convention.

## A small example

The complete 3-uniform hypergraph on four vertices has chromatic number **2**, since 2+2 coloring avoids monochromatic triples. On five vertices every 2-coloring has a class of size at least 3 and thus a monochromatic triple; a 2+2+1 coloring works, so its chromatic number is **3**. Both are independently checked by exact enumeration. They are not uncountable chromatic examples.
