## The question in plain language

Fix an integer maximum degree $d$ and a required distance $t$ between edges. Let $h_t(d)$ be the smallest edge count such that **every** simple graph with at least $h_t(d)$ edges and maximum degree at most $d$ contains two edges whose shortest connecting path has length at least $t$. Erdős and Nešetřil asked for good estimates of $h_t(d)$. Here distance between two edges means the minimum distance between their endpoints; one must specify this convention when implementing a verifier. See [Erdős Problem #934](https://www.erdosproblems.com/934).

## A small example

*A small case and a warning*

When $t=1$, the two edges must have no endpoint in common. The graph $K_3$ has three edges, maximum degree $2$, and no two disjoint edges. Therefore the often-quoted formula $h_1(d)=d+1$ is not valid at $d=2$: three edges do **not** suffice. The problem discussion explicitly corrects this exceptional case. For maximum degree $2$, the precise formula is $h_t(2)=2t+2$, according to the catalogue's discussion; this is an attributed claim requiring its own complete proof before certification. For $d\ge3$, the catalogue gives $h_1(d)=d+1$.

## What is known

The original elementary estimate is $h_t(d)\le 2d^t$. For $t=2$, Erdős–Nešetřil and Bermond–Bond–Paoli–Peyrat conjectured $h_2(d)\le (5/4)d^2+1$, with equality for even $d$. Chung, Gyárfás, Tuza, and Trotter proved that conjecture [CGTT90]; see also [related problem #149](https://www.erdosproblems.com/149).

For $t=3$, Cambie, Cames van Batenburg, de Joannis de Verclos, and Kang [CCJK22] proved $h_3(3)=23$ and conjectured

$$
h_3(d)\le d^3-d^2+d+2.
$$

They proposed that equality holds precisely at $d=q+1$ for prime powers $q$. Their general conjectures predict asymptotic upper constant $1+o(1)$ in front of $d^t$ and a matching lower bound along infinitely many $d$, for fixed $t\ge3$.

The same authors proved an improved general bound

$$
h_t(d)\le \tfrac32 d^t+1,
$$

and for sufficiently large $t$, a lower bound of $0.629^t d^t$ for infinitely many $d$. These are distinct proven statements and conjectured sharp constants. They do not amount to a complete evaluation of $h_t(d)$.

## Why the problem is difficult

A maximum-degree restriction bounds how many edges may lie near a chosen edge, but those local neighborhoods can overlap. The extremal constructions efficiently pack many edges while keeping every pair close; improving the threshold requires understanding that overlap globally. A naive breadth-first count supplies an order-of-magnitude bound but not the sharp leading coefficient.
