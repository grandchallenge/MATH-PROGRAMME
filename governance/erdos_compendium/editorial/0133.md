## The question in plain language

A graph has **diameter two** when any two distinct vertices are connected by a path of length at most two, and at least one pair is not adjacent. Let $f(n)$ be the smallest possible maximum vertex degree among all triangle-free graphs with $n$ vertices and diameter two.

Erdős and Pach asked how $f(n)$ grows, and in particular whether

$$
\frac{f(n)}{\sqrt n}\longrightarrow\infty.
$$

That second conjecture predicts the required maximum degree is much larger than the square-root scale, not merely a positive constant times $\sqrt n$.

## A small example

If a graph has maximum degree $d$ and diameter two, choose any vertex. It reaches at most $d$ neighbours in one step and at most $d(d-1)$ new vertices through those neighbours. Thus

$$
n\le1+d+d(d-1)=d^2+1.
$$

Therefore every diameter-two graph has $d\ge\sqrt{n-1}$, whether or not triangles are forbidden. A five-cycle is triangle-free, has diameter two, and has maximum degree two; its $n=5$ satisfies the elementary lower bound exactly.

## What is known

**The prediction $f(n)/\sqrt n\to\infty$ is DISPROVED; the sharp leading constant remains OPEN.** Hanson and Seyffarth (1984) constructed Cayley graphs giving

$$
f(n)\le(\sqrt2+o(1))\sqrt n.
$$

Füredi and Seress, [*Maximal triangle-free graphs with restrictions on the degrees*](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.3190180103), *Journal of Graph Theory* 18 (1994), 11–24, improved the upper constant to

$$
f(n)\le\left(\frac2{\sqrt3}+o(1)\right)\sqrt n.
$$

Together with the elementary lower bound, the order is $\Theta(\sqrt n)$, but the precise asymptotic constant is not determined. The maintained [Erdős #133](https://www.erdosproblems.com/133) attributes a conjectural constant one to Alon; it is **not** a proved limit.

A separate simpler construction due to Alon, documented on the maintained site, gives $f(n)\ll\sqrt{n\log n}$ by starting with a high-chromatic triangle-free example and adding edges without creating triangles until diameter two. This is weaker asymptotically than the cited constant-times-square-root construction.

## GCL's current state

The pinned catalogue and maintained page classify the proposed divergence as **DISPROVED**. The original issue is [#1402](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1402). The exact degree-two neighbourhood inequality is independently verified. The full 1984 and 1994 Cayley/construction proofs are cited from the maintained source and primary publication trail but were not independently rebuilt.

The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1402#issuecomment-6165501447). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded successor enumerates triangle-free graphs on up to seven vertices, checks graph diameter and maximum degree, and compares the smallest maximum degree with $\lceil\sqrt{n-1}\rceil$. Acceptance: $C_5$ is recognized as triangle-free, diameter two, and degree two; disconnected graphs are rejected as having infinite diameter. Stop at finite consistency checks, not a new leading-constant theorem.
