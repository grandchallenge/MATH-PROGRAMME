## The question in plain language

Erdős Problem 319 asks how large a minimally balanced collection of reciprocals can be. Fix a positive integer $N$. Choose a set $A$ of distinct integers from $1$ through $N$, and give each chosen integer a sign $delta_n$ equal to $1$ or $-1$. The total signed reciprocal must vanish:

$$
\sum_{n\in A}\frac{\delta_n}{n}=0.
$$

The balance must be irreducible: whenever $A'$ is a nonempty proper subset of $A$, its signed reciprocal sum is not zero:

$$
\sum_{n\in A'}\frac{\delta_n}{n}\ne 0.
$$

The problem asks for the largest possible $|A|$ as a function of $N$. It is useful to call this extremal quantity $c(N)$. The signs are part of the witness, while minimality is checked against every proper subcollection using the same signs. This is not merely a question about finding any relation among reciprocals; it asks for a relation with no smaller zero-sum relation hidden inside it.

## A small example

Take $N=6$, $A=\{2,3,6\}$, and set $\delta_2=1$, $\delta_3=-1$, and $\delta_6=-1$. Exact arithmetic gives

$$
\frac12-\frac13-\frac16=\frac{3-2-1}{6}=0.
$$

The six nonempty proper subsets have signed sums

$$
\frac12,\quad -\frac13,\quad -\frac16,\quad \frac16,\quad \frac13,\quad -\frac12,
$$

in the order $\{2\}$, $\{3\}$, $\{6\}$, $\{2,3\}$, $\{2,6\}$, and $\{3,6\}$. None is zero, so this is a verified admissible witness and proves only the finite lower bound $c(6)\ge 3$. It does not identify $c(6)$, predict the asymptotic size of $c(N)$, or say anything by itself about the unresolved exact extremal function. The calculation is reproducible with integer numerators over the common denominator $6$; no floating-point approximation is involved.

## What is known

The maintained problem page attributes a substantial lower bound to an observation of Sarosh Adenwalla using Ernest S. Croot III's paper, [“On unit fractions with denominators in short intervals”](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa99-2-1), *Acta Arithmetica* 99 (2001), 99–114. Croot's primary source is also available as [arXiv:math/9904181v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9904181). In the introduction and Main Theorem, Croot proves that for a fixed rational $r>0$ and sufficiently large $N$, one can represent $r$ as a sum of distinct unit fractions whose denominators lie just above $N$ and whose largest denominator is at most $\left(e^r+O_r(\log\log N/\log N)\right)N$. His proof starts from almost all reciprocals in an interval, removes a controlled set, and completes the residual.

The catalogue explains the transfer as follows. Apply the unit-fraction construction with the lower endpoint scaled near $N/e$. The resulting denominators lie below $N$ and the construction supplies a set $B$ with reciprocal sum $1$ and the asymptotic size reported on the maintained page as $(1-1/e+o(1))N$. Then $A=B\cup\{1\}$, with sign $-1$ on $B$ and sign $+1$ on $1$, has total signed sum zero. Because all elements of $B$ are larger than $1$, every nonempty proper subset omitting $1$ has negative sum, while a proper subset containing $1$ has sum $1-\sum_{b\in B'}1/b>0$. Thus the transfer has the required minimality mechanism. The exact counting transfer is recorded here as the catalogue's source-backed consequence; it is not a new proof or GCL certification.

The strongest safe status statement is therefore that the exact extremal question remains listed open, while a linear lower bound is externally reported and the trivial inclusion $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ gives $c(N)\le N$. The pinned formal source is [google-deepmind/formal-conjectures commit `85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020`](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/tree/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020), file [`FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/319.lean`](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/319.lean), blob `4ab11d3f0e80356ae760316796721f95e8e42582`.
