## The question in plain language

Let W(3,k) be the smallest N such that every red/blue colouring of {1,...,N} yields either a RED three-term arithmetic progression or a BLUE k-term arithmetic progression. Erdős asked for nontrivial lower bounds and an upper bound W(3,k)<exp(k^c) for some absolute c<1. The maintained page marks SOLVED because both specific challenges have been met, but the true asymptotic growth remains unsettled.

## A small example

For k=3, the classical van der Waerden number W(3,3)=9. Every red/blue colouring of {1,...,9} contains a monochromatic three-term progression, while there exists a colouring of eight positions without one. The orientation red/blue is irrelevant when k=3 but matters for k>3, so this is only a finite check of the definition.

## What is known

The maintained #721 attributes a superpolynomial lower bound to Ben Green [Gr22], improved by Zachary Hunter [Hu22] to W(3,k)≥exp(c(log k)²/loglog k). Schoen [Sc21] first proved a subexponential upper bound exp(k^c) for some c<1; Kelley–Meka and Bloom–Sisask methods yield an improved upper bound W(3,k)≤exp(O((log k)^9)) as presented by the catalogue (last edited Apr 4 2026). The upper is subexponential in k, but the large gap between exp((log k)²/loglog k) and exp((log k)^9) remains. The named original manuscripts and exact constant conditions have not been independently read in full.
