## The question in plain language

List the numbers from 1 to $n$ in some order. Consider every **consecutive block** of the list and form its sum. Can the number of *different* block-sum values always be much smaller than $n^2$ as $n$ becomes large?

[Erdős Problem #34](https://www.erdosproblems.com/34) conjectured that the number was $o(n^2)$ for every permutation. This is **disproved**: suitable permutations produce a quadratic-order number of different consecutive sums. Consecutive blocks are not the same as arbitrary subsets.

## A small example

For the permutation $(1,2,3)$, the six nonempty consecutive blocks yield sums

$$
1,\quad2,\quad3,\quad1+2=3,\quad2+3=5,\quad1+2+3=6.
$$

There are **five** distinct values, because 3 appears twice. Define the prefix sums by $P_0=0$ and $P_j=a_1+\cdots+a_j$. Every consecutive block sum can be written exactly as

$$
a_i+\cdots+a_j=P_j-P_{i-1}.
$$

Thus the possible sums are *forward differences* between prefix sums. There are $n(n+1)/2$ blocks, giving a trivial quadratic upper bound; the conjecture asked for strictly smaller asymptotic order.

## What is known

The negative answer was developed through counterexamples of Hegyvári and subsequent constructions studied by Jakub Konieczny, *On consecutive sums in permutations* (2015). The primary work produces permutations with a positive multiple of $n^2$ distinct consecutive sums. A finite illustration of prefix differences cannot establish that asymptotic construction.

## GCL's current state

[Source audit #1303](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1303#issuecomment-6121343173) checks the exact distinction between consecutive and arbitrary subset sums, derives the prefix-difference identity and specifies a bounded permutation enumeration task. The registry reports external Lean work, not a GCL-protected kernel replay.

## What remains

The original little-oh conjecture has been refuted. Further inquiry concerns sharp extremal constants, explicit optimal permutations and fully source-pinned formal verification of the published constructions, not another claim that the original question is open.
