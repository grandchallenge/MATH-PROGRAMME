## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1207](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1207) has maintained status **OPEN BROADLY — PLANAR POWER SAVING SOLVED 2026** at this audit.

For $n$ distinct points in $\mathbb R^d$, let $P_d(n)$ be the **largest guaranteed size** of a subset with **no isosceles triangle**: any three selected points must have **three pairwise different** distances. The main original test asks whether some fixed $c>0$ satisfies $$P_2(n)<n^{1-c}$$ for all sufficiently large $n$. Since $P_d$ is a **minimum over input point sets of the maximum size** of an isosceles-free subset, a hard planar point configuration gives the desired **upper bound**, while random deletion and distance arguments give **lower bounds**. This is not the stronger all-pairwise-distances-distinct condition of #1208.

## A small example

For vertices of a regular $n$-gon, a triple forms an isosceles triangle exactly when its vertex indices form a nontrivial **three-term arithmetic progression mod $n$** (including the geometry of cyclic distance). Exhaustive enumeration for $5\le n\le10$ finds maximum 3AP-free angular subset sizes recorded in the phase24 receipt; for a regular hexagon the maximum is **4**. This exact finite polygon calculation illustrates the older construction's limitation but **does not establish** any general planar polynomial exponent.

## What is known

The catalogue https://www.erdosproblems.com/1207, updated 7 September 2026, reports that **Lee–Pohoata–Zhu [LPZ26] (assisted by ChatGPT)** proved $P_2(n)<n^{1-c}$ for some absolute $c>0$, resolving that specific proposed power-saving upper bound.
