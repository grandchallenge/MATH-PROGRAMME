## The question in plain language

Start at a point of $\mathbb R^d$ and repeatedly move by a **positive coordinate unit vector** $e_i$: exactly one coordinate increases by one at each step. Erdős and Graham asked for which dimensions $d$ **every infinite** such walk contains three distinct visited points in an arithmetic progression:

$$
p,\ q,\ r,\qquad p+r=2q.
$$

Every step must be a positive basis vector, never its negative or an arbitrary Euclidean unit vector. The selected triple may occur at **nonconsecutive** times on the walk.

## A small example

Encode the sequence of steps as a word on an alphabet of $d$ letters, the letter $i$ representing $e_i$. Consider two consecutive blocks of steps with the same multiset of letters but perhaps in different order. Their displacement vectors are equal, so the vertices at the beginning, boundary and end form a three-term arithmetic progression.

For example, the two-letter word $1212$ contains consecutive blocks $12$ and $12$. In $\mathbb R^2$ the walk goes $(0,0),(1,0),(1,1),(2,1),(2,2)$, and the points at times 0, 2, 4 form an arithmetic progression. The blocks could instead be $12$ and $21$; they need only be **anagrams**, not identical strings.

## What is known

**SOLVED by the theory of abelian squares in words.** A word contains an *abelian square* when two adjacent nonempty blocks have the same letter-count vector. For a walk with positive basis-vector steps, the corresponding three vertices form an arithmetic progression **if and only if** its step word contains an abelian square.

Infinite abelian-square-free words do **not** exist on alphabets of at most three letters: the relevant finite obstruction can be checked by exhaustive search, with the maintained catalogue claiming that every ternary word of length seven contains an abelian square. **That finite claim is false:** the word $1213121$ has length seven and no adjacent equal-multiset blocks. Exact enumeration instead finds no abelian-square-free ternary word of length eight. This numerical correction does not change the infinite-word classification. On a four-letter alphabet, Evgenii Keränen, [*Abelian squares are avoidable on 4 letters*](https://www.erdosproblems.com/192) (1992), constructed an infinite word with no abelian square.

Consequently **every infinite walk has an arithmetic triple for $d\le3$**, but a counterexample walk exists when $d\ge4$ by embedding the four-coordinate construction in higher dimension. This answers the dimension-classification question exactly. The distinction between ordinary repeated adjacent blocks and equal **letter counts** is essential.

Original source issue: [#1461](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1461). Keränen's original 1992 morphism and the external Lean artifact were not independently rebuilt or axiom-audited. The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1461#issuecomment-6169560590). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded task enumerates all step words over alphabets of size two and three up to length seven, detects adjacent equal-multiset blocks with exact Parikh vectors, and verifies the equivalence to arithmetic triples in the resulting coordinate walk. Acceptance: both $1212$ and $1221$ yield arithmetic triples, the length-seven counterexample $1213121$ passes the abelian-square-free test, whereas all length-eight ternary words fail, and the transformation is proved bidirectionally. Stop at this finite semantic result; no claim of independently constructing Keränen's infinite four-letter word.
