## The question in plain language

[Erdős Problem #1196](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1196) is **EXTERNALLY PROVED IN 2026** in the source as of 15 October 2026.

A set of integers $A\subseteq[x,\infty)$ is **primitive** if no two distinct elements divide one another. Erdős–Sárközy–Szemerédi conjectured that, as $x\to\infty$, all primitive $A$ satisfy

$$
\sum_{a\in A}\frac1{a\log a}\le1+o(1).
$$

The use of an infinite sum over a possibly infinite primitive set and logarithm convention matter; $a\ge2$ avoids $\log1=0$. The result is a **uniform upper bound** over all primitive sets whose smallest element is large; it is not saying every primitive set has a sum tending to one.

## A small example

The first five primes $\{2,3,5,7,11\}$ are pairwise nondividing, hence a primitive set. Direct numerical calculation gives the finite reciprocal-log sum approximately **1.260353667393**, which is greater than one and is **not a contradiction**: the theorem applies asymptotically when **every** element exceeds a large cutoff $x$, not to $A$ containing 2. This is an exact primitive-set structural fixture with rounded numerical evaluation.

## What is known

The maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/1196 marks **PROVED** and attributes a 2026 GPT-5.4 Pro argument, expounded by Alexeev–Barreto–Li–Lichtman–Price–Shah–Tang–Tao [ABLLPSTT26], yielding even a tail bound $\sum_{a\in A,a>x}(a\log a)^{-1}\le1+O(1/\log x)$ for arbitrary primitive $A$. Earlier Lichtman [Li23] achieved constant $e^\gamma\pi/4\approx1.399$, and GLW24 gives fine near-one behavior of the primitive set of integers with exactly $k$ prime factors. **Original 2026 proof/source, exact hypotheses and potential Lean formalization have NOT been independently reviewed.** This is not a novel GCL proof.
