## The question in plain language

For an integer $k\ge4$, among all simple graphs on exactly $n$ vertices containing **no complete subgraph $K_k$**, how many colors can be forced? Define

$$
f_k(n)=\max_{\substack{|V(G)|=n\\K_k\not\subseteq G}}\chi(G).
$$

This measures how hard a graph can be to color without a clique of order $k$. Erdős asked whether for each fixed $k\ge4$ there exists a **positive** logarithmic exponent $c_k$ such that

$$
f_k(n)\gg_k
\frac{n^{1-\frac1{k-1}}}{(\log n)^{c_k}}.
$$

This is [Erdős Problem #920](https://www.erdosproblems.com/920). Its required polynomial exponent is important; obtaining a smaller exponent does **not** answer it.

A June 2026 improvement in off-diagonal Ramsey theory answers the question **affirmatively**, through a quantitative corollary of a theorem of Domagoj Bradač. The bound has the logarithmic factor in the **denominator**. The original proof of the deep Ramsey construction is imported from the preprint, not independently rederived by GCL.

## A small example

The five-cycle $C_5$ has no triangle and has chromatic number three. More strongly, it has independence number two, meaning no three of its vertices are mutually nonadjacent. Thus the classical Ramsey number $r(3,3)$ exceeds five. Every six-vertex graph has a triangle or three independent vertices, giving the exact equality $r(3,3)=6$.

Why do Ramsey numbers help? If $r(k,t)>n$, there exists a graph on **exactly** $n$ vertices that has no clique of size $k$ and no independent set of size $t$. Each color class of a proper coloring is independent and hence has at most $t-1$ vertices. Therefore this witness requires at least

$$
\left\lceil\frac{n}{t-1}\right\rceil
$$

colors and gives the same lower bound for $f_k(n)$. This is the **Ramsey-to-chromatic transfer**.

The executable finite checker evaluates this implication across all 33,866 labelled simple graphs on 2–6 vertices, calculating their independence and chromatic numbers exactly. In particular, for six vertices it confirms that the largest chromatic number among graphs containing no $K_4$ is four, whereas the largest among triangle-free graphs is three.

## What is known

The original problem appears in Erdős, [*Problems and results in chromatic graph theory*](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1969-13.pdf), *Proof Techniques in Graph Theory* (1969), pp.27–35. Graver and Yackel (1968) established the complementary upper estimate

$$
f_k(n)\ll_k
\left(\frac{n\log\log n}{\log n}\right)^{1-\frac1{k-1}}.
$$

The special case $k=4$ follows from Mattheus and Verstraete's lower bound for $r(4,t)$, published in the *Annals of Mathematics* (2024), which yields

$$
f_4(n)\gg \frac{n^{2/3}}{(\log n)^{4/3}}.
$$

The general breakthrough is Domagoj Bradač, [*Off-diagonal Ramsey numbers*, arXiv:2605.28793, version 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28793), revised **June 16, 2026**. The current v3 abstract states that, for fixed $k\ge3$,

$$
r(k,t)\gg_k \frac{t^{k-1}}{(\log t)^{2k-4}}.
$$

This is a material **version distinction**: the first May 2026 version established a weaker exponent $k-2+o(1)$, which did not furnish the target polynomial exponent for general $k\ge5$. The v3 theorem raises the exponent to $k-1$. Its use here is an imported published-preprint claim; GCL has not independently checked the whole construction.

A July 23, 2026 [source-specific mathematical note by Miroslav Lžičař](https://github.com/mireklzicar/erdos-920/blob/main/erdos_920.tex) proves the elementary exact-order transfer from Bradač's v3 theorem and obtains

$$
f_k(n)\gg_k
\frac{n^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}}
 {(\log n)^{\frac{2k-4}{k-1}}}.
$$

Thus one may choose $c_k=(2k-4)/(k-1)>0$, proving the original requested lower bound. This transfer is mathematical deduction from the cited deep theorem, not a second independent construction of the Ramsey graphs.

There is a **source-display discrepancy** in another [July 2026 online repository](https://github.com/moseslua/erdos920): its README initially writes the same logarithmic exponent as a **multiplicative** factor, while elsewhere giving the familiar denominator and a transfer statement. The correct implication of the cited bound is **division** by the logarithmic power. The [maintained #920 page](https://www.erdosproblems.com/920) continued to describe the question as open in its May 28 revision, before Bradač's v3 update; the protected cohort register reports SOLVED. Preserve these dates rather than treating the older site status as a disproof of the newer theorem.

The protected [Formal Conjectures #920 file](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/85f863718beeec7b58a3a1926ee92e3472bc2020/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/920.lean), exact Git blob **9b3e9f83cc23be7bb87cfbefbc90a7220f5c3d0b**, correctly expresses the target logarithmic denominator. ## GCL's current state

The **finite Ramsey transfer** is an elementary, independently proved statement. A graph containing no $K_k$ with independence number at most $t-1$ cannot have a proper coloring using fewer than $\lceil n/(t-1)\rceil$ colors, because the union of that many independent color classes would contain fewer than $n$ vertices. The definition of $r(k,t)$ supplies such a graph on exactly $n$ vertices whenever $n<r(k,t)$.

For the asymptotic inference, write $d=k-1>1$ and $A=2k-4$. If

$$
r(k,t)\ge a_k\,\frac{t^d}{(\log t)^A},
$$

choose a sufficiently large constant $B$ and $t$ near $B n^{1/d}(\log n)^{A/d}$. The displayed lower bound is then greater than $n$, so the Ramsey transfer yields

$$
f_k(n)\gg_k
\frac{n^{1-1/d}}{(\log n)^{A/d}}.
$$

The **positive** $A$ in the denominator of the Ramsey bound remains a **positive denominator exponent** after inversion. This also explains why a version of Bradač's theorem with power $d=k-2$ would be too weak for the exact #920 target.

The exact regression at governance/erdos_compendium/research/EC-BULK-006/finite_920_921.py enumerates all labelled simple graphs with two through six vertices, computes independence and chromatic numbers by exact backtracking, checks $n\le\alpha(G)\chi(G)$ in every graph, and verifies $r(3,3)=6$ directly. All 33,866 graph instances pass. This validates the finite part of the proof pipeline, **not** the deep off-diagonal asymptotic bound.

## What remains

The bounded formalization obligation is the Ramsey-to-chromatic transfer and its exact-order logarithmic inversion. Inputs: an off-diagonal Ramsey lower bound with positive constants depending on $k$, a power $d>1$ and logarithmic exponent $A\ge0$. Output: the lower bound with exponent $1-1/d$ and logarithmic denominator exponent $A/d$ for **every sufficiently large integer $n$**. Acceptance requires a full proof or a pinned no-placeholder Lean development with literal axiom report and independent exact finite tests including $r(3,3)=6$. A sign error, a missing exact-order argument or invalid rounding of $t$ falsifies the derivation.

The distinct substantive source-review task is to pin and referee **Bradač's v3 Theorem 1.1**, verifying that its theorem for fixed $k$ truly has the stronger exponent $k-1$, that constants and quantifiers match the application, and that no assumptions are silently introduced.
