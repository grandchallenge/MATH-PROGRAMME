## The question in plain language

Choose an increasing sequence of pairwise coprime integers $u_1<u_2<\cdots$ greater than one, with convergent reciprocal sum

$$
\sum_{i=1}^\infty \frac1{u_i}<\infty.
$$

Remove from the positive integers every number divisible by any $u_i$. Arrange the remaining *survivors* as $a_1<a_2<\cdots$.

[Erdős Problem #1101](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1101) asks whether the sieve values $u_i$ can grow quite slowly while the gaps between successive survivors stay asymptotically as small as a sieve-theoretic lower bound permits.

## A small example

Consider the first six squares of distinct primes:

$$
4,\ 9,\ 25,\ 49,\ 121,\ 169.
$$

They are pairwise coprime. If integers from $1$ to $200$ divisible by any of these six numbers are removed, **122 survive**. The corresponding finite density product is

$$
\prod_{u\in\{4,9,25,49,121,169\}}\left(1-\frac1u\right)
=\frac{442368}{715715}.
$$

For the infinite prime-square sequence and $x=1000$, the product of the first three sieve moduli is $4\cdot9\cdot25=900$, while including the next gives $44100$. The scale parameter below is therefore $t_{1000}=3$.

The finite sieve does **not** show that the infinite prime-square sequence has the required gap property. That particular question is linked to another open Erdős problem.

## What is known

Let $t_x$ be determined by

$$
u_1\cdots u_{t_x}\le x
<u_1\cdots u_{t_x+1},
$$

and write $\delta=\prod_i(1-1/u_i)>0$. The sequence is called *good* when for every $\varepsilon>0$ and all sufficiently large $x$, the largest consecutive-survivor gap below $x$ is less than

$$
(1+\varepsilon)t_x\delta^{-1}.
$$

A standard sieve argument supplies an asymptotically matching lower bound. Erdős proved the existence of **some** good sequence of primes, but the main open questions require controlling their growth:

Can one choose $u_n$ of polynomial size in $n$? Can one choose $u_n\le\exp(o(n))$?

These are different assertions; existence of one good sequence with no growth guarantee does not settle either.
