## The question in plain language

Define h(n) as the MINIMUM possible clique number among n-vertex graphs for which EVERY set of seven vertices contains a triangle. Estimate h(n), in particular ask whether there are fixed c1,c2>0 with n^(1/3+c1)<<h(n)<<n^(1/2-c2). The local seven-set hypothesis is universal; it is vacuous for n<7.

## A small example

For n=7, the complete graph K7 has the every-seven-set triangle property and clique number 7. K7 with one edge missing also satisfies the property but clique number is 6. Testing a single seven-set cannot determine h(n) or the asymptotic exponents.

## What is known

Maintained https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/813, read 2026-10-14: Erdős–Hajnal proved n^(1/3)<<h(n)<<n^(1/2); Bucić–Sudakov [BuSu23], *Large independent sets from local considerations*, arXiv:2007.03667, prove h(n)>>n^(5/12-o(1)). This is strong positive movement on the lower side but does not prove the proposed fixed power saving below n^(1/2). Original paper proof and exact exponent uniformity uninspected.

The newer lower bound $h(n)\gg n^{5/12-o(1)}$ lies between the historical scales $n^{1/3}$ and $n^{1/2}$. A result at exponent $5/12-o(1)$ supplies a fixed positive improvement above $1/3$ but no corresponding fixed-exponent improvement below the recorded upper exponent $1/2$. Every seven-set, not merely some seven-set, must contain a triangle.

The historical and newer bounds, in their reported asymptotic senses, are

$$

n^{1/3}\ll h(n)\ll n^{1/2},
\qquad h(n)\gg n^{5/12-o(1)}.

$$

The newer lower exponent is strictly greater than one third, but the upper comparison with one half remains unresolved at the requested fixed-power level.
