## The question in plain language

Let L(r) be the smallest universal number of colours needed to properly colour every finite planar distance graph in which edges have length in some specified set A of exactly r positive distances. The question is whether L(r) is bounded by a POLYNOMIAL in r. The maintained status is OPEN. An arbitrary distance set A is fixed for each graph, and all edges corresponding to its listed lengths count; changing A or allowing different palettes per distance is only a proof technique.

## A small example

When r=1, the question is the Hadwiger–Nelson problem, with 5≤L(1)≤7. For r=2, choose collinear points 0,1,2 with A={1,2}; they form a triangle K3 and require three colours, a finite lower fixture. Any finite chromatic number must be at least the clique number, but small examples do not determine asymptotic growth.

## What is known

There is an elementary exponential upper bound L(r)≤7^r from the known 7-colouring of the Euclidean plane avoiding one specified distance. For each distance d∈A, rescale that fixed 7-colouring to avoid d, and label every point by the r-tuple of its colours. When two points have distance in A, the corresponding coordinate differs, so the tuple is a proper colouring; at most 7^r colours occur. The scaling requires d>0. This proves FINITENESS but does not approach r^O(1). The maintained source only gives the open polynomial question and L1 bounds.

## What remains

Investigate whether colourings for different forbidden distances can reuse classes to improve 7^r, or construct an exact family of planar distance graphs giving superlinear lower bounds. Acceptance is one rigorous finite combinatorial inequality (e.g. product colouring bound, already established) and a source-grounded discussion of where polynomial reduction would require new structure.
