## The question in plain language

For each positive integer n, sum 1/p over primes p≤n for which the least nonnegative remainder r=n mod p lies STRICTLY between p/2 and p. Erdős–Graham–Ruzsa–Straus conjecture this sum is asymptotic to (1/2) loglog n for EVERY sufficiently large n, as n→∞, not merely on average. The maintained source is OPEN. The indicator has to respect strict endpoint p/2<r<p.

## A small example

At n=10, prime p=2 has remainder 0 and contributes zero; p=3 remainder 1≤1.5 zero; p=5 remainder 0 zero; p=7 remainder 3≤3.5 zero, so the sum is 0. At n=11, p=2 remainder 1 equals p/2, so it STILL contributes zero, but p=3 remainder 2>1.5 contributes 1/3; p=5 remainder 1 contributes zero; p=7 remainder 4>3.5 contributes 1/7; p=11 remainder 0. Thus the sum is 10/21. Small n are not asymptotic evidence.

## What is known

For ODD prime p, the indicator equals floor(2n/p)−2 floor(n/p) since p/2 is half-integer and cannot equal integer r. For p=2 the floor difference is 1 when n odd, even though strict r>1 is impossible; hence the unrestricted floor identity popular in the source forum needs a p=2 correction of 1_{n odd}, a bounded O(1) term. The pinned FC #726.lean correctly encodes strict p/2< n mod p and has `answer(sorry)` and `by sorry`. Mertens' theorem says Σ_{p≤n}1/p∼loglog n, motivating the factor 1/2 but giving no control on the residue restriction for each n. Historical EGRS75 primary proof pages not inspected.
