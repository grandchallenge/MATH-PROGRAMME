## The question in plain language

Choose numbers from an arbitrary finite set so that no sum of two different chosen numbers lies in the original ambient set. The forbidden destination is the ambient set, not merely the chosen subset.

Define g(n) as the minimum across n-element A subset R of the largest |B|, B subset A, such that b1+b2 is not in A whenever b1,b2 in B are DISTINCT. The forbidden sum is in the ORIGINAL A, not merely B; diagonal pairs b+b are not forbidden unless separately stated.

## A small example

For A={1,2,3,4}, B={2,3,4} works: the distinct-pair sums are 5,6,7, none in A. But B={1,2} fails because 1+2=3 lies in A.

Finite example A={1,2,3,4}, B={1,2}: 1+2=3 in A, hence invalid. B={3,4} works, because 3+4=7 not in A. If B={2,3}, 2+3=5 not in A, also works. This is stronger than ordinary sum-free B; B={1,2} would be sum-free internally but fails here.
