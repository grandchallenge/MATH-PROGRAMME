## The question in plain language

An integer \(m>1\) is **powerful** if every prime \(p\mid m\) occurs with exponent at least two, equivalently \(p^2\mid m\). Erdős asked whether only finitely many exponents \(n\) give powerful values of either \(2^n-1\) or \(2^n+1\), and whether only finitely many \(n\) give powerful values of either \(n!-1\) or \(n!+1\). The unconditional finiteness questions remain open. The four signs and families must not be conflated. [Problem #936](https://www.erdosproblems.com/936).

## A small example

We have \(2^3+1=9=3^2\), \(4!+1=25=5^2\), \(5!+1=121=11^2\), and \(7!+1=5041=71^2\), all powerful. By contrast, \(2^4-1=15=3\cdot5\) is not powerful. The conventional definition regards 1 as powerful vacuously, but such small boundary cases have no effect on the finiteness question.

## What is known

The maintained problem catalogue attributes to Cushing and Pascoe [CuPa16] an **abc-conditional** theorem: for each fixed nonnegative integer \(K\), only finitely many pairs \((n,x)\) with \(x\) powerful satisfy \(\lvert x-n!\rvert\le K\). Taking \(K=1\) supplies the predicted factorial finiteness under abc. CrowdMath [Cr20] likewise establishes the corresponding conditional finiteness for \(2^n\pm1\). Neither result is unconditional. The page's formalized-statement marker concerns the encoding of the conjecture, **not** a formal proof. <https://www.erdosproblems.com/936>

## Why the problem is difficult

Powerfulness requires simultaneous restrictions on every prime factor, not merely the existence of one repeated factor. Factorizations of isolated examples cannot bound all exponents. The abc conjecture controls radical size relative to sums; it supplies a conditional obstruction but cannot be imported silently as a theorem.

A test for an integer \(m>1\) is exact: compute \(v_p(m)\) for each prime dividing \(m\), and accept precisely if all nonzero valuations are at least two. Finitely many checks can falsify an overstrong bounded assertion, but cannot certify that only finitely many exponents satisfy the condition.
