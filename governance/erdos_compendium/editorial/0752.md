## The question in plain language

For a graph G with minimum degree k and girth greater than 2s, Erdős, Faudree and Schelp asked if the graph must have at least c_s k^s distinct cycle lengths. This is PROVED by Sudakov and Verstraëte (2008). A stronger theorem assumes only average degree k and produces c_s k^s CONSECUTIVE EVEN cycle lengths; the hidden constant depends on fixed s.

## A small example

The six-cycle C6 has minimum and average degree 2 and girth 6. For s=2, the hypothesis girth>4 holds, and C6 has exactly one cycle length, 6. This finite example cannot contradict an asymptotic lower bound with a small unspecified constant c_s and k fixed at 2. K_{k,k} has girth 4, so it does not satisfy s=2's strict girth>4 hypothesis.

## What is known

Erdős–Faudree–Schelp proved s=2; Sudakov and Verstraëte [SuVe08] proved the general result. The maintained 2026 page says at least a constant times k^s consecutive even integers realized as lengths when minimum degree is weakened to average degree. 'Consecutive even integers' means ℓ,ℓ+2,…, not consecutive integers of both parities. Neither the original Sudakov–Verstraëte full proof nor its threshold on k,s was independently rederived in this GCL audit.
