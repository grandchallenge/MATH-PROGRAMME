## The question in plain language

Choose a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers

$$
a_1<a_2<a_3<\cdots
$$

that becomes sparse in the average sense

$$
\frac{a_n}{n}\longrightarrow\infty.
$$

The question is whether the binary-looking weighted sum

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_n}{2^{a_n}}
$$

must be irrational. The quantifiers matter: the assertion is about every such sequence, not about one convenient choice. The same integer $a_n$ appears both as the coefficient and as the exponent. The growth condition says that the $n$ th selected exponent eventually lies farther than any fixed multiple of $n$; it does not require the individual gaps $a_{n+1}-a_n$ to grow. Long clusters of small gaps can be separated by occasional very large gaps.

The sum converges because the exponential denominator dominates the linear numerator, and strict increase makes the exponents distinct. A finite change in the first few terms changes the sum by a rational number, so the irrationality question is fundamentally about the tail. The problem is therefore a question about how sparsity interacts with the base-two expansion, not merely about estimating a numerical series.

## A small example

The sequence $a_n=2n$ is strictly increasing, but it does not satisfy the problem's hypothesis: $a_n/n=2$. It is useful as a warning that a sparse-looking series can still be rational. Directly,

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2n}{2^{2n}}
=2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n}{4^n}
=2\frac{1/4}{(1-1/4)^2}
=\frac89.
$$

The finite calculation is also exact. For every $m\geq1$,

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{m}\frac{2n}{4^n}
=\frac89\left(1-\frac{m+1}{4^m}+\frac{m}{4^{m+1}}\right).
$$

For example, an exact rational-arithmetic check gives the partial sum at $m=20$ as $7635497415/8589934592$, with tail $1/77309411328$.

```python
from fractions import Fraction

m = 20
partial = sum((Fraction(2*n, 4**n) for n in range(1, m + 1)), Fraction(0))
closed = Fraction(8, 9) * (1 - Fraction(m + 1, 4**m) + Fraction(m, 4**(m + 1)))
assert partial == closed
assert Fraction(8, 9) - partial == Fraction(1, 77309411328)
```

This is not a counterexample to Problem 260, because its average density is positive. It isolates the real boundary: rationality is easy for a regular, non-sparse support, while the problem asks what happens when the selected exponents have zero average density.

## What is known

Erdős's primary paper, “Sur l'irrationalité d'une certaine série,” *C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris* 292 (1981), 765–768, proves irrationality under stronger hypotheses. On p. 765 he proves the result when

$$
a_{n+1}-a_n\longrightarrow\infty,
$$

and also records a proof under the growth condition

$$
a_n\gg n\sqrt{\log n\,\log\log n}.
$$

The paper says that the desired condition $a_n/n\to\infty$ very likely suffices, but does not supply that proof or a rational example with unbounded limsup gaps. Thus the classical source is a proof interface for two stronger regimes, not a proof of the exact problem. The same paper notes that the base $2$ can be replaced by an integer base $t>1$ in its stronger results.

Borwein and Loring's paper, “Some Questions of Erdős and Graham on Numbers of the Form $\sum g_n/2^{g_n}$,” *Mathematics of Computation* 54 (1990), 377–394, p. 378, records a related irrationality condition involving

$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n+1}-a_n}{\log a_{n+1}}=\infty.
$$

That condition is also not implied by $a_n/n\to\infty$: average sparsity allows local clusters of short gaps. These results explain why the original formulation remained nontrivial.

The current external claim is Han Wang's arXiv preprint, version 4, “Sparse polynomial-weighted expansions.” Its Theorem 1.1 states that for an integer $b\geq2$, a nonzero polynomial $p\in\mathbb{Q}[x]$, and an infinite set $S\subseteq\mathbb{N}$, rationality of

$$
\sum_{n\in S}p(n)b^{-n}
$$

forces, for every fixed $0<\theta\leq1$, constants $c>0$ and $N_0$ such that

$$
|S\cap(N,N+W]|\geq cW
$$

whenever $N\geq N_0$ and $N^\theta\leq W\leq N$. Corollary 1.2 applies this to $b=2$, $p(n)=n$, and $S=\{a_1,a_2,\ldots\}$, yielding positive lower density and hence contradicting $a_n/n\to\infty$. The preprint describes a carry recurrence and polynomial-height argument; its Lemma 2.1 is the local gap interface used by the corollary. This is a claimed external proof, not an accepted GCL proof.

## What remains

The central unresolved question is status adjudication, not statement access. A specialist review must check the full Theorem 1.1 argument and its specialization, including denominator normalization, the carry orbit, the polynomial-window density conclusion, and the passage from positive lower density to a contradiction. A bounded successor could first audit the binary linear case of Lemma 2.1 and reproduce its exact recurrence before considering the longer theorem. Until that work is completed, “the preprint claims a proof” is the strongest safe wording.

One elementary consequence makes the proposed contradiction transparent. Let $A(x)$ count the selected exponents at most $x$. If $a_n/n\to\infty$, then for every $M>0$, eventually $a_n\geq Mn$. Consequently, for large $x$,

$$
A(x)\leq C_M+\frac{x}{M},
$$

so $A(x)/x\to0$ after letting $M$ grow. Wang's claimed Corollary 1.2 would instead give $A(x)\geq c'x$ for all sufficiently large $x$. The contradiction is rigorous conditional on that external theorem, but it does not validate the theorem's proof. It also does not turn the `sorry`-bearing pinned declaration or an unreviewed repository into certification.
