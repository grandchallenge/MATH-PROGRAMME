## The question in plain language

For integers $1\le k<\ell$ and a positive integer $N$, consider subsets $A$ of the positive integers with exactly $N$ elements. Let $F_k(N,\ell)$ denote the threshold number of **nonconstant $k$ term arithmetic progressions** that forces $A$ to contain an $\ell$ term progression. Erdős asked how large this threshold can be and, particularly, whether

$$
F_3(N,4)=o(N^2).
$$

He also asked whether, for every fixed $\ell>3$, the logarithmic growth exponent of $F_3(N,\ell)$ tends to two. The universal quantifier is over **all $N$ element sets of integers**, not only subsets of $[1,N]$. A progression is counted as a set, with positive common difference to avoid duplicate orientation.

## A small example

The set $A=\{1,2,3,4\}$ has two nonconstant three-term progressions, $(1,2,3)$ and $(2,3,4)$, and a four-term progression $(1,2,3,4)$. A much denser set can have many three-term progressions without a long progression when only a finite, carefully chosen universe is examined; determining the maximum over all $\ell$ progression-free $N$ sets requires an extremal argument.

If $M$ is the maximum number of $k$ term progressions in an $N$ set containing no $\ell$ term progression, then the **smallest forcing threshold** is $M+1$. This precise plus-one convention matters for finite calculations but not for the large-scale estimates.

## What is known

**PROVED** for the highlighted asymptotic questions. Fox and Pohoata, [*Sets without k-term progressions can have many shorter progressions*](https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.20984), established an essentially quadratic growth law, $F_k(N,\ell)=N^{2-o(1)}$ for fixed progression lengths $3\le k<\ell$, together with

$$
F_k(N,\ell)\le \frac{N^2}{(\log\log N)^{C_\ell}}
$$

for some fixed $C_\ell>0$. The bound is $o(N^2)$, but its logarithmic growth exponent is still two. These statements are compatible: the saving grows more slowly than every positive power of $N$.

The maintained [Erdős #179](https://www.erdosproblems.com/179), updated April 2026, also records refinements using improved quantitative versions of Szemerédi's theorem by Leng, Sah and Sawhney, of the form $N^2/\exp((\log\log N)^{c_\ell})$. The source relates the extremal threshold to $r_\ell(N)$, the maximum size of a subset of $[1,N]$ without an $\ell$ term progression. Full quantitative proofs have not been independently reconstructed.

## GCL's current state

The pinned and maintained catalogue classify #179 **PROVED**. Original source issue: [#1448](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1448). The statement's exponent-two limit and a subquadratic upper bound are kept distinct. Any apparent formula with $k=1$ or $k=2$ must be normalized against trivial short-progression counting before transferring the general source claim.

The original issue contains an authenticated [RESULT/1 source report](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/1448#issuecomment-6168939035). This is unadjudicated source evidence only, not independent mathematical certification or editorial admission.

## What remains

A bounded fixture enumerates subsets of $[1,12]$ of size at most six, counts their three-term and four-term progressions exactly, and computes the finite maximum $M$ among sets without four-term progressions. Acceptance: the four-element example has two three-term progressions and one four-term progression; the threshold equals $M+1$. Finite extremal counts are not asymptotic source proofs.
