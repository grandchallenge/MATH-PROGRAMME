## Exact statement and normalization
For integer $t\ge2$ and $m\ge1$, define $s_t(m)=\prod_{p<t}p^{v_p(m)}$ with **strict** $p<t$. Set $f(n,t)=|\{s_t(n+i):1\le i\le t\}|$ for $n\ge0$. The modern uniform question asks whether there is an absolute $c>0$ with $f(n,t)\ge ct$ for every $n,t$. The original Erdős–Graham monograph (1980), printed pp.91–92, writes $n+i=a_i b_i$ with all primes of $a_i$ below $t$ and all primes of $b_i$ at least $t$, asks whether $\min_t f(n,t)/t>\varepsilon$, and reports $f(n,t)\gg t/\log t$. The original printed question is less explicit about the outer $n$ quantifier; preserve this source-to-tracker normalization.

## Strongest verified interface
Proved directly: (1) If two offsets $i\ne j$ share smooth component $s\ge t$, then $s$ divides $|i-j|<t$, contradiction. (2) For $L=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,t)$, $s_t(L^2+i)=s_t(i)$ for $1\le i\le t$, so $f(L^2,t)=t$ if $t$ composite and $t-1$ if $t$ prime. (3) For $1\le y<t$, $P_t=\prod_{p<t}p$, $Q=P_t\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,y)$, and $u\le y$, exact counting gives $\Pr_{m\bmod Q}(s_t(m)=u)=u^{-1}\prod_{p<t}(1-1/p)$. Therefore $\inf_n f(n,t)\le y+t[1-H_y\prod_{p<t}(1-1/p)]$. Taking $y=\lfloor t/\log t\rfloor$ and Mertens's product theorem yields $\limsup_{t\to\infty}\inf_n f(n,t)/t\le1-e^{-\gamma}$. This upper obstruction was already noted publicly in June 2026, and is NOT a disproof of existence of a smaller positive lower constant. Exact finite regression: 176 high-component uniqueness cases, 38 periodic examples, $t=5$ residue counts, and $f(1407302,38)=27$ with Hall-neighbourhood offsets $9,10,13,26,28$. All four tests pass.
