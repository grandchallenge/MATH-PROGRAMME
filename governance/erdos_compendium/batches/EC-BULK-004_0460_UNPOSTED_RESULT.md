## Exact statement and normalization
The 1977 Erdős source, printed p.64, defines $a_0=0$, $a_1=1$ and the increasing greedy sequence with $\gcd(n-a_k,n-a_i)=1$ for every $0\leq i<k$. It asks whether $g(n)=\sum_{i\geq1}1/a_i\to\infty$ as $n\to\infty$. The 1980 Erdős–Graham source, printed p.91, instead uses $a_0=n$, $a_1=1$ and $1\leq i<k$, omitting the coprimality requirement with $n$. Both original displays are UNTRUNCATED. The maintained #460 tracker introduces the mathematically substantive normalization $S_{<n}(n)=\sum_{0<a_i<n}1/a_i$, and asks for pointwise divergence. This is an editorial clarification, not an exact literal transcription of either original display. The 1980 variant needs $n\geq3$ to avoid a degenerate $n=2$ greedy construction.

## Strongest verified interface
For each fixed $n\geq3$, both unrestricted greedy sequences include $n+p$ for every prime $p>n$: $n+p$ is admissible because earlier nonzero $n-a_i$ have absolute value $<p$, so the greedy rule cannot skip it. The divergence of prime reciprocals proves that the literal unrestricted sum diverges for each fixed $n$. This does NOT answer the truncated question. For $1\leq a<n$, define $P^-(1)=\infty$ and $R_n=\{a:P^-(n-a)>a\}$. Every $a\in R_n$ is forced into either greedy sequence: any common prime factor of $n-a$ and an earlier $n-b$ divides $a-b<a$, impossible; in the 1977 version the same argument includes $b=0$. Therefore $S_{<n}(n)\geq f(n)=\sum_{a\in R_n}1/a$ pointwise. Independent exact fixtures checked all $n=3,\ldots,250$ for both variants (496 inclusion checks), plus the reciprocal-weight inequality and six exact sequences. No pointwise divergence follows from these finite tests.

SOURCE-CRITICAL CORRECTION: The inspected 9-page external note's Theorem 8 claims $N^{-1}\sum_{n\leq N}f(n)\gg\log\log N$. Its printed proof restricts $a$ to $[N^{1/3},N^{1/2}]$ and then incorrectly asserts $\sum 1/(a\log a)\asymp\log\log N$ over that range. In fact the integral is $\log(3/2)$, a constant. The displayed argument proves at most a positive constant lower bound for the mean, not the claimed growth. The theorem might be repairable with stronger uniform sieve estimates, but it is NOT verified by the cited proof. A weaker divergent mean bound follows rigorously from elementary inclusion-exclusion plus Mertens: with $A=\lfloor\sqrt{\log N}\rfloor$, for $3\leq a\leq A$, $\Phi(N-a,a)=(N-a)\prod_{p\leq a}(1-1/p)+O(2^{\pi(a)})\gg N/\log a$ uniformly, since $2^A=N^{o(1)}$. Consequently $N^{-1}\sum_{n\leq N}f(n)\gg\sum_{3\leq a\leq A}1/(a\log a)\gg\log\log\log N$, which gives $\limsup_{n\to\infty}S_{<n}(n)=\infty$ but NOT $S_{<n}(n)\to\infty$.
