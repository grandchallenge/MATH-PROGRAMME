Two genuinely distinct functions are involved: $f(n)$ is the **largest edge count** among particular connected graphs $G$ with $R(K_3,G)=2n-1$; $F(n)$ is the largest threshold for which **every** connected graph with that many or fewer edges has the same Ramsey number. In particular $F(n)\le f(n)$.

Brandt [Br96] reportedly proved $F(n)\le84n$, which answers the source's **final** question $F(n)/n\to\infty$ **negatively**. This does not solve the remaining open sharp growth questions for $f(n)$ and $F(n)$. Do not mark the entire problem solved merely because a final yes/no subquestion is refuted.

The exact small case $R(K_3,P_3)=5$ has been exhaustively checked among all red/blue colorings of $K_4$ and $K_5$, but is not a bound on the two extremal functions.
