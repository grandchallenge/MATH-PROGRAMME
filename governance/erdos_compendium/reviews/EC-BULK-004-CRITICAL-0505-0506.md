## #505: Borsuk conjecture DISPROVED, dated records distinct

Original P. Erdős (1961), printed p.244 Problem III.3, primary https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1961-22.pdf#page=24 verified by original paper scan. General claim: every bounded Euclidean R^d set of positive diameter D can be covered by d+1 subsets of STRICTLY smaller diameter. Positive D is needed for zero-diameter edge cases; scaling D=1 fine. Small-dimensional d<=3 cases true. For finite S of positive diameter, exactly define diametral graph joining pairs at distance D. An independent set in that graph iff its diameter STRICTLY <D (finite maximum attained), and the minimum number of smaller-diameter partition classes equals chromatic number. This equivalence is a self-contained elementary lemma. It does NOT automatically cover infinite sets where supremum may be unattained.

Kahn–Kalai 1993, *A counterexample to Borsuk's conjecture*, Bull. AMS 29, 60–62, https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9307229 original printed pp.1–3 visually read. Theorem 1: b(d)>=(1.2)^sqrt(d) for sufficiently large d; proof constructs equal-partition incidence vectors and invokes Frankl–Wilson intersection theorem, Stirling, PNT. Authors expressly give d=1325 and all d>2014 as failures. This conclusively disproves general conjecture without relying on later records.

## #506: DECIDABLE, not fully enumerated; degeneracy and error correction

Erdős 1961 printed p.245 Problem III.4 source https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1961-22.pdf#page=25 primary printed text extracted; asks minimum distinct circles for n points not all on a circle. Non-all-collinear must be imposed too, otherwise a wholly collinear point set produces no circles; NO-THREE-COLLINEAR is strictly stronger and invalidates known equality witnesses. Distinct circles counted by incidence with noncollinear triples, not number of triples.

Elliott 1967 *On the number of circles determined by n points*, Acta Math. Acad. Sci. Hungar. 18, 181–188, DOI 10.1007/BF02020972, full proof body NOT retrieved, initially asserted uncorrected n>393 bound C(n-1,2). Purdy–Smith correction reports n>393 bound C(n-1,2)+1-floor((n-1)/2). The bibliographic Purdy–Smith document could not be located (the maintained catalogue explicitly gives '[PuSm] No reference found'). Bálintová–Bálint 1994 *Acta Math. Hungar.* 63, 283–289, DOI 10.1007/BF01874133, corroborating published mathematical provenance, full paper unread. No independent transitive full lower-bound proof, so large-n value is literature-grounded, not GCL certified.

Exact witness includes m=n-1 unit-circle points arranged in floor(m/2) antipodal pairs plus at most one unmatched point, and O=(0,0). O and opposite points are collinear, producing no circle, exactly floor(m/2) missing triples. Original unit circle counted once. Every other unordered outer pair determines a unique circle through O. Two different outer pairs cannot share a new circle because any three distinct outer points would then lie on two different circles, impossible. Thus there are exactly 1+C(m,2)-floor(m/2) circles. One OFF-circle point in arbitrary position is insufficient for this sharp construction; choosing CENTER creates the missing collinear triples. For n=8 count19<21=C(7,2) disproves Elliott's uncorrected unconditional numerical bound directly.

A fully independent exact rational test enumerates every three-point subset for n=4..15, solves x²+y²+D*x+E*y+F=0 with Fraction for noncollinear triples, normalizes and deduplicates (D,E,F), and checks the predicted count. Additional tests verify 0 circles for all-collinear input, antipodal triple degeneracy and order-independent normalization. Eight focused tests for #505+#506 passed. In particular n=5→5, n=8→19. These supply constructive upper bounds and semantic guards, not the universal n>393 lower bound. For each FIXED n, realization of circle counts by finitely many real points is a first-order real-closed-fields predicate and in-principle decidable through quantifier elimination. However c(n) for every 4<=n<=393 is not established. Preserve exact catalogue special DECIDABLE status, neither SOLVED nor ordinary OPEN, until governed source reconciliation.
