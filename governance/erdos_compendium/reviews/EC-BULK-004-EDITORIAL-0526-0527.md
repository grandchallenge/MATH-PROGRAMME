## #526 — SOLVED by Shepp (1972), exact monotonicity and coverage caveats

Original P. Erdős, *Some unsolved problems* (1961), printed pp.253–254 V.4, https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1961-22.pdf#page=33 and #page=34 (PDF pages indexes32–33), directly extracted and reviewed. On circumference-one circle, independent uniform arcs of deterministic lengths a_n≥0 tending to zero, Σa_n=∞; find criterion for EVERY point covered with probability one. Fixed-point uncovered probability ∏(1−a_n)=0 implies uncovered circle set has Lebesgue measure zero a.s. by Fubini, NOT that the set is empty. Dvoretzky 1956, *On covering a circle by randomly placed arcs*, PNAS 42 199–203, primary https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC528251/ accessible; Kahane 1959 C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 248 184–186; original Erdős credits Kahane (1+c)/n, Erdős unpublished n^(-1) positive and (1−c)/n negative.

Larry A. Shepp, *Covering the circle with random arcs*, Israel J Math 11(3) (1972) 328–345, DOI 10.1007/BF02789327, original JOURNAL ABSTRACT reviewed. Crucial theorem hypothesis: ℓ_1≥ℓ_2≥...>0, ℓ_n<1, ℓ_n→0. Full circle covered a.s. iff Σ_(n≥1) n^(-2) exp(Σ_(k≤n)ℓ_k)=∞. Original Erdős packet and maintained erdosproblems.com/526 omit monotone hypothesis and show formula with a_n in their chronological order. For general nonmonotone a_n→0, geometric covering law unchanged under deterministic permutation of independently uniform arcs; positive lengths can be rearranged nonincreasing, so apply Shepp to SORTED sequence. This is an explicit semantic normalization, not a new theorem. If some a_n≥1, one arc itself covers circle and criterion unnecessary; zeros do not affect coverage. Published Shepp full 18-page proof not independently reconstructed, do not claim end-to-end verification.

Exact ℓ_n=c/n example, early length ≥1 handled separately: S_n=cH_n and log(n+1)≤H_n≤1+log n, so n^(c-2)-comparable Shepp terms. If 0<c<1 convergence and noncoverage; if c≥1 divergence and full coverage (at c=1 true nontrivial boundary). Borderline WARNING: condition ℓ_n~1/n ALONE is not enough, as ℓ_n=1/n−2/(n log n) eventually, with positive decreasing prefix, gives S_n=log n−2 loglog n+O(1) and summand ∼1/[n(log n)^2], convergent; the article explicitly corrected a preliminary overgeneralization before any commit. Correct historical threshold applies exactly to c/n or controlled asymptotics, not arbitrary relative-error perturbations.

## #527 — PROVED by Michelen–Sawhney (2025), convergence set Hausdorff dimension one

Original Erdős (1961), printed p.254, https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1961-22.pdf#page=34, directly inspected. Deterministic complex a_n with sqrt(n)|a_n|→0, iid fair ±1 signs ε_n, ask whether almost surely exists a RANDOM-REALIZATION-DEPENDENT unit circle z such that Σ ε_n a_n z^n converges. Original motivating case Σ|a_n|²=∞ and initial special coefficients monotonicity; neither hypothesis is required in 2025 final theorem. Dvoretzky–Erdős *Divergence of random power series*, Michigan Math J 6 (1959) 343–347 gives opposite threshold |a_n|≳n^-1/2 implies almost sure divergence EVERYWHERE. Original complete proof of 1959 result unread.

Marcus Michelen and Mehtaab Sawhney, *Convergent points for random power series on the unit circle*, arXiv:2509.02729v1 (September2 2025), COMPLETE primary 14-page PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.02729 downloaded to CHAD /tmp/ec004-michelen-2025.pdf; original web screenshot page1 and page2 visually inspected, Theorem1.1 (exists convergent point a.s.) and Theorem1.2 (set of convergent points has Hausdorff dimension exactly1 a.s.) directly verified. Primary no extra monotonicity, no mandatory Σ|a_n|²=∞. For non-square-summable coefficients, each FIXED z diverges a.s. and the set has Lebesgue measure zero a.s. by Fubini, consistent with exceptional Hausdorff dimension1. This is NOT a contradiction and quantifier ∀sign a.s. ∃z(signs) must not be swapped. Paper uses multiscale alive-point branching, Lemma2.3 good-point convergence, Lemma2.5 Lindeberg replacement and Gaussian correlation; full 14-page proof not independently rederived, though primary theorem read.

Elementary sharpness example: a_n²=1/[n(1+floor(log2 n))] => sqrt(n)|a_n|→0 while dyadic block n∈[2^j,2^(j+1)) contributes ≥1/[2(j+1)] to Σ|a_n|², so sum diverges. Exact iid orthogonality for any fixed |z|=1: E|Σ_(n=m)^N ε_n a_n z^n|²=Σ_(n=m)^N |a_n|², finite check on unit roots 1,−1,i for rational weights. tests/test_erdos_compendium_0527_convergent_points.py: four focused tests PASS using rational dyadic block sums, all 256 sign assignments for length8 and exact Gaussian-rational pair arithmetic, plus coefficient-scope examples. This does not prove probabilistic existence or fractal dimension.
