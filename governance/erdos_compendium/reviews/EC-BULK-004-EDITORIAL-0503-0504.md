## #503: local isosceles triples, not global two-distance restriction

Original Erdős, *Some unsolved problems* (1961), printed p.244 III.1, https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1961-22.pdf#page=24 visually inspected. The mathematical question is largest |A| among configurations in R^d where EVERY THREE distinct points have at least two equal pairwise distances. Original least scalene-forcing number is I(d)+1. It does not require all pairwise distances across the entire set to belong to a fixed two-element set. OPEN for arbitrary d, with I(2)=6 Kelly 1947, I(3)=8 Croft 1962 reported by the maintained catalogue and pinned formal statement.

Primary newer Z. Kovács, *A note on Erdős's mysterious remark*, 2024 arXiv:2412.05190v2, printed p.1 and problem introduction visually inspected: the regular pentagon and centre is the only plane six-point all-isosceles configuration up to similarity; primary original full computer-algebra elimination NOT independently replayed. The simple six-point construction is exact: five outer vertices have only two possible chord lengths, and the centre is equidistant from all outer vertices. Kelly/Croft upper-proof bodies were NOT independently retrieved; treat their exact I(2)/I(3) as established attributed results with source-replay boundary.

General upper I(d)<=C(d+2,2) attributed to Blokhuis, *Few-distance sets* 1984 thesis, original full thesis uninspected. Lower I(d)>=C(d+1,2)+1 for d>=3 is explicitly known from Alweiss weight-two vector construction plus Weisenberg centre improvement on the maintained Oct 2025 catalogue. These points all belong to affine hyperplane sum coordinates=2 in R^(d+1), of dimension d; the centroid has coordinates 2/(d+1), all squared radii 2-4/(d+1); squared distances among distinct weight-two vectors are 2 and 4. Therefore for every 3-point triple, either all three vertices are original (at most two lengths by pigeonhole), or the centroid is among them (two equal radii), proving isosceles. Importantly the union has exactly THREE distinct global squared distances. At d=3 it gives 7 vs Croft I(3)=8, so cannot claim the simple lower construction is sharp.

## #504: source correction with two distinct erroneous small-N branches

Blumenthal alpha_N = inf over all distinct N-point planar sets P of the largest angle of any triple in P. Original Erdős 1961 printed p.244 III.2 visually inspected. Complete FIRST-HAND 1960 original Erdős–Szekeres, *On some extremum problems in elementary geometry*, Ann. Univ. Sci. Budapest. 3 (1960), 53–62, https://renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1960-09.pdf#page=2 printed p.54 visually inspected. It explicitly states alpha_3=pi/3, alpha_4=pi/2, alpha_5=3pi/5 and alpha_6=alpha_7=alpha_8=2pi/3. The historic dyadic formula needs an appropriate small-index cutoff; extending to alpha_(2²-1) conflicts with alpha_3.
