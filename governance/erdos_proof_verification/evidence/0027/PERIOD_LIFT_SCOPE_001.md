Let P>0, k>0, T=kP. Each integer 0<=x<T has the unique form x=qP+r, 0<=q<k, 0<=r<P. If Q is any predicate on residues modulo P, exactly k representatives in the T-period project to each residue r. Consequently count_T(Q)=k count_P(Q) and count_T(Q)/T=count_P(Q)/P. This does not require gcd(k,P)=1.

For a finite ordered family of positive moduli n_i and integer residues r_i, take T any positive common multiple of all moduli. At step j write m=n_j and B=lcm(n_i: i<j, gcd(n_i,m)=1), with empty lcm=1. Every selected n_i divides B, gcd(m,B)=1, and m,B both divide T. Coprimality implies mB divides T. The protected product-period joint independence identity now lifts from mB to T by the quotient/remainder argument. For the event A_j of residue r_j modulo m and the set I_j avoiding every earlier coprime modulus, mass_T(A_j intersect I_j)=mass_T(I_j)/m. Also mass_T(A_j)=1/m, using period lift from m to T (no coprimality assumption on the multiplier).

Let D_j be the union of the earlier noncoprime events, U_j=I_j minus D_j, and U_{j+1}=U_j minus A_j. Then mass_T(D_j)<=sum_{i<j,gcd(n_i,m)>1}1/n_i by the finite union bound, whether events coincide, overlap or are disjoint. With q_j=mass_T(U_j), p_j=1/n_j, e_j=p_j times that sum, the protected finite recurrence gives q_{j+1}>=(1-p_j)q_j-e_j. Starting q_0=1 yields the weighted product-error bound q_n>=prod_j(1-p_j)-sum_j e_j prod_{k>j}(1-p_k), and its weaker unweighted version. This proves the ordinary arithmetic bridge for the common period, including repeated moduli, modulus one and negative residues. It is not a Lean proof of the composed arithmetic lemma.
