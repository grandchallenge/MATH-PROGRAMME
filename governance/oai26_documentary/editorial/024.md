# OAI-26 family 024: an asymptotic formula for the number of totients

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** This article describes source-indexed claims rather than independently certifying their proofs.

## The question in plain language

Euler's totient function counts positive integers up to a given integer that are relatively prime to it. Many different inputs produce the same totient, so counting the number of *distinct totient values* below a threshold is different from counting inputs.

Family 024 concerns the counting function \(V(x)\): the number of different positive integers of the form \(\varphi(n)\) that are at most \(x\). The pinned OAI-26 overview claims an asymptotic formula for this count and, importantly, a scaling law that settles a question attributed to Erdős and Hall.

## A small example

The first few totient values illustrate the repeated outputs:

$$
\varphi(5)=4,\qquad \varphi(8)=4,\qquad \varphi(10)=4.
$$

All three inputs contribute only one distinct value to \(V(x)\). The totient values can be sparse among all integers, yet their total number may grow in a systematic way.

A proposed asymptotic equivalent for \(V(x)\) must capture its size accurately up to a ratio tending to one. A weaker estimate that only bounds \(V(x)\) between constant multiples of another function does not establish the same precision.

The stated scaling result is

$$
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{V(cx)}{V(x)}=c
$$

for every fixed positive real \(c\). This resembles the scaling behavior of a function growing linearly, even though the proportion of integers that occur as totients can be much smaller and may change slowly.

## What is known

The pinned OAI-26 catalogue identifies one manuscript, *An asymptotic formula for the number of totients* (September 25, 2026).

The publisher overview claims an asymptotic equivalent for \(V(x)\) featuring a **positive bounded phase-dependent factor** derived from convergent arithmetic approximations. It further asserts the scaling law above for all fixed \(c>0\), answering the Erdős–Hall scaling question.

The source's phase-dependent factor must not be replaced with a fixed universal constant without proof. Nor does the scaling law by itself determine a unique explicit asymptotic formula: different slowly varying factors can have the same scaling behavior.

These are indexed manuscript claims. The actual formula, its auxiliary functions, the meaning of the phase and how convergence is established need examination of the exact pinned source PDF.
