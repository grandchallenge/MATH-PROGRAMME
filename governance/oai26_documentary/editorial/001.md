# OAI-26 family 001: Milne's rationality conjecture for abelian varieties

## The question in plain language

Suppose an abelian variety (a higher-dimensional analogue of an elliptic curve) defined in characteristic zero has good reduction modulo a prime. A Hodge class is a cohomological object on the original variety. The October 7 manuscript claims that, after reduction, pairing this class with any complementary collection of divisor classes produces the *same rational number* across the available cohomology theories. This is weaker than proving the original Hodge class is represented by an algebraic cycle. The manuscript explicitly separates its main rationality theorem from a stronger algebraicity consequence requiring an additional CM Hodge theorem.

## A small example

For a codimension-zero class, namely the unit class on an elliptic curve, pairing with the class of a divisor gives its integer degree. This is a basic model of why intersection numbers should be rational. It **does not** establish the manuscript's main assertion for arbitrary rational Hodge classes, new divisors appearing only after reduction, or crystalline and ell-adic compatibility. In the opposite endpoint $r=d$, the manuscript specifies an empty product of divisor classes, interpreted as $1$.

## What is known

The pinned PDF states Theorem 1.1 (source label `thm:main`, PDF pp. 1–2) for an abelian variety over the algebraic closure of the rationals with good reduction at a chosen prime. It fixes a rational Hodge class of codimension between zero and the dimension of the variety, and considers every complementary list of Cartier divisors on the special fiber. It asserts one common rational number for the corresponding top-degree traces in all ell-adic realizations away from the reduction prime and in the de Rham–crystalline realization at that prime. The quantifier applies to each fixed class and divisor list; it does not compare different reduction places.

The introduction attributes absolute-Hodge compatibility, Lefschetz pairing theory, CM transport and integral deformation inputs to earlier sources (PDF pp. 2–4). The manuscript's claimed argument uses two different auxiliary constructions: CM transport of the original specialized class (Corollary 3.4, p. 17), and a possibly non-CM lift with a prescribed filtration preserving a divisor (pp. 27–28). Their comparisons are between marked *special fibers*; the PDF explicitly claims no map between their generic fibers (Figure 1, p. 5). For balanced Weil classes, Proposition 5.2 (pp. 24–28) claims a dichotomy: all complementary pairings vanish, or the specialized class is a simultaneous rational Lefschetz class, expressible by rational divisor products in every realization. Lemma 6.1 (pp. 29–31) reduces CM classes to these balanced Weil classes, and the proof of Theorem 1.1 is on p. 31. Corollary 6.3 (pp. 31–33) concerns membership in a fixed cohomological theory, **not** an algebraic cycle; Corollary 6.4 (p. 33) additionally invokes the CM Hodge theorem to conclude algebraicity on the special fiber. These are *manuscript assertions*, not independently established results in this editorial article.
