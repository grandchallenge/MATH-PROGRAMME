## The question in plain language

An elliptic curve is a geometric object defined by a cubic equation that also carries an arithmetic group law. Modularity connects the arithmetic information of such a curve to automorphic forms, which have strong symmetry and analytic properties.

Over the rational numbers, modularity is an established theorem. Imaginary quadratic fields are a different base: they arise by adjoining the square root of a negative integer to the rationals. The pinned OAI-26 overview claims that **every elliptic curve over every imaginary quadratic field is modular**, including compatibility of local parameters at every place.

## A small example

An elliptic curve can be written, in a suitable characteristic-zero model, as

$$
y^2=x^3+ax+b,
$$

where the coefficients belong to the chosen number field and the discriminant is nonzero. Changing the coefficient field from the rationals to an imaginary quadratic field changes the arithmetic problem, even though the equation may look familiar.

A modularity statement does not merely say that two objects have the same number of solutions modulo one prime. It seeks an automorphic representation whose arithmetic data match that of the elliptic curve. The additional requirement that *local parameters match at every place* concerns the primes of the field and its archimedean places, not just a density-one set of well-behaved primes.

## What is known

The publisher overview describes its headline result as full modularity for **all** elliptic curves over **all** imaginary quadratic fields, with matching local parameters **at every place**.

Those quantifiers are important. An argument covering only curves with a specified residual representation, only a finite set of quadratic fields, or only unramified local places would not establish the indexed statement.
