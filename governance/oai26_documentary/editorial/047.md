# OAI-26 Family 047: Cancellation and affine fibrations over the complex numbers

## The question in plain language

Suppose a geometric space becomes ordinary affine space after adding one extra independent coordinate. Must the original space already have been affine space? This is the *cancellation problem*. A related question asks whether a family whose individual fibers all look like affine space must locally be a simple product. The pinned Family 047 source claims counterexamples to both expectations.

## A small example

Adding a free coordinate to a space X produces its product with the affine line. Two spaces could appear identical after this operation even if they were different before it. Cancellation would infer that X is affine four-space from an isomorphism of X times the affine line with affine five-space.

Similarly, knowing that every fiber of a map resembles affine three-space does not automatically supply compatible local coordinates across the base. A *Zariski-locally trivial* fibration requires exactly that extra compatibility.

## What is known

The pinned OAI-26 overview attributes the following claimed constructions to *An explicit failure of complex affine-space cancellation* (September 23, 2026):

- An integral complex affine fourfold X which is **not** isomorphic to affine four-space, although its product with the affine line **is** isomorphic to affine five-space.
- Smooth surjections from X to affine one-space and from affine five-space to affine two-space whose fibers over residue fields are all isomorphic to affine three-space, yet which are **not Zariski-locally trivial**.

The claims distinguish a global product isomorphism from a local trivialization of a morphism. The ambient complex field, dimension, integrality, smoothness of the specified maps, and residue-field qualification are part of the advertised statements. These statements should not be extended automatically to other dimensions or arbitrary ground fields.
