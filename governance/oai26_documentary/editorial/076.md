# OAI-26 family 076: ultraflat real Littlewood polynomials

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** This article explains three pinned publisher-reported manuscripts; it does not independently verify the constructions.

## The question in plain language

A Littlewood polynomial has coefficients that are all either plus one or minus one. Despite this restriction, could one arrange the signs so that the polynomial's magnitude stays almost constant as its input moves around the unit circle?

The pinned OAI-26 overview claims the answer is yes, even for real Littlewood coefficients and for every sufficiently large polynomial length. It also reports binary sequences whose merit factors grow without bound, contradicting a conjectured uniform bound.

## A small example

A real Littlewood polynomial of length $N$ has the form

$$
P_N(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}a_jz^j,\qquad a_j\in\{-1,1\}.
$$

For $|z|=1$, orthogonality implies that the average of $|P_N(z)|^2$ around the circle equals $N$. So the natural magnitude scale is $\sqrt N$.

Being *ultraflat* means much more than attaining that scale on average: the magnitude is close to $\sqrt N$ **uniformly at every point** of the circle. Real coefficients make the values at $z=1$ and $z=-1$ sums of signs; ensuring the bound at these endpoints is an especially important qualification.

## What is known

The protected catalogue identifies three manuscripts: *Ultraflat real Littlewood polynomials* and *Nearly minimal maxima and positive minima of Littlewood polynomials* (both October 5, 2026), and *Asymptotically minimal maxima of real Littlewood polynomials* (September 23, 2026).

The pinned publisher overview claims constructions for **every sufficiently large integer length $N$** satisfying

$$
\sup_{|z|=1}\left|\frac{|P_N(z)|}{\sqrt N}-1\right|\longrightarrow0.
$$

The stated uniformity covers the *entire* unit circle, including the real endpoints. The indexed overview also claims that the binary merit factors associated with the constructions tend to infinity, contradicting Turyn's bounded-merit-factor conjecture.

Uniform approximation of the modulus is stronger than a near-minimal maximum alone; a lower envelope that approaches the same scale is required. The exact definition and normalization of the merit factor, and whether all lengths or a selected sequence are used, demand source-level checking.
