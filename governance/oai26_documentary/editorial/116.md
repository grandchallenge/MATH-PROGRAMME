# OAI-26 family 116: noncommutative identity testing with matrix hitting points

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** This is a source-pinned documentary account of the publisher's claims, not an independent proof audit.

## The question in plain language

A polynomial identity test asks whether a formula computes the zero polynomial, even when cancellation makes that hard to see from the syntax. In *noncommutative* algebra, multiplication order matters: $xy$ and $yx$ need not be equal. A common way to detect nonzero noncommutative expressions is to substitute matrices for the variables.

A *hitting point* is a chosen tuple of matrices at which a nonzero expression evaluates to something nonzero. A *hitting list* provides several candidate tuples so that at least one detects each eligible nonzero expression. Family 116 describes claimed deterministic constructions with polynomial dimensions or list sizes.

## A small example

Consider $f(x,y)=xy-yx$. It vanishes when $x$ and $y$ are ordinary commuting numbers, yet is nonzero in the free noncommutative polynomial algebra. Substituting, for example, a diagonal matrix for $x$ and an off-diagonal matrix for $y$ can yield a nonzero commutator.

For *rational* formulas, division introduces a separate issue: evaluation is meaningful only where every required inverse exists. A matrix tuple that makes a rational formula undefined cannot count as a valid nonzero witness for that evaluation. Thus division-free identity testing and rational-formula hitting lists require different guarantees.

## What is known

The protected Family 116 catalogue identifies three manuscripts on uniform matrix hitting points in positive characteristic, a rational matrix hitting point, and polynomial hitting lists for rational formulas.

The pinned publisher overview claims that **for each characteristic** there is a deterministic polynomial-bit-time construction of a polynomial-dimensional matrix tuple that detects **every nonzero division-free noncommutative formula of bounded size** over **any field of that characteristic**. Exact interpretation of the characteristic parameter and the dependence on the formula-size bound requires review of the primary manuscript.

For noncommutative **rational formulas over $\mathbb Q$**, the overview separately claims polynomial-size hitting lists, whenever a formula admits a **defined rational-matrix evaluation**. This qualifier is substantive: testing must distinguish an expression that is identically zero from one that has no defined evaluation in the permitted domain.

Neither the division-free claim nor the rational-formula statement by itself authorizes a uniform characteristic-independent construction over arbitrary fields for all division-bearing expressions.
