# OAI-26 family 040: Bloch's conjecture for complex surfaces

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** This article reports the pinned manuscript's claims; it does not independently verify the proof.

## The question in plain language

An algebraic surface has points that can be combined formally into *zero-cycles*. Two such combinations can be considered equivalent if they differ by rational equivalences arising from algebraic curves. Bloch's conjecture concerns how much of this complicated information is captured by a simpler geometric group, the Albanese variety.

The pinned OAI-26 overview claims that for every smooth connected projective complex surface whose geometric genus is zero, the Albanese map on degree-zero zero-cycles is an isomorphism. This would mean that the Albanese variety records all the relevant zero-cycle information, with neither extra hidden classes nor missing points.

## A small example

For a smooth projective surface $S$, a zero-cycle is a finite integer combination of points. Its degree is the sum of the coefficients. The degree-zero group modulo rational equivalence is written

$$
\mathrm{CH}_0(S)^0.
$$

There is a natural homomorphism from this group to the complex points of the Albanese variety:

$$
\mathrm{CH}_0(S)^0\longrightarrow\mathrm{Alb}(S)(\mathbb C).
$$

Calling this map an *isomorphism* asserts both injectivity and surjectivity. It is stronger than saying its kernel is finite, its image is dense, or an analogous statement holds after tensoring with rational numbers. The pinned theorem is explicitly about integral zero-cycles.

## What is known

The protected source catalogue identifies one Family 040 manuscript, *Bloch's conjecture for surfaces with p_g=0* (September 24, 2026). Its repository PDF path contains the narrower phrase “pg equals q equals 0,” which matters when reconstructing the proof's scope.

The pinned overview attributes the full $p_g=0$ statement to a combination of a new theorem for surfaces with $p_g=q=0$ and a classical theorem of Bloch, Kas and Lieberman. Here $p_g$ is the geometric genus and $q$ the irregularity.

Thus the publisher's headline claim is broader than the new manuscript's indicated special case. A source critic must examine the classical input and verify that the two cases really cover **every** smooth connected projective complex surface with $p_g=0$, rather than conflating the two results.
