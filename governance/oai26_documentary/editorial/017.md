# OAI-26 Family 017: irrationality exponent of pi

## The question in plain language

Pi is irrational, but rational fractions can approximate it very closely. The irrationality exponent measures just how closely rational approximations can approach a number as their denominators grow. The pinned OAI-26 overview asserts that the irrationality exponent of pi equals 2.

## A small example

The fractions 22/7 and 355/113 are familiar approximations. A bound on the error for every sufficiently large denominator is more substantial than any numerical example. According to the pinned overview, for each positive epsilon and all sufficiently large positive integer denominators q, the error between pi and any rational p/q is at least q raised to the power -2-epsilon. The threshold may depend on epsilon.

## What is known

The OAI-26 publisher overview at pinned revision 1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a makes two claims for Family 017: the irrationality exponent of pi is exactly 2, and the Flint–Hills series with terms 1/(n cubed times sin(n) squared), with angles in radians, converges.

The second is asserted as a consequence of the first. These claims are not established here. An individual approximation or a simple numerical experiment does not prove a uniform denominator-dependent inequality or convergence of the full infinite series.
