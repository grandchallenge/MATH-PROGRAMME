# OAI-26 family 081: Riesz transforms and rectifiability in higher codimension

## The question in plain language

Can the behavior of a singular integral reveal that a geometrically complicated set is, at most places and scales, substantially similar to a smooth surface? The Riesz transform compares pairs of points using both their relative direction and distance. Uniform rectifiability is stronger than saying a set merely looks smooth at almost every point: it requires quantitatively large pieces that can be parametrized by Lipschitz maps at every location and admissible scale.

## A small example

A flat plane inside a higher-dimensional Euclidean space has an obvious Lipschitz parametrization. A highly irregular set can have the same scaling of mass in balls but lack such large controlled pieces. The question is whether the Riesz transform's uniform operator bound rules out this irregularity. The flat-plane illustration motivates the conclusion but does not establish the theorem.

## What is known

The pinned manuscript, *Riesz transforms and uniform rectifiability in higher codimension*, asserts the following: for integers d at least four and n between two and d minus two, an n-Ahlfors–David regular Radon measure in d-dimensional Euclidean space is uniformly n-rectifiable if all positive **hard truncations** of its n-dimensional Riesz transform obey one common scalar-to-vector L² operator bound. In plain language, n-Ahlfors–David regularity means mass inside every support-centered ball of admissible radius is bounded above and below by fixed constants times the radius to the n-th power.

The claimed conclusion is quantitative. At every support point and admissible radius there is a Lipschitz image of an n-dimensional ball capturing at least a fixed fraction of the corresponding mass. The mass fraction and Lipschitz constant depend only on dimensions and the original regularity and operator bounds. The source explicitly permits unbounded support and does not assume principal-value convergence or a stronger variation estimate.

This range is the higher-codimension portion of the David–Semmes Riesz-transform question. The manuscript distinguishes earlier codimension-one and dimension-one implications from the remaining range, and separates the uniform truncation hypothesis from stronger variation bounds and distinct principal-value criteria.

## Claimed proof mechanism

The source uses flat-subball and packing estimates, compactness toward planar measures, normalized normal-height functions, a positive fractional-energy estimate, and rigidity of limiting height equations modulo constants. In a second limit, the heights are divided by initial geometric excess to prove improvement *relative to that excess*. Affine rigidity then forces better approximation by planes. A dyadic counting argument controls failures of improvement, and the David–Semmes geometric implication produces uniformly large Lipschitz images. The claimed variation and principal-value consequences belong to a separate later section, not to the main theorem's assumptions.
