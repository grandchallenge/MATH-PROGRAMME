## The question in plain language

Complex geometry studies spaces on which holomorphic functions and curves behave consistently. A compact complex manifold is *Brody hyperbolic* when every holomorphic map from the complex plane into the manifold is constant. For compact manifolds, this corresponds to Kobayashi hyperbolicity.

The *canonical bundle* packages holomorphic differential forms of top degree. When it is *ample*, its sufficiently high tensor powers have sections that embed the space into projective space. Kobayashi's canonical-ampleness conjecture asks whether the absence of entire curves forces this strong positivity property.

## A small example

A map from the whole complex plane into a compact complex manifold is called an *entire curve*. The absence of nonconstant such maps is a substantial geometric restriction, but by itself it does not directly exhibit the differential forms or projective embedding implied by canonical ampleness.

The proposed theorem bridges those two kinds of information: a negative statement about maps into the space leads to a positive statement about its canonical line bundle.

## What is known

The pinned September 23, 2026 primary PDF, *Canonical ampleness of compact hyperbolic Kähler manifolds*, explicitly states **Theorem 1.1**: for every compact connected Kähler manifold of **positive complex dimension**, if every holomorphic map from the complex plane into the manifold is constant, then the canonical bundle is ample. In particular, a positive tensor power defines a holomorphic embedding into complex projective space.

The claim applies to compact Kähler manifolds without assuming projectivity at the outset; projectivity is a consequence. The manuscript describes earlier results for negative or quasi-negative curvature assumptions, which are not to be confused with its substantially broader asserted hyperbolicity hypothesis.
