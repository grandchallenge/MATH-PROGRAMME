# OAI-26 family 042: Oka classification for compact complex surfaces

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** This reports the publisher's pinned assertions and is not an independent proof check.

## The question in plain language

Complex geometry studies spaces whose local coordinates are complex numbers and whose coordinate changes are holomorphic. The *Oka property* expresses an unusual flexibility: under suitable topological conditions, continuous maps from Stein spaces can be deformed to holomorphic maps, with more precise interpolation and approximation formulations.

K3 surfaces are compact complex surfaces with distinctive geometric properties. Some are projective algebraic varieties; others are not. The pinned OAI-26 source overview claims that **every complex K3 surface is Oka**, including nonprojective ones, and places this result within a broader classification of minimal compact complex surfaces.

## A small example

Compare two questions about a map into a complex manifold: Can it be drawn continuously? Can it be chosen holomorphic? For a flexible target satisfying an Oka principle, a suitable continuous map can often be replaced by a holomorphic map without changing its relevant topological class.

The Oka property is stronger than the mere existence of a few nonconstant holomorphic curves. Nor is it equivalent to a surface being algebraic or projective.

A *minimal* complex surface is one from which no exceptional curve of the first kind can be blown down further. Kodaira dimension and the Enriques–Kodaira classification divide such surfaces into important families. The publisher's claim treats Kodaira-dimension-zero surfaces and class VII surfaces differently, so these settings must be distinguished.

## What is known

The source-locked catalogue lists one manuscript for Family 042: *Every complex K3 surface is Oka* (September 23, 2026).

The pinned overview attributes three assertions to this manuscript:

- Every complex K3 surface is Oka, without restricting to projective K3 surfaces.
- Every **connected minimal compact complex surface of Kodaira dimension zero** is Oka.
- A **connected minimal compact complex surface of class VII** is Oka if and only if it is a Hopf or Enoki surface.

The last assertion is an exact classification within class VII, not a claim that every class VII surface is Oka. The condition of minimality is material; the indexed overview does not automatically classify arbitrary nonminimal compact complex surfaces.

The result's exact definition of the Oka property, treatment of non-Kähler targets, and use of the classification of complex surfaces require direct examination of the pinned PDF.
