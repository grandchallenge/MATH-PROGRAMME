# OAI-26 family 032: the rational Hodge conjecture for CM abelian varieties

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** This article describes claims in five pinned manuscripts, not an independent validation of their proofs.

## The question in plain language

Algebraic varieties have shapes that can be studied using both geometry and topology. The Hodge conjecture asks whether certain topological patterns, called Hodge classes, arise from actual algebraic subvarieties through rational linear combinations of their cycle classes.

An abelian variety is a higher-dimensional analogue of an elliptic curve. An abelian variety with *complex multiplication* (CM) has unusually rich endomorphisms. The pinned Family 032 overview claims the rational Hodge conjecture for every complex CM abelian variety, without limiting its dimension or the codimension of the Hodge classes.

## A small example

For a smooth complex projective variety $X$, an algebraic subvariety of codimension $r$ gives a cohomology class of degree $2r$. Complex Hodge theory decomposes such cohomology into types, and the algebraic class belongs to type $(r,r)$.

The Hodge conjecture asks for the converse for classes with rational coefficients: whether every rational class of the relevant Hodge type is a rational combination of algebraic cycle classes. Establishing the conjecture for divisors alone does not automatically handle higher codimension, and proving it for one specific abelian variety does not establish it for all CM abelian varieties.

The adjective **rational** matters: algebraicity after tensoring with a larger coefficient field is not automatically the exact rational-cycle statement.

## What is known

The pinned Family 032 overview reports that *The rational Hodge conjecture for CM abelian varieties* (October 6, 2026) proves the rational Hodge conjecture for every complex CM abelian variety, in every dimension and codimension.

It further reports consequences through theorems of Milne: the Tate conjecture for all abelian varieties over finite fields and the Hodge standard conjecture for abelian varieties in every characteristic. These claimed consequences depend both on the new CM result and on the precise hypotheses of the invoked comparison theorems.

The catalogue groups four additional papers with the lead manuscript: *Algebraic Kuga–Satake correspondences and Hodge conjectures on a K3 quadratic locus*; *Weil classes and Hodge classes on abelian powers*; *Abelian covers, Gale correspondences, and the Hodge conjecture for powers*; and *A Conditional Reduction for Algebraic Kuga–Satake Correspondences*.

The word **Conditional** in the last title requires particular care. An intermediate conditional reduction must not be reported as a standalone unconditional theorem without verifying that its hypothesis is supplied elsewhere. Nor may results on a restricted K3 locus be silently generalized to all K3 surfaces.
