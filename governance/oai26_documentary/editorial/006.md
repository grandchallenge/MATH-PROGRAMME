# OAI-26 family 006: Goldfeld's conjecture for quadratic twists

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** This exposition reports claims of two pinned OAI-26 manuscripts and does not certify them.

## The question in plain language

An elliptic curve over the rational numbers has an associated L-function. A *quadratic twist* produces a family of related curves by changing the defining equation using a squarefree integer. For each twist, the L-function can vanish at its central point to order zero, one, or more; this order is its *analytic rank*.

Goldfeld's conjecture predicts that when quadratic twists are ordered by the absolute value of their squarefree parameter, half have analytic rank zero and half rank one. In particular, twists of analytic rank at least two should form a density-zero subset. A separate assertion is that the average analytic rank should approach one half. Those conclusions are related, but the density assertion by itself does not guarantee convergence of the mean: a very small number of twists with extremely large ranks could affect the average.

The OAI-26 Family 006 source overview claims proofs of both conclusions for every elliptic curve over the rational numbers.

## A small example

Imagine classifying twists into boxes labelled by analytic rank. The density prediction says that, in an increasingly large collection ordered by the absolute value of their signed squarefree parameters, approximately 50 percent land in box 0 and 50 percent in box 1, while the proportion in boxes 2 and above tends to zero.

That does not yet prove that the average of the box labels is one half. For a finite illustrative dataset, even a single exceptionally large label among very many small labels can substantially increase the mean. A proof about the *mean analytic rank* therefore needs additional control over the contributions from the tail of larger ranks.

The signed ordering matters. Counting only positive twists, only negative twists, or ordering twists by conductor instead would change the population being counted and cannot be silently substituted for the manuscript's stated family.

## What is known

The pinned OAI-26 catalogue associates two distinct source manuscripts with Family 006:

- *Goldfeld's analytic density conjecture and the 2-converse for elliptic curves* (October 7, 2026), reported as proving the analytic-rank zero/one density statement for quadratic twists.
- *The mean analytic rank of quadratic twists of elliptic curves* (October 6, 2026), reported as establishing convergence of the mean analytic rank to one half.

The source overview explicitly states that both results cover quadratic twists of **every elliptic curve over the rational numbers**, and that the count uses signed squarefree twist parameters ordered by absolute value. The density PDF's abstract directly claims the **full low-corank 2-converse**: when the full $2$-power Selmer corank is zero or one, analytic and Mordell–Weil ranks coincide with that corank and the Tate–Shafarevich group is finite. It treats this as part of its claimed route to Goldfeld's analytic-rank densities; its proof has not been independently verified.

Neither a general prediction of parity nor known Selmer-rank statistics should be confused with a verified proof of the analytic rank distribution. In particular, replacing an analytic-rank statement with an algebraic or Selmer-corank statement requires a justified bridge.
