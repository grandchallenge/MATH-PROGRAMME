# OAI-26 family 041: Hyperkähler SYZ and projective-space bases

## The question in plain language

A hyperkähler manifold is a geometric space with unusually rich symmetries. One important question asks whether certain numerically nonnegative line bundles actually provide enough global functions to define a map. The technical word for this property is *semiample*. The strong hyperkähler SYZ conjecture predicts this for nonzero nef isotropic line bundles on compact irreducible holomorphic symplectic Kähler manifolds.

A related question concerns the *base* of a Lagrangian fibration, a geometric map whose typical fibers occupy half the dimension of the total space. Family 041's overview claims that in the projective setting this base must be projective space.

## A small example

For a holomorphic line bundle, nefness roughly says that its numerical intersection with curves is nonnegative. Isotropy imposes a further vanishing condition on its first Chern class with respect to the relevant quadratic form. Neither condition by itself visibly produces the sections necessary for a morphism. The conjecture is a bridge from numerical geometry to an actual geometric map.

A projective-space base is particularly rigid. The claim is stronger than saying that the base is a rational, smooth, or Fano variety: it identifies its isomorphism type.

## What is known

The pinned OAI-26 overview attributes two assertions to the September 23, 2026 manuscripts.

**Strong hyperkähler SYZ.** Every holomorphic line bundle with *nonzero nef isotropic first Chern class* on a *compact irreducible holomorphic symplectic Kähler manifold* is claimed to be semiample. The compact Kähler hypothesis is part of the claim; this is not confined to the projective case.

**Base rigidity.** The overview claims that every *projective Lagrangian fibration* with *normal projective base* has projective space as its base, without restriction to a particular dimension or deformation type. This does not automatically say anything about arbitrary nonprojective fibrations or nonnormal bases.

These statements are related but logically distinct. Neither is independently established by a summary of the other.
