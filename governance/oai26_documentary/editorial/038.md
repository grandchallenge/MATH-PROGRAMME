# OAI-26 family 038: Fujita's freeness conjecture

## The question in plain language

A line bundle on an algebraic variety can have global sections—functions defined locally that fit together consistently. A bundle is *globally generated* when these sections can provide a nonzero value at every point, so there are no unavoidable base points.

Fujita's freeness conjecture predicts a universal numerical threshold beyond which a canonical bundle made more positive by an ample line bundle is globally generated. The pinned OAI-26 overview claims the sharp threshold in every dimension for smooth complex projective varieties.

## A small example

Let $X$ be a smooth projective complex variety of dimension $n$, let $K_X$ denote its canonical divisor, and let $L$ be an ample line bundle. The predicted global-generation threshold is

$$
K_X+mL\quad\text{for every integer }m\geq n+1.
$$

The notation $K_X+mL$ describes the tensor product of the canonical line bundle with the tensor power $L^{\otimes m}$. Global generation means that for every point of $X$ there is a global section not vanishing there.

On a smooth projective curve, the numerical threshold specializes to $m\geq2$. The general conjecture is stronger than proving the claim for curves or surfaces individually: its bound depends only on the dimension, not on the particular variety or ample line bundle.

## What is known

The source-locked Family 038 catalogue identifies one manuscript, *Fujita's freeness conjecture* (September 23, 2026).

The pinned primary PDF, Theorem 1.1, states that for every **smooth connected projective variety over $\mathbb{C}$ of dimension $n\geq1$** and **every ample line bundle** $L$, $K_X+(n+1)L$ is globally generated. Corollary 6.3 extends this to **every integer $m\geq n+1$**, even when $L$ itself is not globally generated. Connectedness is not essential, and the paper separately notes that the zero-dimensional case is trivial. The bound is sharp for $X=\mathbb{P}^n$ with $L=\mathcal{O}(1)$: $K_X+(n+1)L$ is trivial, whereas $K_X+nL$ has no nonzero sections.

Global generation is a different property from very ampleness, which additionally ensures that the sections embed a variety into projective space. A proof of Fujita's freeness conjecture should not be reported as a proof of Fujita's separate very-ampleness bound without checking an additional theorem.

Likewise, the indexed claim does not cover singular varieties or varieties over arbitrary positive-characteristic fields. Extending the conclusion to those settings requires separate arguments.
