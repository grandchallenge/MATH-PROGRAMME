# OAI-26 family 071: Koebe's circle-domain conjecture

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. Mathematical status: NO_CERTIFICATION.** This is a source-pinned description of two manuscript claims, not an independent proof validation.

## The question in plain language

A planar domain is a connected open subset of the Riemann sphere. A *circle domain* is one whose complementary connected components are either closed round disks or individual points. Koebe asked whether every planar domain can be transformed conformally into such a domain. Conformal transformations preserve infinitesimal angles. The difficulty is not finite collections of boundary components but arbitrarily complicated complements, potentially with uncountably many components.

## A small example

The open annulus between two concentric circles is already a circle domain on the Riemann sphere: its complement has two components, each a closed round disk (one containing infinity). The unit disk is another example, with one complementary closed disk on the sphere. Neither elementary example addresses the infinitely connected case.

## What is known

The pinned September 23, 2026 manuscript *Koebe's Circle-Domain Conjecture* asserts in its introduction's main theorem that **every nonempty connected open subset of the Riemann sphere is conformally equivalent to a circle domain**, without connectivity or geometric regularity restrictions. The source distinguishes its claimed unrestricted theorem from classical finite and countably connected cases and from earlier results under additional boundary conditions. It describes a construction using countably selected complementary components, compatible finite-energy analytic tests, boundary barriers, path probability laws and limiting circle-domain uniformizations. Its proof strategy must ensure that every unmarked complementary component collapses to a point and every marked component equals its limiting distinguished round disk, with no protrusion.

The separately pinned companion *Removable Boundaries and Rigidity of Circle Domains* asserts that a circle domain with conformally removable boundary is conformally rigid regardless of the number of complementary components. Its abstract describes this as the removability-to-rigidity direction of the He–Schramm conjecture. The converse, and a statement that every circle-domain boundary is removable, do **not** follow from that abstract. Uniformization existence and rigidity are distinct assertions.
