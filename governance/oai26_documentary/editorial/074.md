# OAI-26 family 074: Kakeya problems in three and four dimensions

**Editorial status: SOURCE_CRITIC_COMPLETE; protected acceptance is determined by the admissions ledger. Mathematical status: NO_CERTIFICATION.** This article reports the claims of two pinned manuscripts without independently validating their proofs.

## The question in plain language

A Kakeya set contains a unit line segment pointing in every possible direction. Such a set can be surprisingly small in volume. The Kakeya dimension problem asks whether it must nevertheless have the largest possible Hausdorff dimension. A related *maximal-function* problem asks how strongly collections of thin tubes in different directions can overlap.

The pinned OAI-26 overview reports two results: a sharp up-to-small-loss Kakeya maximal bound in three dimensions and full Hausdorff dimension for Kakeya sets in four dimensions.

## A small example

Imagine placing one unit-length, very thin tube in each direction. A tube of radius $\delta$ has small volume as $\delta$ approaches zero, yet many different tubes can overlap. A Kakeya maximal operator measures how large the average of a function can be over tubes pointing in each direction.

A maximal bound controls this concentration quantitatively. In dimension three, the reported estimate allows a factor $\delta^{-\varepsilon}$ for *every* positive $\varepsilon$, rather than one fixed power loss. That distinction is central.

Hausdorff dimension is different from volume: a set may have zero ordinary four-dimensional volume while still having Hausdorff dimension four.

## What is known

The protected Family 074 catalogue identifies *The Kakeya maximal conjecture in three dimensions* (September 23, 2026) and *Every four-dimensional Kakeya set has full Hausdorff dimension* (September 24, 2026).

The three-dimensional primary PDF, Theorem 1.1, defines the maximal operator by taking the supremum over centers of the average of $|f|$ on unit cylinders of transverse radius $\delta$. Each such tube has volume $\pi\delta^2$; the target $L^3$ space uses the usual surface measure on the direction sphere $S^2$. For every $0<\delta<1$, it claims an operator estimate from $L^3(\mathbb{R}^3)$ to $L^3(S^2)$ with operator norm

$$
O_{\varepsilon}(\delta^{-\varepsilon})\quad\text{for every }\varepsilon>0.
$$

The four-dimensional primary PDF, Theorem 1.1, claims that **every subset of $\mathbb{R}^4$**, without any measurability, compactness, or regularity assumption, has Hausdorff dimension four whenever it contains a unit line segment in each direction of $S^3$. The witnessing line segments themselves need not form a regular or measurable family.

These are distinct assertions with distinct hypotheses and consequences. The four-dimensional result is a dimension claim, not necessarily the corresponding four-dimensional maximal inequality. Conversely, the three-dimensional maximal result should not be represented merely as a qualitative dimension statement.
