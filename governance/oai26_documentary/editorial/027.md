# OAI-26 family 027: Integral points on curve character varieties

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. Mathematical status: NO_CERTIFICATION.** This article documents claims in the pinned OAI-26 overview; it does not certify the proposed proof.

## The question in plain language

A character variety organizes algebraic representations of the fundamental group of a curve. One can regard a point as describing how loops on the curve act through matrices, identifying descriptions that differ only by a common change of coordinates. A natural arithmetic question is whether representations whose matrix data are defined over algebraic integers occur abundantly enough to fill the variety in the algebraic-geometric sense.

Here “dense” means *Zariski dense*: there is no smaller closed algebraic subset containing all the points under discussion. It does not assert that the points are uniformly spread in an ordinary metric.

## A small example

A representation assigns invertible matrices to loops while preserving the rules for composing loops. Requiring determinant one restricts the matrices to the special linear group. Two representations related by simultaneous change of basis describe the same point of the character variety.

An arithmetic restriction, such as using entries in the ring of integers of a number field, can be difficult to reconcile with geometric abundance. A few integral examples are not enough: to show Zariski density one must rule out their collective confinement to any proper algebraic subvariety.

## What is known

The publisher overview for OAI-26 Family 027 claims to resolve the **determinant-one curve case** of Litt's integral-density question. Specifically, it asserts that for every smooth connected complex algebraic curve and every rank, integral points become Zariski dense in each component of the corresponding special-linear character variety over the *full ring of integers of one number field*.

It also states that prescribed quasi-unipotent boundary conjugacy classes are permitted, including nonsemisimple classes. That qualification matters: replacing nonsemisimple boundary data by their semisimplifications would weaken the advertised statement.

This is not a claim about all reductive groups, all higher-dimensional varieties, or arbitrary integral structures. Those generalizations are not justified by this overview entry.
