# OAI-26 family 022: the weak inhomogeneous Duffin–Schaeffer conjecture

## The question in plain language

Diophantine approximation asks how closely a real number can be approached by fractions. The Duffin–Schaeffer problem predicts when infinitely many approximations are possible for almost every real number, based on the collective size of the allowed errors.

In the *inhomogeneous* variant, a fixed shift changes which rational approximations count. The pinned OAI-26 synopsis asserts that the weak version holds for every real shift, with no monotonicity assumption on the error function and no Diophantine restriction on the shift.

The qualifier *weak* matters. This particular claim allows unrestricted numerators; it should not be promoted to a statement requiring a coprimality condition on numerators and denominators.

## A small example

For a real number $y$, let $\|y\|$ denote its distance to the nearest integer. An approximation condition can be written

$$
\|qx-\gamma\|<\psi(q),
$$

where $q$ is a positive integer, $\gamma$ is a fixed real shift, and $\psi(q)$ determines the allowed error.

For example, $\|qx-\gamma\|<0.01$ says that $qx-\gamma$ is within one hundredth of an integer. Equivalently, $x$ lies within $0.01/q$ of a shifted fraction $(a+\gamma)/q$ for some integer $a$. The numerator $a$ is not required to be relatively prime to $q$ in this weak formulation.

A divergent sum of candidate interval sizes suggests that infinitely many approximation events ought to occur. But overlap between events can destroy naive independence, so divergence alone does not prove the conclusion without a rigorous correlation argument.

## What is known

The source-locked catalogue names one manuscript, *The Weak Inhomogeneous Duffin–Schaeffer Conjecture* (September 25, 2026). The pinned primary PDF, Theorem 1.1, states that for every fixed real $\gamma$ and every finite-valued nonnegative function $\psi$, divergence of

$$
\sum_{q\geq1}\frac{\varphi(q)\psi(q)}{q}
$$

implies the inequalities

$$
\|qx-\gamma\|<\psi(q)
$$

hold for infinitely many positive integers $q$, for almost every real $x$. Here $\varphi$ denotes Euler's totient function.

Theorem 1.1 explicitly says that numerators are unrestricted. It also states that neither monotonicity of $\psi$ nor a Diophantine assumption on $\gamma$ is required.

The phrase *almost every* refers to a measure-theoretic assertion, not to every real $x$. The exact normalization of the measure space and any reductions for large values of $\psi$ should be checked in the source PDF rather than inferred from the synopsis.
