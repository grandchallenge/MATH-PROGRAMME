# OAI-26 family 004: Hilbert's tenth problem over the rational numbers

## The question in plain language

Given a polynomial with integer coefficients, can a computer always decide whether it has a solution in rational numbers? A rational number is a ratio of two integers. **Hilbert's tenth problem over the rational numbers** asks for one algorithm that works for every polynomial, even when the number of variables varies. The pinned October 6, 2026 manuscript *claims there is no such algorithm*. This is an announcement by its authors, not a proof independently certified by GCL.

The analogous decision problem for **integer** solutions is known to be undecidable. That fact alone does not settle the rational problem: permitting denominators changes the set of solutions, and a polynomial might have rational solutions without integer solutions.

## A small example

The polynomial $x^2-2$ has no rational zero, even though it has real zeros. By contrast, $2x-1$ has the rational zero $x=1/2$ but no integer zero. Both are readily decided examples. An impossibility result about *one algorithm handling all polynomials* is much stronger than proving that some individual equations are difficult.

The manuscript describes a way of building specially constrained polynomial families for which rational solvability would encode integer solvability. A correct encoding would be the key bridge; these simple examples do not establish it.

## What is known

**Main pinned paper:** *Hilbert's tenth problem over the rational numbers*, October 6, 2026, PDF Theorem 1.1 (physical p. 3), claims that there is no algorithm which, given an arbitrary polynomial in an arbitrary finite number of variables with integer coefficients, decides whether it has a zero in $\mathbb Q$. Its proof overview (§1.2, p. 5) describes reducing integer-solvability questions to a computable sequence of finite rational-solvability tests. The proposed machinery involves elliptic-curve indices, local comparisons, height bounds at selected primes, and a prime-pattern construction. Corollary 7.1 (p. 56) additionally claims a Turing-degree statement ($0'$) and restricted quartic and sums-of-squares-of-quadratics forms. These remain *claims of the pinned manuscript*.

**Companion pinned paper:** *A pointwise 2-converse for elliptic curves with rational two-torsion*, October 7, 2026, Theorem 1.1, claims that if an elliptic curve over $\mathbb Q$ has a nontrivial rational point of order two and its **full** $2^\infty$-Selmer group has corank zero or one, then the analytic and Mordell–Weil ranks equal that corank and the full Tate–Shafarevich group is finite. It does **not** claim the exact BSD leading-coefficient formula. The main Hilbert-ten paper invokes the companion as reference [48] in the proof chain around Lemma 3.4.

The key logical distinction is between a conditional local construction of rational points and a uniform reduction proving undecidability for *every* input. The source claims both; inspecting one local lemma is not a validation of the final reduction.

$$
\operatorname{rank} E_l(\mathbb Q)+\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\Sha(E_l/\mathbb Q)[2]=1.
$$
