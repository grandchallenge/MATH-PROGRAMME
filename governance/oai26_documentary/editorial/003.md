# OAI-26 family 003: A zero-free region for the zeta function

## The question in plain language

The Riemann zeta function is a complex-valued function whose zeros encode information about prime numbers. One manuscript in this family claims that no zeros occur in the half-plane where the real part of the complex argument is greater than $7/8$. This would be a striking proposed strengthening of established zero-free regions, but **it is not the full Riemann hypothesis**, which concerns the line with real part $1/2$.

A second part of the family treats Dirichlet $L$ functions and the exceptional real zeros associated with Landau–Siegel questions. The scopes and exceptional points must not be silently conflated.

## A small example

At the real point $s=2$, the series

$$
\zeta(2)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{6}>0
$$

provides a direct and familiar nonzero value in the claimed region. Checking one value is only an illustration: it cannot prove that an entire complex half-plane is zero-free.

## What is known

For complex arguments with real part strictly greater than one, the classical Euler product already supplies a nonvanishing argument for the zeta function. Extending this assertion into the critical strip is mathematically far stronger. The upstream manuscript makes a proposed $7/8$-region claim; this compendium does not independently establish its truth or resolve the Riemann hypothesis. The related uniform Landau–Siegel-zero and Hecke-family statements are separate questions.
