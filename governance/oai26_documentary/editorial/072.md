# OAI-26 family 072: Brennan's conjecture and integral-means spectra

## The question in plain language

A conformal map preserves angles, but it may distort distances dramatically near the boundary of a planar domain. Brennan's conjecture predicts precisely how integrable the size of such a map's derivative is. The same boundary distortion is studied through *integral-means spectra*, which measure the growth of average powers of derivatives near the unit circle.

The pinned OAI-26 overview reports a resolution of Brennan's conjecture, a sharp universal integral-means result for large negative exponents, and a strict inequality contradicting Kraetzer's predicted spectrum for bounded univalent functions.

## A small example

Let $\phi$ be a conformal bijection from a simply connected plane domain onto the unit disk. The quantity $|\phi'(z)|$ measures local scale distortion. For a real exponent $s$, one asks whether

$$
\int_{\Omega}|\phi'(z)|^s\,dA(z)<\infty.
$$

The indexed conjecture's exponent range is $4/3<s<4$. An integral-means spectrum instead examines boundary integrals, often taking a logarithmic growth rate as circles approach the boundary. These related measurements are not identical, and the classes of functions used in their universal estimates must be distinguished.

## What is known

The source-locked catalogue identifies two manuscripts: *Brennan's conjecture and sharp inverse-square integral means* and *A strict inverse-first-power bound for univalent functions*, both dated September 24, 2026.

The publisher's pinned overview claims that for every conformal bijection $\phi$ from a simply connected plane domain onto the disk, $|\phi'|^s$ is area-integrable for every $4/3<s<4$. Its formulation requires verification of any implicit normalizations on the domain.

It also claims the sharp universal integral-means identity

$$
B_{\mathcal S}(t)=|t|-1\quad\text{for }t\leq-2.
$$

Finally, it reports a strict inequality $B_b(-1)<1/4$ for **bounded univalent functions**, contradicting Kraetzer's prediction at that parameter. The spectrum $B_{\mathcal S}$ and the bounded-class spectrum $B_b$ must not be conflated.
