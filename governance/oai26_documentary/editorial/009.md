# OAI-26 family 009: Reconstructing function fields from Milnor K-theory and Galois data

## The question in plain language

A *function field* is a field whose elements can be thought of as rational functions on an algebraic variety. Can one recover that field knowing only restricted algebraic information about its multiplication, or about symmetries of its algebraic extensions? The three pinned Family 009 manuscripts claim that, for function fields of transcendence degree at least two over algebraically closed constant fields, several particularly economical invariants do determine the field. Their statements include precise compatibility requirements and unavoidable ambiguities; they are **publisher claims**, not independently certified GCL theorems.

## A small example

Two rational functions may multiply to give a third, but this alone does not tell us how they *add*. Milnor K-theory remembers multiplicative classes together with the *Steinberg relation*: for a nonzero function $x$ with $x\ne1$, the symbol of $x$ and $1-x$ vanishes in degree two. This is a trace of addition hidden inside multiplicative data.

For example, in a rational function field, the two functions $t$ and $1-t$ have an especially structured relationship. The reconstruction question asks whether sufficiently many such relations pin down the entire field, rather than just allowing isolated identities. A single identity is an illustration, not a reconstruction proof.

## What is known

The first pinned October 5 paper, *Reconstruction of Function Fields from Mod-ℓ Milnor K-Theory*, considers a prime $\ell$ **different from** the field characteristic. Its Theorem 1.1 uses the first and second Milnor K-groups modulo $\ell$ **and their full product**. It claims that every compatible isomorphism of these data is induced by a field isomorphism, up to multiplication by one nonzero scalar modulo $\ell$. In positive characteristic the reconstructed field is its *perfect closure*, with a Frobenius ambiguity; in characteristic zero the perfect closure is the field itself. The constants are also recovered. The hypothesis is relative transcendence degree at least two over algebraically closed constants.

The second October 5 paper, *Reconstruction from Milnor K-theory modulo the characteristic*, treats a different setting: **characteristic $p$ with coefficients modulo the same prime $p$**. Its Theorem 1.1 asserts that compatible maps on the degree-one classes preserving degree-two Steinberg relations come from unique field isomorphisms, up to a nonzero scalar in $\mathbb F_p$. It works directly with the field rather than stating the first paper's perfect-closure/Frobenius quotient. The introduction describes a recovery of projective lines over $p$th-power subfields using derivations; this is not the same construction as the prime-to-characteristic argument.

The third pinned September 23 paper, *The Bogomolov–Pop reconstruction theorem*, uses instead a **pro-ℓ abelian-by-central Galois quotient**. Its Theorem 1.1 claims that the compatible isomorphisms of the abelian quotient with its commutator pairing recover the perfect closures and their constants, modulo the Frobenius and $\mathbb Z_\ell^\times$ scalar ambiguities. This paper does **not** assume that valuations, inertia groups, curve quotients, or even matching characteristics are provided as part of the input. A compatible isomorphism is claimed to force the required matching properties.

These three formulations are related by the idea that reduced algebraic invariants remember geometry. They do not assert literally identical hypotheses or coefficient rings, and the article must not replace one formulation with another.
