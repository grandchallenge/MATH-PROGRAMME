## The question in plain language

An algebraic structure may have infinitely many generators yet still be *free*: apart from relations forced by the rules defining that structure, there are no unexpected identities tying its generators together.

The Deligne–Drinfeld conjecture concerns the rational Grothendieck–Teichmüller Lie algebra, a structure arising in the study of symmetries of fundamental groups and related algebraic objects. Its operation is the Ihara bracket. The conjecture predicts a remarkably simple description: one free generator in each odd weight beginning with three.

The pinned OAI-26 overview reports that a September 23, 2026 manuscript proves this statement. The claim would organize an intricate collection of symmetries into independent building blocks, but the claim of proof remains distinct from verification.

## A small example

For ordinary vectors, a basis allows every vector to be expressed as a linear combination, with no redundant basis vectors. A *free Lie algebra* has a richer analogue of this idea. Starting from named generators, one forms nested brackets such as

$$
[x,[y,z]],
$$

subject to the standard Lie identities: antisymmetry and the Jacobi identity. Freeness says there are no further relations among these formal bracket expressions.

A weight grading assigns a size to each generator and adds weights under brackets. In the Deligne–Drinfeld prediction, basic generators have weights 3, 5, 7, and so on. Higher-weight elements arise from combinations of those generators. Showing that certain elements exist at the right weights is not enough: a proof of freeness must also rule out *additional* relations and account for the entire Lie algebra.
