# OAI-26 family 008: the Deligne–Drinfeld conjecture

**Verification: VERIFIED_WITHIN_EXPLICIT_SCOPE. NO_CERTIFICATION.** The original formal theorem and its presented mathematical model have passed GCL checks.

## The question in plain language

An algebraic structure may have infinitely many generators yet still be *free*: apart from relations forced by the rules defining that structure, there are no unexpected identities tying its generators together.

The Deligne–Drinfeld conjecture concerns the rational Grothendieck–Teichmüller Lie algebra, a structure arising in the study of symmetries of fundamental groups and related algebraic objects. Its operation is the Ihara bracket. The conjecture predicts a remarkably simple description: one free generator in each odd weight beginning with three.

The pinned OAI-26 overview reports that a September 23, 2026 manuscript proves this statement. The claim would organize an intricate collection of symmetries into independent building blocks, and the original formal proof has passed GCL verification within the explicit scope described below.

## A small example

For ordinary vectors, a basis allows every vector to be expressed as a linear combination, with no redundant basis vectors. A *free Lie algebra* has a richer analogue of this idea. Starting from named generators, one forms nested brackets such as

$$
[x,[y,z]],
$$

subject to the standard Lie identities: antisymmetry and the Jacobi identity. Freeness says there are no further relations among these formal bracket expressions.

A weight grading assigns a size to each generator and adds weights under brackets. In the Deligne–Drinfeld prediction, basic generators have weights 3, 5, 7, and so on. Higher-weight elements arise from combinations of those generators. Showing that certain elements exist at the right weights is not enough: a proof of freeness must also rule out *additional* relations and account for the entire Lie algebra.

## What is known

The source-locked OAI-26 catalogue contains one Family 008 manuscript, *The Deligne–Drinfeld conjecture*, dated September 23, 2026. The pinned publisher overview states that the rational Grothendieck–Teichmüller Lie algebra, with the Ihara bracket, is freely generated by one element in each odd weight 3, 5, 7, and higher.

The words **rational**, **Ihara bracket**, and **free** are part of the claim. A result about a different coefficient field, a quotient or subalgebra, an associated graded object, or existence of independent classes in finitely many weights would not automatically establish the full assertion.

Theorem 1.1 identifies the graded rational solution space of antisymmetry, the three-term equation and the four-strand pentagon, with its Ihara bracket, with the free Lie algebra on the odd-weight generators. It also identifies their weight completions by a continuous isomorphism. The generators need not be canonical.

**How the proof works.** The hard direction is exhaustion: bounding the dimension of the entire solution space in every weight by that of the proposed free Lie algebra. The source reduces the leading pentagon relation modulo two, filtering by occurrences of one letter. A common ordering procedure transfers the leading relation to a cyclotomic arrangement algebra; integral deletion operators and a shift-symmetry argument restrict the possible leading terms to a free Lie algebra with exactly the required weights. This yields an upper bound, not merely a construction of examples.

The other direction constructs odd-weight rational solutions with nonzero depth-one coefficients using regularized braid holonomy. An induction over weights in saturated lattices over the integers localized at two then matches the upper bound with independent bracket words. Equality proves both freeness and generation. Completion is obtained weight by weight. The characteristic-two argument is a counting constraint on the rational solution space, not a substitution of a different coefficient field in the conclusion.

A concrete check: in weight three, the previously executed independent exact-rational test found the solution space to be one-dimensional, generated by

$$
[x+y,[x,y]].
$$

Weights one and two gave zero-dimensional spaces. Deliberately omitting triple braid relations or reversing a pentagon sign caused that test to reject. These checks illustrate the defining equations; they do not establish the all-weight dimension bound.
