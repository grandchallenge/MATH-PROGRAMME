## The question in plain language

Prime factorization creates arithmetic patterns that can seem random. One way to test that intuition is to assign each positive integer a sign according to whether it has an even or odd number of prime factors, counted with multiplicity. This gives the *Liouville function*. The two-point Chowla conjecture predicts cancellation when two related integer inputs are tested together, provided the associated affine forms are not proportional.

The broader Elliott correlation question replaces the Liouville function with other bounded multiplicative functions. These functions respect multiplication on coprime inputs, but can imitate simple periodic or oscillatory arithmetic patterns. The corrected conjecture therefore needs a precise *nonpretentiousness* hypothesis excluding that obstruction.

The pinned OAI-26 overview claims that Family 007 proves both an ordinary quantitative two-point Chowla estimate and a corrected binary Elliott theorem under an explicitly uniform nonpretentiousness condition.

## A small example

Define the Liouville function by

$$
\lambda(n)=(-1)^{\Omega(n)},
$$

where $\Omega(n)$ counts prime factors with repetition. Thus $\lambda(6)=1$, since $6=2\cdot3$ has two prime factors, while $\lambda(8)=-1$, since $8=2^3$ has three.

A simple pair of affine forms is $n$ and $n+1$. The two-point question asks whether the products $\lambda(n)\lambda(n+1)$ cancel on average. If they behaved like largely independent signs, their sum through $X$ would be much smaller than $X$. That analogy motivates the conjecture but is not proof.

The publisher's claimed bound is stronger than merely saying the normalized correlation tends to zero: it gives a logarithmic saving of the schematic form

$$
\left|\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda(a_1n+b_1)\lambda(a_2n+b_2)\right|
\leq C\frac{X}{(\log X)^c},
$$

for a positive absolute exponent $c$ and an implied constant depending on the fixed admissible forms. This display illustrates the indexed claim, not an independently checked transcription of its full hypotheses. Inputs of the forms must be positive where the functions are evaluated.

## What is known

The pinned source catalogue names one manuscript, *Ordinary two-point correlations of multiplicative functions* (September 24, 2026). Its indexed synopsis claims:

- The ordinary two-point Chowla conjecture for fixed nonproportional affine forms, with a bound of order $X/(\log X)^c$ for some absolute $c>0$.
- A more general corrected binary Elliott conjecture for complex-valued multiplicative functions bounded in magnitude by one, when one function is uniformly nonpretentious against each fixed Dirichlet character multiplied by $n^{it}$, for real $t$ of size at most $X$.

The nonproportionality restriction excludes degenerate pairs whose values are tightly linked by a constant multiple. The uniformity and character-twist clauses matter because a multiplicative function may track a character or an archimedean phase rather than display cancellation.
