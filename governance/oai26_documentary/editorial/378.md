# OAI-26 family 378: Disproof of the Exponential Time Hypothesis

**Status:** source and formal scope inspected; independent proof replay not established. NO_CERTIFICATION.

## The question in plain language

Testing every assignment of a Boolean formula with $n$ variables takes order $2^n$ tests. ETH asserts that worst-case 3-SAT cannot be solved in subexponential time in the variable count. The October 25 manuscript claims an exact count of satisfying assignments for every fixed clause width, not just a decision procedure.

## A small example

For $(x\lor y)\land(\neg x\lor y)$, precisely the two assignments with $y$ true satisfy both clauses. Counting gives two and establishes satisfiability. This elementary enumeration does not test the claimed speedup.

## What is known

The manuscript's main theorem gives, for fixed $k\ge2$, $n\ge20$ declared variables and $m$ clauses, deterministic exact counting in polynomial space and time

$$
(n+m)^{O_k(1)}2^{O_k(n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n})}.
$$

For fixed clause density $m\le Dn$, the logarithmic numerator in the exponent can be omitted. A weaker rational-interpolation route has $(\log\log n)^2$ in that numerator. Testing whether the count is positive decides SAT, so the claimed theorem refutes ETH, not P versus NP. The source uses shared linear summaries, low-degree replacement forms and counting common zeros of sparse equations. The sharper bounds use tensor contraction.

## What remains

Variable-count Weak ETH rules out $2^{O(n^{1-\eta})}$ time for a fixed positive $\eta$ with polynomial input overhead; the logarithmic improvement does not by itself refute that weaker hypothesis.
