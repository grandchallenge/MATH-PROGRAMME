# OAI-26 Family 049: A stable coordinate that is not a coordinate

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. Mathematical status: NO_CERTIFICATION.** This article reports the pinned publisher overview's claims, not an independently validated proof.

## The question in plain language

A polynomial in several variables is a *coordinate* if it can be completed to a polynomial change of variables with a polynomial inverse. A *stable coordinate* becomes a coordinate after adding extra independent variables. The stable-coordinate conjecture asked whether this additional freedom can ever help: does a polynomial that becomes a coordinate after adjoining a variable have to have been a coordinate already?

Family 049's publisher claims an explicit four-variable counterexample. It also claims that every level set of its polynomial looks like ordinary affine three-space, even though the polynomial cannot be straightened into a coordinate.

## A small example

The polynomial x+y in two variables is a coordinate: together with y it forms a polynomially invertible change of variables. The polynomial x squared, by contrast, is not a coordinate: the level set at zero is not the same kind of smooth hyperplane. These are simple illustrations, not tests for the much harder four-variable example.

The striking distinction in the claimed counterexample is that adding **one new variable** makes the polynomial a coordinate although it was not one before. Having individually ordinary-looking fibers is also weaker than having a single global coordinate change that straightens all fibers simultaneously.

## What is known

The pinned OAI-26 publisher overview makes two closely connected assertions.

First, it claims a polynomial in four complex variables which **is not a coordinate** but **becomes a coordinate after adjoining one variable**. This would disprove the stable-coordinate conjecture in four variables.

Second, it claims that **every fiber is affine three-space** while **none of its embeddings is rectifiable**, which the overview presents as disproving the Abhyankar–Sathaye conjecture even when all fibers are affine spaces. The difference between a fiber's abstract isomorphism type and its embedding inside affine four-space is essential.

The GCL catalogue identifies two manuscripts: *A stable coordinate that is not a coordinate in four variables* (October 5, 2026), and *An explicit noncoordinate polynomial with affine three-space zero fibre* (September 24, 2026). The second title addresses the zero fiber specifically; it should not be read as independently establishing the all-fibers claim without consulting the manuscript.
