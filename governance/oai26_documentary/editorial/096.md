# OAI-26 family 096: the Gaussian propeller conjecture

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** This article reports the pinned publisher's claims and does not independently verify the proof.

## The question in plain language

Imagine dividing a high-dimensional cloud of Gaussian-distributed points into finitely many groups. Each group has a first moment, describing its weighted displacement from the origin. The Gaussian propeller conjecture asks how large the sum of the squared lengths of these moments can be.

The pinned OAI-26 overview claims one sharp universal upper bound, attained by a simple three-part arrangement resembling a propeller. It also connects this inequality, together with a *separate* Unique Games theorem, to a claimed computational hardness threshold for a form of kernel clustering.

## A small example

In the plane, divide space into three equal angular sectors, each with angle $2\pi/3$. The sectors meet at the origin and point in three directions, like a three-bladed propeller. If a standard Gaussian point falls in each sector, its weighted first moment points toward the middle of that sector.

The mathematical quantity is the sum of the squared Euclidean norms of these unnormalized Gaussian first moments. A partition may have more than three pieces, and the surrounding Gaussian space may have arbitrarily many coordinates. The asserted theorem says that these extra freedoms never improve on the three-sector example.

## What is known

The protected Family 096 catalogue identifies one manuscript, *The Gaussian propeller bound in every dimension* (September 24, 2026).

The pinned publisher overview claims that **every finite measurable partition** of a Gaussian space satisfies the first-moment inequality

$$
\sum_i\left\|\int_{A_i}x\,d\gamma(x)\right\|_2^2
\leq \frac{9}{8\pi},
$$

where $\gamma$ denotes standard Gaussian measure and the $A_i$ form the partition. In **dimension at least two**, three equal planar sectors extended orthogonally into the remaining dimensions reportedly attain equality.

The overview further describes a conditional combination: together with a **separate Unique Games theorem**, the propeller inequality yields NP-hardness of improving the stated loss factor

$$
\frac{8\pi}{9}\left(1-\frac1k\right)
$$

for identity-target kernel clustering at **fixed $k\geq3$**, on **rational centered positive-semidefinite inputs**. This computational implication must not be attributed to the Gaussian inequality alone, nor generalized to arbitrary kernel-clustering formulations.
