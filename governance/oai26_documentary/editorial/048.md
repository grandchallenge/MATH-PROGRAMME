# OAI-26 family 048: a counterexample to Lipman–Zariski in characteristic zero

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** This is documentary coverage of a publisher-reported construction, not an independently validated counterexample.

## The question in plain language

The tangent directions to a smooth geometric space vary together in a well-behaved vector bundle. On a singular space, the analogous object—the tangent sheaf—can fail to have this simple local structure. The Lipman–Zariski conjecture predicted that if a complex algebraic variety has a locally free tangent sheaf, then the variety must be smooth.

The pinned OAI-26 overview claims a counterexample: a normal affine complex surface that is singular but nevertheless has a **free rank-two tangent sheaf**. Freeness is stronger than local freeness, so such an example would directly contradict the unrestricted conjecture in characteristic zero.

## A small example

At a smooth point of a complex surface there are two independent infinitesimal tangent directions. A rank-two free tangent sheaf means that, across the whole surface, tangent vector fields admit two global algebraic generators with no module relations beyond those of a free module.

The intuition behind Lipman–Zariski is that having such consistently organized tangent directions ought to rule out singularities. But a singular point is defined by the failure of the local algebraic geometry to be regular; an apparently well-behaved tangent module does not automatically prove local regularity.

The distinction between **normal**, **smooth**, **locally free**, and **globally free** is central. A normal surface can have isolated singularities, and freeness of its tangent sheaf is a precise module-theoretic property rather than a statement that the underlying space looks smooth.
