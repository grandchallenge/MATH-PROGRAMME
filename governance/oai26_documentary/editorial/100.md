# OAI-26 family 100: a claimed counterexample to Bang's cylinder-covering bound

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** The statements below are attributed to the pinned publisher overview; the four underlying proof manuscripts have not undergone independent GCL theorem-level audit.

## The question in plain language

How little cross-sectional area can a finite collection of infinitely extended cylinders use to cover a three-dimensional solid? A cylinder here means a set swept out along a fixed direction from a perpendicular planar base—not necessarily a circular tube. Bang's proposed half-area covering bound says, roughly, that a covering should cost at least half a relevant projected area of the solid.

Family 100 reports a construction that beats this benchmark even when the solid is a regular tetrahedron and the cylinder bases are compact triangles.

## A small example

Imagine shining a light on a tetrahedron. Its orthogonal shadow on a perpendicular plane has a certain area, and the area depends on the viewing direction. A cylinder with that direction has a planar perpendicular cross-section; extending the cross-section along a line yields a three-dimensional set. Several such cylinders, potentially pointing in different directions, may jointly cover the tetrahedron.

The quantity at issue is the **sum of the areas of the individual perpendicular bases**, not the volume of the infinitely long cylinders, nor the area of their union in one common plane. The claimed construction uses *finitely many* cylinders and covers the *entire closed* regular tetrahedron, including the boundary.

## What is known

The protected Family 100 catalogue names four manuscripts: finite angular covers, finite approximation of ruled sets, slope-field perturbations of a two-cylinder covering, and triangular approximation of radial sweeps.

The pinned publisher overview claims a finite covering of a closed regular tetrahedron $T$ by cylinders $C_1,\ldots,C_N$ with compact triangular bases $B_1,\ldots,B_N$ satisfying

$$
T\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}C_i,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathrm{area}(B_i)
<\tfrac12\min_{u\in S^2}\mathrm{area}(\mathrm{proj}_{u^\perp}T).
$$

This formula expresses the overview's stated claim, **not** a independently established mathematical fact. On its face, it is stronger than merely beating half the projected area in one chosen direction: the right side is half the **smallest** projection area across all directions.

According to that overview, the construction disproves both Bang's half-area cylinder-covering conjecture and a stronger directionwise-normalized conjecture in dimension three. The exact formulations of both conjectures must be checked against the manuscripts before interpreting the purported counterexample.
