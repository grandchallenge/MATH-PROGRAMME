# OAI-26 family 019: The local p-adic section conjecture and global consequences

## The question in plain language

A curve can be studied by its points, or indirectly by recording how its finite algebraic covers fit together. The latter information is encoded by the arithmetic étale fundamental group. Grothendieck's section conjecture asks whether the group-theoretic information can recover rational points, particularly for curves of genus at least two.

Family 019's main paper claims that rational points can be recovered from every continuous section of the full arithmetic étale fundamental group of a smooth proper curve of genus at least two over a finite extension of the p-adic numbers. It also claims a global consequence for specified modular curves over the rational numbers.

## A small example

Think of a finite covering of a curve as several overlapping copies, locally arranged above it. The étale fundamental group records the compatible symmetries of all finite covers. A rational point provides a way to identify a section of the associated arithmetic fundamental-group sequence, up to a suitable conjugacy relation.

The conjecture's difficult direction is the converse: showing that every relevant section comes from a rational point. Merely constructing a section from an existing point is not a proof that all sections arise this way.

The analogy is explanatory only; it does not establish the conjecture.

## What is known

The two pinned primary PDFs were read directly, and their statements distinguish a covering construction from the section-conjecture theorem.

**Local claim.** The main manuscript, Theorem 1.1, claims that for every prime and every finite extension of the corresponding p-adic field, each smooth proper geometrically connected curve of genus at least two has a bijection between rational points and continuous sections of its *full arithmetic étale fundamental-group sequence*, modulo conjugation by the entire geometric étale fundamental group. The statement does not replace the full group by an abelian or birational quotient. The manuscript separately states a rank-one valuation-completion theorem (Theorem 1.2), used in its claimed local proof.

**Global claim.** The main manuscript, Corollary 8.5, claims the section-conjecture bijection for the smooth projective modular curves $X_0(N)$ and $X_1(N)$ over the rational numbers whenever their genus is at least two. The route uses a **separate finite-descent criterion** (Theorem 8.2) and Stoll’s results, with published corrections. It does not claim the conjecture for every curve over the rational numbers. For an arbitrary genus-at-least-two curve over a number field, the source requires equality between its finite-cover descent locus and its rational points; the local theorem alone does not supply that equality.

**Geometric covering result.** The separate 7-page *Étale covers with a prescribed exterior sheet* paper, Theorem 1.1, addresses a smooth proper hyperbolic curve over an algebraic closure of a p-adic field, a nonempty compact exterior domain obtained by deleting specified smaller open disks, and a point of that exterior. It claims a **single connected finite étale cover** with one entire exterior sheet mapped isomorphically, while **every component** over **each original specified disk** has degree divisible by the residue prime. Its proof uses the resolution-of-nonsingularities input of Mochizuki–Tsujimura and a Galois stabilizer construction. This is a covering theorem, **not** itself the local section-conjecture theorem. The main 24-page section paper develops a completion-and-patching route to its stronger local conclusion.
