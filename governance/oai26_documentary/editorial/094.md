# OAI-26 family 094: subpolynomial dimension reduction in Lp

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** This article documents the pinned publisher claim, not an independently checked proof.

## The question in plain language

A finite set of points in a function space carries distances between every pair. Dimension reduction asks whether the same pattern of distances can be represented in a much smaller finite-dimensional space without changing them very much.

For Euclidean distances, powerful dimension-reduction theorems are known. The question becomes more difficult when distances are measured with an $L_p$ norm for an exponent other than two. The pinned OAI-26 overview claims that a set of $n$ points in real $L_p$ can always be represented with a fixed arbitrarily small multiplicative distortion using a dimension that grows slower than every fixed positive power of $n$.

## A small example

Imagine collecting $n$ signals, each represented by a real-valued function. The distance between two signals can be measured by taking the $p$-th root of the integral of the $p$-th power of their difference.

An embedding with distortion at most $D$ preserves ratios of pairwise distances up to a factor $D$, after a permitted common rescaling. A distortion slightly larger than one allows approximation; an exact embedding requires no distance distortion at all.

The claimed target dimension is

$$
d=n^{o(1)}.
$$

This does not mean that the dimension is bounded by a constant. It means that for each fixed positive exponent, the dimension eventually grows more slowly than that power of $n$.

## What is known

The protected Family 094 catalogue identifies one manuscript, *Subpolynomial dimension reduction in Lp* (September 23, 2026).

The pinned publisher overview claims that for **every fixed exponent $1<p<\infty$** and **every fixed distortion allowance $D>1$**, every finite $n$-point subset of **real $L_p$** embeds into a finite-dimensional $\ell_p^d$ with distortion at most $D$ and $d=n^{o(1)}$.

The overview describes this as answering Naor's sublinear-dimension question for $p\ne2$. It separately claims a contrasting **worst-case exact-embedding dimension of order $\Theta(n^2)$** for $p\ne2$.

The approximate and exact conclusions have different quantifiers. In particular, fixed distortion greater than one must not be silently replaced by distortion exactly one, and the real-valued setting must not be generalized to complex $L_p$ without source evidence.
