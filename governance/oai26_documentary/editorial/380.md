# OAI-26 family 380: Strongly polynomial linear programming and polytope diameter

**Status:** source and formal scope inspected; independent replay not established. NO_CERTIFICATION.

## The question in plain language

Linear programming optimizes a linear objective under linear inequalities. Polynomial-time algorithms for rational coefficients can depend on their binary length. Smale's ninth problem asks for polynomially many operations in the number of variables and constraints, irrespective of coefficient magnitude. Another question asks whether one can always travel between polytope vertices in polynomially many edge moves. The November 24 papers claim an affirmative answer to the former and a negative answer to the latter. These are compatible: an algorithm need not walk along edges.

## A small example

Maximize $x+y$ over $0\le x,y\le1$. The point $(1,1)$ achieves 2; adding the two upper-bound inequalities proves no feasible point exceeds 2. This gives a primal and dual certificate. Opposite vertices of the square are two edge moves apart, but certificate-based optimization need not traverse those edges.

## What is known

The linear programming paper claims a deterministic strongly polynomial algorithm for arbitrary real coefficients, with exact scalar operations, comparisons and charged control work. On rational inputs the intermediate bit lengths are polynomial in the binary input length. It returns matching primal and dual optima (strictly complementary in the manuscript), a feasible point and improving ray, or a sparse Farkas infeasibility certificate.

The proof strategy puts feasibility alternatives into orthogonal subspaces. Queries at different coordinate scales reveal reliable signs, short substitutes for logarithmic subdeterminant magnitudes preserve the comparisons, and support reduction permits exact recovery by linear algebra. The resource question is whether both the number of queries and the auxiliary computations are bounded. The paper extends this search to convex quadratic programming and mean-payoff games.

The polytope paper claims bounded simple rational polytopes with exactly $n$ facets, subpolynomial dimension and graph diameter exceeding every fixed polynomial in $n$ for all sufficiently large $n$. Even allowing moves across faces of any prescribed subpolynomial dimension leaves superpolynomial distances. A further result separates vertices by a prescribed fraction of an objective's range. If correct, this refutes the polynomial Hirsch conjecture, not merely the original linear bound.
