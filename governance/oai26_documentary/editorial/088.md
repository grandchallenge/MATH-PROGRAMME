# OAI-26 family 088: projection-body inequalities and simplex counterexamples

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** This article explains claims indexed in two pinned manuscripts, without independently verifying their proofs.

## The question in plain language

A convex body has a volume, and it casts shadows—orthogonal projections—whose sizes vary with direction. A *projection body* packages the volumes of those shadows into a new convex body. Projection-body inequalities compare the size and shape of the original body with the size of this derived object.

The pinned OAI-26 overview reports two contrasting conclusions. It claims that ellipsoids minimize projection-body volume among convex bodies of fixed volume in dimensions at least four, while products of simplices defeat a conjectured simplex-based maximum for a normalized projection-body volume.

## A small example

Imagine a three-dimensional convex solid and measure the areas of its shadows from every direction. Those measurements describe its projection body. In $n$ dimensions the relevant shadows have dimension $n-1$.

One can ask two separate optimization questions: Which body has the *smallest* projection-body volume when its own volume is fixed? Which body has the *largest normalized* projection-body volume? Answers to the minimum problem do not determine the maximum.

An ellipsoid is an affine image of a Euclidean ball. A simplex is the higher-dimensional analogue of a triangle or tetrahedron. A product of simplices is generally not itself a simplex, and its projection behavior may differ substantially.

## What is known

The protected catalogue identifies two Family 088 manuscripts, both dated September 24, 2026: *Petty's projection-volume conjecture in dimensions at least four* and *A product counterexample to the simplex maximum for projection-body volume*.

The pinned publisher overview claims that in **every dimension $n\ge4$**, ellipsoids uniquely minimize projection-body volume at a fixed volume of the original convex body. It also reports the full Lutwak–Petty projection inequalities. The exact normalization and interpretation of uniqueness up to affine transformations require inspection of the paper.

By contrast, the second manuscript reportedly constructs **products of simplices** that exceed Brannen's proposed simplex maximum for normalized projection-body volume by an **exponential factor** in every sufficiently large dimension.

The two statements address different extremal directions. The second is a counterexample to a proposed maximum, not a contradiction of the minimum theorem. Nor does a counterexample in all sufficiently large dimensions imply one in every dimension.
