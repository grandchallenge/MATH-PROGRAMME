# OAI-26 family 052: tangent-bundle splittings and product decompositions

## The question in plain language

The tangent bundle of a manifold organizes all directions in which one may move at each point. Sometimes it splits into two holomorphic subbundles, suggesting two independent systems of directions. That does not automatically make the whole manifold a product: the directions must be compatible with actual families of submanifolds, a property called *integrability*.

The pinned OAI-26 synopsis claims that when both summands are integrable on a compact Kähler manifold, its universal covering space decomposes compatibly as a product. It further claims that on smooth rationally connected projective manifolds, the required integrability follows automatically from the splitting.

## A small example

For a product $X=Y\times Z$, its tangent bundle naturally splits:

$$
T_X\cong \operatorname{pr}_Y^*T_Y\oplus\operatorname{pr}_Z^*T_Z.
$$

The two sets of directions come from moving in $Y$ while holding $Z$ fixed, and vice versa. Each set is integrable because there are actual factor submanifolds tangent to it.

The converse is more demanding. A splitting of abstract tangent directions need not obviously come from a global product. Passing to the *universal cover* removes fundamental-group identifications that may prevent the original manifold itself from being a product. Thus a universal-cover splitting is not the same statement as a product decomposition of $X$.

## What is known

The protected Family 052 catalogue lists two manuscripts, both dated September 23, 2026:

- *Universal-cover splitting for compact Kähler manifolds*.
- *Integrability of split tangent bundles on rationally connected manifolds*.

The publisher's overview reports a two-summand form of **Beauville's splitting conjecture**: for a compact Kähler manifold whose tangent bundle splits into two *integrable holomorphic subbundles*, the universal cover admits a compatible product decomposition.

It also reports **Höring's conjecture** for smooth rationally connected projective manifolds: a two-summand splitting of their tangent bundles has both summands automatically integrable. The indexed synopsis then obtains the corresponding universal-cover product decomposition.

The integrability hypothesis in the general compact Kähler result must not be omitted. Nor should the rationally connected projective conclusion be extended to every compact complex manifold without a separate theorem. The scope here is explicitly two summands, not an arbitrary number of summands.
