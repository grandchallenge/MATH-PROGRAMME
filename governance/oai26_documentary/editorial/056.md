## The question in plain language

The minimal model program attempts to simplify an algebraic or complex-analytic variety through controlled birational transformations. Some steps contract geometric features; *flips* replace one small contraction with another model. A fundamental question is whether the permitted transformations eventually stop or could continue indefinitely.

Family 056 reports termination results in complex dimension four. Its publisher overview treats both compact Kähler fourfolds with generalized log canonical data and projective log canonical fourfolds with rational boundaries. These are related but not interchangeable settings.

## A small example

Imagine simplifying a complicated geometric model by repeatedly making a permitted local replacement that improves a chosen numerical direction. Each replacement may make progress locally, but that alone does not establish that an infinite sequence is impossible. A termination theorem supplies precisely this global guarantee.

For a pair $(X,B)$, the divisor $K_X+B$ guides the transformations. A *generalized pair* has additional nef data, often encoded as a b-divisor. That added data changes the admissible steps and hypotheses.

An assertion that **every existing permitted flip sequence terminates** does not necessarily prove that the requisite flip exists at each stage. Termination and existence must be checked separately.
