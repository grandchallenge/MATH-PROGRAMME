## The question in plain language

How small can the product of a convex body's volume and the volume of its polar be? The sharp conjectured bounds differ between symmetric and arbitrary bodies. The related symplectic problem asks how large a standard symplectic ball fits inside a symmetric body times its polar.

## A small example

A symmetric interval of radius a has polar interval of radius 1/a, so their lengths multiply to four. A centered square of side two has area four and polar diamond of area two, giving product eight. General triangles belong to the nonsymmetric setting.

## What is known

The pinned symmetric Mahler manuscript claims the sharp all-dimensional inequality with constant four to the power n divided by n factorial, and equality exactly for linear Hanner bodies, assembled recursively from intervals. The separate general Mahler manuscript claims the sharp Santaló-centered lower bound (n+1) to the power n+1 divided by the square of n factorial, with equality exactly for simplices. These are distinct claims and constants.

A third pinned manuscript claims that the Gromov width of the interior polar product of any origin-symmetric convex body in dimension at least two equals four. Symplectic balls of every capacity strictly below four embed; a ball at capacity exactly four is not asserted. Volume preservation gives the symmetric volume-product inequality, not its equality classification.

The symmetric proof roadmap uses a holomorphic lens, feasible simplices and metric-median equality rigidity. The general proof roadmap uses cones, Gaussian projections, spectral layers and scalar inequalities. The symplectic proof roadmap uses holomorphic ball construction, conformal slice bounds, approximation and nonsqueezing. Functional inequalities are separate corollaries.
