## The question in plain language

Compact complex surfaces are four-real-dimensional spaces whose geometry uses complex coordinates. Class VII surfaces are a particularly challenging nonalgebraic part of their classification. The Global Spherical Shell conjecture asks whether minimal class VII surfaces with positive second Betti number contain a special neighborhood modeled on a round three-sphere sitting inside punctured complex two-space.

The pinned OAI-26 overview claims an affirmative answer for every connected minimal compact complex surface of class VII whose second Betti number is positive. This would supply a strong uniform geometric structure for the entire indicated class.

## A small example

The standard sphere of real dimension three is the boundary of a small ball in complex two-space. Remove the origin from that complex space and take a thin open neighborhood of the sphere. A *global spherical shell* on a complex surface is a holomorphic embedding of such a neighborhood with the additional requirement that its complement in the surface is connected.

The complement condition matters. A local neighborhood looking like a sphere is not sufficient by itself: the shell must sit in the surface in the globally prescribed way.

The second Betti number $b_2$ counts, roughly, independent two-dimensional homology classes. The publisher's theorem is restricted to $b_2>0$; it should not be silently extended to all class VII surfaces.

## What is known

The protected Family 060 catalogue identifies a single manuscript, *Global Spherical Shells on Minimal Surfaces of Class VII* (September 24, 2026).

The pinned publisher overview claims that **every connected minimal compact complex surface of class VII with $b_2>0$ contains a global spherical shell**. It defines the shell as a holomorphically embedded neighborhood of the standard three-sphere in punctured complex two-space whose complement is connected.

The claim is restricted to minimal compact complex class VII surfaces with positive second Betti number. It does not automatically establish a corresponding statement for arbitrary blown-up surfaces, nor does it subsume separate classification questions involving other Kodaira dimensions.

The exact definition of class VII, possible requirements on smoothness or connectedness of the neighborhood, and any case distinctions in the proof must be checked against the manuscript.
