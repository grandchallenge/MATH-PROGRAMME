# OAI-26 family 084: the geometric case of the Erdős similarity conjecture

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** This article reports two source-pinned manuscript claims without independently verifying their proofs.

## The question in plain language

The Erdős similarity conjecture concerns whether a set of real numbers with positive measure must contain a shifted and rescaled copy of a prescribed infinite pattern. For patterns whose points approach zero geometrically, the pinned OAI-26 overview claims that one can build sets occupying almost all of the unit interval while avoiding every nontrivial similar copy of the pattern.

The result is about an infinite geometric sequence. It does not establish the full Erdős similarity conjecture for arbitrary infinite patterns.

## A small example

Fix a ratio $q$ strictly between zero and one and consider

$$
E_q=\{q,q^2,q^3,\ldots\}.
$$

A similar copy is obtained by replacing each point $x$ with $a+bx$, where $a$ is a translation and $b\ne0$ is a dilation. Negative values of $b$ reflect the sequence; positive ones preserve its orientation.

The claimed construction gives a compact set $K$ inside $[0,1]$ with Lebesgue measure as close to one as desired, but with no set $a+bE_q$ contained in $K$ for any $a$ and nonzero $b$. The absence of a copy is stronger than simply avoiding one particular translation or one particular scaling.

## What is known

The protected Family 084 catalogue identifies two manuscripts: *The geometric case of the Erdős similarity conjecture* (October 5, 2026) and *The dyadic case of the Erdős similarity conjecture* (September 25, 2026).

The pinned publisher overview claims that for **every fixed ratio $q\in(0,1)$** and every desired measure deficit, there is a compact subset of the unit interval whose measure is correspondingly near one and which contains **no translated, nontrivially dilated copy** of the infinite sequence $\{q^n:n\ge1\}$.

The statement covers dilations of either sign. The dyadic case, associated with a specific ratio, is related but does not alone imply the theorem for every ratio; the relationship between the two manuscripts requires a source audit.

The result supplies arbitrarily large-measure avoiders, not necessarily a single measure-one avoiding set. It is also not a theorem about avoiding all geometric ratios simultaneously with the same set.
