# OAI-26 family 018: the Margulis–Platonov conjecture over global fields

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** This article reports the scope of two pinned manuscripts; it does not independently verify their proofs.

## The question in plain language

An algebraic group defined over a global field has rational points that form an abstract group. Such a group can have normal subgroups: collections of elements preserved when conjugated by any group element. The Margulis–Platonov conjecture predicts a rigid description of the noncentral normal subgroups of certain simple algebraic groups over global fields.

The pinned OAI-26 catalogue claims the conjecture is resolved for both number fields and global function fields, including the delicate case of characteristic two. Its description expresses the normal subgroups using finitely many local groups, rather than arbitrary global constructions.

## A small example

For a group $H$, a subgroup $N$ is normal when

$$
hNh^{-1}=N
$$

for every element $h$ of $H$. The centre consists of elements commuting with every element of the group. A *noncentral* normal subgroup is one not entirely confined to that centre.

A global field admits completions at its places. These local fields capture arithmetic information near a prime or another valuation. The conjectural classification says that certain global normal subgroups can be recognized by taking inverse images of open normal subgroups of a finite product of relevant local groups.

This is an analogy for the structure of the classification. It does not mean that every normal subgroup of every algebraic group has such a description.

## What is known

The pinned source catalogue assigns two manuscripts to Family 018:

- *The Margulis–Platonov conjecture over global function fields* (October 5, 2026).
- *The Margulis–Platonov conjecture over number fields* (September 23, 2026).

The publisher's overview claims the conjecture for **every global field**, explicitly including global function fields of characteristic two. Its central statement concerns an **absolutely almost simple simply connected** algebraic group $G$ over a global field $k$. The synopsis says that every noncentral abstract normal subgroup of $G(k)$ is the inverse image of an open normal subgroup in the finite product of its anisotropic nonarchimedean local groups.

Each qualifier is material. The claim concerns abstract rather than only closed normal subgroups, an algebraic group with specified simplicity and connectivity properties, and particular anisotropic nonarchimedean factors. It cannot automatically be extended to arbitrary reductive groups or unrestricted products of local completions.

The exact map defining the inverse image, treatment of the centre and the possible exceptional local factors must be confirmed against the pinned theorem statements.
