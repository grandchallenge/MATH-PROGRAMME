# OAI-26 family 025: Short Egyptian fractions

## The question in plain language

An *Egyptian fraction* writes a positive rational number as a sum of reciprocals of **distinct** positive integers. For instance, one half can be written as one third plus one sixth. How few terms suffice for *any* fraction with a prescribed denominator?

The September 25, 2026 manuscript *Short Egyptian fractions* claims to settle an Erdős conjecture: for each sufficiently large denominator $b$, every numerator $1\le a<b$ admits a distinct-unit-fraction expansion with at most a constant times $\log\log b$ terms.

## A small example

For the fraction $5/6$, the identity

$$
\frac56=\frac12+\frac13
$$

uses two distinct positive unit fractions. The requirement that denominators differ prevents simply repeating the same reciprocal. The challenge is uniformity: one must find short representations **simultaneously for every numerator** with the same large denominator.

A fraction such as $1/b$ already has a one-term expansion. The worst case, not the easiest numerator, governs the conjecture.

## What is known

The pinned primary PDF is a 33-page manuscript whose displayed **Theorem 1.1** defines $N(a,b)$ as the minimum number of distinct unit fractions with denominators at least two representing $a/b$, and $N(b)$ as the maximum over $1\le a<b$. It claims absolute constants $c_1,c_2>0$ and $b_0$ such that, for $b\ge b_0$,

$$
c_1\log\log b\le N(b)\le c_2\log\log b.
$$

The lower-order bound concerns the *worst numerator*. It is not a claim that every individual $a/b$ requires this many terms. The source explicitly permits arbitrarily large denominators in the Egyptian-fraction expansion; an additional bound on their size is **not** part of Theorem 1.1.

Its introduction distinguishes earlier larger upper bounds from the new claimed double-logarithmic order. Its **Corollary 1.2** also states that the number $F(k)$ of expansions of one into exactly $k$ distinct unit fractions satisfies $\log\log F(k)$ of order $k$ for sufficiently large $k$. The abstract further claims a doubly exponential scale for the least denominator at least two excluded from all such expansions of length $k$. These counting and prescribed-denominator results must not be conflated with the main uniform-length bound.
