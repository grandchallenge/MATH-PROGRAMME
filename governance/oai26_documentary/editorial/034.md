## The question in plain language

An algebraic or complex-analytic space has a canonical bundle that reflects its geometry. The *abundance* problem asks whether a canonical-type divisor that is numerically nonnegative produces enough global sections to define a geometric map. In the logarithmic version, a boundary divisor is included and mild singularities are allowed.

Family 034 combines several strong claims: log abundance for certain compact Kähler pairs, projective log abundance in characteristic zero, effective Iitaka fibrations, and uniform bounds on indices of semi-log-canonical log Calabi–Yau pairs. These claims have different hypotheses and should not be treated as one theorem.

## A small example

Suppose a line bundle has a nonnegative intersection number with every curve. Such numerical nonnegativity is called *nefness*. But nefness does not, without further hypotheses, imply that some tensor power has enough global sections to define a map with no base points.

*Semiampleness* supplies this missing structure: a positive tensor power, or suitable positive multiple of a rational divisor, becomes base-point-free. The abundance conjecture asks when canonical-type nef divisors are semiample. An *Iitaka fibration* uses sections of multiples of such divisors to reveal the part of a variety measured by its Kodaira dimension.

This analogy distinguishes numerical positivity, the existence of nonzero sections, semiampleness, and effective bounds on the multiple required; none should be inferred from another without proof.

## What is known

The pinned OAI-26 overview reports these results:

- Using logarithmic Iitaka subadditivity, log abundance in every dimension for **normal compact Kähler log canonical pairs with effective rational boundary**, provided the rational Cartier adjoint is analytically nef. The claimed conclusion is semiampleness.
- Projective log abundance over algebraically closed fields of **characteristic zero**, and an effective Iitaka fibration theorem for smooth projective varieties of nonnegative Kodaira dimension over such fields.
- A uniform index theorem for connected projective semi-log-canonical log Calabi–Yau pairs, for each fixed dimension and fixed finite set of rational boundary coefficients. The indexed bound is independent of the number of components.

The family contains 14 related manuscripts, including an explicitly *conditional* compact Kähler fourfold paper and separate work on nonvanishing, injectivity, canonical models, denominator bounds and uniform fibrations. A conditional intermediate step does not automatically constitute an unconditional result until its assumptions have been discharged.

The phrase **effective Iitaka fibration** means more than existence of a fibration: the precise uniform multiple and its allowed dependencies must be checked in the manuscript. Equally, the uniform index claim is not uniform over arbitrary dimensions or arbitrary boundary coefficient sets.
