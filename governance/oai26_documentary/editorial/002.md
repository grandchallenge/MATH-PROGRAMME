# OAI-26 family 002: the exact Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer formula in low Selmer corank

**Documentary state:** SOURCE-BASED DRAFT. The source announces a proof; this article does not validate that proof or certify the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture.

## The question in plain language

An elliptic curve is a cubic equation with a distinguished point whose rational solutions form an abelian group. Two different measurements of that curve often seem to agree. The *arithmetic rank* counts independent rational points of infinite order; the *analytic rank* counts how many times the curve's associated L-function vanishes at its central point.

The Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer (BSD) conjecture says more than just that these two ranks agree. It predicts the exact first nonzero Taylor coefficient of the L-function in terms of the height regulator of rational points, real periods, local correction factors, torsion, and the Tate–Shafarevich group. The last group measures failures of a local-to-global principle and is not known to be finite in general.

The October 7 OAI-26 manuscript claims a complete leading-coefficient formula for every elliptic curve over the rational numbers **provided that the full power-Selmer group has corank zero or one at at least one prime**. The condition concerns a Selmer group, not merely a small observed number of rational points.

## A small example

Imagine measuring the same landscape by two methods. The number of independent directions you can travel is analogous to the rank of rational points. A measurement of *volume* along those directions is analogous to the BSD leading coefficient. Establishing the correct number of directions does not establish the exact volume: it leaves several multiplicative factors to determine.

At analytic rank zero, the BSD formula predicts a nonzero central L-value expressed through a real period, local factors, a finite Tate–Shafarevich group and squared rational torsion. At rank one, the central L-value vanishes and the *first derivative* is controlled by the same factors together with a height regulator. The regulator equals one by convention in rank zero. Higher ranks would involve higher derivatives and a higher-dimensional regulator, but they are not covered by the low-corank hypothesis here.

As a schematic numerical analogy, knowing that the product of several positive factors is finite cannot tell us their exact product. The manuscript claims an exact equality of such quantities, not just that both sides are finite or have the same order of vanishing. This analogy is not evidence for its proof.

## What is known

For an elliptic curve over the rational numbers, let the analytic rank be the order of vanishing of its L-function at the central point, and let the arithmetic rank be the rank of its rational point group. For each prime, the full prime-power Selmer group has a corank. The main manuscript claims:

- If *some* prime has full power-Selmer corank zero or one, then the analytic rank and arithmetic rank both equal that corank.
- The entire Tate–Shafarevich group is finite.
- The leading Taylor coefficient obeys the full BSD formula, including **every prime's contribution**, not just its valuation at one selected auxiliary prime.
- No additional restriction is imposed on reduction type, rational torsion, rational isogenies, complex multiplication, or residual Galois representations.

With standard normalizations specified in its introduction, the predicted formula has the following shape:

$$
\frac{L^{(r)}(E,1)}{r!}
=
\frac{\Omega_E\,\mathrm{Reg}_E\,\#\Sha(E/\mathbb Q)
\prod_{\ell}c_\ell(E)}
{\#E(\mathbb Q)_{\mathrm{tors}}^{2}}.
$$

Here the product is over finite primes, the period is taken with the manuscript's differential convention, and the regulator uses a basis of the *full* free Mordell–Weil group. Using a finite-index subgroup without correcting its squared index would give the wrong leading coefficient.

The manuscript presents the low-corank Selmer converse and a two-primary leading-term formula as *companion inputs*. It identifies the remaining new claim as the exact leading-term valuation at odd primes. The Family 002 index includes three manuscripts: this exact-formula paper; an all-prime Selmer converse; and a two-primary BSD formula. These are logically distinguishable: the converse establishes ranks and finiteness, the two-primary result addresses the prime two, and the main paper asserts the remaining complete equality.

The source overview also says that, together with Family 006 on quadratic twists, Family 002 would imply full BSD on a density-one subfamily. That is a **combined conditional dependency statement about the claimed manuscripts**, not a standalone theorem proved in this documentary article.
