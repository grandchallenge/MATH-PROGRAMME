## The question in plain language

How much work must a computation perform even when it can share intermediate results and exploit cancellations? A short product of sums can encode exponentially many terms, so counting terms is not a lower bound. OpenAI proposes measuring the information transport in tensor networks geometrically. Seven November 24 papers apply this idea to exact exponents, arithmetic complexity separations and combinatorics. These are not equivalent to resolving Boolean P versus NP.

## A small example

A triangle with side lengths 3, 4 and 5 has perimeter 12. Under the proposed Euclidean price law a single bit shared among all three sites costs 6; three independent bits on the edges cost 12. Correlation matters, not merely location. This illustrates the price formula, not the existence theorem.

## What is known

### Matrix multiplication

The new manuscript claims that the square matrix multiplication exponent is $9/4$ over every field, with formal arithmetic identities even over finite fields. For every fixed positive $\varepsilon$, the upper bound gives $O(n^{9/4+\varepsilon})$ operations; the lower bound forbids every strictly smaller exponent. The stronger finite-size claim is at least $\lceil n^{9/4}/2\rceil$ nonscalar multiplication or division gates.

The paper compares the price of three shared indices of the multiplication tensor with the price of one common rank label. Euclidean triangles alone give exponent two. A tripod with three unit arms has pairwise distances two; the critical shared-label rate $8/3$ gives $6/(8/3)=9/4$. Assuming a strict saving below $9/4$, the source derives rigidity of sphere-tensor prices and uses it to repeatedly compress tensor powers of networks. The resulting batched distinctness tests would violate a stated volume lower bound. This is a proof strategy for a lower bound, not a search for faster algorithms.

The publisher's Lean scope also claims the exact dual exponent $1/2$ and the rectangular exponent for positive weights sorted as $x\ge y\ge z>0$:

$$
x+y+\frac{\max(0,(x+y)z-xy)^2}{4xyz}.
$$

This evaluates to $9/4$ at equal unit weights. The exact dual value is stronger than the interval stated in the matrix paper's introduction.

The geometry paper uses scaled logarithms of spectral invariants: direct sums add, tensor products multiply and local linear maps cannot increase the invariant. The hierarchy paper constrains graph state sums; paths, trees and grids exhibit increasingly powerful computation models. The sunflower paper reduces sets to words and uses local tensor operations to eliminate columns with partial repetitions; a price bound limits surviving shared labels.

The November 23 VP versus VNP manuscript is preserved as a historical edition, subsumed by the November 24 hierarchy and circuit papers.
