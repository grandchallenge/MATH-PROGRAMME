## The question in plain language

The Hilbert transform is a fundamental operation in Fourier analysis that averages a function against a singular, sign-changing kernel. Its trilinear counterpart combines three functions evaluated at different shifts. The interactions between those shifts create difficult cancellation problems.

Family 086 reports an estimate at the symmetric exponent three: when the three inputs are each in the space $L^3$ on the real line, the trilinear Hilbert transform is claimed to produce an integrable output in $L^1$.

## A small example

For well-behaved functions $f_1,f_2,f_3$, the operator in the indexed synopsis is associated with the principal-value expression

$$
T(f_1,f_2,f_3)(x)
=\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb R}
f_1(x-t)f_2(x-2t)f_3(x-3t)\,\frac{dt}{t}.
$$

The principal value means that one excludes a symmetric interval about $t=0$ and then takes a limit. The claimed estimate has the form

$$
\|T(f_1,f_2,f_3)\|_{L^1(\mathbb R)}
\leq C\prod_{j=1}^{3}\|f_j\|_{L^3(\mathbb R)}.
$$

The three shifts have slopes $1,2,3$. This exact configuration is part of the publisher's assertion: a result for these slopes should not automatically be described as a theorem for arbitrary slopes or other exponent triples.

## What is known

The protected Family 086 catalogue lists one source manuscript dated October 5, 2026: *An L³ bound for the trilinear Hilbert transform*.

The original 93-page manuscript, Theorem 1.1, first defines the **symmetric principal-value trilinear Hilbert transform for Schwartz inputs** with the fixed shifts $x-t$, $x-2t$, and $x-3t$, and claims a **strong output-norm bound** from

$$
L^3(\mathbb R)\times L^3(\mathbb R)\times L^3(\mathbb R)
\longrightarrow L^1(\mathbb R).
$$

The source states that this bound implies a **unique bounded trilinear extension** to all triples of $L^3$ inputs. It presents the result as settling the symmetric-exponent case of the standard conjecture for these **fixed slopes $1,2,3$**; it expressly says the full conjecture asks for a broader range of exponents.

A unique bounded extension to general $L^3$ inputs does **not by itself** assert that the symmetric principal-value truncations converge pointwise for every such input. The source theorem establishes the principal-value formula for Schwartz inputs and a bounded output in $L^1$; do not infer a separately proved maximal truncation estimate or pointwise convergence for arbitrary $L^3$ triples.
