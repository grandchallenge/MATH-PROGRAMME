# OAI-26 family 035: Threefold log abundance in numerical dimension one

## The question in plain language

A divisor is a way of recording geometric information on an algebraic variety. The *abundance conjecture* asks when a divisor that is numerically nonnegative can be used to construct a geometric map through its global sections. In this setting, a divisor is *nef* when it has nonnegative intersection with every curve, and *semiample* when some positive multiple has enough global sections to define a morphism.

Family 035 studies projective three-dimensional varieties in positive characteristic, in the special case when the relevant canonical or log-canonical divisor has *numerical dimension one*. It contains a theorem claimed for terminal threefolds and a broader theorem claimed for log canonical pairs.

## A small example

Consider a divisor on a threefold which intersects positively with a suitable square of an ample divisor, but whose square has zero intersection with that ample divisor. This is a numerical-dimension-one situation. Those intersection conditions constrain the divisor, but do not themselves produce global sections.

A semiampleness theorem bridges this gap: some positive Cartier multiple is globally generated, hence determines a map. For a log pair, the relevant divisor is the canonical divisor plus an effective boundary; the boundary can change the geometry substantially.

## What is known

**Log canonical generalization (October 5, 2026).** The pinned 16-page *Log abundance in numerical dimension one for threefolds in positive characteristic*, Theorem 1.1, claims: over an algebraically closed field of characteristic strictly greater than three, if a normal projective log canonical threefold pair has an effective rational boundary and its log-canonical adjoint is Q-Cartier, nef and of numerical dimension one, then that adjoint is semiample. It explicitly **does not** require terminality or Q-factoriality of the original threefold. The source allows arbitrary rational boundary coefficients consistent with log canonicity.

**Terminal predecessor (September 24, 2026).** The pinned 46-page *Abundance in numerical dimension one for terminal threefolds in positive characteristic*, Theorem 1.1, claims semiampleness for the nef canonical divisor of a normal projective Q-factorial **terminal** threefold over an algebraically closed field of characteristic greater than three, provided its numerical dimension is one. It further claims Kodaira dimension one and a corresponding morphism to a projective curve. The source says no special first Betti number, irregularity or Albanese hypothesis is required.

The later log canonical theorem is broader in singularities and boundary data. The two theorem statements must not be conflated, and neither asserts all-dimensional abundance or covers characteristic two or three.
