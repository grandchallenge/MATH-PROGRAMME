# OAI-26 family 083: Hilbert transforms along Lipschitz directions

**Status: source-grounded editorial exposition. NO_CERTIFICATION.**

## The question in plain language

The directional Hilbert transform integrates a function along a line whose orientation is determined by a unit vector field. In contrast to the constant-direction case, a variable direction field produces a singular integral that is not a translation-invariant Fourier multiplier. The question is whether Lipschitz control of the direction guarantees a uniform L2 estimate on sufficiently short segments.

## A small example

For a constant horizontal vector field, the operator is a copy of the ordinary one-dimensional Hilbert transform on each horizontal line, so rotation and Fubini give the familiar L2 bound. A field depending on both planar coordinates cannot be reduced in this way. A Lipschitz condition controls its turning but not automatically the interactions of its frequency components.

## What is known

At pinned source revision `openai/math@1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a`, the manuscript *A uniform Hilbert transform estimate for Lipschitz directions* states universal constants: every planar Lipschitz unit direction field with Lipschitz seminorm at most one has an L2 bound for each symmetric hard inner truncation at one fixed short outer length. Its direction is held fixed at the **output point**. The supremum over inner truncations is outside the L2 norm: it is an operator bound for each cutoff, **not** a maximal truncation operator assertion.

The introduction also states a symmetric principal-value estimate for Schwartz inputs, an L2 extension by density, and a weak-(2,2) level-set inequality. Scaling provides an outer length proportional to the reciprocal positive Lipschitz seminorm. Constant fields fall under the classical one-dimensional theory. No claim is made for arbitrary long outer lengths for variable fields.
