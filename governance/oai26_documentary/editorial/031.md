# OAI-26 family 031: Open homomorphisms of Galois groups

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. Mathematical status: NO_CERTIFICATION.** This exposition describes the pinned manuscript's theorem claims, not an independently verified proof.

## The question in plain language

A number field is a finite algebraic extension of the rational numbers. A larger algebraic field has symmetries that fix the smaller field; these symmetries form its *Galois group*. Remarkably, a sufficiently rich Galois group can reveal information about the field from which it came.

Uchida's conjecture asks whether a **continuous homomorphism with open image** between appropriate Galois groups must arise from an actual embedding between the underlying fields. Family 031's pinned paper claims the answer is yes, even when the homomorphism is not injective.

## A small example

A field embedding from one large field into another makes symmetries of the larger field act compatibly on the embedded smaller field. This creates a homomorphism of Galois groups **in the opposite direction** from the field embedding. The theorem claims the reverse reconstruction: from the group map alone, recover a unique compatible embedding.

For a general abstract group homomorphism such reconstruction need not hold. Continuity, openness and the arithmetic setting matter. “Open” means the image is open in the relevant profinite topology; it is not a claim that the map is injective.

## What is known

The pinned October 5, 2026 manuscript *Open Homomorphisms of Global Solvably Closed Galois Groups* states **Theorem 1.1** as follows in substance. Let the two base fields be number fields, and let each corresponding (possibly infinite) Galois extension be *solvably closed*. Every continuous open homomorphism between their Galois groups is induced by a **unique embedding of the extensions in the opposite direction**. The theorem explicitly imposes **no restriction on the kernel**.

The manuscript defines a solvably closed algebraic field as one with no nontrivial abelian algebraic extension. In its introduction, it says the result needs no separate cyclotomic-compatibility assumption: the proposed proof derives the necessary arithmetic compatibility from openness.

The introductory argument identifies earlier work by Uchida and a cyclotomic-character criterion due to Hoshi. It describes two principal routes toward its claimed result: deriving cyclotomic compatibility from partial local prime correspondences, and controlling exceptional primes through logarithmic-character and Kummer arguments. These descriptions are the source's proof plan, not independently verified theorems.
