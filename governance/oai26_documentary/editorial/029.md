# OAI-26 family 029: Primitive roots for admissible integer bases

**Editorial state: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. Mathematical status: NO_CERTIFICATION.** The two source manuscripts assert results about primitive roots; this document describes their stated theorems and does not certify their proofs.

## The question in plain language

Modulo a prime $p$, multiplying a nonzero number by itself repeatedly eventually gives one. The smallest positive exponent giving one is its *multiplicative order*. A number is a *primitive root* when this order is exactly $p-1$: its powers visit every nonzero residue modulo $p$.

Artin's primitive-root conjecture predicts that every integer other than $-1$ or a perfect square is a primitive root modulo infinitely many primes. The pinned Family 029 main paper claims the infinitude statement for every such admissible integer base.

## A small example

Modulo 7, the powers of 3 are 3, 2, 6, 4, 5 and 1, so 3 has order 6 and is a primitive root. By contrast, the powers of 2 return to 1 after three steps and do not cover every nonzero residue.

The conjecture concerns infinitely many *different primes* for a fixed base, not just one successful small prime. A square cannot generate the full multiplicative group modulo an odd prime, since its powers stay in the subgroup of squares; the number $-1$ has order at most two.

## What is known

**One integer base.** The October 4, 2026 manuscript *Primitive roots for every admissible integer base* (92 PDF pages), Theorem 1.1, claims that for each fixed admissible integer $a$ there are constants $c_a>0$ and $x_a$ such that, for all sufficiently large $x$, at least $c_a x/(\log x)^2$ primes in $(x,2x)$ have $a$ as a primitive root. The claimed lower bound implies infinitely many such primes; it is weaker than Artin's conjectured positive-density asymptotic.

**Simultaneous prime bases.** The second October 4 manuscript *Simultaneous primitive roots: a conditional lower bound for prime bases* (21 pages), Theorem 1.1, assumes **Inputs 3.1, 4.1, 5.1 and 5.2**: a Hecke zero-free assertion, a marked Type II estimate, a block sieve and a rough-number density estimate taken from the companion construction. Under those explicitly stated assumptions, every fixed finite set of distinct positive prime bases is claimed to be simultaneously primitive modulo at least $c x/(\log x)^2$ primes in $(x,2x)$ for all sufficiently large $x$. The second theorem is **conditional as stated in its own manuscript**; it must not be silently promoted to an independently unconditional result.

The simultaneous result is not the same as the main paper's assertion about a single arbitrary admissible integer. Its hypotheses, the fixed finite collection, and the constants depending on that collection matter.
