## The question in plain language

In algebraic geometry, a divisor is a way to encode geometric constraints on a variety. A *nef* divisor has nonnegative intersection with every algebraic curve, but this numerical positivity does not automatically produce a map from the variety.

Numerical semiampleness asks whether a divisor can be represented, up to numerical equivalence, by a semiample divisor—a divisor whose sufficiently large multiple has enough sections to define a base-point-free map. Family 036 claims such a result for certain canonical-type divisors, and separately reports the existence of minimal models or Mori fibre spaces for generalized log canonical pairs.

## A small example

For an algebraic variety $X$, the canonical divisor is written $K_X$. One often studies an *adjoint divisor* of the form

$$
K_X+B+M,
$$

where $B$ records a boundary and $M$ is another nef rational divisor. If the adjoint is nef, that says it intersects curves nonnegatively. To conclude **numerical semiampleness**, one needs a semiample divisor with the same numerical class.

This distinction matters: numerical equivalence is weaker than linear equivalence, and numerical semiampleness is not the same assertion as ordinary semiampleness of the original divisor.

For a generalized pair, a minimal model is an appropriately birationally simplified space with favorable canonical geometry. A Mori fibre space is a different outcome, generally associated with failure of pseudo-effectivity. These two alternatives must be kept distinct.

## What is known

The pinned source overview assigns four manuscripts to Family 036 and claims:

- Numerical semiampleness for nef adjoints $K_X+B+M$ when $K_X+B$ is pseudo-effective and $M$ is nef and rational, for **projective klt rational pairs** over algebraically closed fields of characteristic zero.
- A compact Kähler counterpart for **smooth compact Kähler rational klt simple-normal-crossing pairs**, formulated using Bott–Chern cohomology.
- For **projective generalized log canonical rational pairs** in characteristic zero, existence of minimal models when the relevant adjoint is pseudo-effective, and Mori fibre spaces otherwise, with nef b-data fixed.

The titles also identify a supporting study of minimal models in numerical dimension one. The overview does not give its precise hypotheses, so it should not be promoted into a universal low-dimensional theorem without reviewing that PDF.

The category of pair, characteristic assumption, nature of numerical equivalence, and distinction between projective algebraic and compact Kähler settings are all material qualifiers of the publisher's claims.
