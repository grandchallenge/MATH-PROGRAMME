# OAI-26 family 050: a counterexample to Griffiths' positivity conjecture

**Editorial status: SOURCE_CRITIC_COMPLETE; protected acceptance is determined by the admissions ledger. Mathematical status: NO_CERTIFICATION.** This article reports the source claim, not independent verification of the construction.

## The question in plain language

A vector bundle attaches a vector space to every point of a geometric space. An *ample* vector bundle satisfies an algebraic positivity condition. Griffiths' positivity conjecture proposed that such positivity should be expressible through a smooth Hermitian metric whose curvature is strictly positive in the Griffiths sense.

The pinned Family 050 overview claims counterexamples: ample rank-two holomorphic vector bundles on the quadric surface that admit no smooth Hermitian metric with strictly Griffiths-positive curvature. The assertion separates algebraic positivity from this particular differential-geometric notion of positivity.

## A small example

For a complex vector bundle, a Hermitian metric measures lengths and angles in each fiber. Its curvature describes how these measurements vary over the base. *Griffiths positivity* requires a strict positive curvature inequality when evaluated against nonzero tangent directions and nonzero bundle vectors.

Ample line bundles admit positive-curvature metrics in the familiar projective setting. A rank-two bundle has more directions to compare, so extending intuition directly from line bundles can be misleading.

The base in the indexed counterexample is

$$
\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1,
$$

the product of two complex projective lines, also called the quadric surface. The claim is therefore not confined to a high-dimensional or nonprojective base.

## What is known

The protected Family 050 catalogue lists one manuscript, *Ample rank-two bundles on the quadric surface without Griffiths-positive metrics* (September 24, 2026).

The pinned primary PDF, Theorem 1.1, constructs one rank-two algebraic bundle $G$ on $S=\mathbb{P}^1\times\mathbb{P}^1$. For every positive integer $m$, the power-map pullback $E_m=f_m^*G\otimes\mathcal{O}(1,1)$ is **ample**; there exists a threshold $m_0$ such that **for every $m\geq m_0$**, $E_m$ admits **no smooth Hermitian metric with strictly Griffiths-positive curvature**. The source explicitly notes that $m_0$ is ineffective. This produces counterexamples in rank two on the quadric surface.

This is an existence statement: it does not say all ample rank-two bundles fail Griffiths positivity. Nor does the absence of a strictly Griffiths-positive metric automatically rule out every weaker metric positivity condition.

The exact bundle construction, proof of ampleness, and obstruction to positive curvature require direct verification from the pinned manuscript rather than inference from the overview.
