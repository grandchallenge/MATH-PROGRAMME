# OAI-26 family 037: The ordinary-double-point volume gap

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. Mathematical status: NO_CERTIFICATION.** This article reports the claim in the pinned publisher overview, not an independently audited proof.

## The question in plain language

An algebraic variety can have a singular point where its local geometry differs from smooth space. A numerical invariant called *normalized volume* measures aspects of the severity of a singularity. The ordinary-double-point volume-gap question asks for the largest normalized volume possible among singular klt germs of a fixed dimension, and whether equality characterizes a particular singularity.

The pinned OAI-26 publisher overview attributes a sharp bound and its equality classification to the manuscripts in Family 037.

## A small example

An *ordinary double point* can be modeled locally by a nondegenerate quadratic equation. It is a standard kind of singularity that serves as a comparison object in higher-dimensional algebraic geometry.

The claimed normalized-volume threshold for complex singular klt germs of dimension n at least two, without boundary, is twice the nth power of n minus one. In dimension two this expression equals 2; in dimension four it equals 54. These numerical substitutions illustrate the formula but are not evidence of the proposed bound.

The distinction between bounding normalized volume and identifying equality cases matters: a sharp numerical upper bound alone does not show that only ordinary double points attain it.

## What is known

The exact OAI-26 publisher overview states that every **singular complex algebraic klt germ** of dimension at least two, with **zero boundary**, has normalized volume at most **2 times (n minus 1) to the nth power**. It further asserts that equality holds precisely for an **analytic ordinary double point**. These are the source's claims, not independently established conclusions of this editorial exercise.

The family contains two September 24, 2026 manuscripts: *The ordinary-double-point gap in every dimension* and *The normalized-volume gap in dimension four*. The overview presents the all-dimensional inequality as the family-level result. The dimension-four manuscript must not be silently treated as a proof of all dimensions without checking the argument and dependencies.

The hypothesis of a **singular** germ is essential. The overview does not claim that the same upper bound applies to nonsingular germs, nor does it state an analogous result with arbitrary boundary divisors.
