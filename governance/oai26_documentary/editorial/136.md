**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** This article records the pinned publisher's claimed theorem, not an independently verified reduction.

## The question in plain language

PPAD is a complexity class of search problems in which solutions are guaranteed by a combinatorial parity argument. Its standard representative, End-of-Line, asks for an endpoint of an implicitly represented directed graph whose vertices have at most one predecessor and one successor, aside from a specified starting point.

A probabilistically checkable proof (PCP) lets a verifier assess a large mathematical claim by inspecting only limited local information. Family 136 claims a PCP-like robust local-verification formulation of PPAD search, with a nearly linear increase in the size of the instance.

## A small example

Imagine a directed path through a very large network. Rather than list every vertex, compact circuits describe each vertex's successor and predecessor. Knowing one path start ensures another endpoint exists, but locating that endpoint may still be computationally difficult.

In a local-constraint formulation, variables describe a purported solution to a transformed problem. The claimed robustness says even an assignment that violates a small, fixed fraction of approximate numerical constraints can be converted to a genuine endpoint of the original path problem. Merely satisfying most constraints is not automatically sufficient for arbitrary reductions.

## What is known

The protected Family 136 catalogue lists one manuscript, *The PCP-for-PPAD conjecture: a quasilinear reduction*.

The pinned publisher overview claims a reduction from an End-of-Line instance of encoded length $N$ to **numerical circuit constraints** of total size

$$
N(\log N)^{O(1)}.
$$

The source claims that **any polynomially encoded rational assignment** satisfying all but a **fixed fraction** of the constraints to **fixed numerical accuracy** yields an End-of-Line endpoint solution. The overview also says such assignments **always exist**. These are distinct completeness and robustness claims: the transformed instance is satisfiable in the relevant approximate sense, and sufficiently good approximate assignments permit solution extraction.

The result is described by the publisher as a resolution of the **quasilinear PCP-for-PPAD conjecture**. Its scope is a search-problem reduction; it should not be interpreted as a proof that PPAD problems are NP-hard or as an ordinary NP-PCP theorem.
