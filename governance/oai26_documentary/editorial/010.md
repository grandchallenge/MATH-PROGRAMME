## The question in plain language

A Galois representation encodes how arithmetic symmetries act on a vector space. A modularity theorem says that some of these representations can be obtained from modular forms or their associated Hecke algebras, which are built from highly structured arithmetic functions.

The prime two is particularly delicate: several arguments that work with odd-prime residual representations can require extra assumptions at two. The OAI-26 Family 010 manuscripts claim results designed to remove residual restrictions in a two-adic setting.

Two conclusions must be distinguished. *Pro-modularity* places a continuous representation in an appropriate completed Hecke-algebra setting. *Classical modularity* identifies certain geometrically constrained representations with those arising from classical modular forms. The first description cannot automatically be substituted for the second.

## A small example

Think of a representation as a rule assigning a matrix to each symmetry while preserving composition. A two-dimensional representation assigns two-by-two matrices. Knowing its matrices modulo powers of two is like knowing a sequence of increasingly fine arithmetic approximations.

A completed Hecke algebra can organize compatible systems of such approximations, rather than a single classical eigenform at a fixed finite stage. In this analogy, being represented somewhere in the completed system is a different assertion from coming from one classical eigenform.

The distinction matters especially when the claim includes every continuous, odd, absolutely irreducible representation subject to finite ramification, rather than only a smaller class with familiar residual properties.

## What is known

- *The Dimension of the Two-Adic Hecke Algebra at Odd Level* (October 5, 2026).
- *Fontaine–Mazur modularity at the prime 2* (October 6, 2026).

Its first claim is that every continuous, odd, absolutely irreducible, two-dimensional two-adic representation of the absolute Galois group of the rational numbers, unramified outside finitely many primes, occurs in a completed Hecke algebra at some odd tame level.

Its second claim concerns irreducible odd representations satisfying the same ramification condition and additionally being de Rham at two with distinct Hodge–Tate weights. For these, it claims classical modularity up to a Tate twist, without residual restrictions.
