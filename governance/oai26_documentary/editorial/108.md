# OAI-26 family 108: a claimed cubic lower bound for border determinantal complexity

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** This source-pinned account describes the publisher's claim and does not independently verify its proof.

## The question in plain language

The determinant and permanent both combine products of entries of a square matrix. The determinant attaches alternating signs to these products; the permanent does not. The determinant is efficiently computable, whereas computing a permanent is a central counting-complexity problem.

*Determinantal complexity* asks how large a matrix of affine-linear forms must be before its determinant can equal the permanent polynomial. *Border* determinantal complexity also permits limits of such determinant polynomials. Family 108 claims that even with this greater freedom, an $n\times n$ permanent requires a determinant matrix with size at least cubic in $n$.

## A small example

For the matrix with first row $a,b$ and second row $c,d$, its determinant is $ad-bc$, whereas its permanent is $ad+bc$. Affine-linear substitutions inside larger determinants can generate polynomials with quite different appearances.

A border representation is more permissive: take determinants of affine-linear matrices in a sequence and ask whether their polynomial coefficients approach those of the permanent. An argument ruling out exact formulas may fail to rule out these limits. The claimed theorem covers the stronger border setting.

## What is known

The protected Family 108 catalogue lists one manuscript, *A cubic lower bound for border determinantal complexity of the permanent*.

The pinned publisher overview claims, over $\mathbb C$, that

$$
\overline{\mathrm{dc}}(\mathrm{perm}_n)\geq c n^3
$$

for an absolute $c>0$ and every sufficiently large integer $n$. Here the left-hand quantity is the smallest determinant matrix size when coefficientwise limits of determinants of matrices of affine-linear forms are permitted.

An exact representation is a special case of a border representation. Therefore any valid border lower bound also applies to exact determinantal complexity. The reverse inference does not follow.

The claimed cubic bound is not an exponential circuit lower bound, not a proof of $\mathsf P\ne\mathsf{NP}$, and not automatically valid over every field or for unrestricted arithmetic circuit models.
