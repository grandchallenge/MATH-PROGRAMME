# OAI-26 family 090: universal optimality of the triangular lattice

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** This article documents four publisher-indexed manuscripts, not independently verified proofs.

## The question in plain language

How should points be arranged in the plane to minimize their mutual repulsion? The triangular lattice—the arrangement in which each point has six nearest neighbors—often gives remarkably efficient configurations. Universal optimality asks whether it minimizes energy not merely for one interaction law but for a broad family of repulsive potentials.

The pinned OAI-26 overview claims such an optimality theorem for certain planar configurations, along with renormalized Riesz and Coulomb energy results and a consequence for logarithmic energy of points on the sphere.

## A small example

Think of points as identical particles that repel one another. A potential assigns an interaction cost according to distance, and the total energy sums the costs for pairs. A triangular array spreads neighbors evenly in six directions.

A *completely monotone potential of squared distance* belongs to a special class of interaction functions with alternating derivative signs. It is much broader than one particular inverse-power law, but it is not the class of every conceivable interaction.

For infinite point configurations, the raw sum can diverge. One therefore studies an energy **per particle** or a **renormalized energy** after subtracting a background contribution. The exact normalization changes what a minimization claim means.

## What is known

The source-locked catalogue identifies four Family 090 manuscripts covering an atomic certificate, universal triangular-lattice optimality, a planar Fourier circle-packing certificate, and planar Coulomb renormalized energy.

The pinned publisher overview claims that the triangular lattice minimizes the **lower limit of energy per particle** for **every nonnegative completely monotone potential of squared distance**, among **locally finite planar point configurations** of **centered-disk density one**.

It additionally claims minimality for **unit-background renormalized Riesz energies** in the range $0<s<2$ and for **Coulomb energy**. The overview presents these as resolving the Sandier–Serfaty conjecture and the two-dimensional Brauchart–Hardin–Saff conjecture concerning the linear term in optimal spherical logarithmic energy.

These are separate energy and asymptotic statements. The universal-optimality claim must not be extended to arbitrary signed potentials, arbitrary density notions or unqualified infinite sums; the spherical conclusion requires its own transfer argument.
