# OAI-26 family 342: Taming and compatibility in four-dimensional geometry

## The question in plain language

In symplectic geometry, a symplectic form can *tame* an almost-complex structure when it evaluates positively on the pair consisting of a nonzero direction and its image under that structure. Compatibility asks for something stronger: an invariant symplectic pairing that also defines a positive metric. The indexed manuscript addresses when taming can lead to compatibility in dimension four. A local example is easy; the claimed global implication is much harder.

## A small example

On ordinary four-dimensional space, identify coordinates with two copies of the complex plane. Let $J$ rotate each $(x_j,y_j)$ coordinate plane by a quarter-turn, and take

$$
\omega_0=dx_1\wedge dy_1+dx_2\wedge dy_2.
$$

For nonzero $v$ we have $\omega_0(v,Jv)=\lVert v\rVert^2>0$, and $\omega_0(Jv,Jw)=\omega_0(v,w)$. This is a concrete compatible (hence taming) example. It does **not** prove that arbitrary taming structures are compatible or deformable to compatible ones on a curved four-manifold.
