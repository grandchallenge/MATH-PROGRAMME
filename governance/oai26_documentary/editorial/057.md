## The question in plain language

The fundamental group records how loops in a space can be combined and deformed. Campana's notion of a *special* complex variety is intended to describe spaces that lack certain forms of geometric general type. Family 057 asks how restrictive this specialness is for fundamental groups and their linear representations.

A group is *virtually abelian* if some finite-index subgroup is abelian: its elements then commute. A group is *virtually nilpotent of class at most two* if a finite-index subgroup has commutators that commute with everything, which is weaker than being abelian.

## A small example

The integers under addition form an abelian group. A finite extension of an abelian group need not itself be abelian, but is virtually abelian. Likewise, a two-step nilpotent group can have nontrivial commutators even though those commutators are central. Thus the two conclusions advertised for compact Kähler manifolds and quasi-projective representations must not be conflated.

## What is known

The pinned OAI-26 overview reports three related results:

- **Compact Kähler:** It claims that every special compact Kähler manifold has virtually abelian fundamental group, described as a proof of Campana's abelianity conjecture.
- **Smooth quasi-projective:** It claims that for each smooth special complex quasi-projective variety, every finite-dimensional complex linear representation of its fundamental group has virtually nilpotent image of class at most two. This is a statement about *representation images*, not necessarily the entire fundamental group.
- **Root orbifolds:** It claims virtually abelian fundamental groups for order-two root orbifolds of smooth projective complex fourfolds along one nonempty smooth connected divisor, when special according to the stated differential-line criterion. The catalogue calls the associated third manuscript a **conditional** abelianity theorem. Its exact hypotheses and dependencies must be checked before calling this an unconditional independent result.
