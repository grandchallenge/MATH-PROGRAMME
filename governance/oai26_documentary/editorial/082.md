## The question in plain language

The Hilbert transform is a classical singular integral: it measures how a function behaves by averaging with a kernel that changes sign and becomes singular. The *triangular Hilbert transform* couples three functions along a triangular pattern, making its cancellation much harder to control.

Family 082 reports bounds at the symmetric exponent three, including a *maximal* bound that controls multiple choices of truncation and a *variation* bound that measures how much truncated expressions fluctuate as the truncation scale changes.

## A small example

A singular integral is often first defined by excluding a tiny neighborhood of its singularity. If the excluded neighborhood has size $\varepsilon$, the resulting truncated expression may depend on $\varepsilon$. Showing a bound for each fixed truncation does not automatically show a bound for the *supremum* over all truncations.

A variation estimate is stronger in a different direction: it controls accumulated changes of an expression along an ordered sequence of scales. The exponent $r>2$ governs how these changes are measured; the publisher's claim does not include the endpoint $r=2$.

The asserted operator estimate takes two inputs in $L^3(\mathbb R^2)$ and produces an output controlled in $L^{3/2}(\mathbb R^2)$. Pairing that output with a third $L^3$ function yields a scalar trilinear estimate at the symmetric point.

## What is known

The pinned publisher overview claims maximal and annular $r$-variation bounds for **every $r>2$**, mapping

$$
L^3(\mathbb R^2)\times L^3(\mathbb R^2)\longrightarrow L^{3/2}(\mathbb R^2).
$$

It states that the maximal estimate controls **both hard-truncation endpoints** and implies almost-everywhere as well as norm convergence. Upon pairing with a third input, the claimed bound addresses the triangular Hilbert transform trilinear form at the symmetric $L^3$ exponent.

The distinction between maximal, dyadic, and annular variation estimates is important. None should be automatically identified with an unrestricted bound at every other exponent triple or at the excluded endpoint $r=2$.
