# OAI-26 family 082: maximal and variational triangular Hilbert transform bounds

**Editorial status: SOURCE_CRITIC_COMPLETE; protected acceptance is determined by the admissions ledger. Mathematical status: NO_CERTIFICATION.** This article summarizes three pinned manuscript claims without independently verifying their estimates.

## The question in plain language

The Hilbert transform is a classical singular integral: it measures how a function behaves by averaging with a kernel that changes sign and becomes singular. The *triangular Hilbert transform* couples three functions along a triangular pattern, making its cancellation much harder to control.

Family 082 reports bounds at the symmetric exponent three, including a *maximal* bound that controls multiple choices of truncation and a *variation* bound that measures how much truncated expressions fluctuate as the truncation scale changes.

## A small example

A singular integral is often first defined by excluding a tiny neighborhood of its singularity. If the excluded neighborhood has size $\varepsilon$, the resulting truncated expression may depend on $\varepsilon$. Showing a bound for each fixed truncation does not automatically show a bound for the *supremum* over all truncations.

A variation estimate is stronger in a different direction: it controls accumulated changes of an expression along an ordered sequence of scales. The exponent $r>2$ governs how these changes are measured; the publisher's claim does not include the endpoint $r=2$.

The asserted operator estimate takes two inputs in $L^3(\mathbb R^2)$ and produces an output controlled in $L^{3/2}(\mathbb R^2)$. Pairing that output with a third $L^3$ function yields a scalar trilinear estimate at the symmetric point.

## What is known

The protected catalogue identifies three manuscripts: *Annular variation of the triangular Hilbert transform at the symmetric point* (October 5, 2026), *An L³ bound for the dyadic triangular Hilbert form* (October 5, 2026), and *The maximal triangular Hilbert transform at the symmetric point* (September 24, 2026).

The annular-variation paper, Theorem 1.1, claims that for **every real $r>2$**, complex inputs satisfy a pointwise variation bound of order $r$ with output-dependent partitions of arbitrary positive radii, mapping

$$
L^3(\mathbb R^2)\times L^3(\mathbb R^2)\longrightarrow L^{3/2}(\mathbb R^2).
$$

The 37-page annular paper uses rational partition endpoints solely for measurability; endpoint continuity gives the full supremum, with no limit on how many endpoints occur in one dyadic scale interval. Its Theorem 1.1 implies a **two-hard-endpoint maximal bound** and joint almost-everywhere and norm principal values. Independently, the 27-page maximal paper, Theorem 1.1, states the complex-input two-endpoint maximal inequality directly and establishes joint convergence. Pairing the bilinear output with a third $L^3$ input gives the scalar symmetric triangular form.

The 11-page dyadic paper gives a **separate model theorem for real inputs**: Theorem 1.1 bounds the sum of absolute local contributions over any finite set of dyadic scales by **40 times the product of three $L^3$ norms**. It consequently allows independently varying coefficients of modulus at most one for admissible local interval triples. This dyadic estimate is not itself the continuous complex-input maximal or variation theorem. None of the manuscripts claims the excluded variation endpoint $r=2$ or other exponent triples.
