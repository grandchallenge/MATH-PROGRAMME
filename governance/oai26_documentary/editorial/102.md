## The question in plain language

Some optimization problems are easy to state but hard to solve exactly. Approximation algorithms instead seek solutions close to the best possible value. A *hardness threshold* identifies how close an efficient algorithm could get before a standard complexity assumption would imply that no such algorithm exists.

The Unique Games Conjecture (UGC) concerns a special family of constraint-satisfaction problems in which each edge constraint specifies a permutation of possible labels. It has long served as a conditional foundation for many proposed sharp approximation thresholds. Family 102 claims a resolution of the conjecture and separately claims direct NP-hardness results for four graph problems.

## A small example

In Max-Cut, divide a graph's vertices into two groups to maximize the number of edges crossing between them. A random partition cuts about half the edges on average; sophisticated algorithms achieve a stronger guaranteed ratio. The Goemans–Williamson semidefinite-programming algorithm has an approximation factor approximately $0.87856$.

To say this ratio is optimal under NP-hardness assumptions is different from exhibiting the approximation algorithm. It requires an appropriate reduction proving that any polynomial-time algorithm achieving a better guaranteed factor would also solve NP-hard instances beyond the allowed gap. The distinction matters further because a *conditional UGC implication* and an *independent direct reduction* are different mathematical statements.

## What is known

The protected Family 102 catalogue identifies five manuscripts: a Unique Games theorem and direct hardness constructions for Max-Cut, Vertex Cover, Min-UnCut, and directed feedback vertex set.

The pinned publisher overview **claims a proof of Khot's Unique Games Conjecture**. It separately says that direct reductions, based on established PCP and Label Cover hardness, establish these NP-hardness boundaries **on unweighted graphs**:

- **Max-Cut:** approximation better than the Goemans–Williamson ratio.
- **Vertex Cover:** approximation better than factor two.
- **Min-UnCut:** approximation within any fixed constant factor.
- **Directed feedback vertex set:** approximation within any fixed constant factor.

The direction of a ratio depends on whether a problem is being maximized or minimized. Max-Cut seeks a larger objective; the other three seek smaller objectives, so the threshold descriptions must not be interchanged. The overview distinguishes the direct reductions from any dependence on the claimed UGC theorem; it does not establish here that every consequence often associated with UGC follows from the five manuscripts.
