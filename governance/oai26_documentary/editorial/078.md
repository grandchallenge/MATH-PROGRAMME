# OAI-26 family 078: the three-dimensional Bochner–Riesz conjecture

**Editorial status: SOURCE_CRITIC_COMPLETE; protected acceptance is determined by the admissions ledger. Mathematical status: NO_CERTIFICATION.** This article describes a theorem claimed in a pinned paper without independently verifying its proof.

## The question in plain language

Fourier analysis decomposes a function into frequencies. The Bochner–Riesz operator reconstructs it using frequencies inside a ball while gradually tapering the contribution of frequencies near the ball's boundary. The fundamental question is when this reconstruction acts as a bounded operator on the space of functions whose absolute values have an integrable power of order $p$.

Family 078 reports a solution to this boundedness problem in three-dimensional Euclidean space, with a precise threshold for the degree of tapering. The strict inequality at the threshold is essential.

## A small example

For a function on three-dimensional space, a Bochner–Riesz multiplier of order $\delta$ weights frequency $\xi$ by

$$
(1-|\xi|^2)_+^\delta,
$$

where the positive part sets the weight to zero outside the unit frequency ball. Larger $\delta$ makes the transition at the boundary smoother; the challenge is controlling the resulting operator on $L^p$.

The publisher's claimed threshold is

$$
\delta>\max\left\{3\left|\frac1p-\frac12\right|-\frac12,\ 0\right\}.
$$

For example, at $p=2$, any positive $\delta$ satisfies this sufficient condition. At $p=1$ or $p=\infty$, the stated sufficient condition requires $\delta>1$. These examples illustrate the formula but are not independent verification of its sharpness.

## What is known

The protected catalogue lists one Family 078 manuscript, *Bochner–Riesz multipliers in three dimensions* (September 24, 2026).

The primary PDF, Theorem 1.1, claims that the multiplier $T_\delta$ is bounded on $L^3(\mathbb{R}^3)$ for **every positive fixed order** $\delta$. The paper then states that complex-order interpolation and duality (see Corollary 12.4) imply that, for **every $1\le p\le\infty$**, the multiplier extends boundedly on $L^p(\mathbb{R}^3)$ whenever

$$
\delta>\max\left\{3\left|\frac1p-\frac12\right|-\frac12,\ 0\right\}.
$$

The Fourier multiplier is $\left(1-|\xi|^2\right)_+^\delta$, using the Fourier transform with phase $e^{-2\pi i x\cdot\xi}$. The order is fixed before the constant is chosen; the operator bound may depend on $p$ and $\delta$. Scaling gives uniform bounds for the associated multipliers at radius $R$ for every $R>0$. The paper asserts boundedness only in the **strict range**, not equality at the threshold and not higher-dimensional analogues.

The broader notion of a Bochner–Riesz conjecture includes endpoint and dimension questions; the indexed claim must not be silently enlarged to settle them.
