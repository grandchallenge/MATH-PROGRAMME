# OAI-26 family 026: positive lower density of large prime gaps

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** This source-qualified article describes a pinned manuscript claim, not a checked proof.

## The question in plain language

Prime numbers become less frequent as integers grow. Near a large prime \(p\), the typical separation between neighboring primes is on the order of \(\log p\). Nevertheless, some gaps are far larger than that typical scale.

Family 026 asks for more than the existence of occasional exceptionally large gaps. The pinned OAI-26 synopsis claims that, for any fixed multiplier \(C>0\), gaps larger than \(C\log p\) occur with **positive lower density** among consecutive prime gaps. This means they remain a nonvanishing proportion along every sufficiently large initial segment, rather than appearing only along a sparse sequence of favorable cutoffs.

## A small example

Let \(p_n\) denote the \(n\)-th prime and define its following gap by

$$
g_n=p_{n+1}-p_n.
$$

The statement that \(g_n>C\log p_n\) for some indices is weaker than saying that such gaps account for a positive proportion of all indices up to \(N\). In the stronger assertion, for each fixed \(C\), there is a positive constant \(\delta_C\) such that sufficiently large \(N\) satisfy

$$
\#\{n\leq N:g_n>C\log p_n\}\geq\delta_C N.
$$

The constant may depend on \(C\). Nothing in this formulation asserts that a single proportion works uniformly as \(C\) grows, or that the proportion has a limit.

The pinned overview also gives a consequence for the ratio \(p_n/n\). This ratio increases precisely when

$$
\frac{p_{n+1}}{n+1}>\frac{p_n}{n}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p_{n+1}-p_n>\frac{p_n}{n}.
$$

The equivalence is elementary algebra; proving that it occurs with positive lower density still requires quantitative control of the prime gaps and the asymptotic size of \(p_n/n\).

## What is known

The source-locked catalogue names one Family 026 manuscript, *Positive lower density of large prime gaps* (September 25, 2026).

The pinned publisher overview claims that **for every fixed positive \(C\)**, a positive proportion of consecutive prime gaps exceed \(C\log p_n\) throughout all sufficiently large prime initial segments. The proportion may depend on \(C\).

It further reports that the indices at which the ratio \(p_n/n\) increases have positive lower density, answering a question attributed to Erdős and Prachar. The second statement is not the assertion that this ratio is eventually increasing; it concerns a positive proportion of individual increases.

These are reported manuscript results. Exact quantifier placement, the nature of any error estimates, and the derivation of the corollary must be checked against the pinned PDF.
