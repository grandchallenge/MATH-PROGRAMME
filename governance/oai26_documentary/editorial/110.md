# OAI-26 family 110: randomized k-server on arbitrary metric spaces

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** This account reports source-pinned publisher claims, not an independently checked competitive-analysis proof.

## The question in plain language

Imagine maintaining $k$ movable servers that must respond to a stream of requests at points of a metric space. Whenever a request arrives, a server must be placed at its location, and moving a server costs the distance traveled. An online algorithm must decide without knowing future requests, while an offline benchmark knows the entire sequence.

The *competitive ratio* measures how much more movement the online policy may incur than the optimum offline solution. Family 110 claims a randomized policy with squared-logarithmic dependence on $k$ for **arbitrary metric spaces**, including infinite and unbounded ones.

## A small example

With two servers on a line, a request arriving far from both servers forces a choice about which server to move. Moving the nearest server is locally appealing but can be costly if later requests concentrate near the server's former location.

For a randomized policy, one evaluates expected total movement over the algorithm's private random choices. An **oblivious** request sequence is chosen independently of those random choices. This is distinct from a fully adaptive adversary allowed to select new requests after observing the policy's random outcomes.

## What is known

The protected Family 110 catalogue identifies two manuscripts: *Squared-logarithmic randomized k-server on arbitrary metrics* and *Uniform computation of the squared-logarithmic k-server bound*.

The pinned publisher overview claims a randomized competitive guarantee of order

$$
O(\log^2(k+1))
$$

for every metric space and every finite oblivious request sequence, matching the stated worst-case lower-bound order. The guarantee is described as coming from **one policy**, not one separately tailored to each request sequence. The claimed scope extends to infinite, unbounded metric spaces.

The companion manuscript addresses constructive computation on **finite rational metrics**. The overview asserts polynomial preprocessing and per-request bit cost in the input length and $\log(t+1)$ at request index $t$, with a finite *instance-dependent additive movement constant*. Such an additive allowance is not itself part of the multiplicative competitive ratio and must be retained in any faithful statement.

Neither claim automatically covers adaptive adversaries, arbitrary real-number metric encodings in a finite Turing-machine model, or finite worst-case movement for unbounded request sequences without the stated competitive comparison.
