# OAI-26 family 124: polynomial-time three-machine precedence scheduling

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** This documentary article describes a claim in the pinned publisher overview, not an independently validated scheduling algorithm.

## The question in plain language

A collection of jobs must be scheduled across three identical machines. Every job takes one unit of time, can run on only one machine at a time, and cannot be interrupted. Certain jobs must finish before others can begin. The goal is to finish everything as early as possible, minimizing the *makespan*.

Family 124 claims a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm for this three-machine precedence-constrained unit-job problem, resolving a scheduling question attributed to Garey and Johnson.

## A small example

Consider five unit-length jobs: $A$ and $B$ must finish before $D$ starts, while $C$ must finish before $E$. With three identical machines, $A,B,C$ may occupy the first time slot, followed by $D,E$ in the second. A two-slot schedule meets all precedence constraints.

On a larger instance, choosing which available jobs to run now affects which successors become eligible later. Simply filling idle machines greedily does not by itself prove an optimal schedule.

## What is known

The protected Family 124 catalogue identifies one manuscript, *A Polynomial-Time Algorithm for Three-Machine Unit-Job Scheduling*.

The pinned publisher overview claims that for **three identical parallel machines**, **nonpreemptive unit-length jobs**, and **arbitrary precedence constraints**, there is a **deterministic polynomial-time algorithm** that minimizes makespan.

For an **explicitly given precedence graph**, the overview separately claims that the algorithm determines whether a deadline is feasible and constructs a feasible schedule when one exists. Deadline feasibility and minimum makespan are related but distinct output specifications; the conversion between them must be verified, including its time horizon and input encoding.

The claim does not automatically extend to variable job lengths, more than three machines, unrelated machines, release dates, or additional resource constraints. The number three and unit processing times are structural assumptions, not illustrative details.
