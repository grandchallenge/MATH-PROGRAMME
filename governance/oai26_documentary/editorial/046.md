# OAI-26 family 046: counterexamples to Shafarevich holomorphic convexity

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** This article describes two publisher-indexed constructions; it does not independently verify their proofs.

## The question in plain language

A complex projective variety may have an infinite universal covering space. Shafarevich's holomorphic-convexity question asks whether universal covers of certain projective varieties must have a strong complex-analytic finiteness property called holomorphic convexity. A stronger property, being Stein, supplies abundant holomorphic functions and has particularly useful analytic consequences.

The pinned OAI-26 overview reports counterexamples. It describes both a complex surface refuting an unrestricted holomorphic-convexity assertion and a projective fourfold with a large fundamental group whose universal cover has no positive-dimensional compact analytic subvarieties but is neither Stein nor holomorphically convex.

## A small example

For an ordinary Euclidean space, an infinite sequence escaping every compact set can be detected by coordinate functions growing large along a suitable subsequence. Holomorphic convexity makes an analogous demand using holomorphic functions, but on a general complex space such functions may be too scarce to detect all escaping sequences.

A Stein manifold is holomorphically convex, but the converse need not hold in general. Consequently, **non-Stein** alone does not establish **non-holomorphic-convexity**. The publisher's fourfold claim is stronger because it asserts both failures separately.

Likewise, having no compact analytic subvarieties of positive dimension does not by itself imply Steinness. The proposed construction is intended to separate these two features.

It additionally reports an independent **smooth projective complex surface** counterexample to the unrestricted Shafarevich holomorphic-convexity claim.

The word *unrestricted* is significant. An example that disproves a conjecture without auxiliary hypotheses need not contradict a stronger or modified theorem incorporating additional assumptions. The exact definition of “large fundamental group,” the covering-space structure, and the relation of the two constructions require direct examination of the PDFs.
