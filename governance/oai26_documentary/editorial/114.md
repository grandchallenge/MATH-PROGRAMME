# OAI-26 family 114: approximate counting of common polymatroid bases

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** This article documents source-pinned publisher claims, not independently verified proofs of randomized approximation schemes.

## The question in plain language

A matroid describes which subsets of a finite ground set count as independent, generalizing independence in vector spaces and forests in graphs. Two matroids on the same ground set may impose different independence constraints. Counting the subsets satisfying both can be much harder than finding just one.

An integral polymatroid is a related structure with nonnegative **integer vectors** instead of only zero-or-one subsets. Family 114 claims efficient randomized approximation schemes for counting vectors that are bases of two integral polymatroids, even when their capacities are written in binary.

## A small example

Suppose three kinds of resource can be selected in integer quantities $x_1,x_2,x_3$. One polymatroid limits the aggregate amount available to each subset of resources; a second represents independent constraints. A *common integer base* satisfies both families of upper bounds and reaches each total-rank equality.

Counting means counting each admissible integer vector **once**, regardless of how many exchanges or descriptions produce it. A binary-encoded capacity such as $2^{100}$ uses only about a hundred input bits; an algorithm that takes time polynomial in the numerical value of that capacity is therefore not polynomial in the encoded input length.

## What is known

The protected Family 114 catalogue lists two manuscripts: *An FPRAS for Common Integer Polymatroid Bases with Binary Capacities* and *Approximate counting of common bases of two matroids*.

The pinned publisher overview claims a **fully polynomial randomized approximation scheme** (FPRAS) for the number of common integer bases of **two integral polymatroids with equal total rank**, accessed through **exact rank-value oracles**. The capacities are binary-encoded. The overview further specifies that **both oracle-call counts and bit operations outside the oracles are polynomial on every execution**, rather than only in expectation.

An FPRAS for a nonzero counting quantity $Z$ normally returns an estimate within multiplicative factors $1\pm\varepsilon$, with success probability at least a fixed constant that can be amplified; running time must be polynomial in the encoded instance size and $1/\varepsilon$. The exact zero-instance handling must be checked in the manuscript rather than inferred from this general definition.

For matroids accessed through **independence oracles**, the publisher overview also claims approximate counts of common independent sets of **specified size, unrestricted size, and maximum possible size**, even if the two matroid ranks differ. This is a distinct input and oracle model from integer polymatroid bases; the equal-total-rank condition must not be accidentally imposed on the matroid corollaries.
