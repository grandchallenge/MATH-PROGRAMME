# OAI-26 family 134: generalized star height at most three

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** This is a source-pinned account of the publisher's theorem claim, not an independent verification.

## The question in plain language

A regular expression describes a language of finite strings. The Kleene star means “repeat a pattern any number of times,” while union and concatenation combine patterns. When expressions can additionally take complements, a longstanding question asks whether arbitrarily many nested stars are truly necessary to describe some regular languages.

Family 134 claims that **three nested stars always suffice** for every regular language over any finite alphabet.

## A small example

For alphabet $\Sigma=\{a,b\}$, the expression $(ab)^*$ describes zero or more copies of the block $ab$ and has star nesting depth one. The complement $\Sigma^*\setminus(ab)^*$ describes all remaining strings; complement is allowed in the *generalized* regular-expression model.

Star height measures the **maximum number of stars nested along a syntax-tree path**, not how many stars appear anywhere in the expression. For example, $(a^*b)^*$ has nesting depth two. Equivalent expressions can have different heights, so the generalized star height of a language minimizes this depth across *all* equivalent generalized expressions.

## What is known

The protected Family 134 catalogue lists three manuscripts: *Finite Monoid Computations and a Uniform Generalized Star-Height Bound*, *Generalized Star Height at Most Four*, and *Generalized Star Height at Most Three*. These titles indicate successive proposed bounds, but their proof dependencies and revisions must be verified from the primary texts.

The pinned publisher overview claims that for **every regular language** $L\subseteq\Sigma^*$ over a **finite alphabet** there exists a generalized regular expression $E$ satisfying

$$
L(E)=L,\qquad \mathrm{sh}(E)\le 3.
$$

The permitted operations are **union, concatenation and complement over the same alphabet**, in addition to Kleene star. This is an **absolute bound independent of automaton size**, not merely a function of the number of states or alphabet cardinality.

The claimed theorem resolves the **uniform-boundedness** version of the generalized star-height problem. It does not say that every regular language has generalized star height zero or one, nor does the pinned overview establish that the bound three is optimal.
