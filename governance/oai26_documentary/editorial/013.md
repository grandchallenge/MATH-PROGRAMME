# OAI-26 family 013: Ostmann's inverse Goldbach conjecture

## The question in plain language

The classical Goldbach problem concerns expressing even integers as sums of primes. An *inverse* question instead asks whether the collection of primes itself could be built as a sum of two simpler infinite sets, apart from finitely many exceptions.

For two sets of nonnegative integers $A$ and $B$, their *sumset* consists of all numbers $a+b$ formed with $a$ in $A$ and $b$ in $B$. A decomposition is called *nontrivial* when both sets contain at least two elements. Ostmann's inverse Goldbach conjecture predicts that no set differing from the primes in only finitely many places can have such a decomposition.

The pinned September 24, 2026 manuscript *The additive indecomposability of the primes* claims to resolve this conjecture. This remains a source-attributed claim, not an independently checked GCL theorem.

## A small example

If $A=\{0,2\}$ and $B=\{1,4\}$, then the sumset is $A+B=\{1,3,4,6\}$. Its structure forces several pairs of numbers to appear together. That rigidity suggests why prime numbers might resist a sumset decomposition: shifting every member of one component by two different values in the other imposes simultaneous additive constraints.

But a finite example shows neither the behavior of all primes nor the possibility of allowing finitely many changes. The full conjecture must defeat every pair of subsets of the nonnegative integers with at least two elements apiece, including infinite sets.

## What is known

The locked OAI-26 publisher overview identifies Family 013 as *Ostmann's inverse Goldbach conjecture* and states:

> No finite modification of the primes can be written as the sum of two subsets of the nonnegative integers, each containing at least two elements.

The overview attributes this proposed result to the manuscript *The additive indecomposability of the primes* dated September 24, 2026. The formulation is stronger than simply saying that the primes themselves are not such a sum: it excludes adding or removing any finite collection of integers as a repair.

The qualification “finite modification” is important. It allows disagreement at finitely many small integers but requires agreement at all sufficiently large integers.
