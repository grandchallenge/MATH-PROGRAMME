# OAI-26 family 098: a doubling set without finite-dimensional bi-Lipschitz coordinates

**Editorial status: DRAFT_UNACCEPTED. NO_CERTIFICATION.** This is a source-pinned documentary account, not an independent verification of a theorem.

## The question in plain language

If a metric space looks small at every scale, must it admit a faithful geometric representation in a finite number of coordinates? The *doubling* property says that every ball can be covered by a bounded number of balls of half the radius. It captures a kind of local geometric simplicity, but does not itself provide coordinates.

A bi-Lipschitz embedding is a map that neither collapses distances nor stretches them beyond a fixed multiplicative factor. Family 098 concerns the Lang–Plaut question: whether doubling subsets of Hilbert space necessarily possess such embeddings into finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces.

## A small example

A line segment in an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space is doubling, and it embeds isometrically into the real line. A finite-dimensional cube is also doubling and already has ordinary Euclidean coordinates.

These examples may suggest that doubling geometry always has a finite-dimensional description. The Family 098 manuscript claims otherwise. Its exceptional set is compact and has a uniformly bounded doubling constant, yet no finite target dimension and no finite bi-Lipschitz distortion suffice. The issue is not that one needs a *large* dimension: the asserted obstruction rules out **every finite dimension**.

## What is known

The source-locked Family 098 catalogue identifies one manuscript, *A doubling Hilbert subset with no finite-dimensional bi-Lipschitz embedding*. The pinned overview reports that **every infinite-dimensional real Banach space contains a compact doubling subset admitting no bi-Lipschitz embedding into any finite-dimensional real normed space**. It additionally states that the doubling constant is universal, independent of the ambient Banach space.

In particular, because real infinite-dimensional Hilbert space is a Banach space, the result would negatively resolve the Lang–Plaut question even for a compact Hilbert subset.

The quantifiers matter. The claim is the existence of a specially constructed subset in each infinite-dimensional real Banach space; it does **not** say that every compact doubling subset fails to embed. Nor is this a statement against general nonlinear embeddings with uncontrolled distortion, or against embeddings into another infinite-dimensional target.
