# OAI-26 Family 001 — Milne's rationality conjecture

**Editorial status: source-based draft; mathematical verification and editorial acceptance pending.**

## The claim in plain language

A geometric object may be studied using several mathematical languages. A complex abelian variety can have a particularly well-behaved reduction modulo a prime, and its geometric classes can then be measured using ordinary algebraic geometry, prime-to-characteristic cohomology, or crystalline cohomology. Those methods need not present the same numbers in the same form.

Milne's rationality question asks for a common *rational* answer when a rational Hodge class is specialized to a good reduction and paired with divisor classes there. A Hodge class is a cohomological class of a prescribed type, not necessarily the class of a known algebraic cycle. The fact that several cohomology theories can carry compatible versions of such a class does not by itself establish the existence of one rational intersection number.

OpenAI's October 7, 2026 manuscript asserts this common-rational-value statement for abelian varieties, including reduction in characteristic two. This page explains the statement and what would be required to assess it. It does not certify the claimed proof.

## A small example or intuition

For a smooth projective curve, a divisor is a finite integer combination of points. Its *degree* is the sum of those integer coefficients. For example, a divisor consisting of two points minus one point has degree one. Divisors with rational coefficients likewise have rational degrees.

In higher-dimensional geometry, intersecting complementary-dimensional algebraic cycles produces a number analogous to a degree. If the cycles are genuinely algebraic and have rational coefficients, a rational intersection number is unsurprising.

The difficult situation is different: begin with a rational Hodge class on a complex abelian variety whose algebraicity has not been established, reduce the variety modulo a prime, and pair the specialized class with divisors on the reduction. Some divisors on the reduction need not come from divisors upstairs. It is therefore not enough to say that ordinary intersection numbers of known algebraic cycles are rational. The theorem must control the specialized Hodge class against these potentially new divisors.

This elementary divisor example motivates the pairing; it does **not** reproduce the general theorem, which concerns higher-codimension cohomology and simultaneous comparison theories.

## What the manuscript asserts

Let an abelian variety of dimension $d$ over an algebraic closure of the rational numbers have good reduction at a prime of residue characteristic $p$. Choose an integer $r$ between zero and $d$, a rational Hodge class of codimension $r$, and a collection of $d-r$ Cartier divisors on the reduction.

The manuscript's Theorem 1.1 asserts that there is **one rational number** equal to the trace of the product of the specialized Hodge class and these divisor classes in every prime-to-$p$ cohomology realization and in the corresponding crystalline realization. The rational number can depend on the chosen class and divisors; the statement is not a claim that every such pairing has the same value.

The result requires good reduction. Its prime-to-$p$ comparison and characteristic-$p$ crystalline comparison are distinct constructions. The manuscript explicitly includes $p=2$.

The introduction distinguishes two logical steps:

1. Theorem 1.1 concerns *rationality of pairings* and does not require algebraicity of every rational Hodge class.
2. A later corollary claims that a specialized Hodge class is represented by a single rational algebraic cycle across the stated realizations **using an additional input**, the asserted rational Hodge theorem for CM abelian varieties (catalogue Family 032). The algebraicity conclusion must not be presented as an unconditional consequence of pairing rationality alone.

The paper describes a proof using transport to a CM abelian variety, reduction to balanced Weil classes, an alternative based on an endomorphism-compatible divisor, and a controlled lift with a prescribed filtration. These are claimed proof steps, not steps independently reproduced in this editorial draft.
