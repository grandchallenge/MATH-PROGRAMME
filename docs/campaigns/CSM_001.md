# Categorical Statistical Mechanics

<p class="page-deck">A GCL research programme about composition, probability, equilibrium and the passage from microscopic systems to macroscopic behaviour.</p>

**Programme:** CSM-001 · **Status:** active research, not a certified mathematical result · **Authority:** Human Steward approval, 4 November 2026 · **Tracker:** [MATH-PROGRAMME #3136](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/3136).

## The question

Statistical mechanics describes large collections of interacting objects by assigning weights or probabilities to microscopic configurations. We ask whether a common language of *systems, interfaces, composition and limits* can reconstruct these familiar calculations, then reveal new theorems about which physical properties survive a change of scale.

Category theory offers a precise vocabulary for composition. But a categorical diagram cannot, by itself, prove that a thermodynamic limit exists or that a phase transition occurs. The programme is designed to preserve that distinction.

## A worked example

Put spins $s_i\in\{0,1\}$ on a periodic chain, with a statistical weight of $2$ for each pair of equal neighbours and $1$ for each unequal pair. Write the transfer matrix

$$
T=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}.
$$

When $N$ links are composed and the endpoints identified, the partition function is

$$
Z_N=\operatorname{tr}(T^N)=3^N+1.
$$

For example, for three spins, $Z_3=28$. Matrix multiplication *sums over intermediate states* while multiplying local weights, exactly as required to evaluate the partition function. The example is **established mathematics**, used here as an explicit baseline, not a new GCL result.

This uses **non-normalized** weights. If every component were turned into a stochastic matrix too early, the discarded normalization factors would erase information required for free energies.

## The main obstruction: the microscopic theory may not survive coarse-graining

A coarse-graining map $C:X\to Y$ lumps detailed configurations $x$ into macrostates $y$. For finite sets and weights $w(x)$, the effective weight is exactly

$$
w_{\mathrm{eff}}(y)=\sum_{x:C(x)=y}w(x).
$$

The total weight does not change. Yet the effective interactions can become nonlocal, and infinite-volume coarse-graining can produce measures that are not Gibbsian for any reasonable local interaction. Exact composition **does not imply** physical locality.

The most important CSM research question is therefore not whether finite marginalization works. It does. The question is **which additional mathematical conditions preserve the physical structures of Gibbs equilibrium when systems become large or infinite**.

## What the literature already establishes

Existing research has laid important foundations. Fritz develops Markov categories for probability. Baez, Lynch and Moeller give compositional thermostatics through entropy-maximizing operadic structures. Sergeant-Perthuis studies categorical Gibbs specifications and phases. Van Enter, Fernández and Sokal establish rigorous non-Gibbsian renormalization pathologies.

These are **prior results**, not discoveries credited to CSM. The programme's source ledger and [technical specification](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/blob/main/research/csm-001/SPEC.md) delineate the hypotheses and prospective novelty.

## What happens next

The first bounded research obligation is to finish an exact finite model: a typed finite category with explicit tensor and trace conventions, independently checked calculations, examples with fields and different boundary conditions, and a source-pinned proof of the claimed semantics. The initial pure-Python specimen and regression tests provide only a small starting point.

The decisive later obligation is a theorem explaining when coarse-graining preserves Gibbs structure and identifying where it fails, without repackaging established non-Gibbsian examples as original results.
