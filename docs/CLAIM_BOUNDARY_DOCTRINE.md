# Claim Boundary Doctrine

<p class="page-deck">A mathematical statement must never be stronger than its support. Status is part of the mathematics because it determines what kind of reliance is permitted.</p>

<div class="doctrine-rule">
  <p class="gc-kicker">The central rule</p>
  <p>Discovery may suggest. Evidence may support. Proof may establish. Certification may warrant public reliance. None of these verbs is interchangeable.</p>
</div>

<div class="support-route" aria-label="Claim support and promotion route">

  <b>→</b>
  <div><span>03</span><strong>Claim</strong><small>An exact statement with assumptions</small></div>
  <b>→</b>

  <b>→</b>

</div>

If any arrow is missing, the status remains provisional.

## The five dangerous substitutions

<div class="substitution-register">
  <article><span>01</span><div><h3>Citation for proof</h3><p>A source may establish history, context, or an imported theorem. It does not certify a new derived claim unless the dependency is explicit.</p></div></article>
  <article><span>02</span><div><h3>Computation for theorem</h3><p>A finite computation proves a finite statement. An infinite conclusion requires a proved bridge from the finite domain.</p></div></article>
  <article><span>03</span><div><h3>Exposition for derivation</h3><p>A compelling explanation can reveal the idea. It cannot discharge a missing logical obligation.</p></div></article>
  <article><span>04</span><div><h3>Formal syntax for meaning</h3><p>A theorem prover may verify a statement other than the one humans intended. Semantic correspondence is itself an obligation.</p></div></article>
  <article><span>05</span><div><h3>Specialist confidence for certification</h3><p>Expert judgment guides review and triage. It does not replace a replayable artifact or checked proof.</p></div></article>
</div>

## Downgrade protocol

A trustworthy programme must be able to move claims downward as deliberately as it moves them upward. Downgrade when:

- an assumption was omitted or misstated;
- a finite computation was generalized without a bridge;
- the formal statement does not match the human claim;
- a dependency changes status;
- independent replay fails;
- a counterexample enters the declared domain;
- the support artifact cannot be reconstructed.

<div class="downgrade-note"><strong>Downgrading is not embarrassment.</strong><p>It is the mechanism by which the programme protects accumulated trust.</p></div>

## Review questions

1. What is the exact claim?
2. Which assumptions are active?
3. What is the support type?
4. Which artifact can an independent reviewer inspect?
5. What domain does the support actually cover?
6. What would refute or downgrade the claim?
7. What exact action would promote it one level?

## Motto

> Trust is not a tone. Trust is a boundary.
