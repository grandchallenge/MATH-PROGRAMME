# MATH-PROGRAMME Documentary Library

The Documentary Library translates the programme's hardest mathematical terrain into illuminated, source-conscious monographs for the enjoyment and enlightenment of the lay reader. Each volume follows the **GCL–Chaidez** pattern: wonder first, definitions before claims, proof spines before conclusions, adversarial guardrails against attractive errors, and a technical appendix for readers who wish to descend beneath the imagery.

!!! info "Authority and claim boundary"
    The plates are pedagogical artworks, not proof diagrams. The typeset source, cited literature, campaign ledgers, and claim-level trust matrices govern every mathematical assertion. Open problems remain open; the Poincaré volume is a reconstruction of a solved theorem and makes no new-proof claim.

## Editorial form

The series uses cream parchment, deep navy, restrained gold, classical serif typography, ornamental framing, luminous mathematical imagery, florid explanatory prose, explicit theorem boundaries, and technical appendices that deepen rather than merely repeat the lay account.

## Artifact preservation

The repository stores the authoritative LaTeX text and a checksum manifest for every rendered PDF and complete illustrated source bundle. Large rendered and bundled editions are release-class artifacts rather than ordinary documentation source: their hashes, byte sizes, page counts, and source identities are fixed in [`ARTIFACT_MANIFEST.json`](ARTIFACT_MANIFEST.json). This prevents silent substitution while keeping the documentation history reviewable.

## Reading order

There is no required order. A reader seeking geometry may begin with Poincaré or Hodge; arithmetic with BSD or Riemann; physical law with Navier–Stokes or Yang–Mills; computation with P versus NP. Each volume begins again from first principles.
