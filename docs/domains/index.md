<p class="page-deck">Eight enduring areas of mathematical inquiry, from prime numbers and geometric shapes to the foundations of computation. Explore the questions that interest you, what is already known and why so much remains to discover.</p>

## The questions we study

**20 October 2026:** [Millennium Prize Problems status](../MILLENNIUM_PRIZE_STATUS.md) distinguishes Clay’s seven public classifications from GCL’s separately bounded research. Clay lists Navier–Stokes as **Active** following an announced result under evaluation; GCL’s narrower unforced critical-integrability question remains open.

These subjects are part of GCL's long-term research collection. Some have active projects at the moment; others are maintained as reference material. A place in this collection does **not** mean that GCL has solved the problem or is currently working on every aspect of it.

The linked pages provide fuller descriptions and references to original research. The open problems are not declared solved by their inclusion here.

## Where to begin

If you enjoy **numbers**, try the Riemann Hypothesis or Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer. If you prefer **shapes**, begin with the Hodge Conjecture or the Poincaré reconstruction. Fluid motion and Yang–Mills connect mathematics with **physical theories**. P versus NP concerns **computation**; Union-Closed Sets offers a particularly approachable question about finite collections.

The [Documentary Library](../documentaries/index.md) provides illustrated introductions to many of these subjects. The [Research and Results](../SHOWCASE.md) page describes selected work with published explanations or verified mathematical outputs.

## What is GCL doing now?

Our [Current Work](../CURRENT_WORK.md) page follows particular questions and studies in progress. For example, work on elliptic curves concerns a restricted argument involving the prime 2; it is not a proof of the full Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture. Likewise, the fluid-flow work examines a specific mathematical estimate, not a general solution of the Navier–Stokes problem.

Other research, including condensed mathematics and mathematical models of folding mechanisms, does not fit neatly into these eight historical categories. The estate is an enduring map of subjects, **not a ranking of importance or an exhaustive account of current activity**.

## How to judge a mathematical claim

An open problem can inspire valuable partial results without being solved. A calculation may verify particular cases; a formal proof may establish a precisely stated special case; an explanatory article may report results previously proved by others.

To understand what GCL has actually established, consult the linked research and supporting evidence. An article's publication, a completed research task or a successfully checked computer program should not be read as proof of a stronger conjecture.

For specialists, the [domain registry](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/blob/main/DOMAIN_REGISTRY.yaml) preserves the established list of domains and their source references. The registry records classification and continuity; the mathematical standing of each claim rests with its proofs and sources.

**Riemann Hypothesis verification note.** The original technical record states: `WP01/WP02 implemented, merged, and CI-passed, with formal promotion withheld`. This means those preliminary checks were completed, **not** that the Riemann Hypothesis has been proved or that the relevant results have been formally accepted.

For readers matching these subjects to internal research records, the established campaign identifiers are `UC`, `NS-CI-001`, `HC-001`, `BSD-001`, `PC-001`, `YM-001`, `PNP-001` and `RH-001`, in the same order as the table above. They are reference codes, not mathematical results.
